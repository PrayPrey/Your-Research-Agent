# Abstract

Claim-Conditioned Probability (CCP) uses NLI-based conditioning to detect hallucinations, reporting +0.05–0.10 ROC-AUC improvements. **We could not reproduce the baseline**: claim-type mass ratio ($\rho_j$) values were 20–80× lower than expected (median 0.0354 factual, 0.0103 creative vs inferred range 0.75–0.85^†^), with no statistical separation ($p = 1.0$, Cohen's $d = -0.0635$). Root cause: DeBERTa-v3-base NLI, trained on SNLI/MNLI semantic similarity tasks, assigns ~90% probability mass to "neutral" for claim-context pairs, mechanistically driving $\rho_j \to 0$. This NLI miscalibration for factual verification—a known issue in domain adaptation literature (Pan & Yang 2010)—prevented us from testing our hypothesis (CCP degradation on creative text). Methodologically, this case study illustrates a critical lesson: measurement validity is prerequisite for hypothesis testing. We provide transparent failure documentation and propose four reproducibility practices adapted from Dodge et al. (2019): (R1) report raw metric distributions; (R2) validate NLI calibration on known examples; (R3) document claim decomposition with inter-method agreement; (R4) provide public code with baseline replication notebooks. With 50+ hallucination detection papers published in 2024 citing NLI-based methods, transparent failures prevent costly replication waste across labs—this negative result is our contribution.

^†^Expected range inferred from CCP paper's ROC-AUC claims; see §3.1

**Keywords**: hallucination detection, NLI domain adaptation, reproducibility, negative results, measurement validity
