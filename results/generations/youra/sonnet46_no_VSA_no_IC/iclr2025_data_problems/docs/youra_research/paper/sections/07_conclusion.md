# 7. Conclusion

We began with the question of which Pile domains drive which benchmarks — a question that practitioners care about deeply but that no prior study had answered empirically from a controlled within-family design. We found that the infrastructure to answer this question already exists in Pythia's exact dataloaders and The Pile's documented domain structure; that two necessary empirical prerequisites are confirmed; and that the primary specificity test is blocked by a structural data gap whose exact dimensions we have documented and whose fix is straightforward.

### Summary

We make four concrete contributions. First, we demonstrate for the first time that The Pile's domains are separable by automated cognitive task pattern proxies with extremely large effect sizes (entity density η²=0.9915, narrative coherence η²=0.9142, formal syntax density η²=0.9821; all p≈0) — providing empirical grounding for the domain-specificity mechanism that data mixing theories have assumed but not measured. Second, we validate reusable infrastructure for within-family domain exposure trajectory analysis from Pythia's exact dataloaders, confirming that 10 of 22 Pile domains show sufficient variance (std>0.001) across 154 checkpoints for panel regression analysis. Third, we document a reproducible structural finding: single-shard Pile samples systematically miss 6 of 22 domains (Books3, OWT2, GitHub, OpenSubtitles, BookCorpus2, YoutubeSubtitles), establishing the full 134M-document lookup as the minimum required dataset for domain-benchmark specificity analysis. Fourth, we provide preliminary evidence from the 70M model scale suggesting that Wikipedia exposure may be general-purpose at small scales — improving HellaSwag more than MMLU — though this result is confounded by 70M floor effects and N=10 sampling and requires replication at ≥1B.

### Future Directions

The most important next step is not a new experiment — it is completing the one we started. Full 134M-document domain lookup (all 30 Pile shards, estimated ~150 compute-hours) will provide non-zero Books3, GitHub, and OWT2 exposure signals. Completion of the benchmark evaluation cache (154 checkpoints × ≥3 model sizes, ~460 evaluations) will enable reliable Spearman analysis at N=154 and panel regression at N=3 entities. With both, the fully implemented h-m3 pipeline can execute P1 through P4 tests without modification.

Two theoretical questions opened by our preliminary findings deserve dedicated follow-up. First, does domain-benchmark specificity emerge at a scale threshold? If ρ(Wikipedia,MMLU) > ρ(Wikipedia,HellaSwag) for 6.9B but not 70M, this documents a scale-phase transition in how Wikipedia content is encoded — a finding of independent interest for scaling law research. Second, does the training phase matter? Splitting the 154 checkpoints into early (steps 0–10K, general language modeling) and late (steps 10K–143K, capability specialization) phases and computing phase-specific Spearman correlations may reveal that domain specificity is a late-training phenomenon hidden by early-phase general improvement.

### Closing

The domain-benchmark specificity hypothesis remains open. But its empirical scaffolding has been built: the mechanisms are grounded (η²>0.91), the covariate variation is confirmed (10/22 domains), the regression pipeline is validated (2,943 lines, 21/24 tests), and the exact data requirements are specified (134M docs, N≥100 checkpoints per model). We know precisely where the experiment stopped and exactly what is needed to complete it. In empirical science, that is a contribution in itself.
