# 6. Discussion

## 6.1 Key Findings Interpretation

**The 6.8× efficiency ratio as a practical benchmark.** The efficiency ratio of 6.804× establishes a strong quantitative baseline for weight-space encoder comparison that the field has lacked. This number means that equivariant encoders can operate on model zoos roughly 7× smaller than plain encoders and achieve equivalent relative performance. In practical terms, the critical threshold is the crossing of the 90%-peak R² threshold: flat-MLP needs 1,000 models; GNN-NFN needs approximately 147. This has direct implications for zoo design — an organization collecting model checkpoints for downstream weight-space analysis should target at least 250 models to benefit from equivariant encoders, but can start with as few as 150 and still maintain a significant advantage over plain alternatives.

**The data-regime crossover as a new constraint on equivariant inductive bias.** The N=100 crossover — where PermAug (R²=0.138) outperforms GNN-NFN (R²=−0.016) — reveals something important about how structural equivariance operates in practice. Equivariant inductive bias is often described as a data-efficient prior: by constraining the hypothesis space to symmetry-consistent functions, the encoder needs fewer examples to find the right solution. Our results suggest this characterization is incomplete. At N=100, the graph encoder appears to suffer from underfitting: the GNN-NFN architecture requires a minimum diversity of weight-graph topologies to learn useful node embeddings. Below this threshold, the structural constraint provides no useful training signal — and may actively harm performance by preventing the encoder from exploiting asymmetric correlations that happen to be predictive in this data regime.

This finding connects to a broader pattern in deep learning: specialist architectures require sufficient data to demonstrate their advantages over generalists. CNNs outperform MLPs on images when data is plentiful, but MLPs can match or exceed CNNs on very small image datasets where the convolutional bias has insufficient examples to calibrate. Our results suggest an analogous threshold for equivariant graph encoders on weight spaces.

**PermAug and structural equivariance as distinguishable strategies.** Assumption A3 of Phase 2A predicted that permutation augmentation would provide a valid approximation of structural equivariance. The N=100 crossover refutes this assumption: the two strategies are not interchangeable and produce qualitatively different learning dynamics. This is scientifically valuable — it means practitioners can distinguish between data-level and structural symmetry enforcement, and the distinction matters. PermAug's benefit at N=100 appears to be primarily a data quantity effect (11× expansion provides more gradient signal per epoch); GNN-NFN's benefit at N≥250 appears to be a structural hypothesis-space constraint. The N=500–1000 convergence of the fraction toward 0.5–0.8 suggests that at intermediate scales, both mechanisms contribute and are partially substitutable.

**Connection to expressivity theory.** The full-scale convergence (GNN-NFN≈0.894, flat-MLP≈0.886, Δ=0.008) provides empirical support for Dayan et al. [2026]'s theoretical result that all permutation-equivariant weight-space networks are expressivity-equivalent. Our data suggest that this equivalence is approximately achieved at the full zoo scale (~7,000 models), and that the regime where equivariant encoders provide a practical advantage is N∈[150, 1000] — the medium data regime. This characterization of the *window* of equivariant advantage is a contribution that the theoretical result alone cannot provide.

## 6.2 Limitations

**CIFAR-10 only.** All property-prediction efficiency results are from the CIFAR-10 CNN model zoo. The MNIST MLP zoo — where DWSNets is architecturally appropriate — was not available in our local environment. The core efficiency claim (6.8×) is confirmed on CIFAR-10 only; MNIST extension is the highest-priority follow-up. The relative comparison (GNN-NFN vs. flat-MLP) is preserved regardless of zoo diversity issues.

**DWSNets excluded from property prediction.** DWSNets requires M>2 fully connected layers; the CIFAR-10 CNN zoo has 2 FC layers, making it architecturally incompatible. We confirm DWSNets equivariance on synthetic MLP weights (max_diff=7.45×10⁻⁹) but do not report property-prediction results for DWSNets. The claim "equivariant weight-space encoders" should be read as "GNN-NFN on CIFAR-10 CNN zoo" until DWSNets on MNIST MLP zoo is tested.

**N=100 crossover is single-seed.** The most novel finding — PermAug>GNN-NFN at N=100 — is based on a single random seed. With high variance at small N, the true ordering under multi-seed bootstrap CI is unknown. We present this as a preliminary observation requiring 10-seed replication with confidence intervals before it can be claimed as robust. The mechanistic interpretation (underfitting at N=100) is supported by the sharp R² jump from N=100 to N=250, but the exact crossover point and magnitude need confirmation.

**Low zoo diversity.** The CIFAR-10 zoo accuracy variance is 0.025 (below the diversity threshold), as models cluster near convergence. This may make the prediction task easier for both encoders. The relative efficiency ratio is preserved (both face the same target distribution), but absolute R² values should not be used as a general benchmark for harder, more diverse zoos.

**Hyperparameter fairness at small N.** Adam optimizer hyperparameters were tuned on the full-data condition. At N=100, the learning rate may disadvantage GNN-NFN's more complex graph architecture relative to flat-MLP. A learning rate sweep at N=100 for GNN-NFN is needed to isolate the hyperparameter confound from the architectural underfitting explanation.

## 6.3 Broader Impact

This work provides actionable guidance for practitioners designing weight-space learning pipelines under data constraints. The decision boundary we identify (~150–250 models) reduces the guesswork in encoder selection and zoo design. There are no foreseeable negative societal impacts from this methodological work. Reducing the data requirement for weight-space property prediction may lower barriers to applying these techniques in resource-constrained settings, which is a positive outcome.
