# Related Work

## Weight-Space Self-Supervised Learning

The idea of learning representations from neural network weights — treating checkpoints as data — has attracted growing attention. Hyper-Representations [Schürholt et al., 2022] first demonstrated that training an autoencoder on populations of neural network weights produces latent codes that recover hyperparameters, accuracy, and generalization gap without task labels. SANE [Schürholt et al., 2024] extended this to a hierarchical transformer architecture with contrastive SSL, achieving R²=0.72 on heterogeneous model zoo property prediction. Both methods use flat tokenization: weight matrices are chunked into fixed-size tokens, processed positionally, and aggregated — an approach that implicitly assumes weight token positions are comparable across networks.

This assumption holds when training and test architectures share the same layer structure. When architectures differ substantially (e.g., MLP/CNN vs. ViT), flat tokenization loses meaning: a weight in position *i* of a CNN convolutional filter occupies no corresponding position in a ViT attention projection. Our work identifies the consequence: SANE's latent representations collapse to near-constant codes for cross-architecture inputs (std≈0.0014), and property prediction performance drops to R²=0.072 — near noise for a regression task. The graph encoding approach we adopt directly addresses this structural mismatch.

## Equivariant Graph Neural Networks for Weight Encoding

A parallel research strand represents neural networks as computation graphs and uses equivariant GNNs as encoders. The foundational theoretical work [Maron et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023] characterized all permutation-equivariant/invariant linear layers over weight spaces, establishing that the space of such layers is substantially richer than traditional convolutional or FC architectures. Neural Functional Transformers [Zhou et al., 2023] and NF-Layers [Kofinas et al., 2024 ICLR Oral] demonstrated that graph-based encoders achieve strong performance on property prediction within a fixed architecture family.

Critically, Kofinas et al. [2024] showed that graph-based encoders can generalize *across* architecture families in the *supervised* setting, achieving R²=0.71 on held-out CNNs after training on MLPs. ScaleGMN [Kalogeropoulos et al., NeurIPS 2024 Oral] extended this framework to include scale equivariance (the full monomial matrix group), showing +8-12 R² points over permutation-only baselines in supervised same-architecture settings. Our work differs in two key dimensions: (1) we operate in the *self-supervised* setting, requiring no property labels at training time; and (2) we target *cross-architecture* transfer to a substantially different architecture family (ViTs with LayerNorm), which changes the benefit profile of scale equivariance in unexpected ways.

## Cross-Architecture Weight Representation Transfer

The most directly related work is Ballerini et al. [2025], who applied contrastive SSL to diverse neural radiance field architectures (NeRFs) and demonstrated cross-architecture property transfer without target-domain training data. They use a permutation-equivariant graph encoder and show that SSL representations trained on one NeRF architecture family can predict rendering quality for architectures not seen during training. Our work generalizes this paradigm to general-purpose neural architectures (MLPs, CNNs, ViTs) — a broader and more architecturally diverse setting.

The recent survey by [Navon et al., 2026] characterizes the field into three tracks: Understanding (property prediction), Representation (SSL/generative on weights), and Generation (weight diffusion). Cross-architecture SSL transfer sits at the intersection of Representation and Understanding, but has received less attention than within-family methods. The ViT Model Zoo of [Zhu et al., 2025], which provides 250 real ViT-S/16 ImageNet checkpoints with accuracy labels, opens the possibility of evaluating cross-architecture SSL at scale; we use their dataset as our test set.

## Symmetry and Normalization Layers

The interaction between equivariance symmetry groups and neural network normalization has received recent theoretical attention. Anonymous [2025, arXiv:2510.08300] argues that LayerNorm in transformer architectures fixes the gauge degree of freedom corresponding to activation scale, effectively reducing the symmetry group of ViT weight spaces from the monomial matrix group (scale+permutation) to the symmetric group (permutation only). Our empirical finding — that scale equivariance *hurts* rather than helps ViT cross-architecture transfer — provides experimental support for this theoretical prediction. This interaction between normalization layer choice and the appropriate equivariance group for weight encoding is not captured by existing benchmarks, which typically evaluate within a single architecture family (without normalization diversity).

## Positioning Our Work

Existing weight-space SSL methods achieve strong results within architecture families but fail under architectural distribution shift. Existing equivariant graph encoders achieve cross-architecture transfer in supervised settings but do not address the SSL setting. We bridge these: graph-based SSL with permutation equivariance achieves meaningful cross-architecture transfer (MLP+CNN → ViT) without property labels or target-domain training data. The scale equivariance reversal we discover is a new empirical finding with a clear theoretical grounding, constraining when scale equivariance is a useful inductive bias for weight encoding.
