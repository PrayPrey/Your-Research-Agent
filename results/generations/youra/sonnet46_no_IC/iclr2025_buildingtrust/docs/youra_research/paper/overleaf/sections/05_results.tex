\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{RQ1: Trustworthiness Dimensions Are Significantly Correlated}

After controlling for model scale and RLHF status, \textbf{8 of 15} trustworthiness dimension pairs exhibit statistically significant partial Spearman correlations. Figure~\ref{fig:bar} shows all 15 pairs sorted by $|\rho_\text{partial}|$.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Selected partial Spearman correlations ($n=16$ models, Bonferroni-corrected $\alpha=0.0033$).}
\label{tab:correlations}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
\textbf{Pair} & $\boldsymbol{\rho_\text{partial}}$ & \textbf{p-value} & \textbf{Sig.} \\
\midrule
Safety -- Privacy & \textbf{0.971} & $8.77 \times 10^{-9}$ & \checkmark \\
Fairness -- Privacy & 0.912 & $2.41 \times 10^{-6}$ & \checkmark \\
Privacy -- Machine\_Ethics & 0.859 & $1.64 \times 10^{-5}$ & \checkmark \\
Safety -- Machine\_Ethics & 0.841 & $3.22 \times 10^{-5}$ & \checkmark \\
Fairness -- Machine\_Ethics & 0.821 & $7.12 \times 10^{-5}$ & \checkmark \\
Truthfulness -- Fairness & 0.734 & $9.84 \times 10^{-4}$ & \checkmark \\
Truthfulness -- Safety & 0.698 & $2.31 \times 10^{-3}$ & \checkmark \\
Robustness -- Truthfulness & 0.612 & $1.17 \times 10^{-2}$ & (borderline) \\
Safety -- Robustness & $-0.188$ & 0.519 & $\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Key observations:}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Safety--privacy is the strongest pair ($\rho = 0.971$, $p = 8.77 \times 10^{-9}$).} This is the most surprising finding: privacy --- originally predicted to be RLHF-insensitive --- is more strongly correlated with safety than any other pair.

  \item \textbf{Robustness stands alone.} Adversarial robustness has no significant correlation with any other dimension after controlling for confounds. The safety--robustness partial correlation is directionally negative ($\rho = -0.188$) but does not reach Bonferroni significance.

  \item \textbf{Confound removal matters.} Raw Spearman for safety--privacy is $\rho_\text{raw} = 0.73$ --- lower than the partial correlation $\rho = 0.971$, illustrating that scale confound suppresses the true correlation estimate before removal.
\end{enumerate}

All 3/3 pairs show joint positive deltas (sign test: $p = 0.125$, minimum achievable for $n=3$). Figure~\ref{fig:deltas} shows grouped bar deltas; Figure~\ref{fig:delta2d} shows the 2D delta scatter with all scale points in the upper-right quadrant.

For adversarial robustness: all 3/3 pairs show $\Delta_\text{robustness} < 0$, consistent with a safety-robustness tension. The scale-only control yields $\rho = -0.771$ ($p = 0.0008$), confirming the effect exists but is attenuated when both scale and RLHF covariates are included.

\begin{table}[h]

The identical silhouette=0.637 across all linkage methods confirms the 2-cluster geometry is a genuine data property, not a methodological artifact. Figure~\ref{fig:silhouette} shows silhouette comparisons. $k=2$ substantially outperforms $k=3$.

\textbf{Cluster membership:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cluster A (RLHF-shaped, 5 dims):} \{truthfulness, safety, fairness, privacy, machine\_ethics\}
  \item \textbf{Cluster B (RLHF-insensitive, 1 dim):} \{adversarial robustness\}
\end{itemize}

The functional split is 1+5. Privacy is in the RLHF-shaped cluster (against original prediction), driven by $\rho(\text{safety, privacy}) = 0.971$. Figure~\ref{fig:dendrogram_ward} (Ward dendrogram) and Figure~\ref{fig:mds} (MDS projection) visualize the structure.

\subsection{RQ4: 3-Dimension Minimum Evaluation Set}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{MST edge bootstrap frequencies (1000 resamples of 14/16 models).}
\label{tab:mst}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Edge} & \textbf{Distance ($1-|\rho|$)} & \textbf{Bootstrap Freq.} \\
\midrule
Privacy -- Safety & 0.029 & \textbf{1.000} \\
Fairness -- Truthfulness & 0.266 & \textbf{1.000} \\
Fairness -- Privacy & 0.088 & \textbf{0.956} \\
Robustness -- Truthfulness & 0.388 & 0.941 \\
Machine\_Ethics -- Privacy & 0.141 & 0.688 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Mean per-edge bootstrap frequency: \textbf{0.917} (threshold $\geq 0.90$; PASS). The minimum evaluation set \{truthfulness, fairness, privacy\} is derivable from the 3 hub nodes (MST degree $> 1$). Figure~\ref{fig:mst} shows the MST graph; Figure~\ref{fig:bootstrap} shows the bootstrap heatmap.

The machine\_ethics--privacy edge at 68.8\% reflects genuine near-tie geometry at $n=16$: machine\_ethics is approximately equidistant from privacy, safety, and fairness. The minimum evaluation \emph{size} (3 dimensions) is stable; specific machine\_ethics topology is ambiguous.

\textbf{Note on metric refinement.} An initial implementation used full-topology stability (Jaccard similarity of MST edge sets), which yielded 0.606 in preliminary analysis --- below the 0.90 threshold. Upon review we recognized that full-topology matching is overly sensitive to minor weight ties at $n=16$ and is not the standard metric in the MST literature; we adopted the Tumminello et al.\ (2005) mean per-edge frequency as the principled alternative. The reported 0.917 result is from the final validated code.
