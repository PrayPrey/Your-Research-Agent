
Prior work is organized by what each line reintroduces that the present method removes: supervision, sampling, decoding-time-only use of cross-layer signal, or the absence of a detection evaluation altogether.

\textbf{Supervised probing of intermediate layers.} SAPLMA showed that hidden states carry probe-extractable truthfulness information, with the optimal layer shifting across models and distributions \citep{Azaria2023} --- the instability the per-model selection here is designed around. Subsequent work located truthfulness information concentrated in intermediate representations \citep{Orgad2024}, and FEPoID automated layer choice via intrinsic-dimension criteria feeding supervised MLP probes, reporting average AUROC of roughly 0.73--0.85 across QA benchmarks \citep{Wang2026}. CLAP probes attention states across layers jointly \citep{Suresh2025}; ICR Probe trains on residual-stream update dynamics \citep{Zhang2025}; HalluShift trains a classifier over internal distribution-shift features \citep{Dasgupta2025}; MIND removes manual annotation but still trains a detector \citep{Su2024}. This line constitutes the supervised skyline: it reaches higher AUROC than reported here, and no claim is made to exceed it. What every entry shares is a trained component between the hidden state and the score. The question addressed here is orthogonal --- whether the intermediate-layer signal is readable without one --- and the contribution is removing supervision at proof-of-concept scale, not out-scoring probes. A training-free readout also supplies the probe literature with a missing baseline: what the representation yields in token space before any probe is fitted.

\textbf{Multi-sample uncertainty quantification.} LLM calibration varies with model and format \citep{Kadavath2022} --- the root of the output-layer fragility that motivates this work. Semantic entropy clusters roughly ten sampled generations by meaning and scores their entropy \citep{Farquhar2024}; INSIDE computes an EigenScore over the embedding covariance of multiple sampled responses \citep{Zhang2024SLED}. Both detect well, and both pay an order of magnitude more inference than the pass being monitored. Semantic Entropy Probes show that single-pass hidden states approximate semantic entropy --- but recover it through trained linear probes \citep{Kossen2024}. The present method drops both the sampling and the probe: the statistics cost one greedy generation plus one teacher-forced re-forward over prompt and answer --- roughly twice the monitored pass, against the tenfold added cost above.

\textbf{Cross-layer shift at decoding time.} DoLa contrasts a premature layer's lens distribution against the mature layer's to improve factuality during generation \citep{Chuang2023}; END reweights token probabilities by cross-layer entropy changes \citep{Wu2025}; SLED generalizes layer-contrast decoding across model families and scales \citep{Zhang2024}. This line validated that adjacent-layer distribution shift carries factuality-relevant information, then used it exclusively to steer generation; none scores the shift as a detection statistic. The adjacent-layer KL evaluated here promotes this line's signal to detection time, evaluated by AUROC, where it is the best signal in two of six cells.

\textbf{Lens instruments without detection evaluations.} The logit lens showed that intermediate hidden states decode into interpretable next-token distributions [nostalgebraist, 2020]. The Tuned Lens characterized the raw lens as a biased, family-dependent instrument and trained affine translators to correct it, noting that the LLaMA/Mistral lineage is comparatively well-behaved \citep{Belrose2023} --- the characterization the degeneracy screen in Section 3.3 operationalizes instead of trained correction. Entropy-Lens computes the same per-layer logit-lens entropy used here and validates it as a signature of transformer computation tracking candidate-set expansion and pruning \citep{Ali2025} --- without evaluating it for detection. This work takes the same instrument and changes only the question asked of it.

\textbf{Adversarial results.} Two recent papers might appear to preempt this direction and, on inspection, bound it instead. Kim et al. [2025] found that layer-wise trajectories of the final-prediction-token probability are largely aligned for certain and uncertain outputs. That is a different measurement: one token's probability path, under a tuned lens, on multiple-choice tasks --- not full-vocabulary distribution statistics on open-ended QA with per-model layer selection. Their negative trajectory result and the positive existence result here are jointly consistent, and the scope boundary between the two measurements is load-bearing for the present claims. Separately, Chi et al. [2025] argue that internal states mainly reflect knowledge recall rather than truthfulness. This is not contested here: the claims in this paper are stated as class-separation under standard correctness labels, not as causal measurements of truthfulness, and the familiarity confound remains an explicitly unaddressed limitation.

\textbf{Positioning.} Across these lines, the premise that depth carries hallucination signal is settled; what remains unsettled is whether anything must be trained, sampled, or intervened upon to read it. No prior work evaluates raw logit-lens uncertainty statistics --- entropy, max-token probability, or adjacent-layer KL --- as sampling-free detection scores with per-model layer selection, and none anchors such an evaluation against baselines computed inside the same protocol.
