# Discussion

## 6.1 Key Findings and Interpretation

**Two mechanistically distinct pathways to WGA improvement.** Our results confirm that WGA improvement in the Waterbirds setting does not reflect a single underlying mechanism. DFR achieves WGA = 0.91 by recalibrating the head while leaving backbone features — with their full spurious background encoding (ERM background probe accuracy ≈ 0.984) — entirely unchanged. GroupDRO achieves WGA = 0.88 by modifying backbone representations such that background information is significantly less linearly extractable (probe accuracy drops from 0.984 to 0.953, p = 0.0039, d = 6.48). These are two distinct robustification pathways, not two points on a continuum.

This finding has a non-obvious implication: the backbone's spurious encoding level is neither necessary nor sufficient for WGA improvement in isolation. DFR demonstrates that high spurious encoding in the backbone does not prevent high WGA if the head is correctly calibrated. GroupDRO demonstrates that reducing backbone spurious encoding also yields high WGA. The mechanisms are alternative solutions to the same problem, not hierarchically ordered approaches.

**GroupDRO backbone modification is substantial and consistent.** The backbone/head L2 ratio of 6.47–7.03 across seeds indicates that GroupDRO's group-reweighted gradient signal primarily reshapes backbone weights — the backbone is the dominant site of modification, not a secondary effect. This is consistent with theoretical expectations from Sagawa et al. [2019] (minority group upweighting forces the model to learn features that work for background-atypical examples) but has not been directly quantified at the weight level in prior work. The DFR negative control (backbone/head ratio = 0.000/anything = undefined, since DFR backbone differences are exactly 0) eliminates the possibility that apparent GroupDRO backbone changes are checkpoint-specific artifacts.

**The causal chain holds, with appropriate caveats.** The three-step verified chain (H-M1 → H-M2 → H-M3) establishes that the mechanism is real and measurable at each step. However, we note that our measurement (linear probe decodability) captures the *output* of backbone modification, not the *cause*. We cannot distinguish between two competing mechanistic explanations for the reduced decodability: (a) GroupDRO actively *suppresses* spurious feature directions in layer4 feature space, or (b) GroupDRO *dilutes* spurious information by adding more diverse, non-spurious representations, reducing the proportion of spurious information without actively removing it. Both produce identical probe accuracy signatures. Distinguishing them requires directional feature subspace analysis (e.g., SCER-style spurious subspace extraction [Park et al., 2025]), which we leave for future work.

**Connection to the literature.** Our finding that GroupDRO implicitly reduces background linear decodability aligns with Park et al. [2025], who show that explicitly regularizing spurious feature subspace directions (SCER) improves WGA — validating the link between decodability and WGA improvement that H-P2 captures. Our weight-difference analysis corroborates Raymond et al. [2026], who show GroupDRO reshapes representations across layers. We partially nuance Izmailov et al. [2022], who suggested WGA improvement is "primarily in the head, not backbone" — our H-M2 results show the backbone changes substantially (ratio 6.47–7.03), though this does not contradict the head's importance for WGA per se.

## 6.2 Limitations

**L1: Small n (n=3 seeds per method).** All paired t-tests use n=3 seed pairs. One-sided t-test with n=3 has approximately 55% power to detect Cohen's d = 0.8. The primary finding (H-M3, d = 6.48) is unaffected — power is not a concern when d >> 0.8. However, H-M2's linear probe result (p = 0.0526, d = 1.64) is borderline SUGGESTIVE rather than CONFIRMED, likely because H-M2 used a smaller probe training set than H-M3's full test set (N=5,794). The limitation is structural: izmailovpavel/spurious\_feature\_learning releases exactly 3 seeds per method.

**L2: Single dataset and architecture.** All experiments use Waterbirds WILDS with ResNet-50. Whether GroupDRO reduces backbone spurious encoding on CelebA (hair color spurious correlation), MultiNLI (syntactic spurious features), or with ViT/DINO architectures is an open question. ResNet-50 is standard for this benchmark; findings may not generalize to attention-based architectures where layer4-equivalent feature localization differs.

**L3: H-P2 WGA correlation is SUGGESTIVE, not CONFIRMED, at n=9.** The full 9-checkpoint correlation (r=−0.504, p=0.083) does not meet the pre-registered CONFIRMED threshold (CI upper bound = +0.084 > 0). The root cause is structural: Izmailov et al. [2022] report method-level WGA constants (same WGA for all 3 seeds within a method), creating within-method collinearity that inflates bootstrap CI width at n=9. The ERM+GroupDRO ablation reaches CONFIRMED (r=−0.755, p=0.041), providing converging evidence, but we report the full n=9 analysis as our primary exploratory finding with appropriate uncertainty.

**L4: Linear probe measures decodability, not mechanism.** Reduced probe accuracy establishes that background information is less linearly extractable from GroupDRO layer4 features; it does not establish *why* decodability decreases. The "suppression vs. dilution" mechanistic ambiguity is acknowledged and cannot be resolved from probe accuracy alone. We restrict all mechanistic claims to the measured construct (linear decodability) rather than the underlying cause.

**L5: Core attribute probe not measured.** H-M3 measured background (spurious) probe accuracy; we did not measure core attribute (bird species) probe accuracy from GroupDRO features. This leaves open the question of whether GroupDRO reduces core-feature encoding alongside spurious-feature reduction. Measuring core probe accuracy is an important future validation step.

## 6.3 Broader Impact

This work contributes a diagnostic framework — spurious attribute linear probe accuracy on frozen backbone features — for mechanistically characterizing robustification methods. The framework is low-cost (no new training, forward-pass only), reproducible (standard sklearn tools), and directly interpretable (probe accuracy = information content about spurious attribute in backbone features).

**Positive implications.** Understanding the backbone-vs-head distinction can inform method selection: when group labels for head retraining are available, DFR's simpler head-only intervention may suffice without backbone modification. When backbone-level change is desired (e.g., for transfer learning scenarios where backbone features will be reused across many tasks), GroupDRO's backbone modification may be advantageous.

**Potential concerns.** Our work uses the izmailovpavel/spurious\_feature\_learning checkpoints — trained on Waterbirds, a benchmark constructed to test robustification methods rather than deployed in production. We do not recommend direct application of these findings to production systems without validation on task-specific data. The assumption that "reduced spurious probe accuracy → improved real-world robustness" is validated only in the Waterbirds WILDS setting.
