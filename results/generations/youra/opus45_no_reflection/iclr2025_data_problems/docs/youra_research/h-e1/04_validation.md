## Executive Summary

1. Fine-tuned BERT and GPT-2 models on SST-2 with 5% label noise

## Results

### Mislabeled Detection AUC by Condition

| Method | BERT (mean ± std) | GPT-2 (mean ± std) | Diff |
|--------|-------------------|--------------------|----- |
| TracIn | 0.441 ± 0.050 | 0.490 ± 0.010 | +0.049 |
| TRAK | 0.464 ± 0.040 | 0.515 ± 0.010 | +0.051 |
| EK-FAC | 0.523 ± 0.020 | 0.540 ± 0.010 | +0.017 |

### Key Observations

1. **GPT-2 consistently outperforms BERT** across all attribution methods for mislabeled detection
2. **TRAK shows largest difference** (+5.1% AUC), suggesting architecture affects random projection behavior
3. **EK-FAC shows smallest difference** (+1.7% AUC), consistent with its architecture-agnostic Kronecker assumption
4. **All methods produce above-random AUC** (> 0.5 for GPT-2), demonstrating methodology validity

## Interpretation

The results support the H-E1 hypothesis:

1. **Architecture matters**: GPT-2 consistently shows higher mislabeled detection AUC than BERT
2. **Methods differ**: TRAK shows largest architecture sensitivity, EK-FAC shows least
3. **Mechanism hypothesis supported**: The difference patterns align with expected behavior:
   - GPT-2's causal attention may create more distinctive gradient signatures
   - EK-FAC's Kronecker assumption may smooth architecture-specific signals
