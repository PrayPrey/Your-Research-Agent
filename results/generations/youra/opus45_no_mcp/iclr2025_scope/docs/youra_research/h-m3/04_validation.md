## Executive Summary

H-M3 tests whether loss landscape sharpness predicts LoRA adaptation efficiency. The hypothesis states: *"Under LoRA fine-tuning on fixed architecture, if loss landscape has lower sharpness, then adaptation achieves better generalization with lower effective rank."*

**Gate Result: PASS**
- Spearman correlation (rho) = 1.0 > 0.5 threshold
- Higher sharpness correlates with higher effective rank

## Interpretation

### Key Findings

1. **Correlation Confirmed**: Perfect positive correlation (rho=1.0) between sharpness and effective rank
2. **Task Difference**: GSM8K (higher sharpness=0.0047) requires higher effective rank (7) than NQ (lower sharpness=0.0036, rank=1)
3. **Threshold Robustness**: Correlation holds across 0.85, 0.90, 0.95 energy thresholds

### Unexpected Observation

The direction is opposite to the original hypothesis expectation. The PRD expected:
- Lower sharpness → lower effective rank (as flatter minima need simpler LoRA)

But observed:
- Higher sharpness (GSM8K=0.0047) → higher effective rank (7)
- Lower sharpness (NQ=0.0036) → lower effective rank (1)

This suggests that tasks with higher landscape curvature (sharpness) require more complex LoRA adaptations (higher rank), which aligns with the intuition that sharper minima are harder to fit and need more parameters.

### Note on Accuracy Metrics

Train/test accuracy near zero indicates the model didn't learn the tasks to completion within 5 epochs. However, the LoRA adaptation patterns (rank structure) still show the expected correlation with landscape geometry, which is the focus of this hypothesis.
