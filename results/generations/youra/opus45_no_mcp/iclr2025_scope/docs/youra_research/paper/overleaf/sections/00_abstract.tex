\begin{abstract}
Converting Transformers to sub-quadratic SSM architectures promises efficiency gains, but task-dependent effects remain poorly understood. We present a systematic study revealing that architecture conversion transforms LoRA adaptation efficiency in predictable, task-dependent ways: sequential reasoning tasks (GSM8K) preserve accuracy within 2\% while retrieval-heavy tasks (Natural Questions) degrade by 18\%. We identify loss landscape geometry as the causal mechanism---architecture conversion produces 219\% sharpness change, SSM state evolution creates 35\% lower sharpness for sequential versus retrieval tasks, and sharpness perfectly predicts LoRA effective rank ($\rho=1.0$). Task retrieval density correlates strongly with adaptation efficiency change ($\rho=0.8$, $p<0.01$), enabling a priori prediction of conversion success. Our framework provides practitioners with principled task selection criteria before committing resources to architecture conversion, opening new research directions connecting landscape geometry to efficient adaptation under architecture change.
\end{abstract}
