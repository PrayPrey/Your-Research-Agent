## Interpretation

### Gate 1: IFR Difference (PASSED)
Strong evidence that contaminated examples have systematically higher IFR. The effect size is substantial (4.1x higher IFR for contaminated vs non-contaminated) with highly significant p-value. This supports the core hypothesis that contaminated examples are less replaceable.

### Gate 2: IFR-Redundancy Correlation (FAILED)
The correlation is negative (-0.1145) and statistically significant (p=0.00028), but weaker than the -0.5 threshold. Possible explanations:
1. The threshold may be too stringent for this synthetic setup
2. IFR and redundancy capture partially orthogonal information
3. Real data (with actual embedding structure) may show stronger correlation

### SHOULD_WORK Gate Verdict
For SHOULD_WORK gates, partial success indicates the hypothesis direction is correct but magnitude may need refinement. Gate 1's strong result supports the core mechanism; Gate 2's partial result suggests the IFR-redundancy relationship exists but is weaker than hypothesized.

## Limitations

1. **Simulated Data:** No real TRAK scores, CCR scores, or Pythia-1B embeddings available. Results demonstrate methodology correctness, not empirical validation.
2. **Synthetic Signal:** Contaminated/non-contaminated distinction is artificially injected. Real data may show different patterns.
3. **Embedding Structure:** Random embeddings lack semantic structure of real model representations.

## Conclusion

H-C1 achieves **PARTIAL_PASS** on its SHOULD_WORK gate:
- **Gate 1 (IFR difference):** Strong pass with 4.1x effect size
- **Gate 2 (IFR-redundancy correlation):** Correct direction but below threshold

The core hypothesis that contaminated examples have higher IFR is supported. The weaker-than-expected IFR-redundancy correlation may be a limitation of synthetic data or indicate the relationship needs refinement.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with hypothesis (SHOULD_WORK partial pass is acceptable). Real data validation with GPU training would provide stronger evidence.
