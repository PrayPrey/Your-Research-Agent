### Primary Research Question
Can existing alignment evaluation benchmarks be analyzed through a bidirectional lens to identify gaps between AI-to-human alignment (traditional RLHF, preference modeling) and human-to-AI alignment (user agency, critical evaluation, collaborative decision-making)?

5. **Interdisciplinary Integration:** What insights from HCI, social sciences, and cognitive science are missing from current ML-centric alignment benchmarks that could inform bidirectional alignment evaluation?

## 9. Conclusion

### Key Findings

**Research Gap Structure Identified:**

1. **Human Agency Metrics Gap (PRIMARY)**: RLHF benchmarks measure AI-to-human preference alignment but lack metrics for human-to-AI direction (user agency, critical evaluation, collaborative decision quality).

2. **Static vs Dynamic Evaluation Gap (PRIMARY)**: Current benchmarks use static preference datasets (unidirectional), missing interactive protocols to measure bidirectional mutual adaptation over time.

3. **Interdisciplinary Integration Gap (SECONDARY)**: ML-centric benchmarks lack HCI/social science metrics (user satisfaction, perceived control, trust calibration, cognitive load).

**Conceptual Framework:** Research question structure reveals clear bidirectional lens - traditional benchmarks optimize AI→human alignment but ignore human→AI alignment preservation.

**MCP Limitation Impact:** Without empirical data from Archon/Scholar/Exa, gaps are conceptually sound but lack supporting evidence from actual benchmarks, papers, or implementations.

### Answer to Detailed Question (Preliminary)

**DQ#1 (Unidirectional vs Bidirectional):** Current benchmarks use static preference datasets = unidirectional. Bidirectional requires interactive protocols measuring mutual adaptation.

**DQ#3 (Multi-objective Metrics):** Existing benchmarks optimize single objective (AI preference alignment). Dual objectives (AI adaptation + human agency) not measured together.

**DQ#5 (Interdisciplinary Integration):** ML metrics dominate. HCI user-centered measures (agency, control, collaboration quality) and social science behavioral measures largely absent.
