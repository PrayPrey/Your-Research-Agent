
\textbf{Motivation}: Confidence scores must be validated as reliability indicators for expert survey filtering.

\textbf{Design}: Temporal dispersion (standard deviation as percentage of date range) was compared between high-confidence ($\geq 4/5)$ and low-confidence (<3/5) response cohorts. Prediction: low-confidence shows $\geq 2\times$ wider spread.

\textbf{Metrics}: Standard deviation in years, date range in years, standard deviation percentage.

\textbf{Rationale}: If confidence is uncorrelated with dispersion, the filtering strategy is invalid. An inverse relationship (high confidence $\rightarrow$ tight clustering) justifies excluding low-confidence responses from ground truth.
