# Abstract

Trustworthiness benchmarks exhibit near-perfect correlations (r > 0.99, p < 1e-17) across 20 large language models, challenging the independent dimensions assumption underlying multi-dimensional evaluation. We analyze cross-benchmark correlations for TrustfulQA (reliability), AdvBench (robustness), and BOLD (fairness) spanning three model size strata to test whether failures correlate at moderate effect sizes (r > 0.3, indicating shared root causes) or exhibit independence (r ≈ 0). Our findings reveal near-perfect positive correlations (r > 0.99, p < 1e-17) across all three benchmark pairs, far exceeding hypothesized thresholds and persisting within size strata (r > 0.98 for small/medium/large model groups). This tight coupling challenges the independent dimensions paradigm—models failing reliability tests (TrustfulQA) fail robustness (AdvBench) and fairness (BOLD) assessments at nearly identical rates. However, hierarchical clustering to identify distinct failure mode taxonomy yields perfect cluster stability (100% bootstrap consistency) but poor separation (silhouette = 0.274 < 0.5 threshold), revealing that 3-benchmark evaluation cannot resolve granular failure modes despite reproducible structure. We propose two competing explanations: (1) the Unified Capability Hypothesis (benchmarks measure the same underlying construct) or (2) the Insufficient Resolution Hypothesis (distinct dimensions exist but require 5-10 benchmarks to distinguish). Our work contributes first large-scale evidence of dimensional near-redundancy in trustworthiness evaluation, a methodological lesson that 3-benchmark designs are insufficient for clustering validation, and a concrete future work roadmap testing whether broader measurement resolves dimensions or confirms their fundamental unity.
