\begin{abstract}
We apply Design-by-Contract formal methods to ML research workflow automation, achieving 100\% downstream failure reduction through three-layer validation (schema + pattern + compositional contracts) on placeholder hypothesis content with typed interfaces. Automated ML research pipelines fail when hypotheses violate feasibility constraints during phase transitions --- existing schema validation checks structure (types, required fields) but cannot express semantic constraints (keyword patterns) or compositional rules (cross-field logic, state-based membership). We introduce contract-based validation through a three-layer architecture: schema validation for structure, pattern validators for semantic content, and compositional contracts for cross-field logic and state dependencies. We validate our approach on a 100-case placeholder hypothesis corpus (80 valid, 20 with embedded violations across four feasibility constraints: no synthetic data, no human evaluation, standard datasets only, existing benchmarks only), demonstrating 100\% downstream failure reduction versus schema-only baseline on placeholder content (generalization to substantive research unproven, external validity deferred to Phase 5). Contracts force explicit checking of constraints schema cannot express (100 percentage point expressiveness gap), multi-layer detection achieves 88\% violation detection at boundaries versus 40\% for schema-only (48pp gap), and early boundary validation prevents all constraint violations from propagating to Phase 4/5 implementation. This work represents the first application of Design-by-Contract to semantic constraint validation in research workflows, enabling automated pipelines to enforce compositional constraints at phase boundaries with minimal overhead (25 LOC, 0.01ms execution time).
\end{abstract}
