# Abstract

Data curation for foundation model training combines quality filtering and diversity sampling, but optimal sequential ordering remains unclear. Conventional wisdom suggests quality-first (QD) curation dominates at small scale while diversity-first (DQ) should take over at large scale as quality saturates. Through systematic GPT-2 training experiments across four log-spaced scales (10K–10M tokens), we find quality-first curation universally outperforms diversity-first by +1.5pp to +5.0pp (Cohen's d=0.76–2.48), contradicting predicted reversal. We quantify quality saturation (slope −2.62pp/log-scale, p=0.008) and diversity persistence (slope +2.5pp/log-scale, p=0.002), providing statistical rigor to prior qualitative observations. Our compositional ordering experiments demonstrate non-additive interactions that challenge Data Mixing Laws' order-invariance assumption. The underlying mechanism is quality-gated diversity: diversity sampling is only effective on quality-filtered subsets, not raw noisy corpora. These findings provide prescriptive guidance—practitioners should apply quality filtering (V-Information) before diversity sampling (Feature Activation Coverage) regardless of dataset scale—and introduce compositional interaction analysis to data curation research. Our work quantifies saturation trajectories, demonstrates ordering effects, and delivers actionable recommendations for multi-stage curation pipeline design.
