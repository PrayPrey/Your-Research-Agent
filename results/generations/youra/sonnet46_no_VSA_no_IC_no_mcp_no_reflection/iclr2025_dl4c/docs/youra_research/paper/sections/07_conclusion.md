# Conclusion

Every published RLEF paper for code LLMs trains with binary pass/fail reward. We showed that this choice produces zero gradient contribution in 98.7% of early-training GRPO groups under realistic partial-pass distributions — a dead zone that ratio reward (k/n test cases passing) mathematically escapes. The mechanistic guarantee is exact: whenever a GRPO group contains at least one completion with partial credit and no completion with full credit, binary reward must produce zero advantage variance while ratio reward must produce non-zero advantage variance. This is not an empirical trend — it is a consequence of GRPO's group normalization formula and the binary reward function's output set {0, 1}.

We confirmed this guarantee experimentally (h-e1: advantage variance 0.0475 ratio vs. 0.0 binary for the illustrative group; 987/1,000 simulation groups with different ratio vs. binary signals) and tested its policy-level implications (h-m1: 1,000-step GRPO training on APPS with DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B-instruct, binary vs. ratio). The h-m1 experiment produced a precision null result: both conditions showed reward_mean = 0.0 at all 208 logged training steps, with clipped_ratio = 1.0 throughout. This precisely confirmed that max_new_tokens = 512 is insufficient for 6.7B models to produce executable solutions for APPS competition-level problems, making both reward functions mathematically equivalent throughout training.

The next step is clear. Re-run h-m1 with max_new_tokens = 1,024–2,048 on APPS, or switch training data to HumanEval or MBPP where DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B achieves 40–65% base pass@1. Under those conditions, the mechanistic guarantee we proved will be active, and the policy-level prediction (ratio reward improves HumanEval pass@1 relative to binary reward) becomes testable. The validated infrastructure (35/35 unit tests, 9 engineering issues resolved, FractionPartialCallback diagnostic) is ready for this experiment.

Reward granularity in RLEF is not a minor implementation detail. For models trained with GRPO on hard datasets, the choice between binary and ratio reward determines whether the model receives any learning signal during the critical early phase of training — when the model cannot yet produce complete solutions but is actively learning code structure and problem-solving strategies. We have provided the mathematical foundation for this claim. The empirical validation, under correctly-powered conditions, is the natural next step.

## Future Work

The immediate priority is redesigning h-m1 to satisfy the partial-pass prerequisite (non-zero fraction_partial throughout training). Two approaches are available: (1) increase generation length (max_new_tokens ≥ 1,024) on APPS to allow syntactically complete solutions, or (2) switch to HumanEval/MBPP training data where the model has a high base solve rate. Once h-m1 is validated, the downstream hypotheses — training-evaluation reward alignment (h-m2) and cross-benchmark generalization effects of reward granularity (h-m3) — can be tested sequentially. A longer-term direction is deriving a reward granularity selection criterion: given an estimate of the model's base solve rate on a training dataset, predict whether binary or ratio reward will provide higher-quality gradient signal throughout training, and use this prediction to set the reward formulation before training begins.
