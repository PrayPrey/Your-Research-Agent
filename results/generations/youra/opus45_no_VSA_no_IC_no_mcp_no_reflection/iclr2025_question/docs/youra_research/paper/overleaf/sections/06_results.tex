\subsection{Main Results}

Table~\ref{tab:main} presents AUROC scores for all UQ methods on TruthfulQA mc1.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{AUROC} & \textbf{Threshold} & \textbf{Status} \\
\midrule
Random (baseline) & 0.50 & -- & Reference \\
Choice Entropy & 0.7703 & 0.55 & \textbf{PASS} \\
Max Probability & \textbf{0.8068} & 0.55 & \textbf{PASS} \\
Semantic Entropy & 0.5645 & 0.70 & \textbf{FAIL} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{AUROC scores for UQ methods on TruthfulQA mc1. Max probability achieves best performance; semantic entropy barely exceeds random chance.}
\label{tab:main}
\end{table}

\textbf{Key Finding 1:} Token-level methods effectively discriminate hallucinations. Both max probability (AUROC = 0.81) and choice entropy (AUROC = 0.77) substantially exceed the 0.55 threshold.

\textbf{Key Finding 2:} Semantic entropy underperforms by 0.24 AUROC points. Contrary to expectations from prior work, semantic entropy achieves only AUROC = 0.5645, barely above random chance.

\subsection{Analysis: Format-Dependency}

The resolution lies in the task format. MC responses are single letters (``A'', ``B'', ``C'', ``D''). NLI models cannot meaningfully compare ``A'' vs ``B'' for entailment. Each response forms its own cluster (hence avg 4.92 from 5 samples). Entropy over singleton clusters provides no discriminative signal.

Token-level methods extract uncertainty directly from logits without requiring semantic comparison. They work regardless of answer length.
