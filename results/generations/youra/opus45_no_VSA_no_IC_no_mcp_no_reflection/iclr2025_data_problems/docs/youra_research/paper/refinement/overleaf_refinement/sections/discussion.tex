
\subsubsection{6.1 Findings}

\textbf{Finding 1:} The EDMP pipeline is computationally tractable and produces statistically significant domain differences (ANOVA F=1242.59, p<0.001) with perfect reproducibility. The pipeline completes in under 30 minutes on a single GPU.

\textbf{Finding 2:} Synthetic data produces insufficient variance for domain discrimination (standard deviation 0.0069 versus target 0.05). This is attributable to shared vocabulary (approximately 70\% common terms across synthetic domains).

\textbf{Finding 3:} The random baseline produces scores near zero, confirming that E5 embeddings capture semantic structure rather than noise.

\subsubsection{6.2 Limitations}

\textbf{L1: Only existence validated, not predictive power.} We tested whether EDMP scores can be computed, not whether they predict training utility. The core hypothesis remains untested.

\textbf{L2: Synthetic data used instead of real Pile domains.} Due to data access constraints, we used synthetic domain-distinguishable text lacking vocabulary diversity.

\textbf{L3: Single embedder evaluated.} Only E5-large-v2 was tested. Rankings might differ with BGE-large, OpenAI embeddings, or other variants.

\textbf{L4: Main predictions untested.} None of the three main predictions (P1: EDMP top-3 outperforms perplexity top-3 by 3\% or more; P2: rankings correlate across scales with Kendall's $\tau$ > 0.5; P3: compute cost under 10\% of DoReMi) were tested.

\subsubsection{6.3 Why Variance Was Insufficient}

The low cross-domain variance is likely due to synthetic data limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Synthetic texts share approximately 70\% common vocabulary across domains.
\item E5-large mean-pools all tokens, potentially diluting domain-specific signals.
\item Real domain data (The Pile) would have distinct vocabulary distributions and longer coherent domain-specific passages.
\end{enumerate}

Based on domain adaptation literature, real domain data should yield variance in the 0.1-0.2 range.

\subsubsection{6.4 Causal Mechanism Status}

The EDMP hypothesis posits a three-step causal mechanism:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Embedding extraction captures semantic distribution:} VERIFIED (E5 >> random baseline)
\item \textbf{Similarity scoring correlates with transfer (r > 0.3):} UNVERIFIED (not tested)
\item \textbf{Top-K ranking outperforms random-K selection:} UNVERIFIED (not tested)
\end{enumerate}

Only the first step was verified. Steps 2 and 3 require experiments with real domain data and actual training runs, which were blocked by the h-e1 PARTIAL result.
