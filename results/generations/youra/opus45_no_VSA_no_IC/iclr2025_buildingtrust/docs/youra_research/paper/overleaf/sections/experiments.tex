\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

We design experiments to answer four research questions, organized hierarchically from existence to mechanism validation.

\subsection{Research Questions}

\textbf{RQ1 (H-E1):} Do inter-benchmark correlations between TruthfulQA, HaluEval, and FactScore fall in the moderate range (baseline $<$ r $<$ 0.7)?

\textbf{RQ2 (H-M1):} Is TruthfulQA distinct from general knowledge as measured by MMLU?

\textbf{RQ3 (H-M2):} Is HaluEval's generation coherence dimension distinct from TruthfulQA's misconception resistance?

\textbf{RQ4 (H-M3):} Is FactScore's factual precision distinct from both TruthfulQA and HaluEval, and does factor analysis support multi-dimensional structure?

Each question maps directly to a hypothesis with defined success criteria.

\subsection{Model Population}

We evaluate N=50 models from the Open LLM Leaderboard, selected for diversity across multiple dimensions:

Scale & 4 tiers (7B, 13B, 34B, 70B) & Test whether correlations vary with scale \\

This selection ensures robust correlation estimates not confounded by population homogeneity. Figure~\ref{fig:diversity} shows the diversity distribution across architectures and scales.

\textbf{Why these benchmarks?} Each uses a fundamentally different evaluation paradigm---multiple choice, binary detection, and atomic fact verification---enabling us to test whether paradigm differences translate to independent capability dimensions.

\subsection{Baselines and Reference Points}

\subsubsection{Unrelated-Benchmark Baseline}
To establish a floor for meaningful correlation, we compute r(MMLU-Physics, HaluEval). These benchmarks measure clearly distinct constructs (domain knowledge vs.\ hallucination detection), so their correlation should approach zero.

\subsubsection{Intra-Benchmark Reference}
To establish a ceiling, we compute correlations among MMLU subjects. If truthfulness benchmarks measure a single construct, their inter-correlations should approach this intra-benchmark level.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Score Collection:} Extract benchmark scores from Open LLM Leaderboard for all 50 models.

\item \textbf{Correlation Computation:} Compute Spearman correlation for all benchmark pairs (15 unique pairs). Spearman was chosen for robustness to non-normal distributions.

\item \textbf{Significance Testing:} Apply Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons ($\alpha = 0.05/6 = 0.0083$ for truthfulness benchmark pairs).

\item \textbf{Confidence Intervals:} Generate 95\% CIs via 1,000 bootstrap iterations.

\item \textbf{Factor Analysis:} Apply PCA to standardized scores; count components needed for 80\% cumulative variance.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hypothesis Gates}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Hypothesis} & \textbf{Gate Type} & \textbf{Pass Condition} & \textbf{If Fail} \\
\midrule
H-E1 & MUST\_WORK & $0.10 < r < 0.7$ for all pairs & Stop pipeline \\
H-M1 & MUST\_WORK & $r(\text{TQA, MMLU}) < r(\text{MMLU internal})$ & Document limitation \\
H-M2 & SHOULD\_WORK & $r(\text{HE, TQA}) < 0.7$ & Continue with caveat \\
H-M3 & SHOULD\_WORK & $r(\text{FS, TQA}) < 0.7$ AND $r(\text{FS, HE}) < 0.7$ & Continue with caveat \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

MUST\_WORK gates require passing for the overall hypothesis to be supported. SHOULD\_WORK gates allow continuation with documented caveats.

\subsection{Implementation}

All experiments were implemented in Python using scipy.stats for correlation computation and sklearn.decomposition for PCA. Code, data extraction scripts, and analysis notebooks are available in the supplementary materials.
