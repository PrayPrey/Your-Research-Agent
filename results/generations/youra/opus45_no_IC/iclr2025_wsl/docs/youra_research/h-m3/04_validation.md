## Executive Summary

H-M3 successfully demonstrates that an untrained (randomly initialized) MLP has **no built-in permutation invariance**. When fed permuted weight vectors as input, outputs vary significantly with coefficient of variation (CV) = 0.194, far exceeding the 0.1 threshold. This confirms that permutation equivariance is an architectural property of NFN, not a general property of neural networks.

## Conclusion

H-M3 establishes the negative control for the equivariance hypothesis:

1. **MLP lacks inherent invariance:** Untrained MLP shows high output variance (CV=0.194) when processing permuted inputs
2. **NFN has architectural invariance:** In contrast, NFN (H-M2) shows near-perfect invariance (max_dev=1.19e-07)
3. **Equivariance is architectural:** This confirms that NFN's permutation invariance comes from its equivariant architecture, not from any general property shared by all neural networks

This validates the research hypothesis that permutation equivariance provides a structural advantage that standard MLPs must learn from data (if at all).
