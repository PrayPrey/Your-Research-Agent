# Validation Report: H-M4

**Hypothesis:** Cross-cluster benchmark pairs show failed threshold transfer (AUROC degradation > 0.15)
**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Type:** MECHANISM
**Gate:** SHOULD_WORK

---

## Executive Summary

**Result: PASS**

H-M4 validates that semantic entropy thresholds calibrated on one benchmark FAIL to transfer to benchmarks from a different cluster, with mean AUROC degradation of 0.223 (> 0.15 threshold). This contrasts sharply with H-M3's within-cluster transfer success (degradation 0.032), confirming that cluster membership predicts transfer success/failure.

## Comparison to H-M3 (Within-Cluster)

### Degradation Contrast

| Transfer Type | Mean Degradation | Threshold |

| Cross-Cluster (H-M4) | 0.223 | > 0.15 |

**Cross/Within Ratio: 6.98x**

Cross-cluster degradation is nearly 7x higher than within-cluster, demonstrating the predictive power of cluster membership.

## JS-Divergence Correlation

The degradation correlates with JS-divergence as predicted by H-E1/H-M2:

| Pair | JS-Divergence | Degradation |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| trivia_qa → pop_qa | 0.422 | 0.210 |
| trivia_qa → halueval_qa | 0.526 | 0.237 |

Higher JS-divergence (indicating more dissimilar uncertainty distributions) leads to higher transfer degradation, validating the cluster-based transfer prediction model.

## Mechanism Verification

### Cross-Cluster Transfer Protocol
1. Calibrate threshold on trivia_qa (Cluster 1) at target FPR=0.1
2. Apply frozen threshold to pop_qa and halueval_qa (Cluster 2)
3. Compute AUROC degradation = source_auroc - target_auroc

### Findings
- Cross-cluster transfer shows significant performance loss (mean 0.223)
- Degradation exceeds H-M3's within-cluster result by 7x
- Results support the cluster-based transfer prediction framework

---

## Implications for Main Hypothesis

H-M4 PASS completes the transfer contrast:
- **H-M3 (PASS):** Within-cluster transfer succeeds (degradation ≤ 0.08)
- **H-M4 (PASS):** Cross-cluster transfer fails (degradation > 0.15)

Combined, these results demonstrate:
1. Benchmark clustering based on uncertainty distributions predicts transfer success
2. Thresholds calibrated within a cluster are portable
3. Thresholds calibrated across clusters are NOT portable
4. This validates the conditional transfer framework for hallucination detection
