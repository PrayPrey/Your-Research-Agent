# Conclusion

We set out to answer a deceptively simple question: when AI assistants match user communication style, do users engage more? The intuitive answer—yes, more accommodation is better—turns out to be incomplete.

Our analysis of 111,000 human-AI conversations reveals that formality accommodation follows a Goldilocks principle. AI systems do accommodate to human formality (r=0.152), and this accommodation relates to engagement—but the relationship is non-linear. Moderate accommodation predicts the highest conversation continuation rates (71.4%), while both high accommodation (65.9%) and low accommodation (60.9%) underperform.

This finding refines Communication Accommodation Theory for human-AI contexts. The linear convergence-rapport relationship observed in human-human interaction has a boundary condition: when one party is perceived as artificial, excessive accommodation may trigger avoidance rather than engagement. The AI that adapts "just right" outperforms both the rigid assistant and the over-eager mimic.

We also document a striking asymmetry: AI accommodates to humans 11× more strongly than humans accommodate to AI. This suggests that natural dialogue dynamics cannot be assumed in human-AI interaction—the system bears primary responsibility for adaptive behavior.

**Implications for practice**: Chatbot designers should consider accommodation calibration, not just capability. A system that dampens its accommodation response may improve user engagement. This provides a concrete, measurable design target.

**Implications for theory**: CAT predictions require qualification in human-AI contexts. The uncanny valley extends to linguistic behavior—not just appearance and motion—with practical consequences for conversational AI.

**Future directions**: Causal experiments manipulating accommodation levels would strengthen these findings. Cross-platform replication would test generality. User perception studies could illuminate the mechanisms behind accommodation-artificiality trade-offs.

The Goldilocks zone is not a limitation—it is design guidance. Understanding that "just right" outperforms "as much as possible" moves conversational AI design from intuition toward evidence.
