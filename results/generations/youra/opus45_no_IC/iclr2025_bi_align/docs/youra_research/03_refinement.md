## Final Hypothesis

### Title
Bidirectional Formality Accommodation Predicts Conversation Length in Human-AI Dialogue

### Hypothesis ID
H-BiAcc-v1

### Core Claim
In LMSYS-Chat-1M multi-turn conversations, early bidirectional formality accommodation (measured via DeBERTa at turns 1-2) predicts conversation continuation, with distinguishable effects by direction (AI→Human vs Human→AI).

### Mechanism
1. Human sends initial message with certain formality level
2. AI responds with formality level that may match or diverge (accommodation)
3. Lower formality delta signals attentiveness/understanding
4. Perceived attentiveness increases user engagement → longer conversations

## Novelty

- **N1**: First test of accommodation → outcomes in human-AI setting
- **N2**: First bidirectional decomposition with directional predictions

**Differentiation from Prior Work**:
- Chen et al. (2026): showed accommodation EXISTS; we test it PREDICTS outcomes
- Niederhoffer & Pennebaker (2002): human-human only; we extend to human-AI
- H-M1 (failed): injected calibration signals; we measure natural signals

## Experimental Design

### Dataset
- **Name**: LMSYS-Chat-1M
- **Source**: HuggingFace (lmsys/lmsys-chat-1m)
- **Size**: 1M conversations, 25+ LLMs, 210K unique users

### Tools
- **Formality**: DeBERTa ranker (s-nlp/deberta-large-formality-ranker, 87.8% accuracy)
- **Feature extraction**: ConvoKit for turn-level analysis

### Baselines
1. Within-conversation shuffle (breaks accommodation, keeps topic)
2. Cross-conversation shuffle (breaks both accommodation and topic)

### Precondition
≥50% of conversations have ≥2 turns per side (must verify before main analysis)

## Limitations

- **User intent confound**: Some users want quick answers, others want discussion
- **Model verbosity**: Some models naturally produce longer responses
- **Topic distribution**: Different topics may have different accommodation baselines

**Mitigation**: Cross-model stratification, within-topic analysis if metadata available
