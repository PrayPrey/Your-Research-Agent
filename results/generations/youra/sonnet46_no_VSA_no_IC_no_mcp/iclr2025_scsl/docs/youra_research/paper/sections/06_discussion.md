# 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Key Findings and Their Implications

Our results support a coherent interpretation that runs counter to augmentation-invariance theory: **supervised label correlation is the dominant driver of spurious feature encoding**, not augmentation-based instance-discriminability.

In ERM, the cross-entropy objective trains on a distribution where background texture predicts bird species with 95% reliability. The gradient signal jointly reinforces both task-relevant features (bird morphology) and spurious features (background texture), because both reduce training loss. The frozen representation consequently encodes background at a high level relative to task features (ratio = 1.052). MoCo-v3 receives no such label correlation pressure: its objective maximizes similarity between augmented views of the same image without ever seeing class labels. Background texture remains instance-discriminative (different images have different backgrounds), but without the label-correlation amplification, its encoded strength is lower (ratio = 1.027).

**ERM ≈ DINO** deserves particular attention. DINO uses no explicit class labels, yet its ratio (1.050) is statistically indistinguishable from ERM's (d = 0.60, p = 1.0). This is consistent with the self-distillation interpretation: DINO's momentum teacher generates soft-targets that implicitly carry class-level semantic structure (DINO is known to produce class-discriminative attention maps [Caron et al., 2021]). These class-correlated teacher targets replicate the label-correlation pressure of supervised ERM at the representation level, explaining why the two paradigms show similar spurious encoding on Waterbirds. Testing this interpretation directly — via mutual information between DINO teacher-targets and spurious attribute labels — is an important future experiment.

**The dataset interaction** reveals that the paradigm × spurious-attribute-type relationship is not universal. On CelebA (demographic spurious: gender), MoCo-v3 shows the highest spurious ratio — the reverse of Waterbirds. A plausible explanation is that gender, distributed across face features, is more strongly instance-discriminative for contrastive objectives (since it varies across images under the augmentation set) than background texture is for CelebA. But the label-correlation amplification is weaker in CelebA because gender's correlation with Blond_Hair (~85%) is lower than Waterbirds background correlation with bird species (95%), and because ERM's label signal is less concentrated in a single spurious attribute. The net result is a dataset-dependent balance between the two mechanisms.

### 6.2 Limitations

**L1: Original directional mechanism refuted.** Our hypothesis predicted contrastive SSL would encode spurious features more strongly. The experiments show the opposite with large effect size (d = 5.68 in reversed direction). We treat this refutation as a positive scientific outcome: the refined model — supervised label correlation as primary driver — is better supported by the data and makes more specific predictions testable in future work (see Section 7). However, the paper cannot claim augmentation invariance *amplifies* spurious encoding in contrastive SSL; only that augmentation design is a lever for *suppressing* it.

**L2: h-m1 final statistics incomplete.** The SimCLR-NoBackground mechanism experiment is verified as functionally active (pixel_diff = 0.9656, 19× threshold), confirming the intervention works. Full statistical characterization of the ratio reduction (paired t-test across 5 seeds, both conditions) requires completion of 50-epoch training. We report this as a PoC-level contribution and note that the statistical comparison will appear in follow-up work.

**L3: No downstream worst-group accuracy evaluation.** We demonstrate that pretraining paradigm modulates spurious feature encoding in frozen representations, but we do not test whether this translates to downstream robustness differences. Kirichenko et al. [2022] show that DFR-style last-layer retraining on group-balanced data achieves near-oracle WGA using ERM features — the implication is that features with *lower* spurious encoding (MoCo-v3) may need fewer group-balanced samples to achieve the same WGA. Testing this requires running the full DFR protocol on MoCo-v3 features, which we leave for future work.

**L4: MoCo-v3 as SimCLR proxy.** The original hypothesis named SimCLR as the contrastive representative, but SimCLR-v2 has no official PyTorch checkpoint (TF-native). MoCo-v3 shares the contrastive objective and was used as a well-maintained alternative. The effect size (d = 5.68) is large enough that checkpoint-quality confounds are implausible as explanations (MoCo-v3 reports competitive ImageNet linear probe accuracy), but SimCLR-specific validation is future work.

**L5: Scope conditions.** Results are established for ResNet-50 backbones, ImageNet-1k pretraining, Waterbirds (texture spurious) and CelebA (demographic spurious). Generalization to ViT backbones, larger pretraining scales, or fine-grained spurious attributes requires separate experiments.

### 6.3 Broader Impact

This work produces no new training data or models that could enable harmful applications. Practically, it provides backbone selection guidance for practitioners building fairness-critical applications: contrastive SSL (MoCo-v3, BarlowTwins) backbones partially suppress spurious features by design, without requiring group labels or any robustness intervention. This is actionable and positive for downstream fairness applications.

A potential misuse would be assuming that MoCo-v3's lower spurious encoding on Waterbirds *eliminates* the need for robustness interventions — it does not. All four paradigms show ratios above 1.0 (spurious features are encoded by every method), and the CelebA ranking reversal shows the suppression is not universal. The appropriate use is as an additional criterion in backbone selection, not as a replacement for group-annotated fine-tuning or other robustness methods.
