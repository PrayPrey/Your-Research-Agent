## 5. Interpretation

### 5.1 Key Findings

1. **Higher margin variance (sharpness_ratio=1.65):** DPO produces implicit reward margins with 65% higher standard deviation than RLHF, indicating sharper/more decisive preference encoding.

2. **Better boundary decisions (accuracy=58.9%):** On cases where RLHF showed weak preference (margin<0.1), DPO correctly identifies the preferred response 58.9% of the time — exceeding the 55% threshold.

3. **High confidence on ambiguous cases (77.2%):** DPO makes confident decisions (|margin|>0.1) on 77.2% of boundary cases, demonstrating that it doesn't inherit RLHF's smoothing on difficult examples.

### 5.2 Connection to Main Hypothesis

H-M2 establishes that DPO's closed-form objective produces sharper preference boundaries than RLHF's explicit reward model. Combined with H-M1 (RLHF smoothing validated), this confirms the mechanistic difference underlying the main hypothesis:

- RLHF: reward model learns smooth, interpolating approximation (H-M1)
- DPO: direct optimization preserves sharper preference boundaries (H-M2)

These different training dynamics are expected to produce distinct "alignment signatures" across evaluation benchmarks (to be tested in H-M4).
