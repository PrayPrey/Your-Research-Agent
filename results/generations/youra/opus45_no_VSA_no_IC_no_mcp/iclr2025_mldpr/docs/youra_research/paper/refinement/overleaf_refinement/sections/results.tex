\textbf{Correlation Analysis:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Pearson R = -0.950 (p = 0.050)
\item Spearman $\rho$ = -0.800 (p = 0.200)
\item Bootstrap 95\% CI: [-1.0, 1.0]
\end{itemize}

The point estimate exceeds the -0.4 threshold. Benchmarks with lower DNSI (more saturated) exhibit larger generalization gaps. However, with n = 4, bootstrap confidence intervals span the full range, and the p-value is at the boundary of conventional significance levels.

\subsubsection{5.2 Temporal Prediction (RQ2)}

Pre-2019 DNSI was computed for four benchmarks and correlated with post-2019 gap measurements.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{%
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Benchmark & Pre-2019 DNSI & Post-2019 Gap \\
\midrule
ImageNet & 0.298 & 0.125 \\
CIFAR-10 & 0.987 & 0.040 \\
CIFAR-100 & 0.494 & 0.050 \\
ObjectNet & 0.298 & 0.425 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
}
\end{table}

\begin{itemize}
\item $R^2$ = 0.349 (threshold: 0.3)
\item Slope = -0.328 (negative, as expected)
\item Leave-one-out CV $R^2$ = -3.82
\end{itemize}

The $R^2$ exceeds the 0.3 threshold, and the negative slope confirms directional consistency. However, leave-one-out cross-validation yields $R^2$ = -3.82, indicating that with n = 4, the model does not generalize reliably when any single benchmark is held out. This result should be treated as preliminary evidence.
