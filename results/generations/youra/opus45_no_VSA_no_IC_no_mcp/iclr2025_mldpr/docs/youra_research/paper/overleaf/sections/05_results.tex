\subsection{Main Results: DNSI-Gap Correlation}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{DNSI and generalization gap values for evaluated benchmarks.}
\label{tab:main_results}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Benchmark & DNSI & Gen.\ Gap \\
\midrule
CIFAR-10 & 0.790 & 0.040 \\
ImageNet & 0.720 & 0.125 \\
ObjectNet & 0.550 & 0.425 \\
HANS & 0.450 & 0.400 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Correlation Analysis:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pearson $R = \mathbf{-0.950}$ ($p = 0.050$)
    \item Spearman $\rho = \mathbf{-0.800}$ ($p = 0.200$)
\end{itemize}

The point estimate exceeds our $-0.4$ threshold, though with $n=4$, bootstrap confidence intervals span the full range $[-1, 1]$. Benchmarks with lower DNSI (more saturated) exhibit larger generalization gaps. We emphasize this as preliminary evidence requiring replication with larger benchmark samples.

\subsection{Temporal Prediction}

Pre-2019 DNSI predicts post-2019 gaps: $R^2 = \mathbf{0.349}$ (threshold: 0.3). Negative slope ($-0.328$) confirms directional consistency. However, leave-one-out cross-validation yields $R^2 = -3.82$, indicating high variance with $n=4$; the temporal signal requires validation on larger samples before deployment as a practical leading indicator.
