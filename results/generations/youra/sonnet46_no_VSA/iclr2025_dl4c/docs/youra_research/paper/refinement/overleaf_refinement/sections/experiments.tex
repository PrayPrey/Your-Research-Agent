
Four research questions guide the experiments:

\textbf{RQ1:} Does SFT source identity produce statistically significant pass@1 differences at 1.3B scale under controlled conditions?

\textbf{RQ2:} Is same-source specialization symmetric --- does training on source X confer exclusive advantage on the benchmark most similar to X?

\textbf{RQ3:} Does code-embedding cosine similarity between training source and test benchmark predict pass@1 rank across conditions?

\textbf{RQ4:} Do source identity effects on HumanEval+ persist at 7B scale?

\subsubsection{4.2 Baselines and Controls}

Each of the four SFT conditions serves simultaneously as a treatment (for comparisons against other conditions) and as a baseline (for the cross-benchmark transfer analysis). No zero-shot base model evaluation was conducted in this study; comparisons are between SFT conditions only.

\subsubsection{4.3 Evaluation Metrics}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Primary:} pass@1 on HumanEval+ (available for all 4 conditions $\times$ 2 scales $\times$ 3 seeds)
\item \textbf{Secondary:} pass@1 on MBPP+ (available only for HumanEval-only and MBPP-only conditions at 1.3B scale from H-M1; full 4-condition MBPP+ data is absent from H-E2 output and incomplete from H-C1)
\item \textbf{Mechanistic:} Spearman $\rho$ with permutation test; dual-encoder concordance criterion
\item \textbf{Scale comparison:} Absolute condition spread (max $-$ min condition mean) and $\eta^2$; both are reported due to a known $\eta^2$ artifact at 7B scale (Section 5.4)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{4.4 Compute Infrastructure}

Training and evaluation ran on $5\times$ NVIDIA H100 NVL GPUs (~95 GB each). The 1.3B-scale SFT runs used a single GPU per condition per seed. The 7B-scale runs used 4-GPU DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 per run. Parallel evaluation used a custom batched evaluator across all 5 GPUs.
