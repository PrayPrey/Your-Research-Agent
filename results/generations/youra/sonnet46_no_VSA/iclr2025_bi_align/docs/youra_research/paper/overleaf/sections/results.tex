\subsection{Primary Result --- Fisher $z$ Difference Test (H-M2): 53\% Confound}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Raw vs.\ partial Spearman $\rho$ with BCa 95\% CIs ($N{=}296$, $B{=}5000$).}
\label{tab:hm2}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Metric & Raw (no MMLU control) & Partial (MMLU controlled) \\
\midrule
Spearman $\rho$ & \textbf{0.732} & \textbf{0.343} \\
BCa 95\% CI     & (0.670, 0.780) & (0.180, 0.492) \\
$p$-value       & $5.83 \times 10^{-51}$ & $1.40 \times 10^{-9}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Fisher $z$ difference:} $z = 6.9679$, $p = 3.22 \times 10^{-12}$

The reduction from 0.732 to 0.343 represents a \textbf{53.1\% decrease}. Both pre-registered success criteria are satisfied simultaneously: $p = 3.22 \times 10^{-12} \ll 0.05$, and the BCa confidence intervals are non-overlapping. The finding is not marginal --- by either criterion, MMLU scale confounding of the TruthfulQA $\times$ BBQ correlation is statistically decisive.

Notably, the partial correlation ($0.343$, $p = 1.40 \times 10^{-9}$) is itself highly significant, indicating real alignment-specific co-movement remains after scale control.

\subsection{Robustness Analysis}

\textbf{Family-weighted Fisher $z$.} Using model-family means (51 families; 30 with $\geq$3 models) confirmed the primary result directionally.

\textbf{RLHF Tier~3 (Optional).} $\Delta$BBQ sign test: $k_{\text{positive}}{=}146/300$, proportion$=0.487$, binomtest $p=0.686$. No evidence that RLHF fine-tuning systematically increases BBQ-proxy scores. This contrasts with established RLHF improvement on TruthfulQA MC2 (+3.406 pts), suggesting potential asymmetry in RLHF alignment targets.

\textbf{HarmBench Tier~2 (Skipped).} Zero model name matches between LLM LB v1 and HarmBench Table~2. Safety dimension excluded.
