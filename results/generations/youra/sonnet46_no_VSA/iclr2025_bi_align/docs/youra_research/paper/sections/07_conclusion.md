# 7. Conclusion

We opened this paper with a concrete observation: controlling for MMLU general capability reduces the TruthfulQA × BBQ correlation from 0.732 to 0.343 — more than half of what leaderboard comparisons might attribute to alignment coherence is, in fact, scale-driven confounding. Our work provides, to our knowledge, the first direct application of the Fisher z raw-vs-partial difference test as a formal statistical diagnostic for this confound, and demonstrates it on N=296 open-weight LLMs with a pre-registered hypothesis and 57/57 test cases passing.

The three-stage verified mechanism is clear: (1) MMLU explains 49% of TruthfulQA variance and 76% of BBQ-proxy variance, confirming MMLU as a strong scale covariate; (2) controlling for MMLU reduces the pairwise alignment correlation by 53% with Fisher z p = 3.22 × 10⁻¹², decisively confirming scale confounding; (3) the residual partial_rho = 0.343 remains positive and significant (p = 1.40 × 10⁻⁹), indicating genuine alignment-specific co-movement beyond scale — though the structural interpretation of this residual remains ambiguous at N=296.

To answer the question we opened with: when a model achieves 80% on MMLU, its high TruthfulQA and BBQ scores are substantially predicted by that general capability. After controlling for this, a real but smaller alignment-specific relationship remains — one we cannot yet classify as "coherent" or "independent" without larger N or genuine bias benchmark data. The impression of alignment coherence from raw leaderboard correlations is thus real but inflated.

**Future directions.** The most pressing next step is replication with genuine HELM Lite BBQ per-model accuracy scores, which would disambiguate whether the residual partial_rho = 0.343 reflects true factuality-bias coupling or ARC proxy artifact. Extension to three-dimensional analysis (factuality × bias × safety) requires resolving HarmBench model name compatibility. Applying the same Fisher z diagnostic to LLM LB v2 (N > 1000 models) would provide the statistical power to definitively resolve the scenario classification ambiguity.

**Methodological take-away.** The Fisher z difference test between raw and partial Spearman is a practical, low-cost diagnostic that should become standard practice when interpreting alignment benchmark correlations. If MMLU or an equivalent scale proxy is available, computing it before interpreting co-movement is not optional — it is methodologically necessary.
