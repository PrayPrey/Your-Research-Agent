# Abstract

Hallucination detection in large language models requires uncertainty signals that capture distinct failure modes — yet whether the two most practical signal families (consistency-based Semantic Entropy and token-level minimum log-probability) are statistically independent had not been directly tested. Prior work shows that first-token confidence and semantic agreement correlate at r = 0.54–0.76 on Llama-3.1-8B [Gabriel, 2026], suggesting the two signal families may be redundant. We measure the independence of Semantic Entropy (SE\_N5) and minimum log-probability (min\_logprob) on TriviaQA dev with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct at N=2500 and find Pearson |r|(SE\_N5, min\_logprob) = 0.026: **near-zero and 20× lower than first-token signals**, far below any collinearity threshold. The key insight is that SE aggregates uncertainty over semantic equivalence class masses from N=5 stochastic samples, while min\_logprob captures the minimum token log-probability over an entire greedy decode sequence; these algebraically distinct operations manifest as empirically near-zero correlation. We further demonstrate that a cross-model LM-judge design (Qwen-2.5-7B evaluating Llama-3.1-8B outputs) produces low circularity (Spearman ρ = −0.026 between SE and judge labels), providing a replicable evaluation best practice. However, SE does not add meaningful conditional predictive power beyond min\_logprob at N=2500 (partial R²=0.0005, LRT p=0.442), suggesting that while SE and min\_logprob are statistically independent, their predictive information may overlap in practice on short-answer factual QA.
