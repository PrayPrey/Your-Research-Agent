# Workshop on Machine Learning Multiscale Processes

Given low-level theory and computationally-expensive simulation code, how can we model complex systems on a useful time scale?

Fundamental laws of Nature, Standard Model of Physics, and the most applied part of it, quantum mechanics, are well established. Theoretically, the dynamics of anything starting from a hydrogen atom and all the way to Earth's climate follow those equations. The problem is complexity [Dirac 1929]. An exact computation of a modest system containing 100 atoms is still beyond the capability of modern computers.

Some of the greatest scientific achievements resulted from breakthroughs in scale transitions: renormalization, density functional theory, Higgs boson, multiscale models for complex chemical systems, climate modeling, protein folding. Encouraged by the recent successes, this workshop aims to enable the development of universal AI methods that would be able to find efficient and accurate approximations, and use them for some of the most pressing and high-impact scientific problems that have computational complexity as the limiting factor to an in silico solution, such as:

## Title
RG-STFM: Renormalization Group-Structured Foundation Models for Cross-Domain Scale Transition Transfer

## Motivation
Multiscale modeling remains a fundamental bottleneck in computational science—from fusion reactors to climate prediction. While physics foundation models like PhysiX achieve impressive results through massive data and parameters, they learn scale transitions implicitly without leveraging the deep mathematical structure that governs how physics changes across scales. Renormalization group (RG) theory provides universal mathematical frameworks explaining why vastly different physical systems (magnets, fluids, superconductors) share identical scaling behavior near critical points. This universality has never been systematically embedded into foundation model architectures, leaving a critical gap between theoretical physics insights and modern AI capabilities.

## Main Idea
We propose embedding explicit RG flow structure as interpretable embeddings within a transformer-based foundation model. The core hypothesis: systems sharing the same RG universality class will cluster in embedding space, enabling zero-shot transfer across physically distinct domains. The RG Flow Encoder uses scale-aware positional encodings to capture hierarchical structure, while contrastive learning clusters embeddings by universality class. We will evaluate on The Well benchmark against PhysiX/GPhyT baselines, measuring: (1) zero-shot transfer accuracy, (2) universality class prediction (>80% accuracy), and (3) critical exponent regression (MAE<0.1). Expected outcomes include 10x improved sample efficiency for new domains and—crucially—interpretable embeddings that reveal which physical principles transfer across systems. This bridges RG theory with foundation models, potentially unlocking principled scale transitions for fusion, climate, and materials science.
