## Related Work

**Related Papers**
1. **Title**: A systematic review of AI-driven intelligent tutoring systems (ITS) in K-12 education
   - **Authors**: Létourneau A., Martineau M.D., et al.
   - **Summary**: Systematic review finding that ITS effects are positive but mitigated compared to non-ITS approaches, justifying the need for architectural innovation in educational AI systems.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Leveraging Machine Learning and NLP for Adaptive Education Systems
   - **Authors**: Ahmed K.R., Karim M.R., et al.
   - **Summary**: Proposes using GPT-4/BERT combined with reinforcement learning for children's education, though without incorporating developmental models; serves as a comparison baseline for adaptive education systems.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Personalized AI-driven education for school-age children
   - **Authors**: Atonte B.F.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates effect sizes of 0.15-0.30 standard deviations for personalized AI education, establishing baseline performance targets for child-focused educational AI.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Working Memory Capacity Development
   - **Authors**: Cowan
   - **Summary**: Establishes age-normed working memory capacity limits that can inform context window sizing in educational AI systems.
   - **Year**: 2016

5. **Title**: Cognitive Load Theory
   - **Authors**: Sweller
   - **Summary**: Provides a theoretical framework for computing cognitive load with age-specific thresholds applicable to educational system design.
   - **Year**: Not specified

6. **Title**: [Wang et al. study on ITS and non-cognitive factors]
   - **Authors**: Wang et al.
   - **Summary**: Identifies that intelligent tutoring systems pay less attention to non-cognitive factors, supporting the inclusion of developmental and cognitive factors in educational AI.
   - **Year**: 2023

**Key Challenges**
1. **Limited Architectural Innovation**: Current ITS show positive but mitigated effects compared to non-ITS approaches, indicating that existing architectures may not fully leverage AI capabilities for educational improvement.

2. **Absence of Developmental Models**: Existing adaptive education systems using advanced NLP (GPT-4/BERT) and reinforcement learning lack integration of child developmental models, limiting their age-appropriateness.

3. **Modest Effect Sizes**: Current personalized AI education achieves only 0.15-0.30 SD effect sizes, establishing a baseline that needs to be exceeded through improved approaches.

4. **Implementation Gap for Child-Specific AI**: Knowledge base searches reveal no direct matches for child-specific AI implementations, confirming a significant gap in developmentally-informed AI systems.

5. **Neglect of Non-Cognitive Factors**: Intelligent tutoring systems have historically paid less attention to non-cognitive factors such as developmental and cognitive considerations, limiting their effectiveness for child learners.
