## Related Work

**Related Papers**
1. **Title**: Active Inference as a Model of Agency (arXiv:2401.12917)
   - **Authors**: Da Costa, Tenka, Zhao, Sajid
   - **Summary**: Provides theoretical foundation showing Active Inference naturally integrates exploration/exploitation as minimizing risk/ambiguity, producing inherently explainable behavior.
   - **Year**: 2024

2. **Title**: XGate: Explainable Reinforcement Learning for Transparent and Trustworthy API Traffic Management (PMC11991207)
   - **Authors**: Jin, Xing, Ji, Liu
   - **Summary**: Validates that explainable reinforcement learning achieves 67% trust improvement and 41% faster intervention, while providing trust measurement methodology.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: JaxMARL-HFT: GPU-Accelerated Large-Scale MARL for High-Frequency Trading (arXiv:2511.02136)
   - **Authors**: Mohl, Frey, Leyland et al.
   - **Summary**: Provides 240x training speedup infrastructure and demonstrates multi-agent reinforcement learning feasibility for financial trading applications.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: MASA: Multi-Agent Self-Adaptive Framework for Dynamic Portfolio Risk Management
   - **Authors**: Li, Tam, Yeung
   - **Summary**: Presents multi-agent cooperation framework for finance but lacks an explainability mechanism for agent decisions.
   - **Year**: 2024

5. **Title**: A Comprehensive Review on Financial Explainable AI
   - **Authors**: Yeo et al.
   - **Summary**: Establishes a critical gap in the literature showing that current XAI approaches focus on single models rather than multi-agent systems.
   - **Year**: 2023

6. **Title**: XRL-Bench: A Benchmark for Evaluating and Comparing Explainable Reinforcement Learning Techniques (arXiv:2402.12685)
   - **Authors**: Xiong et al.
   - **Summary**: Provides a standardized evaluation framework for explainable reinforcement learning and identifies the need for unified metrics.
   - **Year**: 2024

7. **Title**: Objective Metrics for Human-Subjects Evaluation in Explainable Reinforcement Learning (arXiv:2501.19256)
   - **Authors**: Gyevnar, Towers
   - **Summary**: Advocates for objective behavioral metrics over subjective trust measures in evaluating explainable reinforcement learning systems.
   - **Year**: 2025

**Key Challenges**
1. **Lack of Explainability in Multi-Agent Financial Systems**: Existing multi-agent frameworks for finance, such as MASA, achieve cooperation but lack mechanisms for explaining agent decisions to human operators.

2. **Single-Model Focus of Current XAI**: Current explainable AI research predominantly addresses single models rather than the complexities of multi-agent systems, leaving a gap in understanding coordinated agent behavior.

3. **Absence of Unified Evaluation Metrics**: The field lacks standardized metrics for evaluating explainable reinforcement learning, making comparison across techniques difficult.

4. **Reliance on Subjective Trust Measures**: Existing evaluation approaches often depend on subjective trust assessments rather than objective behavioral metrics, limiting the reliability of human-subjects evaluations.
