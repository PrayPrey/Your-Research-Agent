## Related Work

**Related Papers**
1. **Title**: Participatory Concept Calibration: How Quantitative Framing Can Help Elucidate Richer Data
   - **Authors**: Eva Fortes
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that quantitative framing enables participants to analyze and validate complex concepts, with participants actively shaping knowledge production through a calibration process.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Scaling Dialogue for Democracy: Can Automated Deliberation Create More Deliberative Voters?
   - **Authors**: Fishkin, Bolotnyy, Lerner, Siu, Bradburn
   - **Summary**: Shows that deliberative microcosms via sortition can spread deliberation benefits and that structured deliberation produces considered opinions qualitatively different from raw preferences.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Institutionalizing ethics in AI through broader impact requirements
   - **Authors**: Prunkl et al.
   - **Summary**: Examines the NeurIPS broader impact requirement to demonstrate feasibility and challenges of institutionalizing ethical reflection, identifying the need for public deliberation but lacking an implementation pathway.
   - **Year**: 2021

4. **Title**: Evaluating the Social Impact of Generative AI Systems in Systems and Society
   - **Authors**: Solaiman et al.
   - **Summary**: Defines seven categories of social impact for generative AI: bias, cultural values, disparate performance, privacy, financial costs, environmental costs, and labor costs.
   - **Year**: 2023

5. **Title**: Wikibench: Community-Driven Data Curation for AI Evaluation on Wikipedia
   - **Authors**: Kuo et al.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that community input is feasible for AI evaluation data curation, focusing on collaborative dataset development.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: Collective Constitutional AI: Aligning a Language Model with Public Input
   - **Authors**: Huang et al.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates multi-stage public input integration at scale for AI value alignment purposes.
   - **Year**: 2024

7. **Title**: Re-imagining Algorithmic Fairness in India and Beyond
   - **Authors**: Sambasivan et al.
   - **Summary**: Argues that Western algorithmic fairness assumptions may not apply globally and that community empowerment approaches are needed for culturally appropriate AI systems.
   - **Year**: 2021

8. **Title**: OECD Participatory AI Framework
   - **Authors**: Not specified
   - **Summary**: Defines four levels of engagement for participatory AI development: Consultation, Contribution, Collaboration, and Co-design.
   - **Year**: Not specified

**Key Challenges**
1. **Missing Implementation Pathway for Public Deliberation**: While institutionalizing ethical reflection in AI (e.g., NeurIPS broader impact requirements) has been shown to be feasible, there remains no clear implementation pathway for integrating public deliberation into evaluation processes.

2. **Cultural Transferability of Fairness Concepts**: Western algorithmic fairness assumptions may not apply globally, necessitating cultural adaptation protocols and community empowerment approaches for AI evaluation criteria.

3. **Distinction Between Data Curation and Criteria Generation**: Existing community-driven approaches focus on data curation for AI evaluation rather than the generation of evaluation criteria themselves.

4. **Value Alignment vs. Evaluation Criteria**: Current participatory AI methods target value alignment but do not address the distinct challenge of generating community-informed evaluation criteria.

5. **Achieving Co-design Level Engagement**: Moving beyond consultation and contribution to true co-design level participation in AI evaluation remains an open challenge in participatory AI frameworks.
