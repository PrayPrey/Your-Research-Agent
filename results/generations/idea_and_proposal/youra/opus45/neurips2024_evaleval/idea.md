# Research Idea

## Title
Deliberative Evaluation Protocol with Participatory Concept Calibration (DEP-PCC) for Stakeholder-Driven AI Impact Assessment

## Motivation
Current AI evaluation frameworks are designed primarily by technical experts, potentially missing societal concerns that non-expert stakeholders experience firsthand. While NeurIPS Broader Impact statements have shifted norms toward considering societal impacts, no standardized methodology exists for meaningfully incorporating diverse public perspectives into evaluation criteria design. This gap risks creating assessments that fail to capture authentic community concerns about generative AI systems.

## Main Idea
We propose DEP-PCC, a two-tier protocol that systematically translates non-expert stakeholder concerns into validated AI evaluation criteria. The core mechanism operates through three causal steps: (1) structured citizens' assembly deliberation with balanced AI briefings elicits authentic concerns grounded in lived experience; (2) Participatory Concept Calibration uses quantitative framing to translate these concerns into formal criteria with iterative stakeholder verification (target fidelity >0.7); (3) broader survey verification (500+ participants) confirms representativeness.

We hypothesize that DEP-PCC-generated criteria will achieve significantly higher stakeholder satisfaction than expert-designed criteria alone (d≥0.5, p<0.05), with >80% comprehension scores among non-expert evaluators. Falsification occurs if satisfaction shows no significant difference or translation fidelity remains below 0.5 after multiple calibration rounds. This methodology could establish standards for participatory AI governance while producing more legitimate, comprehensive evaluation frameworks.
