#### Gap 3: Principle-to-Practice Gap in AI Ethics Implementation

**Relevance Classification:** 🎯 PRIMARY

**Connection Type:**
- ☑️ Blocks answering main research question: Directly addresses barriers to translating evaluation frameworks into practice
- ☑️ Relates to Q3: Adoption barriers include the gap between high-level principles and operational implementation
- ☑️ Relates to Q4: Policy recommendations must bridge the principle-practice divide

**Current State:** Multiple AI ethics principles and guidelines have been published globally (OECD, UNESCO, EU AI Act, IEEE), but practical implementation remains fragmented. Tidjon & Khomh (2023) found significant gaps between AI ethics principles and their operationalization across different continents. Hladikova et al. (2025) identified common barriers: overly abstract language, poor accessibility, and insufficient practical guidance. The NeurIPS broader impact requirement evolved from mandatory statements to checklists partly due to implementation difficulties.

**Missing Piece:** There is no validated methodology for translating high-level AI ethics principles into concrete, context-specific evaluation protocols that practitioners can implement. Current gaps include: (1) actionable toolkits bridging abstract principles and specific metrics, (2) organizational change management frameworks for evaluation adoption, (3) training curricula for non-expert evaluators, (4) integration pathways into existing software development workflows.

**Potential Impact:** High
