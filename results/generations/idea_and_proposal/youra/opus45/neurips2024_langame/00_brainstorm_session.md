## Starting Context

**Background:** Ludwig Wittgenstein, in his seminal work "Philosophical Investigations", introduced the concept of "language games." This framework views language as an adaptive system where words acquire meaning through use, emphasizing its social and interactive nature. Research in cognitive science reinforces this notion, highlighting that genuine language acquisition thrives on dynamic and context-driven interactions. Language emergence simulations further demonstrate the critical role of language transmission within a population of agents in shaping modern languages. Game theory experiments showcase the superiority of interactive self-play loops compared to traditional imitation-based models. Meanwhile, the core training paradigm in language processing remains purely based on supervised and preference losses and has barely changed over the past years. Besides, some limitations in LLMs, e.g., restricted planning abilities and insufficient personalization, suggest a potential deficiency in their training: the lack of interaction.

## Research Question Development

### Initial Question

How can Language Gamification - interactive training paradigms based on multi-agent language games - improve LLM capabilities beyond what static supervised learning achieves?

### Refined Question

**How can multi-agent interactive training loops, inspired by Wittgenstein's language games framework, enable LLMs to develop emergent language grounding, improved planning abilities, and enhanced personalization that purely supervised training paradigms fail to achieve?**

### Detailed Sub-Questions

1. **Cognitive Science Perspective:** What mechanisms from human language acquisition (dynamic, context-driven interactions) can be effectively translated into LLM training paradigms?

2. **Multi-Agent Learning:** What theoretical foundations from language games and game theory can establish principled approaches to multi-agent LLM training?

3. **In-Context Learning:** How does interactive exposure during training affect the plasticity and adaptability of LLMs during inference-time language interactions?

4. **Language Emergence:** Can deep learning tools model natural language game engagement to create more human-like language understanding in LLMs?

5. **Deep Reinforcement Learning:** How can RL approaches leveraging language games specifically foster improved planning and reasoning abilities in LLMs?

6. **Self-Improvement:** What are the most promising self-improvement approaches that enable LLMs to bootstrap their capabilities through interaction?

7. **Embodiment:** What role does language gamification play in developing embodied agents that can ground language in physical or simulated environments?
