#### Gap 1: Compositional Reasoning Limitations in LLMs

**Relevance Classification:** 🎯 PRIMARY

**Connection Type:**
- ☑️ Blocks answering main RQ: Directly affects assessment of "fundamental limitations" and "developmental pathways" toward AGI
- ☑️ Relates to Q4: "What intrinsic capabilities or limitations in LLMs—particularly in reasoning and planning—may impede or enable AGI progression?"

**Current State:** LLMs demonstrate impressive in-context learning and emergent abilities at scale (Wei et al., 2022). However, compositional generalization—combining known components into novel structures—remains inconsistent. Xu et al. (2024) found that scaling helps simple compositional tasks but NOT complex multi-step reasoning. Parashar et al. (2025) confirmed no single inference-time technique works across all reasoning tasks.

**Missing Piece:** There is no unified understanding of WHY compositional reasoning fails in complex scenarios. Current evaluations (Sys2Bench, TransportBench) identify failures but don't explain the underlying architectural or representational causes. The gap between "simple compositional tasks" and "complex multi-step reasoning" lacks formal characterization.

**Potential Impact:** HIGH - Without understanding compositional reasoning limits, we cannot reliably predict which AGI capabilities will emerge at scale and which require fundamental architectural changes.

### Key Findings

**Research Question**: What are the fundamental capabilities, limitations, and developmental pathways that determine the proximity of current LLMs and foundation models to AGI?

**Finding 1 - Emergent Abilities are Real but Unpredictable**: Wei et al. (2022, 3194 citations) established that LLMs exhibit capabilities not present in smaller models, but these emergent abilities cannot be predicted by extrapolating smaller model performance. This fundamentally complicates AGI proximity assessment—we cannot reliably forecast which capabilities will emerge at future scales.

**Finding 2 - Compositional Reasoning Remains a Fundamental Barrier**: Despite strong performance on simple compositional tasks, LLMs consistently fail on complex multi-step reasoning (Xu et al., 2024; Parashar et al., 2025). No current inference-time technique or architectural modification has resolved this limitation across diverse tasks. This represents a potential structural barrier to AGI.

**Finding 3 - Neurosymbolic Integration Shows Promise but Lacks Scalable Implementations**: Multiple theoretical frameworks exist for integrating symbolic reasoning with neural networks (Šír 2024, Jaysingha 2025), but production-ready implementations compatible with modern LLMs are absent. The computational complexity barriers identified by Šír (2024) remain unaddressed.

**Finding 4 - AGI Governance Frameworks are Emerging but Untested**: Novel governance approaches (Institutional AI, MI9, Governable AI) propose sophisticated mechanisms for safe AGI development, but none have empirical validation at scale.
