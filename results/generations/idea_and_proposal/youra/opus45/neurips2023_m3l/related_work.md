## Related Work

**Related Papers**
1. **Title**: Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models (arXiv:2001.08361)
   - **Authors**: Kaplan, McCandlish, Henighan, Brown et al.
   - **Summary**: Establishes power-law scaling relationships between loss and model size, dataset size, and compute, providing foundational baseline scaling laws for neural language models.
   - **Year**: 2020

2. **Title**: What needs to go right for an induction head? (arXiv:2404.07129)
   - **Authors**: Singh, Moskovitz, Hill, Chan, Saxe
   - **Summary**: Identifies three subcircuits that interact to drive induction head formation, demonstrating that phase changes in loss coincide with induction head emergence.
   - **Year**: 2024

3. **Title**: When can in-context learning generalize out of task distribution? (arXiv:2506.05574)
   - **Authors**: Goddard, Smith, Ngampruetikorn, Schwab
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that task diversity drives the transition from specialized to generalized in-context learning, establishing dependence on task distribution.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Statistical Physics for Artificial Neural Networks (arXiv:2512.06518)
   - **Authors**: Pei
   - **Summary**: Establishes topological similarities between artificial neural networks and Ising-type models, validating the transfer of statistical physics concepts to neural network analysis.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: Broken Neural Scaling Laws (arXiv:2210.14891)
   - **Authors**: Caballero, Gupta, Rish, Krueger
   - **Summary**: Proposes smoothly broken power law models that can capture non-monotonic transitions including double descent phenomena in neural network scaling.
   - **Year**: 2022

6. **Title**: The Evolution of Statistical Induction Heads (arXiv:2402.11004)
   - **Authors**: Edelman, Edelman, Goel, Malach, Tsilivis
   - **Summary**: Documents multi-phase emergence patterns of statistical induction heads during training, supporting frameworks for understanding multi-phase transitions.
   - **Year**: 2024

7. **Title**: Learning to grok: Emergence of in-context learning (arXiv:2406.02550)
   - **Authors**: He, Doshi, Das, Gromov
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that GPT-style transformers exhibit phase transitions from in-distribution to out-of-distribution generalization as the number of pretraining tasks increases.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges**
1. **Lack of Emergence Mechanism**: Existing scaling laws establish power-law relationships but do not provide mechanisms to explain the emergence of capabilities in neural networks.
2. **Quantifying Phase Transitions**: While phase transition phenomena have been observed in transformer learning dynamics, frameworks for precisely quantifying these transitions remain underdeveloped.
3. **Modeling Non-Monotonic Behavior**: Standard scaling laws struggle to capture non-monotonic transitions and phenomena like double descent, requiring alternative modeling approaches.
