## 1. Research Questions

### Primary Research Question
How can developmental moral psychology frameworks and pluralistic value theories from moral philosophy inform novel value alignment methodologies that address the limitations of RLHF and ensure diverse, representative values are incorporated into AI systems?

### Detailed Research Questions
1. **Developmental Perspective:** What can theories of developmental moral psychology (e.g., Kohlberg, Gilligan, Turiel) teach us about structuring AI value learning as a progressive, stage-based process rather than static optimization?

2. **Pluralistic Value Integration:** How can we move beyond monolithic value alignment to incorporate diverse voices, views, and values from underrepresented groups into AI systems?

3. **Beyond RLHF:** What are theoretically-grounded alternatives to reinforcement learning from human feedback that better capture the complexity of moral reasoning (e.g., deliberative processes, moral exemplar modeling)?

4. **Value Consistency Audit:** How can we systematically assess whether values embedded in current AI systems are consistent with the pluralistic values of society at large?

5. **Bidirectional Advancement:** How can AI tools and methods contribute back to advancing the fields of moral philosophy and moral psychology themselves?

## 9. Conclusion

### Key Findings

1. **Developmental Psychology Gap Confirmed:** Literature search confirms that while developmental psychology has successfully informed AI architecture design (Piloto et al. 2022 on intuitive physics), this approach has NOT been applied to value alignment. Multiple papers evaluate LLMs against Kohlberg's stages but none use developmental theory to design training curricula or architectures.

2. **Pluralistic Alignment is Active but Incomplete:** There is substantial recent work on pluralistic value alignment (2022-2025), but current methods lack formal representation guarantees. The field recognizes the problem (Schuster & Kilov 2025 call it "an open problem") but has not solved it.

3. **RLHF Limitations Well-Documented:** Multiple papers document RLHF and DAP method limitations including overoptimization (Rafailov 2024), failure to accommodate moral disagreement (Schuster 2025), and over-refusal vs. helpfulness tradeoffs (Jin 2026).

4. **Deliberative Approaches Emerging:** Recent work on deliberative alignment (2025-2026) shows promise but remains early-stage. Case-based reasoning and reflective equilibrium approaches are being explored.

5. **Evaluation Benchmarks Proliferating:** Multiple 2025 papers introduce moral reasoning benchmarks for LLMs (PRIME, BengaliMoralBench, LLM Ethics Benchmark), indicating field maturation in assessment capabilities.

### Answer to Detailed Question (Preliminary)

**Q1 (Developmental Perspective):** Developmental moral psychology (Kohlberg stages) has NOT been systematically applied to AI value learning, despite successful applications in other AI domains (intuitive physics). This represents a significant opportunity for novel methodology development.

**Q5 (Bidirectional Advancement):** LLMs are being used to simulate moral reasoning for research purposes (Smith-Vaniz 2025), but systematic contributions back to moral philosophy/psychology remain limited.
