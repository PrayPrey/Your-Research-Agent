# Research Proposal: Synthetic-Guided Adaptive Data Mixing: Closed-Loop Feedback Control for Preventing Model Collapse in Mixed Synthetic-Real Training

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background

The remarkable progress of modern machine learning, particularly deep learning, has been fundamentally driven by access to large-scale, high-quality training data. Recent advances in large language models and vision systems demonstrate that model performance scales predictably with data quantity and diversity. However, this data-hungry paradigm faces significant practical barriers: privacy regulations (GDPR, HIPAA), copyright restrictions, fairness concerns, and the prohibitive cost of manual annotation create substantial obstacles to data access across domains including healthcare, finance, and autonomous systems.

Synthetic data generation has emerged as a promising solution to these challenges. Generative AI systems—including diffusion models, large language models, and GANs—can produce virtually unlimited training samples that circumvent many legal and ethical constraints associated with real data. Recent work demonstrates that synthetic data can improve model performance in domain-specific applications, enhance reasoning capabilities, and address class imbalance in long-tailed distributions. The appeal is clear: if we can generate high-quality synthetic data on demand, the data access problem may be fundamentally solved.

However, a critical limitation threatens this optimistic vision: **model collapse**. Recent theoretical work by Seddik et al. (2024) proves that training iteratively on synthetic data leads to inevitable performance degradation, as models progressively lose information about the true data distribution. Empirical studies confirm that models trained on synthetic-heavy mixtures exhibit calibration drift, reduced feature diversity, and catastrophic forgetting of tail classes. The fundamental tension is that synthetic data provides diversity benefits but introduces distributional artifacts that accumulate during training.

Current approaches to mixing synthetic and real data employ either fixed ratios (e.g., 50% synthetic, 50% real) or static curricula that follow predetermined schedules. While static curricula like DisCL (ICCV 2025) demonstrate 2-4% improvements over fixed ratios on benchmarks like ImageNet-LT, they cannot adapt to the evolving training dynamics of individual models. A model experiencing rapid divergence on synthetic data continues receiving the same synthetic proportion, accelerating collapse. Conversely, a model that could benefit from more synthetic diversity is unnecessarily constrained.

### 1.2 Research Objectives

This research proposes **SGADM (Synthetic-Guided Adaptive Data Mixing)**, a closed-loop feedback control system that dynamically adjusts synthetic-to-real data ratios based on real-time model state signals. Our primary objectives are:

1. **Develop a principled feedback control framework** that detects distribution drift before collapse onset using three complementary signals: loss divergence, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), and Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD).

2. **Design and validate a PID-style controller** that translates feedback signals into appropriate ratio adjustments, reducing synthetic data when divergence exceeds thresholds while maximizing diversity benefits when the model is stable.

3. **Empirically demonstrate** that SGADM achieves 2-5% accuracy improvement over fixed-ratio baselines and maintains tail-class accuracy above 15% on standard benchmarks (ImageNet-LT, iWildCam).

4. **Establish the causal mechanism** linking feedback-driven ratio adjustment to collapse prevention through systematic ablation studies.

### 1.3 Significance

This research addresses a fundamental question posed by the workshop: *Will synthetic data ultimately solve the data access problem?* Our contribution provides a principled framework for **safe synthetic data utilization**—maximizing the benefits of synthetic augmentation while preventing the collapse dynamics that currently limit its applicability. If successful, SGADM establishes feedback-driven mixing as a new paradigm for data access, enabling practitioners to confidently leverage synthetic data across domains where data scarcity currently limits machine learning deployment.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Problem Formulation

Consider a supervised learning task with access to a real dataset $\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}} = \{(x_i^r, y_i^r)\}_{i=1}^{N_r}$ and a synthetic dataset $\mathcal{D}_{\text{synth}} = \{(x_j^s, y_j^s)\}_{j=1}^{N_s}$ generated by a fixed generator $G$ (e.g., Stable Diffusion). At each training epoch $t$, we construct a mixed batch with synthetic ratio $r_t \in [0, 1]$:

$$\mathcal{B}_t = \{(x, y) : x \sim r_t \cdot P_{\text{synth}} + (1 - r_t) \cdot P_{\text{real}}\}$$

The goal is to learn a model $f_\theta$ that maximizes performance on held-out real test data while preventing model collapse. We define collapse as the condition where:

$$\text{Acc}_{\text{tail}}(f_\theta) < \tau_{\text{collapse}} \quad \text{or} \quad \text{ECE}(f_\theta) > \tau_{\text{ECE}}$$

where $\tau_{\text{collapse}} = 0.10$ and $\tau_{\text{ECE}} = 0.15$ are empirically determined thresholds.

### 2.2 Feedback Signal Design

SGADM monitors three complementary signals computed at each epoch $t$:

**Signal 1: Loss Divergence ($\Delta L_t$)**

$$\Delta L_t = \mathcal{L}(f_\theta; \mathcal{D}_{\text{synth}}^{\text{val}}) - \mathcal{L}(f_\theta; \mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}^{\text{val}})$$

where $\mathcal{L}$ is the cross-entropy loss on validation splits. A positive $\Delta L_t$ indicates the model fits real data better than synthetic, suggesting potential overfitting to real data artifacts. A negative $\Delta L_t$ indicates synthetic overfitting, a precursor to collapse.

**Signal 2: Expected Calibration Error ($\text{ECE}_t$)**

$$\text{ECE}_t = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \frac{|B_m|}{n} \left| \text{acc}(B_m) - \text{conf}(B_m) \right|$$

where $B_m$ are confidence bins ($M=15$), computed on real validation data with temperature scaling. Rising ECE indicates calibration drift from synthetic artifacts.

**Signal 3: Maximum Mean Discrepancy ($\text{MMD}_t$)**

$$\text{MMD}_t^2 = \mathbb{E}[k(z^s, z^s)] - 2\mathbb{E}[k(z^s, z^r)] + \mathbb{E}[k(z^r, z^r)]$$

where $z^s, z^r$ are feature representations from the penultimate layer and $k$ is an RBF kernel. Increasing MMD indicates the model's internal representations are diverging between synthetic and real data.

**Composite Divergence Score:**

To combine signals, we compute a normalized composite score:

$$D_t = w_1 \cdot \sigma(\Delta L_t) + w_2 \cdot \sigma(\text{ECE}_t) + w_3 \cdot \sigma(\text{MMD}_t)$$

where $\sigma(\cdot)$ denotes min-max normalization over a rolling window of 10 epochs, and weights $w_1 = 0.4, w_2 = 0.3, w_3 = 0.3$ are set based on signal reliability.

### 2.3 PID Controller Design

SGADM employs a PID-style controller to translate the divergence score into ratio adjustments:

$$\Delta r_t = -\left( k_p \cdot e_t + k_i \cdot \sum_{\tau=1}^{t} e_\tau + k_d \cdot (e_t - e_{t-1}) \right)$$

where $e_t = D_t - D_{\text{target}}$ is the error signal, $D_{\text{target}} = 0.3$ is the target divergence level, and controller gains are $k_p = 0.1$, $k_i = 0.01$, $k_d = 0.05$.

The ratio update rule with exponential moving average (EMA) smoothing is:

$$r_{t+1} = \text{clip}\left( \alpha \cdot r_t + (1 - \alpha) \cdot (r_t + \Delta r_t), \; r_{\min}, \; r_{\max} \right)$$

where $\alpha = 0.9$ provides smoothing, and $r_{\min} = 0.1$, $r_{\max} = 0.8$ prevent extreme ratios.

### 2.4 Complete Algorithm

**Algorithm 1: SGADM Training**

```
Input: D_real, D_synth, model f_θ, epochs T, initial ratio r_0 = 0.5
Output: Trained model f_θ*

1: Initialize: r_1 ← r_0, integral_sum ← 0, D_prev ← 0
2: for t = 1 to T do
3:    # Construct mixed batch
4:    B_t ← Sample(D_real, D_synth, r_t)
5:    
6:    # Standard training step
7:    θ ← θ - η∇L(f_θ; B_t)
8:    
9:    # Compute feedback signals (every epoch)
10:   ΔL_t ← L(f_θ; D_synth^val) - L(f_θ; D_real^val)
11:   ECE_t ← ComputeECE(f_θ, D_real^val, bins=15)
12:   MMD_t ← ComputeMMD(f_θ, D_synth^val, D_real^val)
13:   
14:   # Normalize and combine signals
15:   D_t ← w_1·σ(ΔL_t) + w_2·σ(ECE_t) + w_3·σ(MMD_t)
16:   
17:   # PID control
18:   e_t ← D_t - D_target
19:   integral_sum ← integral_sum + e_t
20:   derivative ← e_t - e_prev
21:   Δr_t ← -(k_p·e_t + k_i·integral_sum + k_d·derivative)
22:   
23:   # Update ratio with EMA smoothing
24:   r_{t+1} ← clip(α·r_t + (1-α)·(r_t + Δr_t), r_min, r_max)
25:   
26:   # Store for next iteration
27:   e_prev ← e_t
28: end for
29: return f_θ
```

### 2.5 Experimental Design

**Datasets:**
- **ImageNet-LT**: Long-tailed version of ImageNet with 115.8K images across 1000 classes, imbalance ratio 256:1. Synthetic data generated via Stable Diffusion XL with class-conditional prompts.
- **iWildCam**: Wildlife camera trap dataset with 203K images across 182 species, featuring natural domain shift. Synthetic augmentation via ControlNet with pose conditioning.

**Baselines:**
1. **Fixed-Ratio**: Static ratios at $r \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.7\}$
2. **DisCL** (ICCV 2025): State-of-the-art static curriculum for synthetic data
3. **Random Ratio**: Uniformly sampled $r_t \sim U(0.2, 0.8)$ each epoch
4. **Real-Only**: Training without synthetic data ($r = 0$)

**Model Architectures:**
- ResNet-50 (CNN baseline)
- ViT-B/16 (Transformer baseline)

**Training Configuration:**
- Optimizer: AdamW, learning rate $10^{-4}$ with cosine decay
- Batch size: 256 (mixed according to $r_t$)
- Epochs: 100 (ImageNet-LT), 50 (iWildCam)
- Hardware: 4× NVIDIA A100 GPUs

**Evaluation Metrics:**
1. **Overall Accuracy**: Top-1 accuracy on held-out real test set
2. **Tail-Class Accuracy**: Accuracy on classes with <20 training samples
3. **ECE**: Expected Calibration Error (lower is better)
4. **FID**: Fréchet Inception Distance of learned features (collapse indicator)
5. **Training Overhead**: Wall-clock time increase vs. fixed-ratio baseline

### 2.6 Statistical Analysis Plan

Each experiment is repeated with $n = 20$ random seeds. We report:
- Mean ± standard deviation
- 95% confidence intervals
- Cohen's d effect size
- Paired t-test p-values (one-tailed, $\alpha = 0.05$)

**Primary Success Criterion**: SGADM accuracy $\geq$ Fixed-Ratio accuracy + 2% with $p < 0.05$

**Secondary Success Criterion**: SGADM accuracy $\geq$ DisCL accuracy + 1% with $p < 0.10$

### 2.7 Ablation Studies

To establish the causal mechanism, we conduct systematic ablations:

1. **Signal Ablation**: Remove each feedback signal individually to assess contribution
2. **Controller Ablation**: Replace PID with proportional-only (P) and proportional-integral (PI) variants
3. **Smoothing Ablation**: Vary EMA coefficient $\alpha \in \{0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.95\}$
4. **Threshold Sensitivity**: Vary $D_{\text{target}} \in \{0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5\}$

## 3. Expected Outcomes & Impact

### 3.1 Expected Results

**Primary Outcomes:**
- SGADM will achieve **2-5% accuracy improvement** over fixed-ratio baselines on ImageNet-LT and iWildCam, with statistical significance ($p < 0.05$).
- SGADM will maintain **tail-class accuracy above 15%** throughout training, compared to degradation below 10% for suboptimal fixed ratios.
- Computational overhead will remain **below 5%** of total training time.

**Mechanistic Insights:**
- Loss divergence will emerge as the most predictive signal for collapse onset (expected $r > 0.6$ correlation with subsequent accuracy drop).
- The PID controller will demonstrate stable convergence to task-appropriate ratios, typically settling between 0.3-0.5 for balanced datasets and 0.5-0.7 for long-tailed distributions.
- Ablation studies will confirm that all three feedback signals contribute meaningfully, with removal of any single signal degrading performance by 0.5-1.5%.

### 3.2 Broader Impact

**Scientific Contributions:**
1. **Theoretical Framework**: SGADM provides the first principled, feedback-driven approach to synthetic-real data mixing, bridging control theory and machine learning.
2. **Collapse Prevention**: Our work operationalizes the theoretical insights of Seddik et al. (2024) into a practical algorithm that prevents collapse while maximizing synthetic data benefits.
3. **Benchmark Establishment**: We will release standardized evaluation protocols for synthetic data mixing, including collapse indicators and calibration metrics.

**Practical Applications:**
- **Healthcare**: Enable training on synthetic medical images while maintaining diagnostic accuracy on real patient data.
- **Autonomous Systems**: Safely augment rare scenario training (e.g., accident conditions) with synthetic data without degrading common-case performance.
- **Long-Tailed Recognition**: Provide principled augmentation for underrepresented classes without overfitting to synthetic artifacts.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
- Controller hyperparameters may require task-specific tuning; future work will explore meta-learning approaches for automatic gain scheduling.
- Current design assumes availability of real validation data; extensions to fully synthetic scenarios require alternative anchor mechanisms.
- Multi-modal validation (text, tabular data) is needed to confirm generalization beyond vision tasks.

### 3.3 Conclusion

This research addresses a critical gap in synthetic data utilization: the lack of principled methods for preventing model collapse in mixed synthetic-real training. By introducing SGADM—a closed-loop feedback control system that dynamically adjusts data mixing ratios—we provide a practical solution that maximizes the diversity benefits of synthetic data while maintaining model calibration and tail-class performance. If successful, this work establishes feedback-driven mixing as a foundational paradigm for safe synthetic data utilization, bringing us closer to solving the data access problem that currently limits machine learning deployment across critical domains.
