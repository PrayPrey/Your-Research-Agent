## Title
Federated Cultural Evaluation Network: Validating AI Locally, Aggregating Globally

## Motivation
Current AI evaluation practices rely on centralized benchmarks designed by researchers, inadvertently imposing Western-centric criteria that fail to capture diverse cultural values. Studies show these approaches achieve only moderate correlation (r≈0.4-0.5) with local user satisfaction, revealing a critical validity gap. As AI deploys globally, this gap risks homogenizing cultural expression and marginalizing non-Western perspectives. The core problem: who holds epistemic authority to judge cultural appropriateness?

## Main Idea
We propose the Federated Cultural Evaluation Network (FCEN), a distributed architecture that shifts cultural validation authority to local communities while enabling meaningful global comparison. The core mechanism operates through four causal steps: (1) Cultural Evaluation Nodes staffed by trained participatory mediators conduct community-based validation of AI outputs; (2) local assessments map to a bottom-up Shared Cultural Ontology anchored by Wikidata identifiers; (3) a Federated Aggregation Protocol synthesizes scores using Differential Item Functioning analysis to achieve measurement invariance; (4) the resulting Cultural Inclusiveness Score reflects authentic local validations without Western bias.

We predict FCEN will achieve user satisfaction correlation >0.7, significantly exceeding centralized benchmarks. Falsification occurs if correlation ≤0.5 or if inter-CEN reliability falls below acceptable thresholds. This paradigm shift—"validate locally, aggregate globally"—empowers cultural communities in AI governance while providing scalable, valid cross-cultural assessment.
