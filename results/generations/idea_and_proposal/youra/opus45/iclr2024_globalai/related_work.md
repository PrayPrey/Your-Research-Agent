## Related Work

**Related Papers**
1. **Title**: The Human Labour of Data Work: World Wide Dishes (arXiv:2502.05961)
   - **Authors**: S. Hall, S. Dalal, R. Sefala, et al.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that participatory mediators perform crucial labor including trust-building, accessibility, and value contextualization that enables authentic community data collection.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: A Survey on Heterogeneity in Federated Learning
   - **Authors**: T.M. Mengistu, T. Kim, J.W. Lin
   - **Summary**: Surveys how federated learning handles data heterogeneity while preserving privacy through distributed aggregation mechanisms.
   - **Year**: 2024

3. **Title**: The Case for Thick Evaluations of Cultural Representation (AIES 2025)
   - **Authors**: R. Qadri, M. Díaz, D. Wang, M. Madaio
   - **Summary**: Argues that community co-constructed metrics outperform researcher-imposed criteria, advocating for "thick" evaluation approaches that respect situated meaning-making.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: CulturalBench (arXiv:2410.02677)
   - **Authors**: Not specified
   - **Summary**: Presents a benchmark with 1,696 human-written questions covering 45 global regions, developed using Human-AI Red-Teaming methodology.
   - **Year**: 2024

5. **Title**: WorldCuisines
   - **Authors**: Winata et al.
   - **Summary**: Introduces the largest multicultural VQA benchmark with over 1 million datapoints across 30 languages, demonstrating scalability in multicultural dataset creation.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: Exposing Blindspots: Cultural Bias in Generative Image Models
   - **Authors**: Seo et al.
   - **Summary**: Conducts cross-country evaluation revealing Global-North bias in default text-to-image generations, demonstrating systematic cultural biases in generative models.
   - **Year**: 2025

7. **Title**: Cross-Cultural Psychometrics Literature
   - **Authors**: Various
   - **Summary**: Establishes Differential Item Functioning (DIF) analysis methods for testing measurement invariance across cultural contexts.
   - **Year**: 2021-2024

**Key Challenges**
1. **Centralized Design Limitations**: Existing benchmarks like CulturalBench, despite covering multiple regions, employ centralized design approaches that limit cultural authority and community ownership over evaluation criteria.

2. **Global-North Bias Perpetuation**: Centralized approaches to AI development and evaluation perpetuate systematic biases favoring Global-North perspectives, as evidenced by cross-country evaluations of generative models.

3. **Scale vs. Authenticity Trade-off**: While large-scale multicultural datasets demonstrate scalability is achievable, centralized collection methods may compromise authentic cultural representation and community-driven meaning-making.

4. **Measurement Invariance Across Cultures**: Ensuring that evaluation metrics and benchmarks function equivalently across diverse cultural contexts requires specialized techniques like Differential Item Functioning analysis to detect and address cultural bias in measurements.

5. **Community Trust and Accessibility**: Authentic community data collection requires significant labor in trust-building and accessibility that standard data collection approaches often overlook, necessitating participatory mediator roles.
