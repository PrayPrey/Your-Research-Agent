## Related Work

**Related Papers**
1. **Title**: Predictive Coding beyond Gaussian Distributions (NeurIPS 2022)
   - **Authors**: Pinchetti et al.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that predictive coding can train transformers comparably to backpropagation, validating the connection between predictive coding and deep learning.
   - **Year**: 2022

2. **Title**: Bayesian brain theory: Computational neuroscience
   - **Authors**: Bottemanne
   - **Summary**: Establishes that the brain encodes probability densities via hierarchical error signals, providing theoretical foundation for predictive coding frameworks.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Predictive coding and Bayesian inference in brain (Current Opinion in Neurobiology)
   - **Authors**: Aitchison & Lengyel
   - **Summary**: Provides a mechanistic algorithmic account of predictive coding, establishing the prediction error framework for understanding neural computation.
   - **Year**: 2017

4. **Title**: Refining local computations for inference (Advances in Theory of Neuroscience)
   - **Authors**: Yu & Xu
   - **Summary**: Presents predictive coding as a biologically plausible alternative to backpropagation, contributing to recent integration of predictive coding with deep learning.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: Residual Stream Analysis with Multi-Layer SAEs (OpenReview)
   - **Authors**: Lawson et al.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that latents activate at specific layers in neural networks, providing validation methodology for multi-layer sparse autoencoder analysis.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: How LLMs encode theory-of-mind (Nature Communications)
   - **Authors**: Wu et al.
   - **Summary**: Shows that Theory of Mind capabilities are encoded in 0.001% of parameters and linked to RoPE, providing evidence for ToM circuits in LLMs.
   - **Year**: 2025

7. **Title**: Mechanistic Indicators of Understanding (arXiv)
   - **Authors**: Beckmann & Queloz
   - **Summary**: Proposes a tiered framework for understanding consisting of conceptual, state, and principled levels, providing theoretical grounding for mechanistic interpretability.
   - **Year**: 2025

8. **Title**: Towards Automated Circuit Discovery (NeurIPS 2023)
   - **Authors**: Conmy et al.
   - **Summary**: Introduces the ACDC algorithm for automated circuit discovery in neural networks, serving as a complementary tool for mechanistic analysis.
   - **Year**: 2023

9. **Title**: Clever Hans or Neural Theory of Mind? (EMNLP 2023)
   - **Authors**: Shapira et al.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that LLMs rely on shallow heuristics rather than genuine reasoning for Theory of Mind tasks, motivating the need for deeper mechanistic investigation.
   - **Year**: 2023

10. **Title**: Are Emergent Abilities a Mirage? (NeurIPS 2023)
    - **Authors**: Schaeffer et al.
    - **Summary**: Argues that apparent emergent abilities in LLMs may be metric artifacts rather than genuine capabilities, highlighting the need for mechanistic validation.
    - **Year**: 2023

11. **Title**: HI-TOM: Higher-Order Theory of Mind (EMNLP 2023)
    - **Authors**: He et al.
    - **Summary**: Introduces an adversarial benchmark showing that LLMs fail at higher-order Theory of Mind reasoning, providing challenging evaluation scenarios.
    - **Year**: 2023

12. **Title**: ToMBench (ACL 2024)
    - **Authors**: Not specified
    - **Summary**: Provides a standard benchmark for evaluating Theory of Mind capabilities in language models.
    - **Year**: 2024

13. **Title**: TransformerLens
    - **Authors**: Not specified
    - **Summary**: A tool for extracting activations from transformer models, enabling mechanistic interpretability research.
    - **Year**: 2023

14. **Title**: Pythia
    - **Authors**: EleutherAI
    - **Summary**: A family of language models designed for interpretability research, providing accessible models for mechanistic analysis.
    - **Year**: 2023

**Key Challenges**
1. **Shallow Heuristics vs. Genuine Reasoning**: LLMs may rely on shallow heuristics rather than genuine Theory of Mind reasoning, making it difficult to distinguish true understanding from pattern matching.
2. **Metric Artifacts in Emergence**: Apparent emergent abilities in LLMs may be artifacts of evaluation metrics rather than genuine capabilities, necessitating mechanistic validation approaches.
3. **Higher-Order ToM Failures**: Current LLMs fail at higher-order Theory of Mind tasks, indicating limitations in their ability to model nested mental states.
4. **Biological Plausibility Gap**: Bridging predictive coding theory from neuroscience with deep learning implementations remains an ongoing challenge for understanding cognition in artificial systems.
5. **Activation Access Limitations**: Many state-of-the-art models (e.g., GPT-3/4) do not provide activation access, limiting replication and mechanistic analysis of their internal representations.
