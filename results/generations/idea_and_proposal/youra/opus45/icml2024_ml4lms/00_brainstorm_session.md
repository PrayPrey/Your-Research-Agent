## Starting Context

**Background:** This research area aims to highlight translational ML research in biology and chemistry for real-world applications in life and materials science. The goal is to bridge theoretical advances with practical applications and connect academic and industry researchers. Biology and chemistry play a central role in understanding life and are fundamental pillars of human well-being through their roles as medicines, materials, or agro-chemicals.

**Source Type:** Workshop CFP (ICML 2024 - ML for Life and Material Science: From Theory to Industry Applications)

**Motivation:** With increasing challenges associated with climate change, growth of the global population, diseases associated with aging, and the global supply of food and energy, it is becoming increasingly urgent to accelerate the pace at which technical discoveries can be made and translated into practical solutions to these societal issues.

**Key Challenge:** Compared to other modalities such as images or language, the study of biology and chemistry with machine learning is not as industrially established. Multiple factors contribute to this delay, including the need for different research questions requiring many levels and scales of representation - from electronic structure to graph and point cloud representations of (bio)molecules, to protein and nucleic acid sequences, crystals, omics data, cell and tissue-level representations.

## Research Question Development

### Initial Question

How can machine learning effectively bridge the gap between theoretical advances and practical industrial applications in life sciences and materials science?

### Refined Question

How can novel ML models, algorithms, and datasets accelerate the translation of scientific discoveries in biology, chemistry, and materials science into practical solutions for healthcare, drug discovery, and sustainable materials—while addressing the unique challenges of multi-scale molecular representations?

### Detailed Sub-Questions

1. **Dataset & Benchmarking:** What are the critical gaps, opportunities, and pitfalls in current ML datasets and benchmarks for healthcare and materials science applications, and how can dataset curation be improved to enable more robust and generalizable models?

2. **Novel Models & Algorithms:** What novel ML architectures and algorithms can unlock capabilities in molecular and biological systems that were previously only achievable through expensive experimental or simulation-based approaches (e.g., quantum chemistry, molecular dynamics)?

3. **Multi-Scale Representations:** How can ML models effectively handle the diverse levels and scales of biological and chemical representations—from electronic structure through molecular graphs to tissue-level data—in a unified or interoperable framework?

4. **Industry Translation:** What are the key barriers preventing academic ML advances from being adopted in industrial life science and materials applications, and what methodological or infrastructural innovations can accelerate this translation?

5. **Societal Impact:** How can ML research in life and material sciences be strategically directed to address urgent societal challenges including climate change adaptation, aging-related diseases, food security, and sustainable energy materials?
