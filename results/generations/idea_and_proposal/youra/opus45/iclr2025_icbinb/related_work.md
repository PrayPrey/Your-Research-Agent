## Related Work

**Related Papers**
1. **Title**: Qualitative Analysis of General Aviation Pilots' ASRS Incident Narratives Using HFACS
   - **Authors**: Vempati, Woods, Solano
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that HFACS successfully classifies ASRS reports, finding that 53% of incidents involved perceptual errors, 32% involved decision errors, and crew resource management was a common contributing factor.
   - **Year**: 2023

2. **Title**: Weak baselines and reporting biases lead to overoptimism in ML
   - **Authors**: McGreivy, Hakim
   - **Summary**: Reveals that 79% of ML papers use weak baselines and identifies systematic publication bias, calling for cultural change in ML research practices.
   - **Year**: 2024

3. **Title**: Process safety incident prediction using near miss data
   - **Authors**: Tiwari, Malluri
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that near-miss reporting enables predictive incident prevention models in process safety contexts.
   - **Year**: 2023

4. **Title**: ICBINB Workshop
   - **Authors**: Not specified

   - **Year**: 2020-present

5. **Title**: Ghost Policies: Understanding Failure in Deep RL
   - **Authors**: Olaz

6. **Title**: Position: Embracing Negative Results in Machine Learning
   - **Authors**: Karl et al.

   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges**
1. **Lack of Systematic Failure Taxonomy**: Existing venues for discussing ML failures, such as the ICBINB Workshop, lack a systematic taxonomy for classifying and categorizing different types of failures.

2. **Missing Infrastructure for Failure Analysis**: While proposals exist for understanding failures in specific domains like deep RL, there is no supporting infrastructure to enable systematic failure analysis across ML research.

3. **Publication Bias Against Negative Results**: Systematic publication bias leads to overoptimism in ML research, with 79% of papers using weak baselines, suppressing valuable negative findings.

4. **Absence of ML Failure Classification Resources**: Knowledge base searches confirm no existing resources for ML failure classification, indicating a significant infrastructure gap in the field.
