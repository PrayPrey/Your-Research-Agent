## Related Work

**Related Papers**
1. **Title**: Adaptive Explainable AI: Personalizing Machine Explanations Based on User Expertise Levels
   - **Authors**: Mohammed, A.A.A.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that adaptive XAI improves comprehension (+27%), trust (+19%), and decision accuracy (+22%) compared to static explanations, providing core theoretical foundation for personalized explanation approaches.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Explainable AI: roles and stakeholders, desirements and challenges
   - **Authors**: Hoffman, R.R., Mueller, S.T., Klein, G., et al.
   - **Summary**: Establishes that different stakeholders require different ways to "look inside" AI systems, validating the necessity for role-specific explanations.
   - **Year**: 2023

3. **Title**: Explainable Artificial Intelligence in education
   - **Authors**: Khosravi, H., Buckingham Shum, S., Chen, G., Conati, C., et al.
   - **Summary**: Proposes the XAI-ED framework with six key aspects for educational AI explainability, providing design principles for educational explanation systems.
   - **Year**: 2022

4. **Title**: Stakeholder-centric explanations for black-box decisions
   - **Authors**: Haas, S., Hegestweiler, K., Rapp, M., Muschalik, M., Hüllermeier, E.
   - **Summary**: Finds that textual explanations based on local feature importance best fit stakeholder needs and that all stakeholder types agree explanations increase trust.
   - **Year**: 2024

5. **Title**: SHAP Library
   - **Authors**: Not specified
   - **Summary**: Provides baseline implementation for feature attribution methods; serves as foundation for narrative generation approaches built on SHAP outputs.
   - **Year**: Not specified

6. **Title**: GPT-4 Prompt-Only Explanation
   - **Authors**: Not specified
   - **Summary**: Serves as baseline for unconstrained LLM explanation generation without ontology constraints.
   - **Year**: Not specified

7. **Title**: IELAT - Interpretable and Ethical Learning Assessment Transformer
   - **Authors**: S. et al.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates G-SHAP integration with educational assessment but lacks stakeholder-specific adaptation capabilities.
   - **Year**: 2025

8. **Title**: TLEF framework: Accuracy, Fairness, and Learner Perception
   - **Authors**: Dai
   - **Summary**: Includes learner perception in framework design but has limited stakeholder diversity in its approach.
   - **Year**: 2025

**Key Challenges**
1. **Lack of Stakeholder-Specific Adaptation**: Existing approaches like IELAT integrate explainability methods with educational assessment but fail to adapt explanations to different stakeholder types and their specific needs.
2. **Limited Stakeholder Diversity**: Current frameworks such as TLEF consider learner perception but do not adequately address the full range of stakeholders involved in educational assessment contexts.
3. **Static vs. Adaptive Explanations**: Traditional XAI methods provide one-size-fits-all explanations rather than personalizing based on user expertise levels, resulting in suboptimal comprehension and trust.
4. **Gap Between Feature Attribution and Narrative Generation**: While tools like SHAP provide feature importance values, there remains a gap in translating these technical outputs into meaningful, stakeholder-appropriate narrative explanations.
