## Related Work

**Related Papers**
1. **Title**: HumanAgencyBench: Scalable Evaluation of Human Agency Support in AI Assistants (Semantic Scholar ID: 60ac6c04b6089cd01b7f47b2240a60b388c54477)
   - **Authors**: Benjamin Sturgeon, Daniel Samuelson, Jacob Haimes, Jacy Reese Anthis
   - **Summary**: Introduces 6 dimensions of agency and an LLM-as-judge methodology for evaluating AI assistants, finding that RLHF does not consistently produce agency support.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Training a Helpful and Harmless Assistant with RLHF (Semantic Scholar ID: 0286b2736a114198b25fb5553c671c33aed5d477)
   - **Authors**: Yuntao Bai et al. (Anthropic)
   - **Summary**: Establishes the RLHF methodology for training AI assistants to be both helpful and harmless, serving as a foundational approach for AI alignment.
   - **Year**: 2022

3. **Title**: Constructing General Human Agency Indicators (GHAIs)
   - **Authors**: Michael Joseph D'Italia, Adam Okulicz-Kozaryn
   - **Summary**: Develops and validates a 9-item scale for measuring human agency with CFA-confirmed factorial structure, providing psychometric foundations for agency measurement.
   - **Year**: 2024

4. **Title**: Beyond Overreliance: The Human-AI-System Concordance (HASC) Matrix (Semantic Scholar ID: 7231259405e7ca378011efb386cd101df69df220)
   - **Authors**: Wonji Doh, Youngnoh Goh, Sang-Hwan Kim
   - **Summary**: Proposes a diagnostic framework for AI-assisted decision-making and introduces Cognitive Forcing Functions as interventions.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: Evaluating Human-AI Collaboration: A Review and Methodological Framework (Semantic Scholar ID: 00779a37dc55a6dc1e3fee00baf65714a80f7a98)
   - **Authors**: George Michael Fragiadakis et al.
   - **Summary**: Provides a comprehensive review and decision tree framework for selecting appropriate metrics in human-AI collaboration evaluation.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: Beyond Preferences in AI Alignment (Semantic Scholar ID: 57f59779375f700d4288ef2397903d488f49b9a7)
   - **Authors**: Tan Zhi-Xuan, Micah Carroll, Matija Franklin, Hal Ashton
   - **Summary**: Critiques the preferentist approach to AI alignment and advocates for role-based standards, providing theoretical justification for including agency alongside preferences.
   - **Year**: 2024

7. **Title**: Safe RLHF: Safe Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (Semantic Scholar ID: 0f7308fbcae43d22813f70c334c2425df0b1cce1)
   - **Authors**: Josef Dai et al.
   - **Summary**: Introduces multi-objective RLHF with Lagrangian constraints to balance helpfulness with safety considerations in alignment training.
   - **Year**: 2023

**Key Challenges**
1. **RLHF Inconsistency in Agency Support**: Current RLHF methods do not consistently produce AI systems that support human agency, indicating a gap between alignment training and agency preservation.
2. **Preferentist Limitations**: Traditional AI alignment approaches focus primarily on preferences, neglecting the broader concept of human agency and role-based standards.
3. **Lack of Unified Metrics**: Existing frameworks address either AI alignment or human-AI collaboration separately, without integrating agency measurement into alignment evaluation.
4. **Multi-Objective Optimization Complexity**: Balancing multiple objectives (helpfulness, harmlessness, agency support) in alignment training requires sophisticated approaches beyond standard single-objective RLHF.
5. **Psychometric Operationalization Gap**: Translating validated psychological constructs of human agency into practical AI evaluation metrics remains an open challenge.
