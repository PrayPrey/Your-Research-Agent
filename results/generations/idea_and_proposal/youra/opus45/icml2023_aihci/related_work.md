## Related Work

**Related Papers**
1. **Title**: Item Response Theory in AI: Analysing Machine Learning Classifiers at the Instance Level (DOI: 10.1016/j.artint.2019.03.004)
   - **Authors**: Martinez-Plumed, Prudêncio, Martínez-Usó, Hernández-Orallo
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates that IRT successfully extracts latent ability and item characteristics from ML classifier evaluations, showing cross-domain transfer feasibility.
   - **Year**: 2019

2. **Title**: Efficiently Measuring the Cognitive Ability of LLMs: An Adaptive Testing Perspective (arXiv)
   - **Authors**: Zhuang et al.
   - **Summary**: Shows that Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT) achieves approximately 60% reduction in evaluation items while maintaining measurement precision for LLM assessment.
   - **Year**: 2023

3. **Title**: Beyond Bias and Discrimination: Redefining AI Ethics of Fairness
   - **Authors**: Giovanola & Tiribelli
   - **Summary**: Argues that fairness requires a socio-relational dimension that extends beyond demographic parity in AI systems.
   - **Year**: 2022

4. **Title**: Measuring and Understanding Trust Calibrations for Automated Systems
   - **Authors**: Wischnewski et al.
   - **Summary**: Establishes that trust calibration requires a 4-dimensional intervention framework for automated systems.
   - **Year**: 2023

5. **Title**: HumanAgencyBench
   - **Authors**: Sturgeon et al.
   - **Summary**: Represents the first attempt at creating an agency support benchmark, using fixed-form evaluation approaches.
   - **Year**: 2025

6. **Title**: ConSiDERS Framework
   - **Authors**: Elangovan et al.
   - **Summary**: Proposes a 6-pillar human-centered AI evaluation framework for comprehensive assessment of AI systems.
   - **Year**: 2024

7. **Title**: Standardizing Fairness-Evaluation Procedures
   - **Authors**: Genovesi et al.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates the need for minimal ethical requirements in AI evaluation procedures.
   - **Year**: 2023

8. **Title**: Applying IRT to Distinguish Human and AI Responses
   - **Authors**: Strugatski & Alexandron
   - **Summary**: Shows that IRT person-fit statistics can effectively detect AI cheating patterns in responses.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges**
1. **Lack of Unified Evaluation Framework**: No unified framework exists across AI-HCI applications for standardizing fairness evaluation procedures and ethical requirements.
2. **Limited Application of IRT to AI-HCI Systems**: While IRT person-fit statistics have been shown to detect AI patterns, this method has not yet been applied to AI-HCI system evaluation.
3. **Fixed-Form vs. Adaptive Efficiency Trade-off**: Existing benchmarks like HumanAgencyBench use fixed-form approaches, lacking the adaptive efficiency that IRT-based methods can provide.
4. **Framework Comprehensiveness vs. Psychometric Rigor**: Current frameworks like ConSiDERS prioritize comprehensiveness but may lack the psychometric rigor needed for precise measurement.
5. **Multi-dimensional Fairness Assessment**: Fairness evaluation requires consideration of socio-relational dimensions beyond simple demographic parity metrics.
6. **Trust Calibration Complexity**: Trust calibration in automated systems requires multi-dimensional intervention frameworks rather than single-metric approaches.
