## 1. Research Questions

### Primary Research Question
How can we develop technical frameworks and evaluation methodologies that capture the bidirectional learning dynamics in human-AI coevolution, moving beyond static performance benchmarks to assess the long-term impacts of continuous human-AI interaction on individual cognition, collective behavior, and societal institutions?

### Detailed Research Questions

1. **Human-AI Interaction and Alignment:** How do human expectations and trust in AI systems evolve over time, and what design principles can ensure AI systems remain aligned with dynamically changing human values while preserving human autonomy?

2. **Algorithmic Adaptation and Robustness:** What technical enhancements to RLHF and related frameworks can improve AI adaptability to shifting human preferences while reducing bias and ensuring fairness across diverse contexts?

3. **Long-Term Societal Impact and Safety:** How should governance frameworks and AI safety paradigms be reimagined to account for dynamic human-AI interactions and their cascading effects on policy and public decision-making?

4. **Bidirectional Learning Beyond Performance Metrics:** What new evaluation metrics and methodologies can assess AI systems through the lens of HAIC, capturing how prolonged interactions shape both human cognition and AI agency?

5. **Shaping Collective Behavior and Learning:** How does AI influence group decision-making, consensus-building, and the formation of implicit biases in collaborative environments like education and policy-making?

**Finding 2: Current RLHF is Fundamentally One-Way**
Foundational RLHF work (Bai et al. 2022 - 3559 citations) focuses on AI adaptation to human feedback but does not address how AI changes human cognition, preferences, or behavior. The "bidirectional" aspect of HAIC requires new measurement paradigms not present in current alignment literature.

**Finding 3: Longitudinal Measurement is Critically Underexplored**
While Fer et al. (2025) propose predictive models for trust evolution, no comprehensive longitudinal HAIC measurement framework exists. Current evaluation (HumanEval, MMLU, etc.) captures static snapshots, not coevolution dynamics over time.

**Finding 4: Multi-Level Analysis Gap Exists**
Research typically addresses either individual-level interaction or macro-level societal concerns. The ICLR 2025 HAIC Workshop explicitly calls for bridging these levels, but few papers provide frameworks connecting individual coevolution to collective/institutional impacts.
