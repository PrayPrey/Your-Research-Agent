## Related Work

**Related Papers**

1. **Title**: Position Paper: Integrating Explainability and Uncertainty Quantification in Medical AI
   - **Authors**: Fan
   - **Summary**: Proposes "Explainable Uncertainty Estimation" (XUE) as needed integration of UQ+XAI but provides no implementation. Identifies separation of UQ and XAI as Gap 2 in medical AI. Defines XUE as requiring component attribution (epistemic vs. aleatoric).
   - **Year**: 2025 (arXiv 2509.18132v1)

2. **Title**: The challenge of uncertainty quantification of large language models in medicine
   - **Authors**: Atf et al.
   - **Summary**: Comprehensive UQ framework for medical LLMs identifying epistemic (Bayesian inference, ensembles, MC dropout), aleatoric (linguistic entropy), and surrogate modeling for proprietary APIs. Epistemic/aleatoric components validated as core components accounting for >90% in structured tasks.
   - **Year**: 2025 (Semantic Scholar: c775b5b8504da929766ef021b7c1b291bdce945c, 21 citations)

3. **Title**: A Universal Formulation of Uncertainty Relation for Error-Disturbance and Local Representability of Quantum Observables
   - **Authors**: Lee
   - **Summary**: Universal uncertainty relation with operational tangibility; reformulates Heisenberg uncertainty principle for measurement theory. Provides field representation framework and observer effect concepts.
   - **Year**: 2022 (Semantic Scholar: c31500a906780ecaf08d6db30f933d2346f06931, 5 citations)

4. **Title**: A Universal Formulation of Uncertainty Relation for Error and Disturbance
   - **Authors**: Lee & Tsutsui
   - **Summary**: Theoretical foundation for quantum uncertainty formalism with measurement theory applications. Establishes mathematical framework for uncertainty operators acting on state space.
   - **Year**: 2020 (Semantic Scholar: 931b6a4c6152a6f1d4548daa5e9b9d597db19590, 2 citations)

5. **Title**: Examining Effort in 1D Uncertainty Communication Using Individual Differences in Working Memory and NASA-TLX
   - **Authors**: Castro et al.
   - **Summary**: Empirically demonstrated OSPAN (working memory capacity) predicts effectiveness of uncertainty visualization. High-WM individuals (OSPAN >75th percentile) benefit from quantile dotplots (distributional visualization) with lower NASA-TLX workload. Establishes cognitive foundation for adaptive complexity visualization.
   - **Year**: 2021 (Semantic Scholar: e2f1162fa1a57e34d05e68bdde8183fb20d6c807, 35 citations)

6. **Title**: Safeguarding AI in Medical Imaging: Post-Hoc OOD Detection with Normalizing Flows
   - **Authors**: Lotfi et al.
   - **Summary**: Likelihood-based OOD detection using normalizing flows for medical imaging. Achieves AUROC 0.846 (95% CI: 0.81-0.88) on MedOOD benchmark across 3 datasets and 14 tasks. Provides binary OOD detection but lacks spatial localization and explainability.
   - **Year**: 2025 (arXiv 2502.11638)

7. **Title**: Uncertainty Quantification and Quality Control for Heatmap-Based Landmark Detection Models
   - **Authors**: Feng et al.
   - **Summary**: Evidence theory + subjective logic for single-pass uncertainty quantification. Generates spatial uncertainty maps with cross-attention between evidence and uncertainty. Designed for automated quality control in anatomical landmark detection (cephalometric analysis).
   - **Year**: 2025 (Semantic Scholar: 00e8d16bee1cbd7f91eb5bb02ada7016306694be, 3 citations)

8. **Title**: Explainable AI in medical imaging: an interpretable and collaborative federated learning model for brain tumor classification
   - **Authors**: Mastoi et al.
   - **Summary**: Integrates explainability (Grad-CAM, saliency maps) with federated learning for privacy-preserving brain tumor classification. Achieves 94% accuracy but provides explainability without uncertainty quantification.
   - **Year**: 2025 (Semantic Scholar: d923fadd2cd164bfaa320f1bb1f84b7e740de8a6, 30 citations)

9. **Title**: Improving Trustworthiness of AI Disease Severity Rating with Conformal Prediction Sets
   - **Authors**: Lu et al.
   - **Summary**: Distribution-free ordinal prediction sets with coverage guarantee ≥0.90 for disease severity rating. Provides calibrated intervals but lacks spatial visualization and component attribution. Suitable for ordinal regression tasks.
   - **Year**: 2022 (Semantic Scholar: a651d971206e7b85b46d066e80a9e8ffb1548f09, 43 citations)

10. **Title**: XIMED: A Dual-Loop Evaluation Framework Integrating Predictive Model and Human-Centered Approaches for Explainable AI in Medical Imaging
    - **Authors**: Karagoz et al.
    - **Summary**: Dual-loop framework for XAI evaluation with 97 medical experts. SHAP significantly impacts diagnosis changes. Measures trust, confidence, agreement through Likert scales. Demonstrates feasibility of large-scale clinician studies.
    - **Year**: 2025 (Semantic Scholar: d3dcfbd85230a9c794dcf1c0b65ccd87ef07a32d, 0 citations)

11. **Title**: Explainable AI in medical imaging: An overview for clinical practitioners - Beyond saliency-based XAI approaches
    - **Authors**: Borys et al.
    - **Summary**: Reviews XAI methods beyond saliency-based approaches emphasizing clinical practitioner perspective. Identifies need for clinician-centered XAI design and training tools. Notes requirement for "beyond saliency" explanations for trust.
    - **Year**: 2023 (Semantic Scholar: b08ce42005c672f681c6ab4cff96f830ce0bf9fc, 163 citations)

12. **Title**: Evaluating the o1 reasoning large language model for cognitive bias
    - **Authors**: Degany et al.
    - **Summary**: Reasoning models reduce cognitive bias but "gap-closing cues" increase bias by artificially reducing perceived uncertainty. Demonstrates that hiding uncertainty information undermines decision quality.
    - **Year**: 2025 (Semantic Scholar: dfbb9645b5580d4b08e4a1d5e3a21b2998c08531, 3 citations)

13. **Title**: Cognitive Decision Routing in Large Language Models: When to Think Fast, When to Think Slow
    - **Authors**: Du et al.
    - **Summary**: Meta-cognitive layer analyzes query complexity (correlation strength, uncertainty levels) to route between fast (intuitive) vs. slow (deliberative) reasoning. Provides framework for uncertainty-based decision routing.
    - **Year**: 2025 (Semantic Scholar: b3d06e5e5e30bc594c14349b8d47af9c590d3da3)

14. **Title**: Further N-Frame networking dynamics of conscious observer-self agents
    - **Authors**: Edwards
    - **Summary**: Applies quantum Bayesian (QBism) formalism to conscious decision-making. Models consciousness as active participant in wavefunction collapse via internal observer states. Validates quantum-inspired approaches for cognitive modeling.
    - **Year**: 2025 (Semantic Scholar: 871504f3bf8c3752dbeb11dd5df7508d8e50317d, 1 citation)

15. **Title**: NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX)
    - **Authors**: Hart & Staveland
    - **Summary**: Original validation of NASA-TLX retrospective workload assessment tool. Six-dimensional scale (mental demand, physical demand, temporal demand, performance, effort, frustration) for measuring cognitive load.
    - **Year**: 1988

16. **Title**: Operation Span Task (OSPAN) validation
    - **Authors**: Conway et al.
    - **Summary**: Original OSPAN validation showing correlation across diverse tasks including spatial reasoning, reading comprehension, SAT scores. Establishes OSPAN as domain-general measure of working memory capacity.
    - **Year**: 2005

**Key Challenges**

1. **UQ-XAI Separation Gap**: Uncertainty quantification and explainability exist as separate research streams in medical AI. Clinicians must mentally integrate confidence scores (scalar, no spatial info) with saliency maps (spatial, no uncertainty info), creating cognitive load and error-prone dual-task processing.

2. **Lack of Spatial Component Attribution**: Existing uncertainty methods provide total uncertainty per pixel but do not decompose into interpretable sources (epistemic vs. aleatoric vs. distributional). Clinicians see "high uncertainty" but not why (model disagreement? data noise? OOD?).

3. **No Cognitive Adaptation in Medical AI**: While Castro et al. (2021) identified working memory capacity as critical factor for uncertainty visualization effectiveness, no medical AI systems personalize visualization complexity to individual cognitive capacity.

4. **OOD Detection Without Explainability**: State-of-the-art OOD detection methods (normalizing flows, Mahalanobis distance) provide binary OOD scores but lack spatial localization ("which anatomical region is OOD?") and explainability ("why is this OOD?").

5. **Absence of Integrated Decision Support**: Medical AI systems provide separate information streams (predictions, confidence scores, saliency maps) but lack unified frameworks with actionable routing signals for clinical decisions (trust AI vs. escalate to specialist).

6. **Technical Metrics Without Human Evaluation**: UQ methods report technical metrics (AUROC, ECE, calibration) but rarely measure clinician comprehension, decision accuracy, or workflow integration in human-in-the-loop studies.

7. **No XUE Implementation**: Despite Fan (2025) proposing Explainable Uncertainty Estimation as a needed conceptual framework, no prior work has implemented an XUE system with spatial visualization, component attribution, and human evaluation protocol.

8. **Gap-Closing Bias Risk**: Systems that artificially reduce perceived uncertainty through "gap-closing cues" (Degany et al. 2025) increase cognitive bias and undermine decision quality, yet most medical AI interfaces hide or minimize uncertainty information.
