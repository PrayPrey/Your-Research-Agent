# Research Idea: Adaptive Closed-Loop Protein Design via Thompson Sampling and Parameter-Efficient Model Retraining

## Title
Adaptive Closed-Loop Protein Design via Thompson Sampling and Parameter-Efficient Model Retraining

## Motivation
Current biomolecular design suffers from a critical disconnect: generative ML models (ProteinMPNN, RFdiffusion) remain static after pre-training, failing to learn from expensive experimental feedback. Existing approaches like MAGECS demonstrate closed-loop experimental integration but don't update their generative models, while standard Bayesian optimization treats deep generative priors as black boxes. This creates inefficiency—requiring 200-300 wet-lab experiments to reach optimal designs at $250-1000 per assay. Bridging computational and experimental perspectives requires models that continuously adapt from small experimental batches while preserving chemical validity, a challenge unaddressed by current methods.

## Main Idea
We hypothesize that integrating **discounted Thompson Sampling** for exploration-exploitation in generative latent spaces with **parameter-efficient fine-tuning** (LoRA + Bayesian regularization) achieves 30-50% improvement in sample efficiency over static models. The causal mechanism operates through five linked components: (1) Thompson Sampling provides principled probabilistic exploration by maintaining posteriors over latent regions, (2) LoRA enables continuous model adaptation from 10-50 experimental samples without catastrophic forgetting via 99% parameter reduction, (3) Bayesian regularization preserves pre-trained chemical priors through KL-divergence penalties, (4) discount factors (γ=0.9-0.99) adapt to non-stationary latent spaces as models retrain, and (5) multi-fidelity optimization (AlphaFold + wet-lab) maximizes information gain per cost. We validate through two phases: in-silico benchmarking on FLIP protein tasks (10 runs × 5 tasks) and wet-lab validation on enzyme/antibody design (200 assays per task). Expected impact: reduce experimental budgets by 40-60% while discovering high-performers missed by static approaches, with open-source implementation enabling broad adoption across protein engineering applications.
