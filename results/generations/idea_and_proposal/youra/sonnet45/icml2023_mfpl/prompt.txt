# Nash-Fair Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning: Integrating Stakeholder Preferences with Demographic Fairness

## Motivation
Current preference-based learning systems face a critical trilemma: they either maximize stakeholder utilities without fairness guarantees, enforce demographic fairness through post-hoc corrections that violate theoretical optimality, or rely on hand-crafted reward functions prone to misspecification. This creates regulatory compliance risks (EU AI Act) and ethical concerns in high-stakes domains like healthcare and lending. Existing approaches fail to simultaneously satisfy axiomatic stakeholder fairness, statistical demographic parity, and multi-objective preference aggregation.

## Main Idea
We propose Nash-Fair MORL, a three-stage pipeline that uniquely integrates game-theoretic and algorithmic fairness. **Stage 1** derives Pareto-optimal policies from pairwise preference data using multi-objective reward learning, eliminating reward engineering bias. **Stage 2** filters policies by demographic parity constraints (disparity <5%). **Stage 3** selects the final policy via Nash Bargaining solution, maximizing the product of stakeholder utilities while satisfying all four Nash axioms (Pareto efficiency, symmetry, independence, scale invariance). The core mechanism leverages convex hull approximation to preserve theoretical guarantees under discrete constraints. We predict ≥1.5× improvement in stakeholder utility product versus single-objective baselines while maintaining regulatory-compliant fairness. Validation across reinforcement learning, LLM alignment, and recommender systems will test whether sequential integration can resolve the fairness-utility-preference trilemma without sacrificing task performance.

## 3. Causal Mechanism (3-Stage Pipeline)

**Stage 1: Preference → Pareto Frontier**
1. Collect preference pairs D = {(τ₁, τ₂, y)}
2. Train multi-objective reward model r_θ(s,a) = [r₁,...,r_K] via Bradley-Terry
3. Derive Pareto frontier P via scalarized RL with varied weights
   - **Evidence:** Mu et al. (2025) theoretical proof + empirical validation

**Stage 2: Pareto Frontier → Fairness-Compliant Subset**
1. Evaluate demographic parity ΔDP(π) for each π ∈ P
2. Filter: P_fair = {π : ΔDP(π) < ε}
3. Relaxation fallback: if |P_fair| < 3, increase ε by 10% incrementally
   - **Evidence:** Yang et al. (2023) 31% fairness improvement in clinical ML

**Stage 3: Fairness Subset → Nash-Fair Policy**
1. Learn stakeholder utilities {U_k(π)} from stratified preference data
2. Compute convex hull H(P_fair) of utility vectors
3. Solve Nash Bargaining: u* = argmax ∏ₖ(u_k - d_k) on H(P_fair)
4. Select nearest policy: π* = argmin ||u(π) - u*||₂
   - **Evidence:** Nash (1950) axiomatic foundation, Skorin-Kapov (2025) ethical decision-making application

**Key Tension:**
Balancing (1) Axiomatic stakeholder fairness, (2) Demographic group fairness, (3) Preference fidelity - traditionally incompatible, our sequential integration satisfies all three.
