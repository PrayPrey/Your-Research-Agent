# Title
Reflexive Bidirectional Alignment: Preserving Human Agency in AI Systems via Cybernetic Feedback Control

# Motivation
Current AI alignment approaches risk depleting human agency through learned helplessness and deskilling. While bidirectional alignment frameworks propose mutual human-AI adaptation, they lack concrete mechanisms to actively preserve user autonomy during training. This creates a critical gap: AI systems optimized for preference alignment may inadvertently reduce users' decision-making capacity and self-determination over sustained interactions.

# Main Idea
We propose Reflexive Bidirectional Alignment (RBA), which treats human agency as a homeostatic control variable maintained through cybernetic feedback loops during RLHF training. The system continuously monitors composite agency scores (aggregating decision autonomy, self-initiation rates, preference overrides, and task delegation patterns) every K adaptation steps, comparing against user-specific baselines. When agency deviation exceeds 5%, Pareto optimization dynamically adjusts the dual-objective reward function R_total = R_alignment + λ_agency × R_agency_preservation, balancing alignment quality with agency preservation. Testing on conversational AI with n≥25 users, we predict maintaining agency within 5% of baseline while achieving 88-90% preference match accuracy (matching standard RLHF). This approach extends existing learnable protocols (BiCA) by adding explicit reflexive control, validated through behavioral proxy correlation (r≥0.8) with psychometric assessments, with ≤15% computational overhead for production deployment.

### 1.3 Causal Mechanism

**5-Step Causal Chain (Cybernetic Feedback Control Loop):**

**Step 1: Reflexive Feedback Activation → Continuous Agency Measurement**

When reflexive feedback loops are enabled, the system measures composite agency score A = Σᵢ wᵢ * aᵢ at every K adaptation steps during RLHF training. Behavioral proxies (decision autonomy frequency, self-initiation rate, preference overrides, task delegation) are logged in real-time with O(1) per-interaction cost.

*Mechanism*: Continuous monitoring enables deviation detection, analogous to thermostats sampling temperature every T seconds to maintain homeostatic setpoint.

*Falsification point*: If behavioral proxy logging fails or correlation to true agency drops below r=0.6, proxy optimization decouples from actual agency preservation (Goodhart's Law).

**Step 2: Agency Measurement → Deviation Detection from Baseline A₀**

Each measured agency score A_current is compared to established baseline A₀ (from pre-alignment interactions). Tracking error E_agency = |A_current - A₀| quantifies agency deviation as percentage.

*Mechanism*: Error signal generation from control theory; deviation ≥5% triggers corrective feedback to prevent agency depletion.

*Falsification point*: If baseline A₀ is unreliable (<50 interactions) or non-stationary (user preferences change rapidly), deviation measurements become noisy and control becomes unstable.

**Step 3: Deviation Detection → Dynamic λ_agency Adjustment via Pareto Optimization**

Detected agency deviation drives dynamic adjustment of λ_agency weight using Pareto optimization (NSGA-II). The optimizer seeks Pareto-optimal solutions on the frontier of (R_alignment, R_agency_preservation), adjusting λ every M epochs based on convergence progress.

*Mechanism*: Inspired by RiC (Yang 2024) multi-objective alignment achieving Pareto-optimal solutions with dynamic preference adjustment. λ_agency increases when E_agency > threshold, decreases when alignment quality drops.

*Falsification point*: If Pareto optimization fails to converge (conflicting objectives have no non-dominated solutions), dual objectives remain in fundamental tension and no viable λ_agency exists.

**Step 4: Dynamic λ_agency → Dual-Objective RLHF Optimization**

The adjusted λ_agency modulates the dual-objective reward function R_total = R_alignment + λ_agency * R_agency_preservation. PPO gradient updates optimize this combined objective, balancing alignment quality improvement with agency preservation.

*Mechanism*: Multi-objective RL formulation validated by BiCA (learnable protocols with KL-budget constraints for controlled co-evolution) and RiC (10% GPU hours vs standard multi-objective RL baseline).

*Falsification point*: If fundamental goal conflict exists (e.g., safety requires restricting user agency for harmful requests), no trade-off weight achieves both objectives simultaneously.

**Step 5: Dual-Objective RLHF → Agency Preservation + Alignment Quality**

The optimized policy achieves two outcomes: (1) composite agency score A maintained within 5% of baseline A₀ over sustained interactions, and (2) alignment quality matching standard RLHF at 90% preference match accuracy (±2%).

*Mechanism*: Controlled co-adaptation analogous to BiCA's 85.5% success vs 70.3% baseline with 230% better mutual adaptation, enabled by continuous reflexive correction preventing drift.

*Falsification point*: If computational overhead exceeds 30% or training time becomes prohibitive (>2x baseline), practical deployment infeasibility overrides theoretical validity.

### 1.4 Key Assumptions

**Assumption 1: Behavioral Proxy Validity**

Behavioral proxies (decision autonomy frequency, interaction self-initiation rate, preference override rate, task delegation patterns) collectively approximate true human agency with r≥0.8 correlation to ground-truth psychometric assessment.

*Evidence*: FAI Benchmark (Hilliard 2025, SS: 502f37ca5f3790639660f41d87add3e89573f828, 2 cit) proposes 7-dimensional flourishing assessment including autonomy and decision-making as measurable constructs. Agency foundations framework (Mitelut 2023) formalizes agency-preserving interactions with behavioral indicators.

*Consequence if violated*: If proxy-to-truth correlation r < 0.6, optimizing behavioral proxies does not preserve actual subjective agency experience (Goodhart's Law). System appears to preserve "agency metrics" while users experience helplessness, invalidating the entire approach.

**Assumption 2: Cybernetic Transfer to Non-Linear RL**

Feedback control principles from homeostatic systems (linear or near-linear dynamics) transfer to non-linear RL optimization with bounded rewards.

*Evidence*: Adaptive control theory (Åström & Wittenmark) validates model reference adaptive control (MRAC) for non-linear systems with Lyapunov stability proofs. Prior RL+control applications demonstrate successful transfer for exploration-exploitation trade-offs and safe RL with control barriers.

*Consequence if violated*: If RL optimization dynamics are too chaotic or non-stationary for feedback control, reflexive loops may cause oscillation (tuning instability) or fail to converge, making the system impractical despite theoretical soundness.

**Assumption 3: Dimensional Aggregation Preserves Information**

The 7 FAI dimensions (autonomy, decision-making, competence, relatedness, vitality, self-actualization, flourishing) can be meaningfully aggregated via weighted sum A = Σᵢ wᵢ * aᵢ without critical information loss.

*Evidence*: Composite scoring is standard practice in psychology (e.g., well-being indices, personality assessments). Learned weights wᵢ via supervised alignment to ground-truth assessment can capture relative dimension importance empirically.

*Consequence if violated*: If weighted sum destroys critical agency structure (e.g., high autonomy + low competence ≠ low autonomy + high competence, even if sums equal), single composite score A fails to capture agency state, and control mechanism optimizes wrong target.

**Assumption 4: User Acceptance of Alignment-Agency Trade-off**

Users accept <5% reduction in alignment quality (e.g., 88% vs 90% preference match) in exchange for agency preservation.

*Evidence*: Requires user study validation. Analogous to privacy-utility trade-offs where users accept reduced personalization for privacy gains. Situated RLHF (Arzberger 2024, SS: 08628008504b19f811fd6498b2f6fa6c4703b29c, 15 cit) demonstrates context-aware value alignment suggesting users value agency-preserving interactions.

*Consequence if violated*: If users demand >95% alignment quality regardless of agency cost, Pareto-optimal frontier may lie outside user acceptance region, making the approach theoretically valid but practically undeployable.

**Assumption 5: Bounded Rewards Enable Stability**

RLHF rewards are bounded (standard assumption), enabling Lyapunov stability analysis for dual-objective optimization convergence.

*Evidence*: Standard RLHF formulation uses normalized reward models with bounded output ranges. Reward clipping is common practice (Anthropic Constitutional AI, Reward Model Safety).

*Consequence if violated*: If reward model outputs are unbounded or exhibit pathological behavior (reward hacking, mode collapse), stability guarantees break down and system may diverge during training.

### 1.5 Scope & Boundaries

**Applies to:**

- **Conversational AI systems** with repeated multi-turn user interactions (chatbots, virtual assistants, tutoring systems)
- **Personalized recommendation systems** where user agency in choice selection is critical (content recommendations, decision support tools)
- **Educational AI tutors** that must balance guidance with learner autonomy to prevent learned helplessness
- **Healthcare decision support systems** where patient agency and autonomy are ethically mandated
- Any RLHF-based alignment deployment requiring sustained user engagement (≥50 interactions) and where user agency preservation is a key objective

**Does NOT apply to:**

- **One-shot interactions** where no baseline agency profile A₀ can be established (insufficient data <50 interactions)
- **Non-interactive AI systems** where there is no user agency to preserve (batch prediction, autonomous agents without human in loop)
- **Safety-critical domains where agency reduction is necessary** (e.g., preventing users from eliciting harmful content, blocking dangerous requests). In these cases, safety constraints override agency preservation, requiring separate constraint handling.
- **Domains with extremely low latency requirements** (<10ms inference) where computational overhead (+10-15%) is prohibitive

**Known limitations:**

1. **Cold-start problem**: Requires ≥50 user interactions to establish reliable baseline agency profile A₀. During cold-start, system uses population-average agency profile which may not match individual user.

2. **Behavioral proxy validity risk**: If behavioral proxies fail to capture subjective agency experience (Goodhart's Law), optimizing metrics does not preserve actual agency. Validation study with ground-truth psychometric assessment (100 users) is critical.

3. **Pareto trade-off ceiling**: Perfect alignment (100%) + perfect agency preservation (0% deviation) may be impossible if fundamental goal conflicts exist (e.g., safety vs. autonomy). System achieves Pareto-optimal solutions, not perfect outcomes.

4. **Computational overhead**: +10-15% training time may be prohibitive for resource-constrained deployments or extremely large-scale systems (>100B parameters).

5. **Model uncertainty**: Agency dynamics are unknown (no validated plant model), so robust control (σ-modification) mitigates but does not eliminate suboptimal control performance.

6. **Non-stationary users**: If user preferences and behavior patterns change rapidly over time, baseline A₀ becomes stale and deviation measurements lose validity.
