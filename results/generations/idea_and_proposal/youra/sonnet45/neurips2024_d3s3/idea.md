# Physics-Guided Active Learning for Sample-Efficient Simulation-Based Inference

## Motivation
Bayesian parameter inference in expensive PDE-governed simulators (CFD, plasma physics, structural mechanics) requires 10,000+ simulations, taking weeks to months. This prohibits uncertainty quantification in critical applications like fusion reactor calibration and aerodynamic design. Existing approaches either ignore physics constraints (wasting samples on infeasible parameters) or use passive sampling (requiring many simulations). We address this bottleneck by synergistically combining physics-informed priors with active learning.

## Main Idea
We hypothesize that integrating PDE-based physics constraints with information-theoretic active acquisition reduces simulation budgets by **10-100×** while maintaining posterior accuracy within 5% of baselines. The causal mechanism operates through two synergistic pathways: (1) physics-informed priors (PDE residual penalties) reduce the search space by 50-90%, and (2) expected information gain (EIG) acquisition selects maximally informative samples. We test this via factorial experiments across three simulators (Navier-Stokes, linear elasticity, MHD) at dimensions d∈{5,10,20}, comparing four conditions: baseline, physics-only, active-only, and combined. Success requires ≥10× reduction in ≥2/3 benchmarks with demonstrated synergy beyond additive effects. This enables previously intractable applications: compressing 70-day fusion calibrations to 3.5 days, enabling real-time structural health monitoring, and monthly climate model recalibration.
