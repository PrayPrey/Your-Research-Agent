# Title
Continuous Latent Distributional Compression: Eliminating Quantization for Stable Neural Image Compression

# Motivation
Current neural image compression methods universally rely on quantizing latent codes for entropy coding, introducing two critical problems: (1) gradient discontinuities requiring biased straight-through estimators that destabilize training, and (2) training-inference mismatch from switching between soft and hard quantization modes. These issues limit optimization quality and model performance. Inspired by continuous-variable quantum communication principles, we challenge the assumption that compression requires discrete symbols, proposing a quantization-free approach that stores continuous distribution parameters instead.

# Main Idea
We hypothesize that neural encoders outputting continuous Gaussian parameters (μ, σ) stored as 16-bit floats—rather than quantized latent codes—will achieve superior training stability (≥30% gradient variance reduction) and eliminate training-inference mismatch (≥50% gap reduction) while maintaining practical compression ratios (within 2× of state-of-the-art). The causal mechanism operates through three pathways: continuous gradients eliminate quantization discontinuities, enabling smoother optimization; identical train-inference operations (sampling from N(μ,σ²)) remove mode-switching artifacts; and improved gradient flow enables lower latent dimensionality, offsetting parameter storage overhead. We will validate this through controlled experiments on ImageNet/CLIC benchmarks comparing against CompressAI quantization baselines, measuring gradient variance, PSNR gaps between training/inference modes, and bits-per-pixel at matched quality levels. Success demonstrates compression achieves information constraint through learned probability distributions rather than discrete coding.
