# Hierarchical Compositional Attention for Mathematical Reasoning Generalization

## Motivation
Current large language models struggle with compositional generalization in mathematical reasoning—they fail when problems require combining familiar concepts in novel ways or reasoning chains longer than those seen during training. This limits their reliability in education, formal verification, and scientific applications. While recent work explores latent reasoning and scaling, a fundamental gap remains: models lack explicit architectural mechanisms to learn reusable primitives and compose them systematically, as humans do through hierarchical cognitive processes.

## Main Idea
We propose a three-level hierarchical attention architecture that explicitly models mathematical reasoning composition: (L1) primitive operations (arithmetic, algebra, logic), (L2) composition operators (sequence, branching, iteration) via differentiable Gumbel-softmax routing, and (L3) abstract transferable patterns. Dynamic skill routing selects expert combinations based on compositional structure. The causal mechanism: hierarchical decomposition enables learning reusable primitives that compose into novel reasoning chains, reducing sample complexity from O(k^d) to O(k+d). We predict >60% accuracy on 5-7 step problems when trained only on 2-3 steps (vs. <40% baseline), and >70% on novel concept combinations (vs. <50%). Falsification: if 2× scaled flat-attention baselines match performance, explicit compositional structure provides no benefit. This bridges cognitive hierarchy theory with transformer architectures, offering interpretable, sample-efficient mathematical reasoning.
