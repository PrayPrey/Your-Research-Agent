# Research Idea: Hierarchical Domain Bridging for LLM-Based Outdoor Embodied Agents

## Title
Hierarchical Domain Bridging for Weather-Robust LLM-Based Outdoor Navigation: Meta-Learning Perception with Zero-Shot Spatial Reasoning

## Motivation
Current LLM-based embodied agents excel indoors but struggle in outdoor urban environments due to weather/lighting variations and large sim-to-real gaps (≥25pp performance drops). Existing approaches either require expensive end-to-end fine-tuning or achieve only ~50-60% success rates. This research addresses the critical gap of enabling sample-efficient, weather-robust outdoor navigation by recognizing that perception and reasoning require fundamentally different adaptation strategies—perception needs domain adaptation while reasoning can leverage zero-shot prompting.

## Main Idea
We propose hierarchical domain bridging that separates perception adaptation from reasoning transfer. The core innovation combines: (1) **meta-learned perception** trained across 5-10 simulated weather conditions using MAML with controlled deployment gaps, enabling fast adaptation to real-world conditions with only 10-50 labeled samples per weather type; (2) **spatial prompting** for LLMs using reference objects and keyframes, achieving zero-shot reasoning transfer without fine-tuning; (3) **GAN-based progressive domain bridging** with 3-5 intermediate style-transfer steps to systematically reduce the sim-to-real gap. 

The causal mechanism flows through five links: meta-training diversity forces condition-invariant feature learning → few-shot real-world adaptation (≥70% perception accuracy) → reliable semantic understanding → zero-shot LLM spatial reasoning (≥90% of simulation performance) → hierarchical integration achieving ≥70% navigation success with ≤10pp reality gap. We validate against CityEQA's 60.7% simulation baseline through real-world deployment across multiple weather conditions, providing the first standardized outdoor sim-to-real benchmark for LLM agents while reducing data requirements 5-10× compared to standard fine-tuning.

**Key Tension:**

**Tension: Simulation Diversity vs. Sim-to-Real Gap**

The hypothesis assumes meta-training across 5-10 simulated weather conditions creates condition-invariant perception features that transfer to real-world conditions. However, simulated weather may not capture all real-world distribution shifts (e.g., atmospheric scattering, sensor noise, dynamic occlusions from moving objects).

**Trade-off:**
- Increasing simulated diversity (10+ conditions) may improve robustness BUT risks overfitting to simulation artifacts if conditions are not physically realistic
- Using fewer conditions (3-5) reduces training complexity BUT may leave perception brittle to unseen real-world variations

**Resolution Strategy:**
- Use GAN-based intermediate domains to progressively bridge simulation→real gap, not rely solely on meta-training diversity
- Validate with ablation: Compare performance across 3, 5, 7, 10 meta-training conditions to find optimal diversity level
- Incorporate real-world weak supervision (10-50 samples) to fine-tune on actual distribution

- Measurement: Standard mIoU metric averaged across conditions

### 1.8 Statistical Verification Design

**Study Design:** Mixed-Design Experiment with Ablation Studies

**Sample Size Calculation:**

**Perception Accuracy (P1):**
- Effect size: 15pp (Cohen's d ≈ 1.2 assuming σ=12.5pp)
- Power: 0.80, α=0.05
- Required images per condition: n ≈ 12 per condition (80 images total for 5 conditions, 2 models)
- **Planned:** 100 images per condition (500 total) for robust estimation

**Navigation Success Rate (P5):**
- Baseline success: p₀=0.50, Target: p₁=0.70
- Proportion test: n ≈ 45 tasks per group (α=0.05, β=0.20)
- **Planned:** 100 real-world tasks (20 per weather condition) for within-condition analysis

**Statistical Tests:**

**Primary Analysis (P1 - Perception):**
- Test: Paired t-test (meta-trained vs. baseline on same real-world test set)
- Hypothesis: H₁: μ_meta - μ_baseline ≥ 15pp, α=0.05 (one-tailed)
- Correction: Bonferroni for multiple conditions (5 conditions → α'=0.01)

**Secondary Analysis (P2 - Spatial Reasoning):**
- Test: Proportion test (prompted vs. non-prompted LLM accuracy)
- Hypothesis: H₁: p_prompted ≥ 0.90 × p_sim, p_baseline ≤ 0.75 × p_sim, α=0.05
- Sample: 200 spatial queries (100 per condition)

**Ablation Studies:**

**Ablation 1: Meta-Training Diversity**
- Conditions: 3, 5, 7, 10 weather conditions
- Measure: Real-world perception mIoU and navigation success rate
- Purpose: Identify optimal meta-training diversity (test key tension from 1.3)

**Ablation 2: Intermediate Domain Count**
- Conditions: 0 (no GAN), 1, 3, 5 progressive GAN steps
- Measure: Sample efficiency (samples needed to reach 90% target performance)
- Purpose: Validate GAN intermediate domain contribution

**Ablation 3: Spatial Prompting Strategy**
- Conditions: (A) No prompting, (B) Reference objects (SpatialPrompt), (C) Keyframes (SpatialPrompting), (D) Both
- Measure: LLM spatial reasoning accuracy on real-world queries
- Purpose: Identify best prompting strategy for embodied context

**Ablation 4: Multi-Teacher Count**
- Conditions: 1 (single-teacher), 3, 5 specialist teachers
- Measure: Perception mIoU on rare conditions (heavy fog, night rain)
- Purpose: Validate multi-teacher benefit vs. training cost

**Control Variables:**
- LLM model: Fix to Gemini 1.5 Pro (all experiments use same model for consistency)
- Test environment: Same real-world 2km² area for all trials
- Task difficulty: Stratified sampling from CityEQA task distribution
- Evaluation order: Randomize task sequence to avoid learning effects

**Confound Mitigation:**
- Weather variability: Collect data across all target conditions (wait for natural weather variation or use multiple cities)
- Labeling bias: Use 3 independent annotators for semantic segmentation labels, resolve disagreements via majority vote
- LLM prompt sensitivity: Test 3 prompt variations per strategy, report average and std dev
- Simulator fidelity: Validate MetaUrban weather realism by comparing synthetic→real images with human perception study (MTurk: "Is this real or simulated?")

**Reproducibility:**
- Random seeds: Fix seeds for meta-training (seed=42), report results across 3 seeds
- Hyperparameters: Document all hyperparameters in appendix (meta-learning rate, GAN architecture, etc.)
- Code release: Publish code + checkpoints on GitHub (MIT license)
- Data release: Release real-world test set (500 images + 100 tasks) for benchmark

## 2. Contribution Summary

**Theoretical Contributions:**

**T1. Hierarchical Domain Adaptation Framework for LLM-Based Embodied Agents**

Formalize the principle that **perception requires domain adaptation (meta-learning) while reasoning requires only prompting (zero-shot)** for outdoor embodied LLM agents. This challenges the assumption that all components of LLM-based systems need fine-tuning for domain transfer, showing that appropriate abstraction (hierarchical separation) enables selective adaptation.

**Why novel:** Prior work assumes end-to-end fine-tuning or no adaptation (zero-shot everything). We formalize when each strategy is appropriate: perception (low-level, domain-sensitive) vs. reasoning (high-level, domain-invariant via prompting).

**T2. Quantification of Outdoor-Specific Reality Gap Components**

Decompose the sim-to-real reality gap for outdoor environments into measurable components: weather-induced (X%), lighting-induced (Y%), dynamic element-induced (Z%). Extends existing reality gap theory (indoor: texture/layout) to outdoor-specific factors.

**Why novel:** No prior work systematically quantifies outdoor reality gap components for embodied agents. Enables researchers to target specific gap sources.

**T3. Meta-Learning Extension for Multi-Modal LLM Agents**

Extend Controlled Deployment Gaps (CoDeGa) meta-learning framework to multi-modal LLM agents where perception module is meta-learned but language module is frozen (zero-shot). Provides theoretical analysis of when hierarchical meta-learning outperforms end-to-end approaches.

**Why novel:** CoDeGa (18c) is for single-modality (manipulation). We extend to vision-language hierarchical agents with asymmetric adaptation (perception: meta-learned, language: prompted).

**Methodological Contributions:**

**M1. Multi-Teacher Meta-Learning for Weather-Specialized Perception**

Novel training protocol where each teacher model specializes in a specific weather/lighting condition (clear, fog, rain, dusk, night), and knowledge is distilled into a single student model via multi-teacher meta-learning. Student learns to dynamically select appropriate weather-specific features.

**Why novel:** MIFormer (0c) uses multi-teacher for foggy weather (single condition). We generalize to multiple outdoor conditions and integrate with meta-learning (not just distillation). Combines multi-teacher distillation (weather specialization) + meta-learning (few-shot adaptation).

**M2. Spatial Prompting for Embodied LLM Agents**

Adapt SpatialPrompt technique (validated on static images) to embodied navigation with continuous sensor streams. Introduce keyframe selection (SpatialPrompting-inspired) + reference object tracking across timesteps for consistent spatial reasoning.

**Why novel:** SpatialPrompt (41c) is for static images. We adapt to embodied setting with temporal consistency (track reference objects across frames), handling continuous sensor input rather than single-shot images.

**M3. GAN-Based Progressive Domain Bridging for Outdoor Sim-to-Real**

Use CycleGAN to generate 3-5 intermediate domains with progressively increasing realism (sim → light weather transfer → medium → heavy → real-world appearance). Meta-train perception across this progression to systematically reduce reality gap.

**Why novel:** Prior work uses GAN for single-step domain adaptation (sim → real). We use progressive steps as meta-training curriculum, treating each step as a meta-task. Bridges gap gradually rather than directly.

**M4. Source-Free Domain Adaptation Protocol for Embodied Agents**

Combine EDAOD's source-free DA (temporal clustering, no simulation data access) with weak supervision (10-50 human labels) and online refinement during deployment. Enables privacy-preserving adaptation without sharing simulation data.

**Why novel:** EDAOD (1c) is indoor robots. We adapt to outdoor conditions with weather-aware temporal clustering (cluster by atmospheric conditions, not just time) and integrate weak supervision for initial calibration.

**M5. Standardized Outdoor Sim-to-Real Benchmark (MetaUrban→Real)**

Propose evaluation protocol: MetaUrban (simulation) → GAN intermediate domains → Real-world (2km² city area, 5 weather conditions, 100 tasks). Standardized metrics: success rate, mIoU, reality gap (pp drop), sample efficiency.

**Why novel:** No standardized outdoor sim-to-real benchmark for LLM-based embodied agents. Existing: CityEQA (sim-only), Open-Nav (zero-shot, no sim-to-real measurement). Ours: Systematic sim→intermediate→real pipeline with quantified metrics.

**Practical Contributions:**

**P1. Sample-Efficient Real-World Deployment (10-50 Samples per Condition)**

Demonstrate that LLM-based outdoor embodied agents can be deployed with minimal real-world data collection (10-50 labeled samples per weather condition, 40-250 samples total for 5 conditions) via meta-learning + spatial prompting.

**Why impactful:** Standard fine-tuning requires hundreds to thousands of samples. Our approach reduces data collection cost by 5-10×, making real-world deployment feasible for research teams without large-scale data infrastructure.

**P2. Zero-Shot LLM Reasoning (No Fine-Tuning Required)**

Show that spatial prompting enables zero-shot transfer of LLM reasoning to real-world embodied navigation without fine-tuning language model parameters. Reduces computational cost (no LLM training) and maintains model generality.

**Why impactful:** Fine-tuning large LLMs (Gemini, GPT-4) is expensive ($10k-100k) and requires access to model weights. Spatial prompting works with API-only access, democratizing LLM-based embodied AI.

**P3. Privacy-Preserving Adaptation (Source-Free DA)**

Enable deployment without sharing simulation data (intellectual property, bandwidth constraints) via source-free domain adaptation. Agent adapts using only real-world observations + weak supervision.

**Why impactful:** Addresses practical deployment constraints where simulation data cannot be transferred to edge devices (bandwidth, IP protection). Relevant for commercial robotics deployment.

**P4. Modular Architecture for Rapid Iteration**

Hierarchical design (perception module ↔ LLM reasoning ↔ action executor) allows independent development and testing. Perception researchers can improve meta-learning without LLM expertise; LLM researchers can refine prompting without perception expertise.

**Why impactful:** Reduces development complexity, enables parallel development by separate teams. Perception improvements (e.g., better GAN) can be swapped in without retraining LLM component.

**P5. Reality Gap Quantification Tool for Researchers**

Provide open-source toolkit to measure sim-to-real reality gap for outdoor embodied agents: perception gap (mIoU drop), reasoning gap (accuracy drop), task-level gap (success rate drop). Enables systematic comparison across methods.

**Why impactful:** Currently no standardized tool to measure outdoor reality gap for LLM agents. Toolkit enables reproducible benchmarking, accelerates field progress by providing shared evaluation infrastructure.

**Summary Table:**

| Contribution Type | Count | Key Innovation | Differentiator from Prior Work |
|-------------------|-------|----------------|-------------------------------|
| Theoretical | 3 | Hierarchical adaptation principle (perception: adapt, reasoning: prompt) | Challenges end-to-end fine-tuning assumption |
| Methodological | 5 | Multi-teacher meta-learning + spatial prompting + GAN bridging + SFDA + benchmark | First integrated outdoor sim-to-real pipeline for LLM agents |
| Practical | 5 | Sample efficiency (10-50 samples), zero-shot LLM, privacy-preserving, modular architecture, gap quantification tool | Makes real-world deployment feasible for research teams |

## 4. Phase 2B Readiness

### Decomposition Preview

**Main Hypothesis (MH):** Hierarchical domain bridging (meta-learned perception + spatial prompting LLM + GAN intermediate domains + SFDA) enables LLM-based outdoor embodied agents to achieve ≥70% navigation success with ≤10pp reality gap using 10-50 adaptation samples per condition.

**Decomposition Strategy:** Hierarchical by component + causal chain validation

**SH1 (Existence): Does perception meta-learning improve outdoor robustness?**

**Statement:** Meta-training a perception module across 5-10 simulated weather/lighting conditions improves real-world semantic segmentation accuracy compared to single-condition training.

**Variables:**
- IV: Meta-training diversity (3, 5, 7, 10 conditions)
- DV: Real-world perception mIoU (test across 3-5 real weather conditions)
- CV: Perception architecture (SpatialVLM), simulation platform (MetaUrban)

**Experiment:**
- Train 4 perception models with varying meta-training diversity
- Test on 500 real-world images (100 per condition × 5 conditions)
- Measure: mIoU per condition and average across conditions
- Hypothesis: 5-10 condition meta-training achieves ≥15pp higher mIoU than 1-condition baseline

**Success Criterion:** Meta-trained (5-10 conditions) ≥70% mIoU, ≥15pp higher than baseline ~55%

**SH2 (Mechanism): Does spatial prompting enable zero-shot LLM reasoning transfer?**

**Statement:** Spatial prompting (reference objects + keyframes) enables LLM spatial reasoning to maintain ≥90% of simulation performance in real-world embodied navigation without fine-tuning.

**Variables:**
- IV: Prompting strategy (None, Reference Objects, Keyframes, Both)
- DV: LLM spatial reasoning accuracy (% correct on real-world spatial queries)
- CV: LLM model (Gemini 1.5 Pro), task type (spatial queries from embodied navigation)

**Experiment:**
- Collect 200 spatial reasoning queries from real-world navigation (e.g., "Is fountain north of statue?")
- Test 4 prompting strategies on same queries
- Measure: Accuracy (% correct), compare to simulation baseline
- Hypothesis: Reference Objects + Keyframes achieves ≥90% of simulation accuracy

**Success Criterion:** Prompted LLM ≥76.5% real-world accuracy (if sim = 85%), non-prompted ≤75% of sim (≤63.75%)

**SH3 (Comparison): Does complete hierarchical system outperform baselines?**

**Statement:** The integrated system (meta-perception + spatial prompting + GAN intermediates + SFDA) achieves higher outdoor navigation success rate (≥70%) than direct sim-to-real transfer (≤50%) and CityEQA simulation baseline (60.7%).

**Variables:**
- IV: System configuration (Complete, Direct Transfer, CityEQA Sim, Ablations)
- DV: Navigation success rate (% of 100 tasks completed)
- CV: Task set (CityEQA-equivalent), real-world environment (2km² city)

**Experiment:**
- Deploy 6 system configurations: (1) Complete, (2) Direct Transfer, (3) No Meta-Learning, (4) No Spatial Prompting, (5) No GAN Intermediates, (6) No SFDA
- Run 100 real-world navigation tasks across 5 weather conditions (20 per condition)
- Measure: Success rate (goal reached within 2m, 30 min)
- Hypothesis: Complete ≥70%, Direct Transfer ≤50%, Ablations show component contributions

**Success Criterion:** Complete system ≥70% success, Direct Transfer ≤50%, each ablation shows ≥5pp contribution

**Sub-Hypothesis Dependency Graph:**

```
SH1 (Perception) ----→ SH3 (End-to-End)
                        ↗
SH2 (Reasoning) -------→ SH3 (End-to-End)
```

- SH1 and SH2 are **independent** (can be validated in parallel)
- SH3 **depends** on SH1 and SH2 (requires both components working)
- If SH1 or SH2 fails, SH3 will fail (necessary conditions)
- If SH1 and SH2 succeed, SH3 may still fail due to integration issues (sufficient but not guaranteed)
