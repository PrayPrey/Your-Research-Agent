- **Representative Work:** "Relational reasoning and generalization using non-symbolic neural networks" (Geiger et al., 2020)
- **Impact:** Challenged assumption that symbolic reasoning requires symbolic architectures
- **Evolution:** Led to investigation of compositional processing in mathematical problem-solving

- **Key Insight:** Large language models exhibit mathematical reasoning capabilities but require systematic evaluation
- **Representative Work:** "A Survey of Deep Learning for Mathematical Reasoning" (Lu et al., 2022)
- **Impact:** Established taxonomy of tasks, datasets, and methods
- **Evolution:** Spurred development of specialized benchmarks and evaluation methodologies

**Phase 3: Interactive Theorem Proving (2021-2024)**
- **Key Insight:** LLMs can assist in formal mathematics through interactive theorem proving
- **Representative Work:** "Proof Artifact Co-training" (Han et al., 2021), "Evaluating language models for mathematics through interactions" (Collins et al., 2024)
- **Impact:** Demonstrated practical applications in mathematical research
- **Evolution:** Influenced development of AI-assisted mathematics research tools

- **Representative Work:** "MathVista" (Lu et al., 2023), "We-Math 2.0" (Qiao et al., 2025)
- **Impact:** Established multimodal benchmarks revealing significant capability gaps
- **Evolution:** Drove research in visual-language integration for mathematics

- **Representative Work:** "Putnam-AXIOM" (Gulati et al., 2025), "LiveBench" (White et al., 2024), "Reasoning or Memorization?" (Wu et al., 2025)
