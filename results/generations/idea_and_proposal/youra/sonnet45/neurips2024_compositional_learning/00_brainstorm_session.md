## Starting Context

**Background:** Compositional learning, inspired by the innate human ability to understand and generate complex ideas from simpler concepts, seeks to imbue machines with a similar capacity for understanding, reasoning, and learning. Compositional learning naturally improves machine generalization towards out-of-distribution samples in the wild, through the recombination of learned components.

**Source Type:** Workshop CFP (NeurIPS 2024 - Compositional Learning: Perspectives, Methods, and Paths Forward)

---

## Research Question Development

### Initial Question

How can we advance compositional learning capabilities in foundation models to achieve better compositional generalization and reasoning in dynamic real-world distributions?

### Refined Question

What are the key factors that enable or hinder compositional generalization in foundation models, and how can we develop transferable, model-agnostic compositional learning methods that work across different domains?

### Detailed Sub-Questions

1. **Perspectives**: In which contexts, and why, should we expect foundation models to excel in compositional generalization or reasoning? What empirical and theoretical aspects (e.g., architecture, scale, composition type, input) influence compositionality in foundation models?

2. **Methods - Transferability**: Can we identify or design compositional learning methods that are transferable across different domains and compatible with existing foundation models? What role can data augmentation and mixture of experts play in achieving this?

3. **Methods and Perspectives - Modularity**: Does modularity in structures guarantee compositional generalization, and is there any correspondence between them? How do different modular learning approaches (adapters, prompts, sparsity) affect compositional capabilities?

4. **Paths Forward - Continual Learning**: What unique challenges arise when extending compositional learning strategies to continual learning environments, and what are possible solutions? How can compositional learning help with memory consolidation and mitigate temporal performance degradation?
