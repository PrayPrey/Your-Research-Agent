# Title
Adaptive Hierarchical Temporal Abstraction for Vision-Language-Action Models: Achieving Real-Time Control and Multi-Step Reasoning Through Cognitive Dual-Process Architecture

# Motivation
Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face a critical efficiency-capability trade-off: large models (7B parameters) excel at complex reasoning but are too slow for real-time control (~100-200ms latency), while compressed models achieve faster inference but sacrifice performance on novel tasks. This limits VLA deployment in safety-critical robotics requiring <5ms reactive control and edge devices with computational constraints. Existing approaches use static solutions—either always-planning (slow, expensive) or always-compressed (limited capability)—missing the opportunity to dynamically allocate computation based on task demands, as humans do through dual-process cognition (automatic System 1 for familiar tasks, deliberate System 2 for novel challenges).

# Main Idea
We propose AHTA-VLA, a 4-level hierarchical architecture (reflexive <5ms, tactical 100ms, strategic 1s, planning 10s) that adaptively allocates computation based on real-time task novelty detection. A fast k-NN clustering mechanism (<0.1ms overhead) estimates novelty from VLA embeddings, activating minimal levels for familiar tasks (10M-parameter reactive pathway) and full hierarchy for novel tasks (7B-parameter planning). Strategic levels define safe region constraints that bound reactive decisions, maintaining hierarchical coherence. This enables dual outcomes: <5ms inference with >80% success on familiar tasks, >90% success on novel multi-step tasks, and 40-60% computational cost reduction versus always-planning baselines. Evaluation on CALVIN benchmark and real-robot validation will test whether cognitive dual-process principles can unlock human-like efficiency in embodied AI.
