# Title
A Verification-Validation Framework for Experimental-Aware Biomolecular Design

# Motivation
Generative ML models for protein design achieve impressive computational benchmarks but suffer from low (10-30%) experimental success rates. This gap exists because models optimize for computational correctness (structure validity, sequence plausibility) rather than experimental utility (expression yield, stability, binding affinity). Current approaches lack systematic frameworks to integrate experimental constraints into ML training, resulting in designs that "look good on paper" but fail in the lab. Bridging this computation-to-experiment gap is critical for translating ML advances into real-world therapeutic and industrial applications.

# Main Idea
We propose a two-tier benchmark framework inspired by Systems Engineering's Verification & Validation (V&V) paradigm. **Tier 1 (Verification)** evaluates computational correctness using existing tools (AlphaFold confidence, ESM perplexity). **Tier 2 (Validation)** employs a predictive oracle trained on (computational_prediction, experimental_outcome) pairs with Bayesian noise modeling to estimate P(experimental_success). The core hypothesis: optimizing generative models (RFdiffusion, ProteinMPNN) for high validation probability rather than verification scores alone will improve experimental success rates by ≥20 percentage points. The oracle learns residual predictive signals beyond computational metrics, enabling pre-experimental filtering of designs. We test this on 200 antibody designs per approach (verification-only, two-stage training, V&V framework), measuring expression yield, thermal stability, and binding affinity. Success requires oracle predictions correlating r≥0.5 with experimental outcomes and demonstrating that verification-validation are weakly correlated (r≤0.6), proving the oracle adds unique predictive value for experimental validity.
