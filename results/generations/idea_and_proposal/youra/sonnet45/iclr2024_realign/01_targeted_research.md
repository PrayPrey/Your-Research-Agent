## 1. Research Questions

### Primary Research Question
What are the most effective methods for measuring and systematically intervening on representational alignment between biological and artificial intelligence systems, and what are the implications of such interventions for computational strategies, behavioral alignment, and value alignment?

### Detailed Research Questions
1. To what extent does representational alignment indicate shared computational strategies among biological and artificial systems?
2. How have current alignment metrics advanced our understanding of computation, and what measurement approaches should we explore next?
3. How can we develop more robust and generalizable measures of alignment that work across different domains and types of representations?
4. How can we systematically increase (or decrease) representational alignment among biological and artificial systems?
5. What are the implications (positive and negative) of increasing or decreasing representational alignment between systems, on behavioral alignment, value alignment, and beyond?

## 9. Conclusion

### Key Findings

**Research Question**: What are the most effective methods for measuring and systematically intervening on representational alignment between biological and artificial intelligence systems, and what are the implications of such interventions for computational strategies, behavioral alignment, and value alignment?

**Finding 1 - Measurement Landscape**: Multiple mature measurement methods exist with distinct strengths:
- **RSA** (rsatoolbox, 232★): Best for neuroscience applications with correlation-based distances; limited to first-order statistics
- **CKA** (311★ implementation): Superior for deep learning due to invariance properties; requires bias correction for biological data (Murphy et al. 2024)
- **Behavioral RSA** (Ogg et al. 2024): Enables human-AI alignment measurement without neural data; complementary to neural methods

**Finding 2 - Intervention Methods Emerging**: Field transitioning from measurement-only to active intervention:
- **REPA** (ICLR'25, 1000+★): Demonstrates alignment-guided training for diffusion models
- **GRADE-Match** (Sucholutsky et al. 2024): Shows alignment improves machine teaching outcomes
- **Gap**: Methods remain architecture/domain-specific; no general intervention framework exists

**Finding 3 - Alignment-Computation Link Unclear**: Strong correlations observed but causality unproven:
- Khosla et al. (2024): Aligned representational axes correlate with reduced wiring costs and better generalization
- Mahner et al. (2024): Humans and DNNs achieve similar task performance via different representational strategies (visual vs semantic dominance)
- **Critical Gap**: Does alignment enable computation, or does shared computation cause alignment?

**Finding 4 - Cross-Field Fragmentation**: Sucholutsky et al. (2023) framework identifies problem:
- Cognitive science, neuroscience, and ML study alignment with limited knowledge transfer
- Different fields prefer different metrics (neuroscience=RSA, ML=CKA)
- **Gap 1 addresses this**: Unified benchmark needed

**Finding 5 - Implications Understudied**: Research focuses on "what" and "how" of alignment, less on "why it matters":
- Behavioral/value alignment implications largely speculative
- Few studies manipulate alignment to test consequences
- **Gap 2 & 3 address this**: Mechanistic understanding + intervention tools enable implication testing
