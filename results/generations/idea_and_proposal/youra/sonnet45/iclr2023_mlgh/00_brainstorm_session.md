## Starting Context

**Background:** During the Covid-19 pandemic, in spite of the impressive advances in machine learning in recent decades, the successes of this field were modest at best. Much work remains, for both machine learning and global health researchers, to deliver true progress in global health. This workshop will start a lasting and consistent effort to close the gap between advances in machine learning, practitioners and policy makers working in public health globally.

**Source Type:** Workshop CFP - ICLR 2023: Machine Learning & Global Health Workshop

**Workshop Focus:** Difficult public health problems and relevant machine learning and statistical methods, bringing together researchers from different communities to share new ideas and past experiences.

---

## Research Question Development

### Initial Question
What is the intersection between machine learning methodological advances and practical applications in global health, particularly in light of lessons from the COVID-19 pandemic?

### Refined Question
How can machine learning advance global health outcomes and close the gap between ML methodological advances and practical public health applications, practitioners, and policy makers?

### Detailed Sub-Questions

1. **Pandemic Lessons**: What lessons can we learn from the COVID-19 pandemic regarding the application of machine learning to global health challenges?

2. **Problem Suitability**: What sorts of questions in global health can machine learning be useful for, and what sorts of questions is machine learning unlikely to be useful for?

3. **Limitations and Solutions**: What are the current limitations in the application of machine learning to solving global health problems, and what are possible solutions to these limitations?

4. **Proactive Applications**: How can we leverage machine learning to promote public health worldwide, be proactive against future pandemics, and understand and address inequalities in health?

5. **Data Infrastructure**: What types of data and data sharing practices would enable better machine learning and global health outcomes?
