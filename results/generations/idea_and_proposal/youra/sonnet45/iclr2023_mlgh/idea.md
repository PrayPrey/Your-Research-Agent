# Title
Economic Inequality Regularization for Fair Medical Machine Learning: Training with Generalized Entropy Indices

# Motivation
Medical AI systems systematically underperform for underrepresented demographic groups due to data inequality—a problem documented across biomedical datasets where non-European populations comprise <10% of samples. Current fairness approaches apply post-hoc corrections after biased learning has occurred, limiting effectiveness. Meanwhile, 70+ years of economic theory on distributive justice offers sophisticated inequality measurement tools (Generalized Entropy indices) that remain unexplored for ML training. This research bridges economics and machine learning to address healthcare algorithmic bias at its source: the training process itself.

# Main Idea
We propose incorporating Generalized Entropy (GE) indices from economics as differentiable regularization terms in neural network training loss functions (L_total = L_task + λ·GE_α). Unlike post-hoc fairness corrections, this creates gradient-based optimization pressure during training toward equitable representation across demographic groups. The mechanism operates through a feedback loop: as model predictions shift during backpropagation, GE indices penalize skewed group-wise representation across three dimensions (sample count, feature coverage, label diversity), steering parameters toward balanced configurations. We adapt small-sample bias corrections from econometrics for minority groups and test sensitivity parameter α to emphasize different distribution segments. Experiments on MIMIC-III hospital data target ≥30% reduction in representation inequality while maintaining clinical accuracy (AUROC ≥0.80). This training-time intervention provides practitioners explicit fairness-accuracy tradeoff control via hyperparameter λ, offering a theoretically-grounded, deployable solution for equitable medical AI.
