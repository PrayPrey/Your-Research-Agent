# Title
Hierarchical Neuro-Symbolic Meta-Learning for Sample-Efficient Compositional Planning

# Motivation
Current AI struggles with sample-efficient generalization in long-horizon sequential decision-making. Deep reinforcement learning achieves strong short-horizon control but requires millions of samples and fails to transfer knowledge. Symbolic planning provides interpretability and generalization but struggles with low-level control and representation learning. Existing hybrid approaches integrate only two components (symbolic+neural OR hierarchical+meta), missing synergistic benefits. This research addresses the critical gap: achieving human-like compositional generalization through unified integration of symbolic reasoning, hierarchical skills, and meta-learning.

# Main Idea
We propose HNS-Meta, a three-layer architecture integrating: (1) symbolic PDDL planner with CLIP-grounded predicates for high-level reasoning, (2) MAML-adapted meta-learned skill library for rapid few-shot transfer, and (3) equivariant GNN controller for low-level execution. The key innovation is **bidirectional feedback**: symbolic predicates guide neural learning while execution errors refine predicate definitions, enabling co-evolution. A staged training protocol (predicate→skill→meta) decouples complexity. We hypothesize this achieves ≥5x sample efficiency versus flat baselines and <10-episode few-shot transfer on compositional tasks (Procgen, Meta-World). The causal mechanism: symbolic abstraction reduces state space (10⁶→10³), enabling tractable planning; meta-learning transfers compositional knowledge across tasks; bidirectional feedback self-improves both layers. Success validates that unified neuro-symbolic meta-architectures unlock human-like compositional reasoning in AI.
