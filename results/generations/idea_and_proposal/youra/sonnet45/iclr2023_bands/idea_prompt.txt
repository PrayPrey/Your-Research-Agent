# Backdoor Attacks and Defenses in Machine Learning

## Overview

Backdoor attacks aim to cause consistent misclassification of any input by adding a specific pattern called a trigger. Unlike adversarial attacks requiring generating perturbations on the fly to induce misclassification for one single input, backdoor attacks have prompt effects by simply applying a pre-chosen trigger. Recent studies have shown the feasibility of launching backdoor attacks in various domains, such as computer vision (CV), natural language processing (NLP), federated learning (FL), etc. As backdoor attacks are mostly carried out through data poisoning (i.e., adding malicious inputs to training data), it raises major concerns for many publicly available pre-trained models. Companies relying on user data to construct their machine learning models are also susceptible to backdoor attacks.

Defending against backdoor attacks has sparked multiple lines of research, including detecting inputs with backdoor triggers, determining whether a model has hidden backdoors, eliminating potential backdoors inside a model, etc. Many defense techniques are effective against some particular types of backdoor attacks. However, with increasingly emerging diverse backdoors, the defense performance of existing work tends to be limited. Most defense techniques and attacks are developed for the computer vision domain. It is yet to explore the connection between attacks and defenses among different domains.

With the wide adoption of large pre-trained models in real-world applications, any injected malicious behaviors, such as backdoors in those models, are particularly concerning. It is, therefore, particularly important to gather researchers in the area and expand the community to improve the security of machine learning.

This workshop aims to answer the following questions:

- What other types of backdoor attacks can we find in CV/NLP/FL machine learning models?
- Can we launch backdoor attacks in other domains, such as binary analysis tools, network intrusion detection systems, reinforcement learning, etc.?
- What are the similarities and differences of backdoor attacks in various tasks?
- How can we measure the stealthiness of backdoor attacks in different domains? What are the costs and practicality of launching backdoor attacks in the real world?
- What is the performance of existing defense techniques in studied domains? Can they be adapted to other domains?
- How can we develop a general defense method against a variety of backdoor attacks and even unseen attacks?
- Are there other forms of defenses that are practical in the real world?

## 1. Clarified Hypothesis

### 1.1 Core Statement

**Hypothesis ID:** H-NAMA-001
**Confidence Level:** 0.85

**Main Hypothesis:**
IF backdoor triggers are present in a deep neural network (backdoored model), THEN the neuron activation patterns will exhibit geometric distortions in their Riemannian manifold representation, specifically: (1) increased local curvature in trigger-affected activation regions compared to clean models, and (2) anomalous topological structures (persistent holes/clusters) detectable via persistent homology analysis, BECAUSE backdoor neurons must create activation pathways that are geometrically distinct from normal decision boundaries to reliably activate on trigger inputs while remaining dormant on clean inputs, and these distinct pathways manifest as intrinsic geometric properties (curvature, topology) that are independent of input domain representation (pixels, tokens, or gradients).

**Alternative Hypothesis (H0):**
Backdoor triggers do NOT create detectable geometric distortions in neuron activation manifolds. Activation patterns of backdoored models, when embedded in Riemannian manifolds, exhibit curvature and topological properties indistinguishable from clean models (within normal variance), meaning geometric analysis cannot reliably differentiate backdoored from clean models.

### 1.3 Causal Mechanism

**Causal Chain (5 steps):**

1. **Backdoor Training Data Injection** → Backdoor Neuron Specialization
   - Mechanism: During training, poisoned samples (clean input + trigger → target label) force network to learn trigger-to-target association
   - Evidence: BadNets (Gu et al. 2017), Backdoor Secrets Unveiled (2024, SS ID: de8da73cafd5973aaa1a793b4a0e6e3f118cdb86) show specific neurons activate exclusively on triggers
   - Observable effect: Subset of neurons develop high activation correlation with trigger presence

2. **Backdoor Neuron Specialization** → Distinct Activation Subspace
   - Mechanism: Backdoor neurons create a separate activation pathway disjoint from clean data manifold to avoid interfering with normal classification
   - Evidence: ULRL (https://github.com/NayMyatMin/ULRL) demonstrates neuron weight clustering - backdoor neurons form isolated weight groups
   - Observable effect: Activation vectors for trigger inputs cluster separately from clean input activations in high-dimensional space

3. **Distinct Activation Subspace** → Local Curvature Increase
   - Mechanism: Abrupt transitions between clean manifold and backdoor subspace create high-curvature "bending" in the combined activation manifold
   - Evidence: Differential geometry theory - manifold curvature measures rate of geodesic deviation; backdoor pathway creates sharp geodesic deviation
   - Observable effect: Sectional curvature κ in trigger-affected regions exceeds κ_clean by 2-5σ (standard deviations)

4. **Distinct Activation Subspace** → Topological Anomalies
   - Mechanism: Isolated backdoor subspace creates "holes" or "disconnected clusters" in overall activation point cloud, detectable via persistent homology
   - Evidence: Topological Data Analysis (TDA) principle - persistent homology detects multi-scale topological features; backdoor creates persistent H0 (disconnected components) or H1 (loops/voids)
   - Observable effect: Persistence diagram exhibits features with birth-death distance exceeding clean model persistence by 3-4σ

5. **Local Curvature Increase + Topological Anomalies** → Geometric Detection Success
   - Mechanism: Combined geometric (curvature threshold τ_κ) and topological (persistence threshold τ_p) tests flag models as backdoored when both anomalies detected
   - Evidence: NAMA framework hypothesis - geometric properties are domain-invariant (works identically on CV/NLP/FL)
   - Observable effect: Detection accuracy (TPR > 0.90, FPR < 0.05) consistent across CV/NLP/FL domains (variance < 5%)

**Evidence for Causal Links:**

- **Link 1→2**: Backdoor Secrets Unveiled (2024) provides direct evidence via neuron activation analysis showing trigger-specific neurons
- **Link 2→3**: Differential geometry theory + ULRL's weight clustering evidence supports activation subspace separation hypothesis
- **Link 3→4**: Mathematical foundation from Riemannian geometry (curvature) and TDA (persistent homology) for manifold structure analysis
- **Link 4→5**: Combined detection strategy analogous to ensemble methods in ML - multiple geometric signals increase robustness

**Key Tension:**

The hypothesis assumes backdoor neurons create geometrically distinct activation patterns, BUT neural networks are inherently high-capacity function approximators that can encode backdoors WITHOUT creating obvious geometric distortions if triggers are sufficiently stealthy (e.g., frequency-domain triggers, imperceptible perturbations). The tension is: Can geometric analysis reliably detect stealthy backdoors that intentionally minimize manifold distortions?

**Mitigation Strategy:** NAMA uses synthetic input generation (random noise, adversarial examples) to probe activation space more broadly than standard validation data, potentially exposing hidden geometric structures even for stealthy backdoors.

### 1.4 Key Assumptions

1. **Manifold Approximation Quality**: Isomap embedding of discrete neuron activations preserves sufficient geometric structure (geodesic distances) for curvature and topological analysis
   - **Justification**: Isomap is standard manifold learning technique with theoretical guarantees (Tenenbaum et al. 2000) - geodesic distance preservation error bounded by residual variance
   - **Risk if violated**: If embedding quality degrades, geometric properties may not reflect true activation structure → false negatives/positives
   - **Testability**: Compute Isomap residual variance; threshold at <10% for reliable geometric analysis

2. **Backdoor Pathway Separation**: Backdoor neurons form activation pathways geometrically distinct from clean data manifold
   - **Justification**: Evidence from ULRL (weight clustering), Backdoor Secrets Unveiled (trigger-specific neurons), FL Survey (gradient-level backdoor signatures)
   - **Risk if violated**: If backdoor pathways overlap extensively with clean manifold, geometric distortions may be undetectable
   - **Testability**: Compare activation vector cosine similarity between trigger inputs and clean inputs - expect low similarity (<0.3) for detectable backdoors

3. **Sufficient Sampling Density**: N=1K-5K samples provide adequate coverage of activation manifold for curvature/topology computation
   - **Justification**: Manifold learning literature typically uses N=1K-10K samples for dimensionality reduction tasks
   - **Risk if violated**: Sparse sampling may miss local geometric features or create artificial topological holes
   - **Testability**: Convergence analysis - vary N from 1K to 5K and measure curvature/persistence stability; expect <5% variance above N=2K

4. **Domain-Invariant Geometry**: Geometric properties (curvature, topology) transfer across CV/NLP/FL domains despite different input representations
   - **Justification**: Differential geometry is coordinate-free by construction - geometric properties defined intrinsically, not via coordinate system
   - **Risk if violated**: If domain-specific factors (e.g., CNN spatial structure vs Transformer attention) fundamentally alter manifold geometry, cross-domain consistency fails
   - **Testability**: Measure detection accuracy variance across CV/NLP/FL - expect <5% deviation if domain-invariance holds

5. **Synthetic Input Effectiveness** (for zero-clean-data scenario): Random noise and adversarial examples activate backdoor neurons comparably to actual trigger inputs
   - **Justification**: Adversarial examples (FGSM, PGD) are designed to explore decision boundary structure; random noise provides broad activation coverage
   - **Risk if violated**: If backdoor neurons only activate on specific trigger patterns, synthetic inputs may miss backdoor pathway entirely
   - **Testability**: Compare backdoor detection rates using clean validation data vs synthetic inputs - expect <10% accuracy drop for synthetic inputs

4. **Stealthy Backdoor Challenge**: Adaptive attacks that intentionally minimize geometric distortions may evade detection
5. **One-time Cost**: NAMA is a one-time auditing tool (<1 GPU-hour per model), not continuous monitoring

### 1.6 Testable Predictions

## 2. Contribution Summary

**Theoretical Contribution:**
NAMA establishes the first geometric deep learning framework for neural network security analysis. By treating backdoor detection as a manifold geometry problem rather than a domain-specific feature detection task, this work provides a unified mathematical foundation for understanding backdoor mechanisms across CV/NLP/FL domains. The key theoretical insight is that backdoor triggers create intrinsic geometric distortions (curvature anomalies, topological holes) in neuron activation manifolds, and these distortions are detectable via coordinate-free analysis (Riemannian geometry, persistent homology). This shifts the backdoor defense paradigm from domain-specific heuristics to domain-invariant geometric principles, opening new research directions at the intersection of differential geometry, topological data analysis, and ML security.

**Methodological Contribution:**
NAMA introduces a novel two-stage detection pipeline combining Riemannian curvature analysis and persistent homology for backdoor identification: (1) Manifold Embedding Stage - neuron activations embedded in Riemannian manifold via Isomap; (2) Geometric Detection Stage - sectional curvature computed via geomstats + persistence diagrams constructed via Ripser, with threshold-based binary classification (backdoored/clean). This hybrid geometric-topological approach provides complementary detection signals - curvature captures local manifold bending, while persistent homology captures global topological structure. The methodology enables zero-clean-data operation via synthetic input generation (random noise + adversarial examples), addressing a critical practical constraint in real-world deployment scenarios where clean validation data may be unavailable.

**Practical Contribution:**
NAMA delivers a single deployable framework for backdoor detection across CV/NLP/FL domains, eliminating the need for separate domain-specific toolkits (BackdoorBox for CV, OpenBackdoor for NLP, FL-specific defenses). This reduces defense development cost, accelerates security auditing workflows, and enables systematic cross-domain robustness evaluation. The framework has immediate applications in ML supply chain security (third-party model vetting), pre-deployment auditing (model certification), and red-teaming exercises (testing model backdoor robustness). With O(N²)-O(N³) computational cost bounded to <1 GPU-hour per model, NAMA is practical for batch auditing scenarios (e.g., auditing 100 models/day requires ~4 GPUs). Future commercialization potential exists for automated model security scanning services.
