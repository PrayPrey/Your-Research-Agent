## Research Question Development

### Initial Question

How can machine learning be applied to address systems challenges in novel ways that move beyond simple replacement of numerical heuristics?

### Refined Question

How can machine learning techniques be applied to solve emerging systems challenges in three key areas: (1) using LLMs for program synthesis in hardware and specialized domains, (2) optimizing compiler partitioning schemes for large-scale LLM training across thousands of accelerator devices, and (3) enabling compute sustainability through energy-aware scheduling and carbon footprint optimization?

### Detailed Sub-Questions

1. How can Large Language Models be leveraged for program synthesis targeting hardware and other specialized domains where traditional approaches fall short?

2. What machine learning approaches can optimize compiler partitioning schemes for training Large Language Models across thousands of GPU or TPU devices at scale?

3. How can ML be applied to compute sustainability challenges, including:
   - Energy-aware job scheduling in datacenter environments
   - Dynamic power management based on workload and carbon predictions
   - ML-driven carbon footprint assessment for cloud datacenters

4. What novel ML techniques can address systems issues that emerge specifically from large-scale training and serving infrastructure?

5. How can we move beyond using ML as a drop-in replacement for numerical heuristics to create fundamentally new approaches to systems problems?

**Significance:** This research addresses critical challenges at the intersection of ML and systems:

1. **Timeliness:** With the explosive growth of LLM training requiring thousands of accelerators, optimizing compilation and partitioning is economically and technically crucial.

2. **Sustainability Impact:** As datacenters consume increasing energy, ML-driven approaches to carbon optimization and energy-aware scheduling directly address global sustainability goals.

3. **Innovation Potential:** Moving beyond heuristic replacement opens new paradigms for systems design, potentially transforming how we build and optimize computing infrastructure.

4. **Venue Validation:** The research is pre-validated by NeurIPS ML for Systems workshop organizers as a significant research direction worthy of community attention.

### Feasibility Check

**Assessment:**

**Feasible Aspects:**
- LLM program synthesis has active research communities and available models (GPT-4, Claude, Codex derivatives)
- Large-scale training infrastructure exists at major labs, providing real-world testbeds
- Energy/carbon data from cloud providers increasingly available through APIs and datasets
- Strong industrial interest provides access to real problems and validation opportunities

**Potential Challenges:**
- Access to large-scale training infrastructure may require industry partnerships
- Carbon/energy optimization requires real deployment or high-fidelity simulation
- Hardware program synthesis needs domain-specific validation environments
