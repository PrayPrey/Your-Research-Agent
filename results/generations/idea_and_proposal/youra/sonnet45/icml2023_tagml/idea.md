# Title
Geometric Mental Models: Unifying Topology, Algebra, and Geometry for Interpretable Graph Neural Network Explanations

# Motivation
Current explainability methods for graph neural networks (GNNs) rely on feature importance rankings (SHAP, LIME) that fail to leverage the rich mathematical structures inherent in graph data. While topology, algebra, and geometry have been applied separately in machine learning, no framework integrates all three for explainability. This creates a critical gap: domain experts in chemistry and biology need intuitive, faithful explanations that align with their spatial reasoning, yet existing methods provide only statistical attributions. We address this by creating "geometric mental models"—human-interpretable visualizations grounded in persistent homology (topology), equivariant representations (algebra), and message-passing graphs (geometry).

# Main Idea
We hypothesize that integrating PyTorch Geometric (geometry), giotto-tda persistent homology (topology), and e3nn equivariant embeddings (algebra) into a unified explainability framework will produce explanations significantly more useful to domain experts than feature-importance baselines. Our causal mechanism: multi-structural representations capture complementary decision aspects (topological features reveal decision boundaries, equivariance ensures rotation consistency, geometric subgraphs identify critical components), which UMAP projects into 2D/3D spatial visualizations aligned with human cognition. We test this through dual evaluation: (1) user study with 20 chemistry experts rating explanation usefulness (predicted ≥1 Likert point improvement, p<0.01), and (2) perturbation-based faithfulness metrics (predicted ≥20% fidelity improvement). Falsification criteria include topological features showing no correlation with predictions (r<0.2) or experts rating geometric mental models as confusing. This advances both TAG-ML theory (first tri-structural integration) and practical drug discovery applications.
