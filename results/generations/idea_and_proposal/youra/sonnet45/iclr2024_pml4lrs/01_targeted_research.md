## 1. Research Questions

### Primary Research Question
What novel algorithms, methods, and industry practices can democratize machine learning in resource-constrained environments by addressing data scarcity, computational limitations, and infrastructure gaps in developing countries?

### Detailed Research Questions
1. **Data Scarcity Solutions**: What methods (weak labels, model-based pre-labeling, teacher-student models, transfer learning, active learning, few-shot/zero-shot learning) can effectively generate and collect training data in limited labeled data settings while avoiding bias from datasets that don't reflect developing country environments?

2. **Computational Resource Optimization**: What approaches (model quantization, compression, distillation, low precision training, pruning, generalized optimizations) enable training and inference on resource-constrained devices without significant performance degradation?

3. **Alternative Learning Methods**: What alternative learning paradigms coupled with deep models are specifically targeted for low-resource settings and can achieve competitive results with reduced computational and data requirements?

4. **Industry Practice & Deployment**: What data science and engineering practices effectively balance accuracy/latency tradeoffs when scaling ML models in low-resource environments, and how do we measure success beyond algorithmic metrics?

5. **Fairness & Explainability**: How can we analyze and ensure ML models deployed in developing countries are fair, explainable, and appropriate for local contexts, including understanding when ML is NOT a viable option?

#### Gap 1: Integrated Multi-Constraint Optimization for Developing Country Deployment

**Current State:** Existing research addresses data scarcity, computational constraints, or infrastructure limitations in isolation. Model compression techniques (quantization, pruning, distillation) are well-established, and few-shot learning methods handle data scarcity effectively. However, these solutions are typically developed and evaluated in controlled lab environments with reliable infrastructure (power, internet, cloud access).

**Missing Piece:** A unified framework that simultaneously optimizes for all three constraints (data, computation, infrastructure) while accounting for developing country-specific challenges: unreliable power, intermittent connectivity, lack of cloud access, limited technical expertise, and local data distribution shifts. The gap is the lack of *integrated* solutions that address the compound effect of multiple simultaneous constraints rather than treating them independently.

**Potential Impact:** HIGH - This gap directly prevents widespread ML adoption in developing countries. Even if a model is lightweight and trained with limited data, it may fail in deployment due to infrastructure issues. An integrated framework could enable truly autonomous ML systems that operate reliably in Sub-Saharan Africa, rural South Asia, and remote Latin America, unlocking applications in agriculture (crop disease detection), healthcare (diagnostic support), education (personalized learning), and microfinance (credit scoring).

#### Gap 3: Context-Adaptive Model Compression for Non-IID Data Distributions

**Current State:** State-of-the-art compression techniques (SmoothQuant, SNIP, knowledge distillation) are developed and evaluated on standard benchmarks (ImageNet, COCO, CIFAR) with IID (independent and identically distributed) data. When deployed in developing countries, models encounter distribution shifts due to: different demographics (skin tones for medical imaging), environmental conditions (lighting, weather), cultural contexts (clothing, architecture), and local languages/dialects. Current compression methods assume the compressed model will be evaluated on data similar to the training distribution, but this assumption breaks in cross-cultural deployment.

**Missing Piece:** Adaptive compression techniques that account for expected distribution shifts during the compression process itself. This includes: (1) domain-aware quantization that preserves features critical for target distribution (even if less important in source data), (2) pruning strategies that retain model capacity for handling novel contexts, (3) distillation methods that transfer robust features rather than just accuracy-optimized features, and (4) compression algorithms that can adapt/fine-tune efficiently with minimal local data when distribution shifts are detected post-deployment.

**Potential Impact:** MEDIUM-HIGH - Current compressed models often see dramatic accuracy drops when deployed in developing countries, despite maintaining accuracy on test sets from developed countries. This gap causes: (1) medical diagnostic systems failing for darker skin tones, (2) agriculture models not recognizing local crop varieties, (3) NLP systems performing poorly on local dialects, (4) computer vision models struggling with different infrastructure (buildings, roads). Addressing this enables truly global ML deployment where compressed models maintain performance across diverse populations.
