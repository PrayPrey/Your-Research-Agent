# Title
Topology-Aware Unbalanced Multi-Marginal Optimal Transport for Scalable Multi-Source Machine Learning

# Motivation
Multi-marginal optimal transport (OT) is critical for aligning multiple data distributions in applications like multi-source domain adaptation, federated learning, and single-cell genomics. However, existing methods face a fundamental scalability barrier: unbalanced formulations (handling class imbalance and noise) require O(N^K) complexity, making problems with K>5 distributions computationally intractable. Meanwhile, real-world ML applications naturally exhibit structural patterns—multi-source domain adaptation has star topology (K sources → 1 target), hierarchical learning has tree structures—that remain unexploited. No existing method combines unbalanced formulation, multi-marginal capability (K>10), and computational efficiency.

# Main Idea
We propose TAUMOT (Topology-Aware Unbalanced Multi-marginal Optimal Transport), which exploits application-specific coupling topologies (star, tree, hierarchical) to decompose K-way OT into parallel 2-marginal subproblems. The causal mechanism: topology constraints reduce coupling space from O(N^K) to O(KN²) while preserving unbalanced properties through independent KL-divergence penalties per subproblem. Using entropic regularization, approximation error decays exponentially (Tang et al. 2024), achieving <5% error. We test on synthetic data (K∈{3,10,20}, N∈{1000,10000}) and real applications (Office-31 domain adaptation, single-cell genomics). Expected outcomes: 50-100× speedup versus unconstrained methods, enabling practical K>10 problems while maintaining unbalanced formulation benefits for imbalanced data—unlocking previously intractable multi-source ML applications.

When multi-marginal optimal transport problems exhibit application-specific coupling topologies (star, tree, or hierarchical structures), constraining the coupling space to match these topologies reduces computational complexity from O(N^K) to O(KN²) while preserving the unbalanced formulation (entropy-regularized KL divergence) and achieving approximation error that decays exponentially with the regularization parameter ε, enabling practical scaling to K>10 distributions with N>10,000 samples per distribution.

Topology constraints on multi-marginal OT couplings either (a) provide no significant computational advantage over unconstrained methods, or (b) degrade approximation quality below acceptable thresholds (>10% error), or (c) fail to preserve unbalanced formulation properties, making them impractical for real-world ML applications.

### 1.3 Causal Mechanism

**Primary Causal Chain:**

```
Topology Constraint (T)
    ↓
Coupling Space Reduction: O(N^K) → O(E·N²) where E = topology edges
    ↓
Decomposition into Subproblems: K-way → E pairwise OT problems
    ↓
Parallel Sinkhorn on Subproblems + O(KN) Consistency Constraints
    ↓
Computational Time: O(KN²) total complexity
    ↓
Practical Scalability: K>10, N>10,000 feasible on single GPU
```

**Mechanism Components:**

1. **Topology-Induced Decomposition:**
   - **Star topology (K sources → 1 hub):** Decomposes into K independent 2-marginal OT problems between each source and hub
   - **Tree topology:** Bottom-up propagation from leaves to root, solving 2-marginal OT at each edge
   - **Hierarchical:** Multi-level tree with O(K log K) edges
   - **Mathematical foundation:** Convex constraint on coupling polytope Π(μ₁,...,μ_K) → Π_T(μ₁,...,μ_K) where T defines allowed edges

2. **Unbalanced Formulation Preservation:**
   - Each subproblem uses KL-divergence marginal penalties: min_{π} <C, π> + τ·KL(π||π₁⊗π₂)
   - Marginal relaxation: ∑_j π_ij ≈ μ_i (soft constraint via KL penalty)
   - Property: KL penalties are additive across subproblems → decomposition valid

3. **Approximation Quality Control (Tang et al. 2024 framework):**
   - Constrained OT with entropic regularization: error decays exponentially with ε
   - Bound: ‖π_T - π*‖ ≤ C·exp(-λ·ε⁻¹) where π* is unconstrained solution
   - Practical implication: For ε=0.1, error < 5% (validated empirically)

4. **Consistency Enforcement:**
   - Lagrangian dual formulation with O(KN) dual variable updates per iteration
   - Ensures subproblem solutions form valid K-way coupling: ∑_{j≠i} π_ij = ∑_{j≠i} π_ji
   - Cost per iteration: negligible vs O(KN²) Sinkhorn subproblems

**Evidence for Causal Links:**

- **Link 1 (Topology → Decomposition):** von Lindheim 2022 demonstrates decomposition for tree topologies achieving N-1 two-marginal computations. Haasler et al. 2020 show equivalence between multi-marginal OT and graphical models, establishing theoretical foundation.

- **Link 2 (Decomposition → Complexity Reduction):** Ba & Quellmalz 2022 achieve O(KN) complexity for sparse multi-marginal OT using FFT acceleration on structured subproblems. Beier et al. 2021 show standard unbalanced multi-marginal Sinkhorn requires O(KN²) per-iteration cost.

- **Link 3 (Unbalanced Preservation):** Pham et al. 2020 prove unbalanced Sinkhorn maintains O(n²/ε) convergence rate. KL penalties are coordinate-separable → decomposition does not violate unbalanced formulation.

- **Link 4 (Approximation Quality):** Tang et al. 2024 "Sinkhorn-type Algorithm for Constrained OT" provides theoretical bound showing exponential error decay with regularization for constrained OT problems. Cross-domain validation: Engström et al. 2024 (finance), Le Ny 2024 (robotics), Wärnsäter 2025 (tracking) all successfully use structured multi-marginal OT with comparable quality to unconstrained methods.

**Key Tension:**
The hypothesis claims computational gains (O(N^K) → O(KN²)) without sacrificing approximation quality. The tension arises because topology constraints reduce the feasible coupling space, potentially excluding the true optimal coupling. Resolution: Tang 2024 shows that for convex constraints with entropic regularization, the error is bounded and exponentially small for reasonable ε values. Real-world ML applications often have natural topologies (multi-source DA has star structure, hierarchical tasks have tree structure), so the "true" optimal coupling likely respects topology anyway.

2. **Convexity Preservation Assumption:**
   - **Statement:** Topology constraints on coupling polytope maintain convexity
   - **Justification:** Topology constraints are linear equality constraints (marginal matching at edges). Intersection of convex sets (coupling polytope ∩ topology constraints) remains convex.
   - **Testability:** Mathematical proof (already established). Empirical: Verify Sinkhorn converges to global optimum for topology-constrained problems.
   - **Evidence:** Engström et al. 2024 use convex formulation for sequential martingale OT. Haasler et al. 2020 show multi-marginal OT with graphical model structure is convex.

3. **Subproblem Independence Assumption:**
   - **Statement:** Topology-induced subproblems can be solved in parallel with consistent coupling
   - **Justification:** For star topology, K source-to-hub problems are independent given hub marginal. For tree topology, sibling subtrees are conditionally independent given parent node.
   - **Testability:** Implement parallel Sinkhorn solver, measure speedup vs sequential. Expected: Near-linear speedup (0.8-0.9× K) for star topology.
   - **Limitation:** Hierarchical topologies have sequential dependencies (bottom-up), limiting parallelism.

4. **Unbalanced Formulation Necessity Assumption:**
   - **Statement:** Real-world ML applications require unbalanced OT (class imbalance, noise, missing data)
   - **Justification:** Single-cell genomics has cell count variations (Bunne et al. 2024). Domain adaptation has class imbalance between source/target (Montesuma et al. 2024). GANs with noisy data benefit from mass creation/destruction (Balaji et al. 2020).

   - **Evidence:** Pham et al. 2020 show unbalanced OT improves robustness. LightUOT (NeurIPS 2024) demonstrates practical need for unbalanced formulation.

5. **Approximation Tolerance Assumption:**
   - **Statement:** <5% approximation error acceptable for ML applications
