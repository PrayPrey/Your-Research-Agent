# Research Idea: Cross-Modal Attention-Driven Active Tactile Exploration (CMADATE)

## Title
Cross-Modal Attention-Driven Active Tactile Exploration for Efficient Robotic Object Understanding

## Motivation
Current robotic tactile sensing relies predominantly on passive, reactive approaches that require extensive touches to understand objects. While vision-based tactile sensors have matured, intelligent exploration strategies that leverage both visual and tactile information remain underdeveloped. This creates inefficiencies in applications like manipulation planning, quality inspection, and prosthetic feedback where minimizing contact time is critical. Existing methods either use vision alone (missing precise contact information), tactile alone (lacking spatial context), or passive fusion (no exploration optimization). We need active exploration policies that intelligently combine modalities to maximize information gain per touch.

## Main Idea
We hypothesize that fusing visual pre-touch predictions with real-time tactile feedback through cross-modal attention, guided by a model-based reinforcement learning policy trained via imitation learning and Bayesian surprise rewards, will reduce required touches by 40-60% compared to uniform sampling. The causal mechanism operates through: (1) vision generates uncertainty maps predicting informative touch locations, (2) cross-modal attention dynamically weights visual priors versus accumulated tactile evidence, (3) a learned policy selects touches maximizing posterior entropy reduction, and (4) hierarchical mode switching adapts between global coverage and local refinement based on uncertainty thresholds. We will validate this on YCB object recognition tasks using vision-based tactile sensors (GelSight/DIGIT), comparing against passive sampling, vision-only, and tactile-only baselines. Success requires demonstrating both efficiency gains and that attention weights shift from vision-dominant (>60%) early to tactile-dominant (>60%) late in exploration, confirming the hypothesized complementary information fusion. This establishes active cross-modal exploration as a generalizable framework for touch processing.
