# Title
Bidirectional Uncertainty Propagation in Hybrid Symbolic-Neural Systems for Safe Clinical Decision Support

# Motivation
Current medical AI systems face critical adoption barriers: black-box models fail silently on unfamiliar patients, lack interpretability for clinicians, and cannot meet multi-stakeholder trust requirements. While existing work demonstrates pairwise integrations—knowledge graphs with deep learning (without uncertainty quantification), hybrid symbolic-neural reasoning (without uncertainty propagation), or deep learning with UQ (without symbolic knowledge)—no framework unifies all three components. This creates a safety gap: models cannot reliably identify when they're uncertain or explain predictions through clinical reasoning pathways.

# Main Idea
We propose BK-DL, the first framework integrating medical knowledge graphs (UMLS), deep learning (graph neural networks), and uncertainty quantification through variational inference with **bidirectional uncertainty flow**. Neural predictions propagate uncertainty through symbolic reasoning (evidential uncertainty), while symbolic constraints guide neural learning (epistemic uncertainty). This enables: (1) **Enhanced safety** via hybrid out-of-distribution detection combining neural uncertainty signals with symbolic knowledge violations (predicted improvement: ≥8% OOD AUROC), (2) **Better calibration** through probabilistic knowledge constraints (≥15% ECE reduction), and (3) **Dual interpretability** providing both learned patterns and symbolic reasoning traces for multi-stakeholder trust. We validate on MIMIC-III/IV diagnosis tasks against neural-only and hybrid-without-UQ baselines, with clinician user studies measuring explanation usefulness and trust calibration.

**Mechanism Decomposition:**

**Component 1: Probabilistic Knowledge Graph Construction**
- Medical knowledge (UMLS, OWL2 ontologies) is annotated with probabilistic edge weights
- Edge probabilities initialized from medical literature evidence strength (meta-analysis confidence, evidence grading)
- Probabilistic constraints represent uncertain medical knowledge (not hard rules)

**Component 2: Uncertainty-Aware Graph Neural Networks**
- GNNs encode medical KG subgraphs (disease-specific, k=3 hop neighborhoods) into latent representations
- Bayesian layers (variational dropout, Bayesian linear layers) quantify epistemic uncertainty from limited data
- Architecture combines graph structure learning with uncertainty quantification

**Component 3: Variational Integration Mechanism**
- Variational inference framework (ELBO optimization) jointly trains:
  - Neural predictions (maximize likelihood of clinical outcomes)
  - Symbolic constraint satisfaction (minimize violations of knowledge graph relations)
  - Uncertainty calibration (align confidence with correctness)
- Bidirectional uncertainty flow:
  - **Neural → Symbolic**: Prediction uncertainty propagates through KG reasoning (evidential uncertainty - "how confident is the data-driven prediction?")
  - **Symbolic → Neural**: Constraint violations inform uncertainty estimates (epistemic uncertainty - "does prediction contradict known medical knowledge?")

**Component 4: Hybrid OOD Detection**
- Traditional OOD signals: Prediction entropy, feature distance from training distribution
- **Novel OOD signal**: Symbolic knowledge violations (predictions contradicting known medical relations in KG)
- Hybrid OOD score = α × (neural_uncertainty) + (1-α) × (symbolic_violation_count), where α learned from validation set

**Component 5: Dual Interpretability Generation**
- **Learned patterns (neural)**: Attention weights show which patient features drive predictions, saliency maps highlight important clinical variables
- **Symbolic rules (knowledge graph)**: Reasoning traces through KG show how prediction aligns or contradicts medical knowledge (e.g., "predicted sepsis because symptoms X, Y match disease relation in UMLS")
- Multi-stakeholder design: Clinicians see both, patients see simplified symbolic traces, regulators audit symbolic compliance

- ❌ Prediction tasks where interpretability is not required (e.g., internal hospital resource optimization models)

- ❌ Non-medical domains without established knowledge graphs (e.g., financial fraud detection, autonomous driving)

**Limitation 1: Subgraph Sampling Reduces Knowledge Coverage**
- **Issue**: Disease-specific k=3 hop neighborhoods may miss distant but relevant medical relations
- **Impact**: Framework may fail to leverage long-range dependencies in medical knowledge (e.g., symptom → intermediate biomarker → rare complication)

**Limitation 2: Probabilistic Annotation Quality Depends on Medical Literature**
- **Issue**: If medical literature has biases (publication bias, demographic underrepresentation) or lacks evidence grades, probabilistic weights inherit those biases
- **Impact**: Framework may propagate systematic biases from literature into predictions and uncertainty estimates
- **Mitigation**: Fine-tune edge weights on diverse clinical datasets; monitor fairness metrics across patient subgroups; use sensitivity analysis to identify high-impact edge weights

**Limitation 3: Interpretability Validation Requires Human Studies**

**Limitation 5: Three-Component Integration Increases Implementation Complexity**
- **Issue**: Combining symbolic reasoning + GNNs + variational inference requires expertise in knowledge representation, graph deep learning, and probabilistic modeling
- **Impact**: Higher development cost, longer implementation time, more debugging challenges compared to simpler baselines

## 2. Contribution Summary

### Theoretical Contribution

**Framework for Bidirectional Uncertainty Propagation in Hybrid Symbolic-Neural Systems**

**Core Theoretical Advance:**
This work establishes the first formal framework for propagating uncertainty THROUGH symbolic medical knowledge (not around it) in hybrid AI architectures. Unlike prior work that treats symbolic reasoning and uncertainty quantification as separate components, we demonstrate how:

1. **Epistemic uncertainty** (model uncertainty from limited data) can be decomposed and propagated through both neural (data-driven) and symbolic (knowledge-driven) reasoning components
2. **Aleatoric uncertainty** (inherent data noise) can be distinguished from epistemic uncertainty in the context of knowledge-constrained learning
3. **Symbolic constraints** act as probabilistic priors that guide neural learning while preserving uncertainty quantification

**Theoretical Gap Addressed:**
Current research has demonstrated pairwise integrations:
- Knowledge graph + deep learning WITHOUT uncertainty quantification (Alawad 2021)
- Hybrid symbolic-neural reasoning WITHOUT uncertainty propagation (Domingues 2026)
- Deep learning + uncertainty quantification WITHOUT symbolic knowledge (López 2025 survey)

**Our framework unifies all three** by treating medical knowledge graphs as **probabilistic generative models** within a variational inference framework, enabling principled integration of symbolic constraints, neural learning, and uncertainty quantification.

**Formalization:**
We extend the variational inference objective to include knowledge graph constraints:

ELBO(θ, φ) = 𝔼_q(z|x)[log p(y|z,x,G)] - KL[q(z|x) || p(z|G)]

where:
- G = Medical knowledge graph with probabilistic edge weights
- q(z|x) = Approximate posterior (neural encoder)
- p(z|G) = Prior constrained by knowledge graph structure
- p(y|z,x,G) = Likelihood incorporating both data and symbolic constraints

This formulation enables uncertainty to propagate bidirectionally:
- **Neural → Symbolic**: q(z|x) uncertainty flows through graph reasoning
- **Symbolic → Neural**: p(z|G) constraints inform neural uncertainty estimates

**Theoretical Implications:**
1. **Calibration guarantees**: Under mild assumptions (knowledge graph correctness, sufficient training data), symbolic constraints provably reduce overconfident predictions on out-of-domain cases
2. **Uncertainty decomposition**: Framework enables separation of data uncertainty (aleatoric), model uncertainty (epistemic), and knowledge uncertainty (probabilistic edge weights)
3. **Generalization to other domains**: Framework generalizes beyond healthcare to any knowledge-intensive domain (legal, scientific, engineering) where domain knowledge graphs exist

### Methodological Contribution

**Probabilistic Knowledge Graph Integration for Deep Learning with Hybrid OOD Detection**

**Novel Methodologies Introduced:**

**Method 1: Evidence-Graded Probabilistic Knowledge Graph Construction**
- **Technique**: Map medical literature evidence strength (meta-analysis confidence levels, evidence grades I-V) to probabilistic edge weights in UMLS medical knowledge graph
- **Algorithm**:
  1. Parse medical literature abstracts and systematic reviews
  2. Extract evidence grading annotations (GRADE system, Cochrane confidence)
  3. Map evidence levels to probability values (Grade I → 0.95, Grade II → 0.85, ..., Grade V → 0.60)
  4. Fine-tune edge weights during training via gradient descent on validation performance
- **Advantage over prior work**: Prior KG integration treats knowledge as binary (edge exists or doesn't) or learns embeddings from scratch. Our method incorporates expert-curated evidence strength, providing stronger inductive bias.

**Method 2: Disease-Specific Subgraph Sampling for Scalable Medical KG Reasoning**
- **Technique**: Extract k=3 hop neighborhoods around patient symptoms/diagnoses to reduce full UMLS graph (4M concepts, 14M edges) to tractable subgraphs (thousands of nodes)
- **Algorithm**:
  1. Identify patient symptoms/diagnoses from clinical data (ICD-10 codes, SNOMED CT concepts)
  2. Perform breadth-first search (BFS) from patient concepts in UMLS graph
  3. Extract k=3 hop subgraph (direct relations + 2 intermediate hops)
  4. Rank edges by clinical relevance (frequency in medical literature, edge probability)
  5. Prune to top-N edges if size exceeds threshold (adaptive)
- **Advantage**: Balances computational scalability with clinical knowledge coverage. Full-graph reasoning is prohibitive; our sampling preserves clinically relevant relations while enabling real-time inference.

**Method 3: Hybrid OOD Detection via Symbolic Violation Signals**
- **Technique**: Combine neural uncertainty signals (prediction entropy, Mahalanobis distance) with symbolic constraint violation count
- **Algorithm**:
  1. **Neural OOD score**: s_neural = α₁ × H(p(y|x)) + α₂ × d_Mahalanobis(z, μ_train)
     - H(p(y|x)) = prediction entropy
     - d_Mahalanobis = distance of latent embedding z from training distribution mean μ_train
  2. **Symbolic OOD score**: s_symbolic = Σ(violations) / |G_subgraph|
     - violations = count of knowledge graph constraints violated by prediction (e.g., predicted disease contradicts symptom-disease relation in UMLS)
  3. **Hybrid score**: s_OOD = β × s_neural + (1-β) × s_symbolic
     - β learned from validation set via ROC curve optimization
- **Advantage**: Captures two failure modes - statistical distribution shift (neural) and semantic inconsistency (symbolic). Prior OOD methods use neural signals only, missing knowledge violations.

**Method 4: Variational Inference with Knowledge Graph Constraints**
- **Technique**: Extend Stochastic Variational Inference (SVI) to jointly optimize neural predictions and symbolic constraint satisfaction
- **Algorithm** (training loop):
  1. **Forward pass**:
     - Encode patient data x → latent embedding z via GNN (q(z|x,G))
     - Sample latent z ~ q(z|x,G) (reparameterization trick)
     - Predict diagnosis y_hat = p(y|z,x,G)
  2. **ELBO computation**:
     - Likelihood term: log p(y|z,x,G) (supervised loss on diagnosis labels)
     - KL term: KL[q(z|x,G) || p(z|G)] (regularizes latent space to align with knowledge graph structure)
     - Constraint term: λ × Σ(soft_violations) (penalizes predictions violating KG relations)
  3. **Backward pass**: Gradient descent on ELBO (including gradients w.r.t. edge probabilities)
- **Advantage**: Standard VI doesn't incorporate symbolic constraints; our formulation enables end-to-end learning of neural and symbolic components.

**Baseline Comparison Strategy:**
- **Baseline 1 (Neural-only + UQ)**: Monte Carlo Dropout, Deep Ensembles - tests if symbolic knowledge adds value over standard UQ
- **Baseline 2 (Hybrid without UQ)**: GNN + deterministic symbolic rules - tests if UQ adds value over hybrid reasoning alone
- **Baseline 3 (Ensemble)**: Simple ensemble of neural models - tests if complex hybrid approach outperforms simpler uncertainty method
- **Metrics**: Accuracy (F1, AUROC), Calibration (ECE, Brier score), OOD detection (OOD AUROC), Interpretability (user study)

**Implementation Details:**
- **Framework**: PyTorch + PyTorch Geometric (GNN) + Pyro (variational inference)
- **Medical KG**: UMLS Metathesaurus (2024 release), domain-specific OWL2 ontologies (SNOMED CT, ICD-10)
- **Datasets**: MIMIC-III/IV (in-distribution), eICU (out-of-distribution)
- **Computational Resources**: Single GPU (NVIDIA A100 or V100), training time ~24-48 hours

### Practical Contribution

**Clinical Decision Support with Dual Interpretability and Enhanced Safety for Multi-Stakeholder Trust**

**Practical Problem Addressed:**
Current clinical AI systems face adoption barriers due to:
1. **Safety concerns**: Black-box models fail silently on unfamiliar cases (OOD patients)
2. **Trust deficit**: Clinicians don't trust predictions without understanding reasoning

**Our Solution:**
BK-DL framework provides clinical decision support with:
1. **Enhanced safety**: Hybrid OOD detection flags underrepresented patients (e.g., rare diseases, demographic groups) where model is unreliable
2. **Dual interpretability**:
   - **For clinicians**: Combined neural attention (which patient features matter) + symbolic reasoning traces (how prediction aligns with medical knowledge)
   - **For patients**: Simplified symbolic explanations (e.g., "predicted sepsis because symptoms X, Y match known infection patterns")
   - **For regulators**: Auditable symbolic constraint violations (compliance with clinical guidelines)
3. **Calibrated uncertainty**: Confidence scores aligned with actual correctness, enabling risk-stratified decision-making

  - Uncertainty quantification flags ambiguous cases requiring additional tests

**Use Case 2: Differential Diagnosis for Complex Cases**
- **Clinical context**: Patients with overlapping symptoms require differential diagnosis (considering multiple possible diseases)
- **BK-DL benefit**:
  - Knowledge graph encodes symptom-disease relations and disease-disease similarities
  - GNN reasoning propagates evidence through disease hierarchy
  - Uncertainty estimates indicate confidence in each differential diagnosis
  - Symbolic traces show which symptom combinations support or rule out each disease

**Use Case 3: Treatment Recommendation with Contraindication Checking**
- **Clinical context**: Medications have contraindications (drug-drug interactions, patient allergies, comorbidities)
- **BK-DL benefit**:
  - Knowledge graph encodes contraindications as hard constraints
  - Neural component learns treatment effectiveness patterns from data
  - Symbolic violations flag contraindications BEFORE recommendation
  - Dual explanations justify treatment choice (efficacy evidence + safety checks)

### Open Questions

**Q1: Probabilistic Edge Weight Initialization - Which Evidence Mapping Works Best?**
- **Question**: How should medical literature evidence grades (I-V) be mapped to probability values? Linear mapping (I=0.95, II=0.85, ..., V=0.60) vs. non-linear (sigmoid) vs. learned from data?
- **Why it matters**: Edge probability initialization affects how strongly symbolic knowledge constrains neural learning. Too strong (high probabilities) may prevent learning, too weak (uniform) may lose knowledge benefit.
- **Resolution approach**: Ablation study comparing 3 initialization schemes: (1) Linear mapping from evidence grades, (2) Learned from validation set via gradient descent, (3) Uniform initialization (0.5 all edges). Measure impact on calibration ECE and prediction F1.
- **Phase 2B action**: Design experiment SH1-Ablation to test initialization schemes

**Q2: Subgraph Sampling - How to Balance Knowledge Coverage vs. Computational Cost?**
- **Question**: Is k=3 hop neighborhood optimal? Should k be adaptive (expand if prediction uncertainty high)? How to rank edges for pruning if subgraph too large?
- **Why it matters**: Larger subgraphs (k=4, k=5) provide more medical knowledge but increase computational cost and risk including irrelevant relations. Smaller subgraphs (k=1, k=2) may miss important indirect relations.
- **Resolution approach**: Sensitivity analysis varying k=1,2,3,4,5 and measuring: (1) Prediction F1, (2) Calibration ECE, (3) Inference time, (4) Explanation completeness (qualitative clinician review). Identify Pareto-optimal k balancing performance and cost.
- **Phase 2B action**: Design experiment SH2-Sensitivity to quantify k impact

**Q3: Hybrid OOD Score Weighting - How Much Weight for Symbolic vs. Neural Signals?**
- **Question**: What is optimal β in hybrid OOD score (s_OOD = β × s_neural + (1-β) × s_symbolic)? Should β be learned per-task or fixed?
- **Why it matters**: If β=1 (neural-only), symbolic violations ignored. If β=0 (symbolic-only), statistical distribution shift ignored. Optimal β may vary by task (e.g., rare diseases may benefit from higher symbolic weight).
- **Resolution approach**: Grid search β ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9} on validation set, maximize OOD AUROC. Compare learned β vs. fixed β=0.5. Stratify analysis by disease prevalence (common vs. rare).
- **Phase 2B action**: Design experiment SH2-Hyperparameter-Tuning to optimize β

**Q4: User Study Design - How to Measure Interpretability Effectiveness?**
- **Question**: Beyond Likert scale usefulness ratings, how to measure whether dual explanations actually improve clinical decision-making? Should user study include diagnostic accuracy with vs. without explanations?
- **Why it matters**: High explanation usefulness ratings don't guarantee improved clinical outcomes. Need to validate that explanations lead to better decisions (higher diagnostic accuracy, faster diagnosis, better calibration).
- **Resolution approach**: Extended user study design: (1) Phase 1: Diagnosis without AI assistance (baseline clinician accuracy), (2) Phase 2: Diagnosis with BK-DL predictions but NO explanations (test if predictions improve accuracy), (3) Phase 3: Diagnosis with BK-DL predictions + dual explanations (test if explanations add value beyond predictions alone). Measure accuracy, time, and trust calibration in each phase.
- **Phase 2B action**: Design experiment SH3-Extended to include diagnostic accuracy measurement

**Q5: Generalization Across Medical Domains - Will BK-DL Work Beyond Diagnosis Prediction?**
