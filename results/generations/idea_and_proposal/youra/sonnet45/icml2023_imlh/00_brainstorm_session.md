## Starting Context

**Background:** Applying machine learning (ML) in healthcare is gaining momentum rapidly. However, the black-box characteristics of the existing ML approach inevitably lead to less interpretability and verifiability in making clinical predictions. To enhance the interpretability of medical intelligence, it becomes critical to develop methodologies to explain predictions as these systems are pervasively being introduced to the healthcare domain, which requires a higher level of safety and security.

**Source Type:** Workshop CFP - ICML 2023 Workshop on Interpretable ML in Healthcare

**Existing Context:** This is a workshop aimed at bringing together researchers in ML, computer vision, healthcare, medicine, NLP, and clinical fields to facilitate discussions about interpretable medical machine intelligence.

## Research Question Development

### Initial Question
How can we develop interpretable and trustworthy machine learning systems for healthcare that provide transparent, verifiable, and clinically aligned medical decision-making?

### Refined Question
What methodologies and frameworks can enhance the interpretability, explainability, and trustworthiness of machine learning systems in healthcare while maintaining clinical reasoning alignment and addressing the unique safety and security requirements of medical decision-making?

### Detailed Sub-Questions

1. **Defining Interpretability in Healthcare Context**
   - How should interpretability be formally defined and measured in healthcare ML systems?
   - What distinguishes medical interpretability from interpretability in other domains?

2. **Uncertainty Quantification and Failure Detection**
   - How can we effectively identify out-of-distribution cases and failure predictions in clinical settings?
   - What frameworks enable robust uncertainty quantification for medical decision-making?

3. **Clinical Reasoning Alignment**
   - How can we design ML methods that align with clinical reasoning processes?
   - What approaches effectively embed medical knowledge and structured clinical information into ML systems?

4. **Robustness and Generalization**
   - What methodologies ensure robustness and generalization of medical ML systems across diverse patient populations and clinical settings?
   - How can we audit and debug diagnostic algorithms for reliability?

5. **Knowledge Integration and Graph Reasoning**
   - How can logic, symbolic reasoning, and medical knowledge graphs enhance interpretability?
   - What composition models effectively integrate medical domain knowledge?

6. **Visualization and Explanation Methods**
   - What visualization techniques best communicate model predictions to clinicians?
   - How can we develop personalized vs. population-level interpretation methods appropriate for different clinical scenarios?

**Potential Impact:**
- Enables safer deployment of ML systems in high-stakes medical decisions
- Builds physician trust through interpretable and verifiable predictions
- Advances the field toward autonomous clinical decision systems with clinical reasoning alignment
- Addresses bias mitigation and identification of relevant medical variables
- Meets regulatory and ethical requirements for medical AI systems

**Field Advancement:** This research bridges the gap between ML capabilities and clinical requirements, potentially accelerating responsible AI adoption in healthcare while maintaining the safety standards essential to medical practice.

### Feasibility Check

**Available Methods:**
- Established interpretability techniques (attention mechanisms, saliency maps, LIME, SHAP)
- Medical knowledge graphs and ontologies (UMLS, SNOMED CT)

- Clinical validation protocols and evaluation metrics

**Realistic Scope:**

**Potential Challenges:**
