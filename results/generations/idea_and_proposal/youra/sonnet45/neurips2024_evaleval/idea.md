# Title
Hierarchical Schema Framework for Standardizing Generative AI Societal Impact Documentation

# Motivation
Generative AI systems lack standardized documentation for societal impacts, making cross-system comparison nearly impossible (<30% success rate with current narrative approaches). While frameworks like NIST AI RMF provide high-level principles, no machine-readable schema exists for systematic evaluation aggregation—analogous to medicine before clinical trial registries. This gap prevents meta-analyses, hinders regulatory compliance (EU AI Act Article 9), and obscures accountability. Addressing this requires adapting proven standardization principles from clinical trials to AI evaluation documentation.

# Main Idea
We propose a hierarchical extensible schema framework adapting WHO clinical trial registry architecture to generative AI societal impact documentation. The three-tier structure includes: (1) Universal Core fields capturing Solaiman's seven dimensions (bias, privacy, disparate performance), (2) Modality Extensions for text/image/audio/video-specific impacts, and (3) Context Annotations for deployment scenarios. Controlled vocabularies with multi-stakeholder governance (NIST working group model) reduce semantic ambiguity, while threshold-based versioning tracks system evolution.

**Testable prediction**: Schema-documented systems will achieve >80% cross-system comparison success (semantic similarity >0.7 + field alignment) versus <30% narrative baseline, validated through EU AI Act pilot deployment (>70% adoption target) and inter-rater reliability (κ>0.60). This enables systematic meta-analyses, regulatory compliance toolkits, and transparent accountability mechanisms for deployed generative AI systems.
