## Research Question Development

### Initial Question

How can we develop and standardize evaluation methodologies for assessing the broader societal impacts of generative AI systems across diverse stakeholder communities?

### Refined Question

**What evaluation frameworks, participatory methodologies, and documentation standards are needed to systematically assess and document the broader societal impacts of generative AI systems (text, image, audio, video) in ways that integrate evaluation science expertise with stakeholder community knowledge and enable policy-relevant recommendations?**

**Specificity:**
- **What exactly:** Evaluation frameworks combining methodological rigor with participatory design
- **Context:** Generative AI systems deployed in society (multiple modalities)
- **Measurable outcome:** Standardized practices that bridge evaluation science, technical development, and policy-making while broadening participation beyond ML/AI experts

### Detailed Sub-Questions

1. **Methodological Foundations:** What existing evaluation methodologies from evaluation science can be adapted or extended to assess generative AI's societal impacts, and what new methods are needed?

2. **Stakeholder Participation Design:** How can evaluation frameworks be designed to meaningfully incorporate expertise from affected communities, practitioners, and domain experts beyond ML/AI specialists?

3. **Impact Documentation Standards:** What documentation and standardization practices would enable systematic comparison, replication, and aggregation of impact evaluations across different generative AI systems and deployment contexts?

4. **Barrier Analysis:** What barriers currently prevent broader adoption of social impact evaluation for generative AI systems, and how can these be addressed through framework design, tooling, or policy?

5. **Policy Translation Pathways:** How can evaluation findings be structured and communicated to inform investment priorities, regulatory approaches, and community-driven governance of generative AI systems?

## Validation Results

### So What Test

**Significance Validation:**

**Why it matters:** Generative AI systems are rapidly proliferating across society (text, image, audio, video generation) with far-reaching implications, yet there's no standardized approach to evaluate their broader societal impacts. This creates:
- **Risk of harm:** Undetected or poorly documented negative impacts on communities
- **Inefficient resource allocation:** Redundant evaluation efforts without shared learning
- **Policy gaps:** Decision-makers lack systematic evidence for governance
- **Exclusion:** Technical evaluations that miss impacts on affected communities

**Potential impact of addressing this:**
1. **Methodological advancement:** Bridge evaluation science with AI technical development
2. **Community empowerment:** Enable affected stakeholders to shape evaluation practices
3. **Policy effectiveness:** Provide systematic evidence base for AI governance
4. **Field maturation:** Establish shared standards similar to clinical trial protocols in medicine

**Venue validation:** NeurIPS workshop indicates established research community recognition of this gap. The Broader Impact statement precedent shows potential for normative shifts in AI research practices.

**Cross-domain relevance:** Findings applicable beyond generative AI to broader responsible AI deployment challenges.

### Feasibility Check

**Feasibility Assessment:**

**Answerability:** YES - Question is researchable through:
- Literature synthesis across evaluation science, HCI, STS, AI ethics

**Available methods/data:**
- **Theoretical foundation:** Evaluation science provides 50+ years of methodology

- **Community access:** Workshop venue facilitates stakeholder engagement
- **Validation pathways:** Can test frameworks through real evaluation projects

**Realistic scope:**
