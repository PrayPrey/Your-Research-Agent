# Research Idea: Precision-Aware Co-Optimization for Data-Efficient Robot Manipulation

## Title
Precision-Aware Co-Optimization: Achieving Sub-Millimeter Robot Manipulation from Minimal Human Supervision via Gradient-Based Physics Calibration and Online Residual Correction

## Motivation
Current robots struggle with high-precision household tasks (threading needles, assembling small electronics, controlled pouring) due to two competing challenges: achieving sub-millimeter accuracy while learning from minimal human demonstrations. Existing sim-to-real methods either require 50+ demonstrations or achieve only centimeter-scale precision. Manufacturing precision control has solved similar challenges through sensitivity analysis and real-time compensation, but these principles haven't been integrated with modern robot learning. This research bridges manufacturing control theory with differentiable simulation to enable robots to learn delicate household tasks from just 5-10 demonstrations while achieving sub-millimeter accuracy in unstructured environments.

## Main Idea
We hypothesize that **jointly optimizing simulation physics parameters and robot policies** using gradient-based sensitivity analysis, combined with lightweight online residual correction, enables sub-millimeter manipulation from minimal supervision. The core mechanism operates in two stages: (1) **Offline co-optimization** computes ∂L_precision/∂θ_sim to identify 5-10 precision-critical parameters (friction, contact stiffness, damping), then jointly optimizes these with the policy using 5-10 human demonstrations in differentiable simulation; (2) **Online residual correction** deploys a lightweight neural network (<100K parameters, <1ms inference) that compensates for remaining simulation imperfections during real-world execution, refined through 10 sparse human interventions. We predict ≥40% sim-to-real gap reduction and <0.5mm position accuracy on tasks like needle threading and PCB assembly—validated through 240 trials across ablation studies comparing against fixed-parameter baselines. This approach uniquely combines manufacturing sensitivity principles with differentiable robot learning, addressing both data efficiency and precision simultaneously.
