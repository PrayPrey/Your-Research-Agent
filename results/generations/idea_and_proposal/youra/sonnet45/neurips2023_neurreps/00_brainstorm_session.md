## Starting Context

An emerging set of findings in sensory and motor neuroscience is beginning to illuminate a new paradigm for understanding the neural code. Across sensory and motor regions of the brain, neural circuits are found to mirror the geometric and topological structure of the systems they represent—either in their synaptic structure, or in the implicit manifold generated by their activity. This phenomenon can be observed in the circuit of neurons representing head direction in the fly, in the activities of grid cells, and in the low-dimensional manifold structure observed in motor cortex. This suggests a general computational strategy that is employed throughout the brain to preserve the geometric structure of data throughout stages of information processing.

Independently but convergently, this very same computational strategy has emerged in the field of deep learning. The nascent sub-field of Geometric Deep Learning incorporates geometric priors into artificial neural networks to preserve the geometry of signals as they are passed through layers of the network. This approach provably demonstrates gains in the computational efficiency, robustness, and generalization performance of these models.

**Source Type:** Workshop Call for Papers (NeurIPS 2023 - NeurReps Workshop)

**Context:** The convergence of these findings suggests deep, substrate-agnostic principles for information processing. Symmetry and geometry were instrumental in unifying the models of 20th-century physics. Likewise, they have the potential to illuminate unifying principles for how neural systems form useful representations of the world.

## Research Question Development

### Initial Question

How do symmetry, geometry, and topology serve as unifying computational principles across both biological neural circuits and artificial neural networks?

### Refined Question

What are the substrate-agnostic principles by which symmetry and geometric structure enable neural systems (both biological and artificial) to form robust, efficient, and generalizable representations of the world?

### Detailed Sub-Questions

1. How do biological neural circuits (e.g., head direction cells, grid cells, motor cortex) preserve geometric and topological structure in their synaptic connectivity and activity manifolds?

2. What theoretical foundations and methods enable learning invariant and equivariant representations in artificial neural networks, and how do these relate to biological implementation?

3. How can geometric priors be systematically incorporated into deep learning architectures to improve computational efficiency, robustness, and generalization performance?

4. What role do dynamics and temporal evolution play in shaping geometric neural representations across biological and artificial systems?

5. How can topological data analysis and geometric statistics be leveraged to analyze and compare representational geometry across different neural systems?
