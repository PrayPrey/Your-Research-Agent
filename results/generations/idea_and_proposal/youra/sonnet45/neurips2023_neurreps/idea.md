# Title
Equivariant Neural Dynamical Systems: Improving Sample Efficiency in Molecular Dynamics Prediction Through Continuous-Time Temporal Integration

# Motivation
Current molecular dynamics prediction models treat each timestep independently, ignoring temporal autocorrelation in atomic trajectories. While E(n)-equivariant graph neural networks preserve geometric symmetries, they lack mechanisms to exploit the smooth, continuous evolution of molecular systems. This gap is critical because acquiring labeled molecular dynamics data is expensive, requiring quantum mechanical simulations. Biological neural systems—from grid cells to motor cortex—suggest a solution: combining geometric symmetries with continuous temporal dynamics improves data efficiency. No existing work unifies equivariant architectures with neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) for sequential prediction tasks.

# Main Idea
We hypothesize that modeling molecular dynamics as continuous-time trajectories via neural ODEs with E(n)-equivariant vector fields will require **30-50% fewer training examples** than static baselines to achieve equivalent prediction accuracy on MD17/ISO17 benchmarks. The causal mechanism operates through temporal smoothness constraints: neural ODE integration enforces Lipschitz-continuous derivatives, reducing the hypothesis space and exploiting autocorrelation in molecular trajectories. We introduce a projection-based integration algorithm that maintains approximate E(n)-equivariance (error <1%) during ODE solving. Experiments across six molecular systems with rigorous statistical testing (180 runs, Bonferroni-corrected significance) will validate three contributions: (1) theoretical unification of symmetry and temporal dynamics, (2) projection-based equivariant integration methodology, and (3) empirical demonstration of improved sample efficiency and out-of-distribution generalization.
