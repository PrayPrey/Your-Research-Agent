**Key Challenges**
1. **Fragmented Evaluation Approaches**: Current AI-HCI evaluation lacks integration between ML benchmarks (technical performance) and user studies (human experience), making systematic system comparison impossible when trading off technical vs. human-centered quality.

2. **Lack of Statistical Rigor**: Existing multi-dimensional approaches (ISO 25010, AttrakDiff) lack psychometric validation and statistical rigor for AI-HCI contexts, operating descriptively rather than predictively.

3. **Single-Metric Optimization Culture**: Benchmark-driven development focuses on single primary metrics (ImageNet accuracy, GLUE score), ignoring human-centered dimensions and leading to systems that may fail in deployment despite high benchmark scores.

4. **No Outcome Validation**: AI-HCI evaluation typically lacks empirical validation linking evaluation scores to real-world deployment outcomes, relying on face validity rather than predictive validity.

5. **Dimension Independence Unknown**: Lack of empirical evidence on whether technical performance, cognitive alignment, and experiential quality represent distinct constructs or redundant measures.

6. **Context-Dependent Quality Criteria**: Different application domains (healthcare, finance, education, entertainment) have different quality priorities, but no validated framework exists for context-adaptive evaluation with domain-specific weighting.

7. **Measurement Instrument Gaps**: While some validated scales exist (Hoffman et al. for XAI), comprehensive psychometrically validated instruments for full AI-HCI evaluation are lacking.

8. **Scalability and Resource Requirements**: Existing comprehensive evaluation approaches require extensive resources (30-50 participants per system, multiple weeks), limiting practical adoption and widespread use.

9. **Temporal Validity Concerns**: User perceptions (cognitive alignment and experiential quality) may change over time as familiarity increases, but temporal stability of evaluation scores is unexplored.

10. **Cross-Cultural Generalizability**: Existing measurement scales (Hoffman et al., UX measures) developed primarily in Western contexts, with unknown applicability to non-Western cultures and international deployment scenarios.
