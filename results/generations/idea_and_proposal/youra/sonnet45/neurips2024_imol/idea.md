# Adaptive Multi-Signal Intrinsic Motivation for Open-Ended Learning

## Motivation
Autonomous agents in open-ended environments require flexible exploration strategies, yet current intrinsically-motivated systems rely on single motivation signals (novelty, dynamics prediction, or competence progress) that excel only in specific contexts. This creates a critical gap: no single signal generalizes across diverse environments—sparse-reward domains favor novelty detection, physics-rich tasks benefit from dynamics learning, and skill progression requires competence tracking. Manually selecting signals per domain undermines autonomy, while fixed combinations waste computational resources on irrelevant signals.

## Main Idea
We hypothesize that **adaptive weighted ensembles** combining three complementary intrinsic motivation signals—RND (novelty), ICM (forward dynamics), and Learning Progress (competence)—will achieve superior exploration and skill acquisition compared to single-signal approaches. The core mechanism uses Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) bandits to dynamically allocate signal weights based on recent effectiveness, enabling environment-specific adaptation without manual tuning. We predict ≥15% higher state coverage and ≥20% better task completion across four environment types (sparse-reward, dense-dynamics, goal-reaching, procedural). Experiments will test 240 conditions (6 signal configurations × 4 environments × 10 seeds), measuring whether UCB adaptation outperforms both single signals and fixed ensembles by ≥10% in cumulative intrinsic reward, with signal weights shifting predictably per environment type.
