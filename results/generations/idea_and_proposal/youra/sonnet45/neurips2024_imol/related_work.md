## Related Work

**Related Papers**

1. **Title**: Functions and mechanisms of intrinsic motivations
   - **Authors**: Mirolli, M., & Baldassarre, G.
   - **Summary**: Proposes comprehensive IM taxonomy distinguishing knowledge-based (novelty-seeking) vs. competence-based (learning progress) motivations, showing they address distinct exploration failures in autonomous learning systems.
   - **Year**: 2013

2. **Title**: Exploration by random network distillation (arXiv:1810.12894)
   - **Authors**: Burda, Y., Edwards, H., Storkey, A., & Klimov, O.
   - **Summary**: Introduces RND method for novelty detection using prediction error on random network targets; achieves SOTA results in Atari sparse-reward environments.
   - **Year**: 2019

3. **Title**: Curiosity-driven exploration by self-supervised prediction (arXiv:1705.05363)
   - **Authors**: Pathak, D., Agrawal, P., Efros, A. A., & Darrell, T.
   - **Summary**: Proposes Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM) using forward dynamics prediction error as exploration bonus; excels in robotic manipulation with rich dynamics.
   - **Year**: 2017

4. **Title**: CURIOUS: Intrinsically motivated modular multi-goal reinforcement learning (Semantic Scholar: 3f56ac0e4b881d25268e83961b93ee95f2807bfb)
   - **Authors**: Colas, C., Oudeyer, P.-Y., Sigaud, O., Fournier, P., & Chetouani, M.
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates modular multi-goal RL with learning progress-driven curriculum learning, enabling IM-driven exploration for multi-goal mastery.
   - **Year**: 2018

5. **Title**: Prioritized sampling with intrinsic motivation in multi-task reinforcement learning (Semantic Scholar: 384d05141cf79d24bbf53df9334bb474928b4a93)
   - **Authors**: D'Eramo, C., & Chalvatzaki, G.
   - **Summary**: Shows adaptive sampling using TD-error weighting improves multi-task RL performance through dynamic task prioritization.
   - **Year**: 2022

6. **Title**: Finite-time analysis of the multiarmed bandit problem
   - **Authors**: Auer, P., Cesa-Bianchi, N., & Fischer, P.
   - **Summary**: Provides theoretical foundation for UCB algorithm with guaranteed sublinear regret O(sqrt(t log t)) in stationary bandits; establishes exploration-exploitation balance framework.
   - **Year**: 2002

7. **Title**: Formal theory of creativity, fun, and intrinsic motivation (1990-2010)
   - **Authors**: Schmidhuber, J.
   - **Summary**: Presents foundational formal theory of curiosity-driven learning using compression progress as intrinsic reward signal for autonomous agents.
   - **Year**: 2010

8. **Title**: Unifying count-based exploration and intrinsic motivation (arXiv:1606.01868)
   - **Authors**: Bellemare, M., Srinivasan, S., Ostrovski, G., Schaul, T., Saxton, D., & Munos, R.
   - **Summary**: Unifies count-based exploration methods with pseudo-count density models for principled novelty quantification in high-dimensional spaces.
   - **Year**: 2016

9. **Title**: Diversity is all you need: Learning skills without a reward function (arXiv:1802.06070)
   - **Authors**: Eysenbach, B., Gupta, A., Ibarz, J., & Levine, S.
   - **Summary**: Proposes DIAYN method for unsupervised skill discovery through mutual information maximization without external rewards.
   - **Year**: 2019

10. **Title**: First return, then explore
    - **Authors**: Ecoffet, A., Huizinga, J., Lehman, J., Stanley, K. O., & Clune, J.
    - **Summary**: Introduces Go-Explore algorithm demonstrating importance of memory and returning to promising states for solving hard exploration problems.
    - **Year**: 2021

11. **Title**: Intrinsically motivated exploration of learned goal spaces (Semantic Scholar: f0f8f9a52b8130550fa2faeebe8d1595ef9c45fc)
    - **Authors**: Laversanne-Finot, A., Péré, A., & Oudeyer, P.-Y.
    - **Summary**: Explores self-organized goal exploration in learned representation spaces using intrinsic motivation for open-ended skill acquisition.
    - **Year**: 2021

12. **Title**: Unsupervised skill discovery through skill regions differentiation (Semantic Scholar: 5c460d8e8e4e6304f2ee82ebae621c0c2f0aaa9e)
    - **Authors**: Xiao, T., Zheng, J., et al.

**Key Challenges**

1. **Single-Signal Limitations**: Current IMOL systems rely on single intrinsic motivation signals (e.g., RND-only, ICM-only, LP-only), which perform well in specific environments but fail when environment characteristics don't match the signal's assumptions (e.g., RND fails in dynamic environments, ICM struggles without rich dynamics).

2. **Environment-Signal Mismatch**: No systematic approach exists for matching intrinsic motivation signals to environment characteristics, leading to inefficient exploration when signal assumptions are violated (e.g., novelty-seeking distracted by "noisy TV" problem, competence-based signals requiring task structure).

3. **Manual Signal Selection**: Practitioners must manually select and tune intrinsic motivation signals for each environment through trial-and-error, lacking principled methods for automatic signal composition and adaptation.

4. **Lack of Adaptive Integration**: Existing multi-signal approaches either use fixed combinations or simple additive weighting, failing to dynamically adapt signal contributions based on environment feedback and exploration progress.

5. **Theoretical Gap in Multi-Signal Composition**: While Mirolli & Baldassarre (2013) taxonomy identifies complementary signal types (knowledge vs. competence-based), no work systematically investigates optimal composition strategies or adaptive allocation mechanisms.

6. **Normalization and Scaling Issues**: Different intrinsic motivation signals operate on incompatible scales, creating challenges for principled combination without manual hyperparameter tuning across diverse environments.

7. **Exploration Efficiency Trade-offs**: Static signal selection forces choice between exploration diversity (multi-signal coverage) and exploitation efficiency (focused signal), with no mechanism to balance based on environment needs.

8. **Generalization Across Environment Types**: Methods optimized for specific domains (e.g., Atari, robotics, mazes) fail to generalize across environment categories with different exploration challenges (sparse rewards, dynamic distractors, contact-rich dynamics).

9. **Evaluation Methodology Gaps**: Lack of standardized benchmarks for comparing multi-signal IMOL approaches across diverse environment characteristics and difficulty levels.

10. **Autonomy Limitations**: Current IMOL systems cannot autonomously learn and thrive in realistic open-ended environments without predefined learning signals or extensive manual engineering per environment.
