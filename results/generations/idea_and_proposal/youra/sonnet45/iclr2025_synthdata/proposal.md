# Research Proposal: Adaptive Data Portfolio Optimization for Dynamic Synthetic-Natural Data Mixing

## 1. Title

**Adaptive Data Portfolio Optimization: A Portfolio Theory Framework for Dynamic Synthetic-Natural Data Mixing in Machine Learning**

## 2. Introduction

### 2.1 Background

The performance of modern machine learning models is fundamentally constrained by the availability of large-scale, high-quality training data. Recent advances in large language models and domain-specific applications have demonstrated that massive, diverse datasets are critical for achieving state-of-the-art performance. However, accessing such data presents significant challenges related to privacy protection, copyright restrictions, fairness concerns, and prohibitive acquisition costs.

Synthetic data generation has emerged as a promising solution to these data access barriers. Generative models such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), and diffusion models can produce artificial data that mimics the statistical properties of real data while potentially addressing privacy and access constraints. Recent work has shown that synthetic data can be valuable for both general-purpose and domain-specific applications, including healthcare, finance, and autonomous systems.

Despite this promise, a critical gap exists between synthetic data's theoretical potential and its practical deployment: practitioners lack principled methods for determining optimal mixing ratios between synthetic and natural data. Current approaches typically employ fixed mixing ratios throughout the entire training process, ignoring several key factors:

1. **Training phase dynamics**: Optimal data proportions may vary across different stages of model training
2. **Quality-risk tradeoffs**: Different synthetic generators produce data with varying quality-risk profiles
3. **Resource constraints**: Privacy budgets (e.g., differential privacy parameters) and computational costs impose practical limitations
4. **Multi-source scenarios**: When multiple synthetic generators are available, determining their optimal combination remains an open challenge

This research addresses a fundamental question posed by the synthetic data community: *How much synthetic versus natural data should be used, and when?*

### 2.2 Research Objectives

This research proposes **Adaptive Data Portfolio Optimization (ADPO)**, a novel framework that treats synthetic-natural data mixing as a dynamic portfolio optimization problem. Our primary objectives are:

1. **Develop a principled optimization framework** that dynamically adjusts synthetic-natural data mixing ratios during training based on empirically validated quality metrics
2. **Establish theoretical connections** between portfolio theory, curriculum learning, and data mixing strategies
3. **Validate the hypothesis** that adaptive mixing achieves superior performance compared to fixed-ratio approaches across diverse datasets and domains
4. **Provide practical guidelines** for practitioners to optimize data mixing under privacy and cost constraints

### 2.3 Research Significance

This research makes several significant contributions to the machine learning and synthetic data communities:

**Theoretical Contributions:**
- Novel formulation of data mixing as a portfolio optimization problem, where data sources are treated as "assets" with quality-risk profiles
- Formal connection between portfolio theory and curriculum learning in the context of synthetic data
- Theoretical analysis of optimal mixing strategies under privacy and cost constraints

**Methodological Contributions:**
- ADPO algorithm with dynamic rebalancing mechanisms and stability guarantees
- Integration framework for empirically validated quality metrics (SDMetrics)
- Comprehensive validation protocol addressing correlation, stability, and curriculum emergence

**Practical Contributions:**
- Principled, constraint-aware answer to the critical question of optimal synthetic-natural data mixing
- Cost-effective training strategies that maximize performance per privacy/cost budget
- Open-source implementation enabling broad adoption across domains (tabular, vision, time-series, text)
- Empirical evidence for curriculum learning patterns in data mixing

The proposed framework addresses key topics identified in the workshop call, including: new algorithms for synthetic data generation and mixing, synthetic data for model training, evaluation of synthetic data quality, fine-grained control of synthetic data generation, privacy-preserving methods, and new paradigms for accessing data in machine learning.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Problem Formulation

Let $\mathcal{D}_{\text{nat}}$ denote a natural dataset and $\{\mathcal{D}_{\text{syn}}^{(1)}, \mathcal{D}_{\text{syn}}^{(2)}, \ldots, \mathcal{D}_{\text{syn}}^{(M)}\}$ denote $M$ synthetic datasets generated by different methods (e.g., CTGAN, VAE, diffusion models). At training epoch $t$, we construct a mixed dataset:

$$\mathcal{D}_{\text{mix}}(t) = \bigcup_{i=0}^{M} \text{Sample}(\mathcal{D}_i, w_i(t) \cdot N)$$

where $\mathcal{D}_0 = \mathcal{D}_{\text{nat}}$, $w_i(t)$ represents the mixing weight for source $i$ at epoch $t$, $N$ is the total batch size, and $\sum_{i=0}^{M} w_i(t) = 1$, $w_i(t) \in [0,1]$.

**Objective:** Find optimal weights $\mathbf{w}^*(t) = [w_0(t), w_1(t), \ldots, w_M(t)]$ that maximize expected model performance while minimizing variance and satisfying privacy/cost constraints.

### 3.2 Data Source Profiling

For each data source $\mathcal{D}_i$, we compute quality-risk profiles using SDMetrics:

**Quality Metrics ($\mu_i$):**
- **Fidelity**: Statistical similarity to natural data (KL divergence, Wasserstein distance)
- **Utility**: Downstream task performance when trained exclusively on source $i$
- **Diversity**: Coverage of feature space

**Risk Metrics ($\sigma_i^2$):**
- **Privacy leakage**: Membership inference attack success rate
- **Variance**: Performance variance across random seeds
- **Stability**: Gradient variance during training

Formally, we define:

$$\mu_i = \alpha_1 \cdot \text{Fidelity}_i + \alpha_2 \cdot \text{Utility}_i + \alpha_3 \cdot \text{Diversity}_i$$

$$\sigma_i^2 = \beta_1 \cdot \text{PrivacyRisk}_i + \beta_2 \cdot \text{PerformanceVar}_i + \beta_3 \cdot \text{GradientVar}_i$$

where $\alpha_j, \beta_j$ are weighting coefficients determined through cross-validation.

### 3.3 Portfolio Optimization Framework

Every $K$ epochs, ADPO solves the following quadratic programming problem:

$$\mathbf{w}^* = \arg\max_{\mathbf{w}} \left[ \sum_{i=0}^{M} w_i \mu_i - \lambda \sum_{i=0}^{M} w_i \sigma_i^2 \right]$$

**Subject to:**
1. **Simplex constraint**: $\sum_{i=0}^{M} w_i = 1$, $w_i \geq 0$
2. **Privacy budget**: $\sum_{i=0}^{M} w_i \cdot \epsilon_i \leq \epsilon_{\text{budget}}$
3. **Cost constraint**: $\sum_{i=0}^{M} w_i \cdot c_i \leq C_{\text{budget}}$
4. **Stability constraint**: $\|\mathbf{w}(t) - \mathbf{w}(t-K)\|_{\infty} \leq \Delta w_{\max}$

where $\lambda$ is the risk-aversion parameter, $\epsilon_i$ is the privacy cost of source $i$, $c_i$ is the computational cost, and $\Delta w_{\max} = 0.1$ is the maximum weight change (damping factor).

### 3.4 ADPO Algorithm

**Algorithm 1: Adaptive Data Portfolio Optimization**

```
Input: Natural data D_nat, Synthetic sources {D_syn^(1), ..., D_syn^(M)},
       Model θ, Rebalancing frequency K, Risk aversion λ,
       Privacy budget ε_budget, Cost budget C_budget

Output: Trained model θ*

1. Initialize:
   - Compute quality profiles {μ_i, σ_i²} for all sources
   - Initialize weights w(0) using Bayesian prior or uniform distribution
   
2. For epoch t = 1 to T:
   
   3. If t mod K == 0:
      4. Solve QP optimization for w*(t)
      5. Apply damping: w(t) = w(t-K) + clip(w*(t) - w(t-K), -Δw_max, Δw_max)
      6. Monitor gradient variance CV_grad
      7. If CV_grad > 0.5:
         8. Suspend rebalancing, revert to w(t-K)
   
   9. Construct mixed dataset D_mix(t) using w(t)
   10. Sample mini-batch B from D_mix(t)
   11. Update model: θ ← θ - η∇_θ L(θ; B)
   
   12. If t mod K_eval == 0:
       13. Update quality metrics {μ_i, σ_i²} on validation set
       
14. Return θ*
```

**Key Components:**

1. **Bayesian Warm-Start**: Initialize $\mathbf{w}(0)$ using prior knowledge from meta-learning or domain expertise
2. **Damped Updates**: Limit weight changes to prevent training instability
3. **Gradient Monitoring**: Suspend rebalancing if coefficient of variation exceeds threshold
4. **Adaptive Quality Estimation**: Update metrics periodically based on validation performance

### 3.5 Experimental Design

#### 3.5.1 Datasets

We evaluate ADPO across five datasets spanning different domains:

1. **CIFAR-10** (Vision): 60,000 32×32 color images, 10 classes
2. **MNIST** (Vision): 70,000 28×28 grayscale digits, 10 classes
3. **Adult Census** (Tabular): 48,842 records, binary income prediction
4. **Fashion-MNIST** (Vision): 70,000 28×28 grayscale fashion items, 10 classes
5. **Bank Marketing** (Tabular): 45,211 records, binary subscription prediction

#### 3.5.2 Synthetic Data Generation

For each dataset, we generate synthetic data using three methods:

1. **CTGAN**: Conditional Tabular GAN (for tabular data) / StyleGAN2 (for images)
2. **VAE**: Variational Autoencoder with appropriate architecture
3. **Diffusion**: DDPM (Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models)

Each generator produces synthetic datasets of size equal to the natural dataset.

#### 3.5.3 Experimental Conditions

**Independent Variables:**
- **Mixing Strategy**: 8 levels
  - ADPO (proposed)
  - Fixed-0% (natural only)
  - Fixed-25%, Fixed-50%, Fixed-75% (fixed synthetic ratios)
  - Fixed-100% (synthetic only)
  - Random Mixing (Dirichlet sampling each epoch)
  - Naive Curriculum (linear schedule: $w_{\text{syn}}(t) = 1 - t/T$)

- **Data Scarcity**: 3 levels (10%, 25%, 50% of natural data available)
- **Privacy Budget**: 3 levels ($\epsilon \in \{1, 3, 10\}$) for subset of experiments

**Dependent Variables:**
- **Primary**: Test accuracy, F1-score, AUROC
- **Secondary**: Epochs to 95% final performance, training stability (gradient CV), computational overhead

**Control Variables:**
- Model architecture (ResNet-18 for vision, MLP for tabular)
- Optimizer (Adam, $\eta = 0.001$)
- Batch size ($N = 128$)
- Random seeds (5 per condition)

**Sample Size**: 5 random seeds × 8 strategies × 5 datasets × 3 scarcity levels = 600 base runs, plus 120 extended runs for privacy analysis.

#### 3.5.4 Baselines

1. **Fixed Ratios**: Maintain constant synthetic-natural proportions throughout training
2. **Random Mixing**: Sample $\mathbf{w} \sim \text{Dirichlet}(\alpha=1)$ each epoch
3. **Naive Curriculum**: Predefined linear schedule from 100% synthetic to 100% natural
4. **Oracle**: Upper bound using validation set to select best fixed ratio (not practical)

#### 3.5.5 Evaluation Metrics

**Performance Metrics:**
- Classification accuracy (primary)
- F1-score (macro-averaged)
- AUROC for binary tasks

**Efficiency Metrics:**
- Convergence speed: Epochs to reach 95% of final accuracy
- Computational overhead: Wall-clock time ratio vs. baseline
- Sample efficiency: Performance per training sample

**Stability Metrics:**
- Gradient variance coefficient of variation: $\text{CV}_{\text{grad}} = \sigma_{\text{grad}} / \mu_{\text{grad}}$
- Weight change magnitude: $\|\Delta \mathbf{w}\|_2$ over time

**Quality Correlation:**
- Pearson correlation between SDMetrics and performance: $r(\mu_i, \text{Acc}_i)$

### 3.6 Hypothesis Testing

We test seven sub-hypotheses using rigorous statistical methods:

**SH1 (Existence)**: ADPO improves over best fixed ratio
- **Test**: Paired t-test, $\alpha = 0.01$ (Bonferroni corrected)
- **Success Criterion**: ADPO accuracy $\geq$ max(Fixed baselines) + 2% on $\geq 2$ out of 3 primary datasets
- **Effect Size**: Cohen's $d \geq 0.5$

**SH2 (Mechanism)**: Quality metrics predict performance
- **Test**: Pearson correlation $r(\text{SDMetrics}, \text{accuracy})$
- **Success Criterion**: $r \geq 0.5$, $p < 0.05$

**SH3 (Comparison)**: ADPO outperforms curriculum baselines
- **Test**: Paired t-test vs. Naive Curriculum and Random Mixing
- **Success Criterion**: ADPO $>$ Naive + 1%, ADPO $>$ Random + 2%

**SH4 (Constraints)**: Privacy and cost constraints satisfied
- **Test**: Constraint violation logging, privacy auditing via membership inference
- **Success Criterion**: 0% violations, empirical leakage $\leq \epsilon_{\text{budget}}$

**SH5 (Stability)**: Damped rebalancing maintains training stability
- **Test**: F-test comparing gradient variance with/without damping
- **Success Criterion**: Damped $\text{CV}_{\text{grad}} \leq 0.3$, Undamped $\text{CV}_{\text{grad}} \geq 0.5$

**SH6 (Curriculum)**: Weights follow curriculum learning pattern
- **Test**: Paired t-test comparing early (epochs 1-T/3) vs. late (epochs 2T/3-T) synthetic weights
- **Success Criterion**: $w_{\text{syn}}^{\text{early}} - w_{\text{syn}}^{\text{late}} \geq 0.15$, $p < 0.05$

**SH7 (Efficiency)**: Computational overhead acceptable
- **Test**: Wall-clock time measurement
- **Success Criterion**: Overhead $\leq 5\%$ compared to best fixed baseline

### 3.7 Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis is **falsified** if any of the following occur:

- **FC1**: ADPO fails to outperform best fixed ratio on $\geq 3$ out of 5 datasets
- **FC2**: Training instability ($\text{CV}_{\text{grad}} > 0.5$) despite damping, or convergence failure
- **FC3**: Training overhead $> 10\%$ AND accuracy gain $< 1\%$
- **FC4**: Quality metric correlation $r < 0.3$ AND fallback to direct measurement is prohibitively expensive
- **FC5**: No curriculum learning pattern emerges (random or inverted weights)

## 4. Expected Outcomes & Impact

### 4.1 Expected Outcomes

Based on our hypothesis and preliminary theoretical analysis, we anticipate the following outcomes:

**Primary Outcomes:**

1. **Performance Improvement**: ADPO will achieve $\geq 2\%$ accuracy improvement over the best fixed mixing ratio across at least 2 out of 3 primary datasets (CIFAR-10, MNIST, Adult Census), with statistical significance ($p < 0.05$, $n=5$ seeds).

2. **Curriculum Learning Emergence**: Mixing weights will exhibit a clear curriculum pattern, with higher synthetic data proportions early in training ($w_{\text{syn}}^{\text{early}} \approx 0.7$) transitioning to higher natural data proportions later ($w_{\text{syn}}^{\text{late}} \approx 0.4$), with $\Delta w \geq 0.15$.

3. **Convergence Acceleration**: ADPO will reach 95% of final accuracy in $\leq 80\%$ of the epochs required by the best fixed baseline, demonstrating improved sample efficiency.

4. **Quality Metric Validation**: SDMetrics (fidelity, utility, diversity) will show moderate-to-strong correlation with downstream performance ($r \geq 0.5$), validating their use as optimization objectives.

5. **Constraint Satisfaction**: All privacy and cost constraints will be satisfied with 0% violations, and empirical privacy leakage (measured via membership inference attacks) will remain within specified $\epsilon$-differential privacy budgets.

**Secondary Outcomes:**

6. **Training Stability**: Damped weight updates will maintain gradient variance coefficient of variation $\leq 0.3$, compared to $\geq 0.5$ without damping, demonstrating the necessity of stability mechanisms.

7. **Computational Efficiency**: ADPO's computational overhead will remain $\leq 5\%$ compared to fixed baselines, making it practical for real-world deployment.

8. **Robustness Under Scarcity**: ADPO's performance advantage will be most pronounced under severe data scarcity (10% natural data available), with gains of $\geq 3\%$ over fixed ratios.

9. **Privacy-Performance Tradeoff**: Under tight privacy budgets ($\epsilon = 1$), ADPO will achieve $\geq 1.5\times$ larger performance gains compared to relaxed budgets ($\epsilon = 10$), demonstrating its value in privacy-critical applications.

### 4.5 Limitations and Future Work

**Acknowledged Limitations:**

1. **Quality Metric Dependence**: ADPO's performance depends on the accuracy of quality metrics; if SDMetrics fail to correlate with performance ($r < 0.3$), fallback to direct measurement may be required.

2. **Computational Overhead**: While expected to be $\leq 5\%$, the overhead of solving QP problems every $K$ epochs may be prohibitive for extremely large-scale applications.

3. **Cold Start Problem**: Initial weight selection requires either domain expertise or meta-learning, which may not be available in novel domains.

**Future Research Directions:**

4. **Multi-Objective Optimization**: Extend ADPO to simultaneously optimize accuracy, fairness, robustness, and other objectives.

5. **Transfer Learning**: Investigate whether optimal mixing strategies learned on one task can transfer to related tasks.

6. **Theoretical Convergence Analysis**: Develop formal convergence guarantees for ADPO under various assumptions.

7. **Production Deployment**: Study ADPO's behavior in continual learning and online settings where data streams evolve over time.

8. **Meta-Learning for Initialization**: Develop meta-learning approaches to automatically initialize weights based on dataset characteristics.
