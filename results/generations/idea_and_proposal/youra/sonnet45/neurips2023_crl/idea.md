# Title
Bridging the CRL Theory-Practice Gap: Automated Deployment Framework for Medical Imaging Domain Experts

# Motivation
Causal Representation Learning (CRL) promises robust, interpretable models that generalize across domains—critical for medical imaging where clinical confounding and multi-site biases plague diagnosis systems. However, deploying CRL requires PhD-level expertise in causal inference theory (identifiability conditions, interventional assumptions), creating a theory-practice gap: domain experts who understand medical problems cannot access CRL tools, while CRL researchers lack medical domain knowledge. Current solutions either sacrifice theoretical guarantees (generic AutoML) or demand prohibitive expertise (manual implementation requiring 12-16 hours). This prevents CRL adoption in high-impact healthcare applications where causal reasoning could reduce diagnostic biases and improve cross-hospital generalization.

# Main Idea
We propose a two-tier CRL deployment framework enabling Master's-level medical imaging experts to deploy causally sound models within 4 hours (vs. <30% success rate for manual deployment). **Core innovation**: Automated identifiability verification acts as a "safety net"—users interact with simplified YAML-template interfaces (novice tier) or full architecture control (expert tier), while Moran & Aragam's (2025) statistical identifiability tests automatically flag causal assumption violations *before* training, preserving theoretical rigor despite accessibility layers. Templates encode validated medical imaging CRL patterns (clinical confounding removal, multi-site generalization from cheng-01037). We test via user study (N=10 domain experts) on three benchmarks (ISIC skin lesions, ChestX-ray14, BraTS brain tumors), predicting ≥70% deployment success within 4 hours, model accuracy within 2% of manual baselines, and ≥90% verification precision—demonstrating 60% barrier reduction (time+expertise) while maintaining causal guarantees.
