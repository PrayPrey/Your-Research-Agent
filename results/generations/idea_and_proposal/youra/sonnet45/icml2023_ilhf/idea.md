# Hierarchical Multimodal Interaction-Grounded Learning for Personalized Assistive Systems

## Motivation
Current interactive learning systems rely on explicit rewards or single-modality feedback, limiting their applicability to assistive technologies where users communicate naturally through multiple implicit channels (gaze, speech prosody, gestures). Existing Interaction-Grounded Learning (IGL) handles arbitrary feedback but only from single modalities, while multimodal RLHF requires explicit preference labels. This creates a critical gap for users with disabilities who cannot provide explicit feedback but naturally emit rich multimodal implicit signals during interaction.

## Main Idea
We extend IGL to multimodal feedback spaces through a hierarchical architecture combining modality-specific encoders (eye-tracking, speech prosody, gesture recognition), learnable temporal pooling for synchronizing heterogeneous sampling rates, and cross-modal attention fusion that adaptively weights modalities based on task context. The core hypothesis: multimodal fusion preserves reward-relevant information better than single modalities while maintaining IGL's theoretical guarantees under extended conditional independence assumptions. Cross-modal attention learns context-dependent weighting (prioritizing gaze for visual tasks, speech for dialogue tasks), while uncertainty quantification flags conflicting signals. We predict ≥0.15 improvement in reward decoder accuracy over best single-modality baselines, translating to ≥10% higher policy success rates across wheelchair navigation, prosthetic control, and educational tutoring domains. This enables assistive systems to learn personalized behaviors from natural multimodal communication without explicit labeling burden.
