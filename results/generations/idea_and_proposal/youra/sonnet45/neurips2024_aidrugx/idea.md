# Title
BioRenorm: Physics-Inspired Renormalization Group Operators for Cross-Scale Drug Response Prediction

# Motivation
Current AI models for drug discovery struggle to bridge molecular, cellular, and tissue scales, relying on simple concatenation or attention mechanisms that lack principled scale-transition frameworks. This gap critically limits in silico screening and therapeutic design for emerging modalities (RNA therapeutics, cell/gene therapies). Existing multimodal foundation models cannot predict tissue-level drug responses from molecular features, requiring expensive wet-lab validation. A physics-inspired approach using renormalization group (RG) theory—which naturally handles scale transitions in physical systems—offers a novel solution by learning biologically-meaningful coarse-graining rules.

# Main Idea
We propose BioRenorm, a multimodal foundation model incorporating learnable RG operators that discover data-driven scale-transition rules from molecular→cellular→tissue levels. The core innovation applies statistical physics RG theory to biological hierarchies, using pathway-aware pooling (KEGG/Reactome priors) and conservation constraints (information/mass balance) to ensure biologically plausible transformations. Skip connections with learnable gating prevent error amplification across scales. Multi-stage training addresses data heterogeneity: separate pretraining per scale, pairwise RG learning, then end-to-end fine-tuning. We test on PRISM organoid drug screening data, predicting tissue responses from molecular features. Expected outcomes: ≥0.7 correlation (≥10% improvement over hierarchical transformers), ≥30% pathway alignment for interpretability, and 90% cost reduction in drug screening. This enables mechanistic insights into conserved biological modules and accelerates therapeutic discovery.
