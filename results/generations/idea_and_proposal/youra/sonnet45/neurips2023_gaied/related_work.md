## Related Work

**Related Papers**

1. **Title**: From Passive Tool to Socio-cognitive Teammate: A Conceptual Framework for Agentic AI in Human-AI Collaborative Learning (Yan, L., 2025)
   - **Authors**: L. Yan
   - **Summary**: Proposes APCP framework with 4-level AI agency model (Adaptive Instrument → Proactive Assistant → Co-Learner → Peer Collaborator) for human-AI collaborative learning. Provides theoretical foundation for AI role spectrum that this hypothesis operationalizes.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Deep Knowledge Tracing (Piech, C. et al., 2015)
   - **Authors**: C. Piech et al.
   - **Summary**: LSTM-based student modeling from interaction sequences achieving 75-85% knowledge state prediction accuracy. Enables real-time competency assessment for ZPD boundary estimation.
   - **Year**: 2015

3. **Title**: The Grey Area: A Computational Approach to Model the Zone of Proximal Development (Chounta, I. et al., 2017)
   - **Authors**: I. Chounta et al.
   - **Summary**: Validates that ZPD can be computationally estimated from student performance data, establishing feasibility of computational ZPD modeling.
   - **Year**: 2017

4. **Title**: Artificial Intelligence Revolution for Enhancing Modern Education Using Zone of Proximal Development Approach (Al-Hamadi, D. & Yousif, J., 2025)
   - **Authors**: D. Al-Hamadi, J. Yousif
   - **Summary**: Demonstrates AI-ZPD integration improves personalized learning through adaptive, data-informed feedback, providing evidence for measurable learning improvements from ZPD-informed AI systems.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: AI-Powered Educational Agents: Opportunities, Innovations, and Ethical Challenges (Córdova-Esparza, D., 2025)
   - **Authors**: D. Córdova-Esparza
   - **Summary**: Systematic review finding hybrid human-AI workflows outperform fully autonomous tutors, validating graduated autonomy approach and supporting ZPD-AI's Tutor→Peer transition design.
   - **Year**: 2025

6. **Title**: Agentic AI as Undercover Teammates: Argumentative Knowledge Construction in Hybrid Human-AI Collaborative Learning (Yan, L. et al., 2025)
   - **Authors**: L. Yan et al.
   - **Summary**: Shows epistemic adequacy (reasoning quality) is more important than participation volume for AI value in learning, informing competency assessment metrics to focus on reasoning quality over interaction frequency.
   - **Year**: 2025

7. **Title**: A Design Trajectory Map of Human-AI Collaborative Reinforcement Learning Systems (Li, Z., 2024)
   - **Authors**: Z. Li
   - **Summary**: Design pattern taxonomy for CRL systems from collaborative levels and capabilities perspectives, providing architectural guidance for implementing different AI collaboration levels.
   - **Year**: 2024

8. **Title**: Zone of Proximal Development in Online Learning (Mulqueeny, K. et al., 2015)
   - **Authors**: K. Mulqueeny et al.
   - **Summary**: Computational estimation of ZPD in MOOCs using performance data, establishing precedent for ZPD boundary estimation from digital learning traces.
   - **Year**: 2015

9. **Title**: Generative AI for Programming Education: Benchmarking ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Human Tutors (Phung, T. et al., 2023)
   - **Authors**: T. Phung et al.
   - **Summary**: GPT-4 approaches human tutor performance across multiple educational scenarios, representing state-of-the-art fixed-role LLM tutoring as a baseline comparison.
   - **Year**: 2023

10. **Title**: Adaptive Learning Systems Powered by Generative AI and Large Language Models (Iqbal, A. & Kene, D., 2026)
    - **Authors**: A. Iqbal, D. Kene
    - **Summary**: Review of LLM-based personalization focusing on content adaptation, predictive analytics, and ITS architectures. Represents content-adaptive systems baseline.
    - **Year**: 2026

11. **Title**: Mind in Society: Development of Higher Psychological Processes (Vygotsky, L., 1978)
    - **Authors**: L. Vygotsky
    - **Summary**: Original ZPD theory establishing that optimal learning occurs when challenge matches current ability plus support. Provides core pedagogical principle for the entire framework design.
    - **Year**: 1978

12. **Title**: Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral Participation (Lave, J. & Wenger, E., 1991)
    - **Authors**: J. Lave, E. Wenger
    - **Summary**: Theory that novices learn through gradually increasing participation with expert guidance, informing the Tutor→Peer transition pathway as graduated autonomy with competency growth.
    - **Year**: 1991

13. **Title**: Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller, J., 1988)
    - **Authors**: J. Sweller
    - **Summary**: Learning effectiveness depends on matching instructional load to working memory capacity, explaining why ZPD-calibrated scaffolding improves outcomes through cognitive load optimization.
    - **Year**: 1988

14. **Title**: Meta-analysis on Intelligent Tutoring Systems (Ritter et al., 2007)
    - **Authors**: Ritter et al.
    - **Summary**: Meta-analysis finding adaptive content systems achieve ~12-15% learning gains over non-adaptive instruction, providing benchmark for adaptive learning systems.
    - **Year**: 2007

15. **Title**: The Relative Effectiveness of Human Tutoring, Intelligent Tutoring Systems, and Other Tutoring Systems (VanLehn, 2011)
    - **Authors**: VanLehn
    - **Summary**: Meta-analysis showing Intelligent Tutoring Systems produce ~20-25% gains versus traditional instruction, establishing benchmark for ITS performance.
    - **Year**: 2011

**Key Challenges**

1. **Gap in Pedagogically-Grounded Human-AI Collaboration Models**: Current AI tutoring systems lack operationalized, empirically-validated collaboration protocols that translate educational psychology theories (like ZPD) into algorithmic frameworks. Most systems adapt content difficulty but not collaboration style.

2. **Fixed AI Roles Cause Competency Mismatches**: Static AI tutoring systems (always-Tutor or always-Peer) create over-scaffolding for advanced learners (reducing autonomy) and under-scaffolding for struggling learners (causing cognitive overload).

3. **Bridging 45-Year Theory-Implementation Gap**: Vygotsky's ZPD theory (1978) has not been computationally operationalized for dynamic AI agency adaptation in collaborative learning systems until now.

4. **Lack of Dynamic Collaboration Style Adaptation**: Existing adaptive learning systems adjust what students learn (content/problem difficulty) but not how the AI collaborates with them (scaffolding intensity and interaction style).

5. **Cold-Start Problem in Student Modeling**: Initial learning sessions lack sufficient data for accurate competency estimation, requiring default strategies until sufficient interaction history accumulates.

6. **Maintaining LLM Persona Consistency**: Ensuring distinct and consistent Tutor versus Peer personas through prompt engineering alone requires validation, as inconsistent AI behavior undermines trust and effectiveness.

7. **Transition Cost Uncertainty**: Unknown whether students adapt smoothly to dynamically changing AI behavior or experience confusion and loss of trust during role transitions.

8. **Single-Domain Generalization**: Most computational ZPD approaches validated in structured domains (mathematics, programming) with unclear generalization to ill-structured domains (creative writing, philosophy).
