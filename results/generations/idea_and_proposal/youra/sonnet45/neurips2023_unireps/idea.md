# Title
Adaptive Alignment Budget: Meta-Learning Optimal Representation Alignment from Dataset Characteristics

# Motivation
Neural models across biological and artificial systems develop similar representations when exposed to similar stimuli, enabling powerful applications like model merging, multimodal learning, and transfer learning. However, recent evidence shows alignment is not universally beneficial—excessive alignment wastes computation and degrades performance when modalities are redundant or tasks require discrimination. Current approaches rely on fixed hyperparameters or manual tuning, lacking principled methods to determine optimal alignment strength. This creates a critical gap: we need automated frameworks that predict when and how much to align representations based on dataset properties.

# Main Idea
We propose the Adaptive Alignment Budget (AAB) framework, which treats representation alignment as constrained resource allocation. Using first-order meta-learning (Reptile), AAB learns a policy network that predicts optimal alignment budget B ∈ [0,1] from dataset characteristics: modality similarity (deconfounded CKA), information redundancy (mutual information), and task structure (label entropy, class separability). The policy allocates representation capacity between aligned components (exploiting shared structure) and unique components (preserving modality-specific information) via differentiable gating: h = B·h_aligned + (1-B)·h_unique. Meta-trained on 50 diverse tasks spanning multimodal learning, model merging, and transfer learning, the policy generalizes across applications. We predict AAB will outperform fixed-budget baselines by ≥3% accuracy, with budget inversely correlating with redundancy (high MI → low B), validated through 12-task evaluation with rigorous statistical testing.
