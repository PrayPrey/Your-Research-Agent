## 1. Research Questions

### Primary Research Question
How can we develop unified models that simultaneously perform reasoning (e.g., QA, dialogue) and decision-making (e.g., planning and control) to tackle the challenges brought by open-world environments, where agents must operate in highly diverse, dynamic, and fully interactive settings requiring continual learning and adaptation?

### Detailed Research Questions
1. How do humans interleave reasoning and decision-making, what are the benefits, and what can machines learn from these mechanisms?
2. How can we build a model that unifies reasoning and decision-making capabilities for open-world environments?
3. How can we develop principled reasoning systems that enable AI agents to plan in previously unseen scenarios?
4. How does prior knowledge play a role in reasoning and decision-making in open-world environments, and how is new knowledge acquired during agent operation?
5. How can we achieve open-world reasoning and decision-making with minimal supervision and human feedback?

## 8. Research Gaps

### User Input Recall

**Primary Research Question:**
How can we develop unified models that simultaneously perform reasoning (e.g., QA, dialogue) and decision-making (e.g., planning and control) to tackle the challenges brought by open-world environments, where agents must operate in highly diverse, dynamic, and fully interactive settings requiring continual learning and adaptation?

**Key Requirements from Workshop Context:**
1. Synergizing reasoning and decision-making (not just one or the other)
2. Operating in open-world environments (diverse, dynamic, fully interactive)
3. Continual learning and adaptation capabilities
4. Handling novelties and unknowns
5. Minimal supervision and human feedback

### Identified Gaps

#### Gap 1: Principled Integration of Reasoning and Decision-Making at the Architectural Level

**Current State:** Existing approaches either emphasize reasoning (LLM-based agents with CoT) or decision-making (RL-based agents) but lack principled architectural frameworks for **tight integration** where reasoning dynamically influences action selection and vice versa in real-time.

**Missing Piece:** A unified architecture that enables bidirectional information flow between reasoning modules (e.g., symbolic planners, neural reasoners) and decision-making modules (e.g., policy networks, action selectors) with explicit mechanisms for: (1) reasoning-to-action translation, (2) action-to-reasoning feedback, and (3) joint optimization.

**Potential Impact:** HIGH - Core to the research question. A principled integration framework would enable agents to leverage abstract reasoning for long-horizon planning while using fine-grained decision-making for reactive control, crucial for complex open-world scenarios.

#### Gap 2: Scalable Continual Learning Without Catastrophic Forgetting in Dynamic Open-World Settings

**Current State:** Current continual learning methods either (1) require task boundaries and don't handle truly open-ended environments, or (2) suffer from catastrophic forgetting when adapting to novelties, especially when novelties accumulate over time.

**Missing Piece:** Scalable continual learning frameworks that can: (1) detect and characterize novelties autonomously, (2) incrementally integrate new knowledge without forgetting previous knowledge, (3) handle evolving OOD categories, and (4) operate without human supervision for labeling or task segmentation.

**Potential Impact:** HIGH - Essential for long-term deployment. Without robust continual learning, agents cannot adapt to the ever-changing nature of open-world environments and will degrade over time.
