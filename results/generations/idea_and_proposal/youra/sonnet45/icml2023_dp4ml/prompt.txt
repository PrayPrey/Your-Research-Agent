# Adaptive Dual-Coordinate Optimization via Kronecker-Factored Information Geometry

## Motivation

Natural gradient methods using Fisher information have shown promise in deep learning, but they treat the Fisher metric merely as a preconditioner rather than exploiting the full geometric structure of parameter spaces. Information geometry reveals that neural networks define dually-flat manifolds with complementary primal (θ) and dual (η) coordinate systems connected by Legendre transformations. However, computing exact dual coordinates is intractable for deep networks. This research addresses whether tractable Kronecker-factored approximations can enable practical dual-coordinate optimization that adapts to local landscape geometry, potentially accelerating convergence beyond current second-order methods.

## Main Idea

We propose **Kronecker-Factored Dual Coordinate Natural Gradient (KF-DCNG)**, which explicitly computes approximate dual coordinates η via K-FAC's Fisher approximation (F ≈ A ⊗ G) and adaptively switches between primal and dual gradient descent based on local curvature. The core mechanism: in high-curvature regions (large eigenvalues of F), dual coordinates capture complementary geometric information that primal coordinates miss, providing better descent directions. We test this by comparing adaptive switching against K-FAC (always-dual) and SGD (always-primal) on CIFAR-10/ImageNet. Success requires: (1) 5-15% iteration reduction beyond K-FAC, (2) positive correlation between curvature and dual-coordinate advantage, and (3) acceptable computational overhead (<7× vs SGD). This formalizes the first explicit dual-coordinate framework for neural optimization.

### 1.3 Causal Mechanism

**Causal Chain:**

1. **Fisher Information Geometry** → Neural networks define probability distributions p(y|x,θ), which induce Fisher information metric F(θ) = 𝔼[∇log p ∇log p^T] on parameter space

2. **Dual Coordinates via Legendre Transform** → Fisher metric defines dually-flat structure where dual coordinates η = ∇ψ(θ) (ψ = log-partition function) are connected to primal θ via Legendre transformation

3. **Kronecker Approximation for Tractability** → Exact Fisher is O(p²) intractable; K-FAC approximates F_l ≈ A_l ⊗ G_l per layer, reducing complexity to O(p)

4. **Dual Gradient Computation** → Dual gradient g_η = F^(-1) g_θ (natural gradient) uses different geometric metric than primal gradient g_θ

5. **Curvature-Dependent Geometry** → In high-curvature regions (large eigenvalues of F), dual coordinates capture complementary information that primal coordinates miss

6. **Adaptive Switching** → Algorithm selects coordinate system based on local curvature: primal when flat (low curvature), dual when sharp (high curvature)

7. **Improved Convergence** → Using favorable coordinate system in each region leads to faster convergence overall

**Evidence for Causal Links:**

- **Link 1→2**: Established by Amari (1998) "Natural Gradient Works Efficiently in Learning" - Fisher metric is the natural Riemannian metric on statistical manifolds, connected to dual coordinates via information geometry (Amari 2016)

- **Link 2→3**: Validated by Martens & Grosse (2015) K-FAC paper - Kronecker factorization preserves essential geometric structure while reducing computational complexity from O(p²) to O(p)

- **Link 3→4**: K-FAC computes natural gradient (dual gradient) efficiently; this is well-established in the natural gradient literature

- **Link 5→6**: Novel hypothesis component - requires empirical validation

- **Link 6→7**: Conditional on Link 5→6 holding; if dual coordinates help in high-curvature regions, then adaptive switching should combine benefits

- Tasks with non-trivial loss landscape curvature (non-convex optimization problems)
- Architectures: MLPs, CNNs, small-to-medium Transformers

**Does NOT Apply To:**
- Networks without probabilistic interpretation (e.g., non-differentiable operations, discrete outputs without relaxation)
- Extremely large networks (billion+ parameters) where even K-FAC becomes intractable without further approximations
- Tasks where first-order methods already converge trivially (e.g., strongly convex objectives)

**Known Limitations:**
- **Approximation Quality**: K-FAC assumes layer-wise independence (Kronecker factorization), which may be violated in highly coupled architectures (e.g., residual connections, attention mechanisms)

- **Incremental Benefit Uncertainty**: May provide only modest improvement over K-FAC alone (5-20% iteration reduction) rather than transformative gains
- **Memory Overhead**: Storing both primal and dual coordinate representations increases memory footprint by ~2x compared to standard SGD

**Boundary Conditions:**
- **Lower Bound**: On strongly convex or near-convex objectives (e.g., linear regression), dual coordinates likely provide no benefit (natural gradient = gradient descent)
- **Upper Bound**: On pathologically ill-conditioned problems (condition number > 10^6), K-FAC approximation may be too coarse for dual coordinates to be accurate

**SH1 (Existence): Dual Coordinates Can Be Computed**

*Can we actually compute approximate dual coordinates for neural networks using K-FAC?*

**Sub-hypothesis**: Given a neural network with L layers and K-FAC approximation F_l ≈ A_l ⊗ G_l per layer, dual coordinates η_l can be computed via η_l = F_l^(-1) θ_l with O(p) complexity (same as K-FAC natural gradient).

**Verification Approach**:
- Implement dual coordinate computation using existing K-FAC infrastructure (PyTorch K-FAC library)
- Measure reconstruction error: ‖θ - θ̂‖ where θ̂ = η_to_θ(η_to_θ^(-1)(θ))
- Compare to exact Fisher dual coordinates on small networks (2-layer MLP on MNIST) where exact computation is tractable
- **Success Criterion**: Reconstruction error < 10% of parameter norm ‖θ‖

**SH2 (Mechanism): Dual Coordinates Improve Optimization in High-Curvature Regions**

*Do dual coordinates actually provide better descent directions when curvature is high?*

**Sub-hypothesis**: In layers/regions where local curvature (measured by λ_max of K-FAC approximation) exceeds threshold τ, dual gradients g_η = F^(-1) g_θ will result in larger loss reduction per step than primal gradients g_θ.

**Verification Approach**:
- Instrument training to log: (1) local curvature λ_max per layer, (2) loss reduction Δℓ from primal vs. dual gradient steps
- Compute correlation between λ_max and (Δℓ_dual - Δℓ_primal)
- Perform A/B testing: force dual updates in high-curvature regions vs. random regions
- **Success Criterion**: Positive correlation (r > 0.5) between λ_max and relative dual gradient advantage; p < 0.05

**SH3 (Comparison): Adaptive Switching Outperforms Fixed Strategies**

*Does adaptive switching between primal and dual coordinates beat always-primal or always-dual?*

**Sub-hypothesis**: KF-DCNG with adaptive switching based on curvature threshold τ will converge faster than: (1) pure K-FAC (always-dual), (2) pure SGD (always-primal), (3) random switching (no curvature criterion).
