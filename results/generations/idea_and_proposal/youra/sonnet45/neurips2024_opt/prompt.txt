# Title
Scaling Law-Guided Architecture-Optimization Co-Design for Compute-Optimal LLM Training

# Motivation
Training large language models costs millions of dollars, yet current approaches optimize model size/data (via Chinchilla scaling laws) separately from architecture and hyperparameter tuning. This sequential optimization misses critical interactions: architectural choices (width/depth ratios) determine FLOPs per training step, while optimization hyperparameters (learning rates, batch sizes) control convergence speed. These coupled factors jointly determine compute efficiency, but no framework exists to optimize them together. Bridging this gap could reduce training costs by 5-10% while improving model quality, saving both money and environmental impact.

# Main Idea
We extend Chinchilla's compute-optimal framework from 2D (model parameters N, training tokens D) to 4D by adding architectural ratios (α: width/depth, attention configuration) and optimization hyperparameters (β: learning rate schedules, batch size). Our two-stage approach trains a Gaussian Process surrogate on 100-200 small-scale models (100M-1B parameters) using Chinchilla power-law priors and recent hyperparameter transfer rules, then performs joint Bayesian optimization over (N,D,α,β) constrained by compute budget C=6ND. The core mechanism: joint optimization exploits coupling between architecture efficiency and optimization effectiveness to discover Pareto-optimal configurations that sequential approaches cannot access. We predict ≥5% validation loss reduction versus Chinchilla+grid-search baselines at fixed compute (1e19-1e21 FLOPs), validated across three scales with rigorous statistical testing. This delivers actionable compute-optimal configurations and unifies architecture search, hyperparameter optimization, and scaling laws into one framework.

### 1.3 Causal Mechanism

**4-Step Causal Chain:**

**Step 1: Surrogate Model Construction**
Meta-model L(N,D,α,β) is trained on small-scale configurations (100M-1B parameters, 1B-100B tokens) using Gaussian Process regression with Chinchilla power-law priors (L ∝ N^(-a) × D^(-b)). The GP incorporates Mlodozeniec 2025 Complete^(d) HP transfer rules as constraints. This produces a surrogate that predicts validation loss for arbitrary (α,β) configurations at target compute scales with R² > 0.9 accuracy.

**Step 2: Joint Optimization Search**
The surrogate enables multi-objective Bayesian optimization over the joint space (α, β, N, D) constrained by C = 6ND. The optimizer identifies configurations where architectural efficiency gains (e.g., wider shallow models use fewer FLOPs per forward pass) compensate for optimization costs (e.g., may require more training steps due to different convergence properties). This discovers Pareto-optimal (N,D,α,β) combinations that sequential approaches (which fix α and β while optimizing N/D) cannot find.

**Step 3: Compute Allocation Exploitation**
Pareto-optimal configurations allocate compute budget C between 'model capacity' (N, determined by α architectural choices) and 'training efficiency' (convergence steps, determined by β optimization choices) based on marginal returns on loss reduction. For instance, a wider shallow model (w/d=1.5) with higher LR may achieve L=2.5 at C=1e20, while standard architecture with lower LR achieves L=2.7 at same C, because the architectural choice enables more efficient use of compute.

**Step 4: Realized Performance Improvement**
When the Pareto-optimal configuration predicted by the meta-model is trained to completion at target scale, it realizes ≥5% validation loss reduction compared to baselines: (a) Chinchilla-optimal N/D with standard architecture + 5×5 LR/BS grid search, (b) Chinchilla-optimal N/D with standard architecture + 50-trial Bayesian HPO. The improvement stems from accessing configurations that sequential optimization cannot explore (different α enables different N/D allocations).

### 1.4 Key Assumptions

1. **Power-Law Extension Assumption**
   - **Statement:** Chinchilla's power-law relationships (L ∝ N^(-a) × D^(-b)) extend to architectural dimensions: L(N,D,α,β) where α = (width/depth ratio, attention heads) and β = (LR, batch size).
   - **Evidence:** Mlodozeniec 2025 shows HP transfer rules exist across width, depth, batch, duration, suggesting systematic scaling relationships. Jiang 2024 ADO demonstrates per-domain scaling laws work without proxy models.
   - **Consequence if violated:** If architectural dimensions exhibit discontinuities or non-power-law behavior, surrogate model predictions fail (R² < 0.9) → Joint optimization selects suboptimal configs → Performance worse than baseline → Hypothesis falsified in Stage 1.

2. **Surrogate Model Accuracy Assumption**
   - **Statement:** Gaussian Process with Chinchilla priors and Complete^(d) HP transfer constraints can predict L(N,D,α,β) with R² > 0.9 using ~100-200 small-scale training runs.
   - **Evidence:** ADO (Jiang 2024) achieves comparable performance to extensive experiments while maintaining computational efficiency. GP with informed priors is sample-efficient (standard ML practice).
   - **Consequence if violated:** If surrogate R² < 0.9, optimization search operates on noisy predictions → Selects configurations that don't transfer to target scale → Higher loss than baseline → Wasted compute in Stage 2.

3. **Loss Landscape Smoothness Assumption**
   - **Statement:** The validation loss landscape across (α, β) space is sufficiently smooth (no abrupt discontinuities, manageable local optima) to enable gradient-free Bayesian optimization.
   - **Evidence:** Hirose 2021 NAS-HPO-Bench-II shows smooth interdependencies in CNN architecture-HP space (192K configs). Neural architecture loss landscapes are generally smooth at architectural hyperparameter level (vs weight level).
   - **Consequence if violated:** If landscape is highly non-smooth with many local optima, Bayesian optimization gets stuck → Fails to find Pareto-optimal configs → No better than random search → Null result (neither confirms nor refutes core mechanism).

4. **Compute Accounting Assumption**
   - **Statement:** C = 6ND (Chinchilla's formula) accurately accounts for total FLOPs across different architectural choices (wider shallow vs narrower deep models have same FLOPs for same N and D).
   - **Evidence:** Standard transformer FLOPs calculation; validated in Chinchilla paper for GPT-style decoders.
   - **Consequence if violated:** If wider architectures incur memory bandwidth bottlenecks not captured in FLOPs (e.g., 2x wall-clock time for same theoretical FLOPs), compute budget violated → Unfair comparison (SLGA uses more actual compute) → Artificial performance gain → False positive.

### 1.5 Scope & Boundaries

**Where Hypothesis Applies:**
- **Model Family:** Transformer-based LLMs (GPT-style decoder-only) with standard attention mechanisms
- **Compute Scale:** 1e19 - 1e21 FLOPs (approximately 100M-10B parameter models)
- **Training Regime:** Pretraining from scratch on web-scale text corpora (C4 or similar)
- **Architectural Dimensions:** Width/depth ratio, attention head configuration (continuous/discrete combinations)
- **Optimization Dimensions:** Learning rate schedules (cosine, linear D2Z, constant+cooldown), batch sizes (256-2048 tokens)

**Where Hypothesis Does NOT Apply:**
- **Fine-tuning scenarios:** Mechanism assumes pretraining; fine-tuning has different loss landscapes and optimal HPs
- **Very small models (<100M):** Below Chinchilla scaling regime; different scaling laws may apply
- **Very large models (>100B):** Hardware constraints (memory, interconnect bandwidth) dominate; C = 6ND may be insufficient accounting
- **Non-transformer architectures:** SSMs (Mamba), hybrid models, or fundamentally different architectures may not follow same scaling relationships
- **Extreme architectural configurations:** Very wide shallow (w/d > 5) or very narrow deep (w/d < 0.1) models may hit numerical instabilities
- **Fixed-schedule constraints:** Scenarios where LR schedule must be fixed (e.g., continual learning) cannot benefit from β optimization

**Known Limitations:**
1. Two-stage approach incurs upfront cost: ~1e18-1e19 FLOPs for Stage 1 surrogate training before target-scale experiments
2. Assumes AdamW optimizer (β optimization limited to LR/batch size); different optimizers (SGD, Lion, Sophia) may have different joint optimization landscapes
3. Validation loss is proxy for downstream task performance; hypothesis does not directly optimize for task-specific metrics
4. Assumes homogeneous compute (single GPU type); heterogeneous hardware may violate C = 6ND assumptions

### 1.8 Statistical Verification Design

**Experimental Design:**
- **Type:** Between-subjects comparison with 3 conditions (SLGA, Baseline 1, Baseline 2) × 3 compute scales (1e19, 1e20, 1e21 FLOPs)
- **Replication:** 3 random seeds per condition per scale (27 total training runs for main experiment)
- **Randomization:** Seeds control for: (1) weight initialization (Kaiming uniform), (2) data shuffling order, (3) Bayesian optimization initial points

**Statistical Tests:**
1. **Primary analysis:** One-tailed paired t-test comparing SLGA vs best baseline at each compute scale
   - H₀: μ_SLGA ≥ μ_baseline (no improvement)
   - H₁: μ_SLGA < μ_baseline (5% improvement)
   - Significance level: α = 0.05
   - Power: 1-β = 0.80 (requires effect size d ≥ 0.5 for n=3 seeds, may need n=5 if variance high)

2. **Secondary analysis:** Two-way ANOVA (method × compute scale) to test scaling consistency
   - Main effect of method (SLGA vs baselines)
   - Interaction effect (method × scale)
   - If interaction significant (p < 0.05), improvement is scale-dependent → Hypothesis partially refuted

**Confound Controls:**
- **Optimizer:** Fixed to AdamW with β₁=0.9, β₂=0.95, ε=1e-8, weight decay=0.1 across all conditions
- **Dataset:** Same C4 corpus, same train/val/test split (controlled by seed)
- **Hardware:** Same GPU type (A100 80GB) to control for hardware-specific optimizations
- **Evaluation protocol:** Same validation set (10M tokens), same perplexity calculation (cross-entropy loss)

**Reporting:**
- **Effect size:** Report Cohen's d and % loss reduction with 95% CI
- **Variance:** Report mean ± std dev across seeds for transparency
- **Pareto front:** Visualize (loss, compute) trade-off curves for SLGA vs baselines
- **Surrogate accuracy:** Report R² and prediction error distribution from Stage 1

Explains why sequential optimization (Chinchilla N/D → then tune α,β) is suboptimal: misses configurations where different α enables better N/D allocation.

**Methodological Contributions:**
1. **Two-Stage Surrogate Modeling Approach:** Novel methodology for meta-scaling law discovery: (a) Stage 1 trains GP surrogate on small-scale models (100M-1B) with Chinchilla priors and Complete^(d) HP transfer constraints, (b) Stage 2 transfers predictions to target scale with targeted refinement.
