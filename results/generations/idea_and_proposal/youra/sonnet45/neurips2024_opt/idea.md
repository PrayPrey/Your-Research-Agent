# Title
Scaling Law-Guided Architecture-Optimization Co-Design for Compute-Optimal LLM Training

# Motivation
Training large language models costs millions of dollars, yet current approaches optimize model size/data (via Chinchilla scaling laws) separately from architecture and hyperparameter tuning. This sequential optimization misses critical interactions: architectural choices (width/depth ratios) determine FLOPs per training step, while optimization hyperparameters (learning rates, batch sizes) control convergence speed. These coupled factors jointly determine compute efficiency, but no framework exists to optimize them together. Bridging this gap could reduce training costs by 5-10% while improving model quality, saving both money and environmental impact.

# Main Idea
We extend Chinchilla's compute-optimal framework from 2D (model parameters N, training tokens D) to 4D by adding architectural ratios (α: width/depth, attention configuration) and optimization hyperparameters (β: learning rate schedules, batch size). Our two-stage approach trains a Gaussian Process surrogate on 100-200 small-scale models (100M-1B parameters) using Chinchilla power-law priors and recent hyperparameter transfer rules, then performs joint Bayesian optimization over (N,D,α,β) constrained by compute budget C=6ND. The core mechanism: joint optimization exploits coupling between architecture efficiency and optimization effectiveness to discover Pareto-optimal configurations that sequential approaches cannot access. We predict ≥5% validation loss reduction versus Chinchilla+grid-search baselines at fixed compute (1e19-1e21 FLOPs), validated across three scales with rigorous statistical testing. This delivers actionable compute-optimal configurations and unifies architecture search, hyperparameter optimization, and scaling laws into one framework.
