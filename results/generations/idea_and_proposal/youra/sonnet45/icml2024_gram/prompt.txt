# Title
Adaptive Physics-Informed Riemannian Neural Networks: Learning Optimal Geometries for PDE Solutions

# Motivation
Current Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) require specifying geometric structure a priori, limiting their effectiveness on complex physical systems where optimal coordinate systems are unknown. This creates a critical gap: while equivariant architectures preserve known symmetries and manifold learning discovers geometric structure, no framework jointly optimizes geometry and physics-informed representations. For turbulent flows, material deformations, and other complex PDEs, the "right" coordinate system can dramatically simplify governing equations—yet discovering it automatically remains unsolved. This research bridges three disconnected threads (equivariant neural networks, manifold learning, PINNs) to enable automatic discovery of physics-appropriate geometries.

# Main Idea
We propose API-RNN, a bi-level optimization framework that jointly learns (1) differentiable Riemannian manifold structure via neural implicit atlases and (2) physics-informed representations satisfying PDE constraints on that manifold. The outer loop discovers manifold parameters minimizing PDE complexity; the inner loop trains neural networks to satisfy physics constraints. A staged training protocol—unsupervised manifold initialization, frozen-manifold PINN training, then joint fine-tuning—ensures stability. Geometric regularization (Jacobian determinant, curvature bounds) prevents trivial solutions. We predict 15-25% PDE residual reduction and 20-30% better generalization versus standard PINNs on turbulent flow benchmarks, validated through three-tier testing: synthetic problems with known geometry, real-world turbulence data, and geometric consistency metrics. This enables automatic coordinate discovery where physical laws become mathematically simpler.
