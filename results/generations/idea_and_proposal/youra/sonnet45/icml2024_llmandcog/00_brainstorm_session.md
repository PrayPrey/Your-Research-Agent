## Starting Context

**Background:** Large Language Models (LLMs) have undoubtedly taken center stage in the AI revolution, showing impressive performance in a wide variety of tasks, including machine translation, standardized tests, and conversational chatbots. It is even more impressive to uncover that these models exhibit unpredictable capabilities in solving unseen tasks. This demonstration of emergent abilities, often credited to the scale of the parameters and data size in the case of LLMs, is being considered as the footprint of intelligence.

**Source Type:** Workshop CFP (Call for Papers) - ICML 2024

## Research Question Development

### Initial Question
How do Large Language Models compare to human cognitive systems in terms of cognitive abilities, and what are the fundamental principles that govern their capabilities and limitations?

### Refined Question
What is the current state of Large Language Models' cognitive abilities across core cognitive tasks (reasoning, navigation, planning, theory of mind), and what fundamental limits, architectural augmentations, and evaluation frameworks are needed to rigorously understand and advance their position in the landscape of intelligent systems?

### Detailed Sub-Questions

1. **Performance Assessment:** Where do LLMs currently stand in terms of performance on core cognitive tasks such as reasoning, navigation, planning, and theory of mind?

2. **Fundamental Limits:** What are the fundamental limits of language models with respect to cognitive abilities, and how do these limits relate to their architectural constraints?

3. **Augmented vs End-to-End:** How do LLMs fine-tuned on specific tasks end-to-end compare to augmented LLMs coupled with external modules in terms of cognitive task performance?

4. **Mechanistic Interpretability:** What are the similarities and differences between mechanistic interpretability approaches in AI and in neuroscience, and what do they reveal about the similarities and differences between LLMs and human brains?

5. **Benchmark Design:** How can we improve existing benchmarks and evaluation methods to rigorously assess cognitive abilities in LLMs?

6. **Multimodal and Multiagent Approaches:** Can multimodal and multiagent approaches address some of the current limits of LLMs with respect to cognitive tasks?
