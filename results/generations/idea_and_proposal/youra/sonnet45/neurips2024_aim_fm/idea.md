# FairLoRA-FL: Privacy-Preserving Fairness in Federated Medical Foundation Models via Subgroup-Adaptive Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning

## Motivation
Medical foundation models face a critical trilemma: achieving demographic fairness across patient subgroups, preserving privacy under federated hospital constraints, and maintaining computational efficiency. Existing approaches address these challenges in isolation—centralized fairness methods violate privacy by pooling sensitive data, while standard federated learning exhibits >20% accuracy gaps across age/gender subgroups. This creates unacceptable disparities in AI-assisted diagnosis for vulnerable populations, particularly in multi-hospital settings where data cannot be centralized due to privacy regulations.

## Main Idea
We hypothesize that training **K=5 subgroup-specific rank-16 LoRA adapters** per hospital on frozen medical foundation models, aggregated via FedProx with differential privacy (ε=8), achieves <10% fairness gap across demographic subgroups while maintaining accuracy within 5% of centralized baselines. The causal mechanism operates through four steps: (1) subgroup-specific adapters capture demographic medical patterns, (2) local differential privacy (ε_local=2) on demographic labels enables privacy-preserving patient routing to appropriate adapters, (3) FedProx aggregation (μ=0.01) stabilizes convergence of K parallel adapter sets despite hospital data heterogeneity, and (4) bi-level optimization enforces equalized odds fairness constraints. Validated on MIMIC-IV diagnosis and MedQA tasks across 8 simulated hospitals, this approach achieves Pareto-optimal fairness-privacy-efficiency trade-offs: 2MB communication overhead (75× smaller than full fine-tuning), ≥90% routing accuracy under privacy noise, and convergence within 100 federated rounds—enabling equitable, privacy-compliant medical AI deployment.
