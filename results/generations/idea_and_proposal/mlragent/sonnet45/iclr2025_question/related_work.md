1. **Title**: A Graph Signal Processing Framework for Hallucination Detection in Large Language Models (arXiv:2510.19117)
   - **Authors**: Valentin Noël
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces a spectral analysis framework that models transformer layers as dynamic graphs induced by attention mechanisms, with token embeddings serving as signals on these graphs. By applying graph signal processing techniques, the study defines diagnostics such as Dirichlet energy and spectral entropy to detect hallucinations in LLM outputs. Experiments across GPT architectures reveal distinct spectral patterns associated with factual statements versus various types of hallucinations, leading to a detector that achieves 88.75% accuracy, surpassing perplexity-based baselines.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Uncertainty Quantification of Large Language Models through Multi-Dimensional Responses (arXiv:2502.16820)
   - **Authors**: Tiejin Chen, Xiaoou Liu, Longchao Da, Jia Chen, Vagelis Papalexakis, Hua Wei
   - **Summary**: This work presents a multi-dimensional uncertainty quantification framework for LLMs that integrates semantic and knowledge-aware similarity analyses. By generating multiple responses and utilizing auxiliary LLMs to extract implicit knowledge, the authors construct similarity matrices and apply tensor decomposition to derive comprehensive uncertainty representations. This approach captures both semantic variations and factual consistency, leading to more accurate uncertainty quantification and improved identification of uncertain responses.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Uncertainty Quantification and Confidence Calibration in Large Language Models: A Survey (arXiv:2503.15850)
   - **Authors**: Xiaoou Liu, Tiejin Chen, Longchao Da, Chacha Chen, Zhen Lin, Hua Wei
   - **Summary**: This survey examines the challenges and methodologies associated with uncertainty quantification (UQ) and confidence calibration in LLMs. It introduces a taxonomy categorizing UQ methods based on computational efficiency and uncertainty dimensions, including input, reasoning, parameter, and prediction uncertainties. The paper evaluates existing techniques, assesses their real-world applicability, and identifies open challenges, emphasizing the need for scalable, interpretable, and robust UQ approaches to enhance LLM reliability.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Improving Uncertainty Quantification in Large Language Models via Semantic Embeddings (arXiv:2410.22685)
   - **Authors**: Yashvir S. Grewal, Edwin V. Bonilla, Thang D. Bui
   - **Summary**: This study proposes a novel approach to uncertainty quantification in LLMs by leveraging semantic embeddings. By capturing semantic similarities without relying on sequence likelihoods, the method reduces biases introduced by irrelevant words in responses. An amortized version models semantics as latent variables in a joint probabilistic model, allowing for efficient uncertainty estimation with a single forward pass. Experiments demonstrate that this embedding-based method provides more accurate and nuanced uncertainty quantification than traditional approaches.
   - **Year**: 2024

7. **Title**: HalluciBot: Is There No Such Thing as a Bad Question? (arXiv:2404.12535)
   - **Authors**: William Watson, Nicole Cho
   - **Summary**: HalluciBot introduces a model that predicts the probability of hallucination before generation for any query posed to an LLM. By employing a multi-agent Monte Carlo simulation with a query perturbator, the model estimates the likelihood of hallucination, enabling users to revise or cancel queries preemptively. This approach aims to reduce computational waste and enhance user accountability by providing insights into the propensity for hallucination in generated responses.

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Temporal Inconsistency Detection**: Existing hallucination detection methods often focus on single responses, lacking mechanisms to track and analyze inconsistencies that emerge over multiple conversational turns.

2. **Computational Efficiency**: Monitoring semantic coherence across extended dialogues requires significant computational resources, posing challenges for real-time applications.

3. **Scalability of Uncertainty Quantification**: Developing scalable methods for uncertainty quantification in LLMs is challenging due to the models' complexity and the need for efficient processing of large-scale data.

4. **Interpretability of Detection Mechanisms**: Providing interpretable explanations for detected hallucinations is crucial for user trust but remains difficult due to the opaque nature of LLMs' decision-making processes.

5. **Balancing Creativity and Accuracy**: Mitigating hallucinations without stifling the generative creativity of LLMs is a delicate balance that requires nuanced approaches to maintain model utility.
