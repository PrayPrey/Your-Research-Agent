1. **Title**: CoTAL: Human-in-the-Loop Prompt Engineering, Chain-of-Thought Reasoning, and Active Learning for Generalizable Formative Assessment Scoring (arXiv:2504.02323)
   - **Authors**: Clayton Cohn, Nicole Hutchins, Ashwin T S, Gautam Biswas
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces CoTAL, an approach that combines Evidence-Centered Design principles with human-in-the-loop prompt engineering and active learning to enhance GPT-4's performance in scoring formative assessments across various curricula. The method achieves up to a 24.5% improvement over non-prompt-engineered baselines and emphasizes the importance of teacher and student feedback in refining grading accuracy and explanation quality.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: A Chain-of-Thought Prompting Approach with LLMs for Evaluating Students' Formative Assessment Responses in Science (arXiv:2403.14565)
   - **Authors**: Clayton Cohn, Nicole Hutchins, Tuan Le, Gautam Biswas
   - **Summary**: This study explores the use of GPT-4 to score and explain short-answer assessments in K-12 Earth Science. By integrating few-shot and active learning with chain-of-thought reasoning in a human-in-the-loop framework, the approach successfully provides meaningful explanations for formative assessment responses, highlighting the potential of such techniques in automated grading for open-ended science assessments.
   - **Year**: 2024

3. **Title**: ELMES: An Automated Framework for Evaluating Large Language Models in Educational Scenarios (arXiv:2507.22947)
   - **Authors**: Shou'ang Wei, Xinyun Wang, Shuzhen Bi, Jian Chen, Ruijia Li, Bo Jiang, Xin Lin, Min Zhang, Yu Song, BingDong Li, Aimin Zhou, Hao Hao
   - **Summary**: ELMES is an open-source framework designed to assess LLMs in educational contexts. It features a modular architecture for creating dynamic, multi-agent dialogues and employs a hybrid evaluation engine to objectively quantify pedagogical metrics. The framework benchmarks state-of-the-art LLMs across scenarios like knowledge explanation and guided problem-solving, providing insights into their strengths and limitations in educational applications.

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Explainability and Trust**: Ensuring that automated scoring systems provide transparent and interpretable feedback that educators and students can trust remains a significant challenge.

2. **Curriculum Alignment**: Developing models that consistently align with diverse curriculum standards and rubrics across various educational contexts is complex.

3. **Generalizability**: Creating scoring approaches that generalize effectively across different subjects, question types, and student populations without extensive retraining is difficult.

4. **Human-in-the-Loop Integration**: Balancing automated processes with meaningful human input to refine scoring accuracy and feedback quality requires careful design and implementation.

5. **Evaluation Metrics**: Establishing robust, standardized metrics to assess the performance of automated scoring systems in educational settings is essential but challenging.
