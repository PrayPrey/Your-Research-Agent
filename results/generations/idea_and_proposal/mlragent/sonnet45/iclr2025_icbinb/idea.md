# Title
**Systematic Taxonomy of Deep Learning Failure Modes through Cross-Domain Meta-Analysis**

# Motivation
While individual deep learning failures are documented within isolated domains, there exists no systematic framework to categorize and compare failure modes across applications. This knowledge fragmentation prevents researchers from identifying recurrent patterns and root causes, leading to repeated mistakes across fields. A structured taxonomy would transform scattered negative results into actionable insights, accelerating progress by helping practitioners anticipate and mitigate common pitfalls before deployment.

# Main Idea
Develop a comprehensive taxonomy of deep learning failure modes by conducting a large-scale meta-analysis of real-world deployment failures across domains (healthcare, robotics, scientific discovery, etc.). 

**Methodology:**
1. Collect and curate failure cases from ICBINB submissions, literature, and industry reports
2. Apply qualitative coding techniques to identify recurring patterns in failure characteristics, contexts, and underlying causes
3. Construct a hierarchical taxonomy organizing failures by: (a) symptom manifestation (performance degradation, safety violations, ethical issues), (b) root cause category (data, model, deployment), and (c) domain-specific vs. universal factors
4. Validate taxonomy through expert surveys and case study applications

**Expected Outcomes:**
- A publicly available, standardized failure taxonomy with decision trees for diagnosis
- Identification of "universal" vs. domain-specific failure modes
- Predictive guidelines linking deployment contexts to high-risk failure categories
- Foundation for automated failure detection tools

**Impact:** Enable proactive risk assessment, inform curriculum development, and establish best practices for robust DL deployment.
