1. **Title**: SeSE: A Structural Information-Guided Uncertainty Quantification Framework for Hallucination Detection in LLMs (arXiv:2511.16275)
   - **Authors**: Xingtao Zhao, Hao Peng, Dingli Su, Xianghua Zeng, Chunyang Liu, Jinzhi Liao, Philip S. Yu
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces Semantic Structural Entropy (SeSE), a framework that quantifies the inherent semantic uncertainty of LLMs from a structural information perspective to detect hallucinations. It constructs a directed semantic graph to capture dependencies and defines SeSE as the structural entropy of the optimal semantic encoding tree, indicating higher uncertainty with higher SeSE values.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Geometric Uncertainty for Detecting and Correcting Hallucinations in LLMs (arXiv:2509.13813)
   - **Authors**: Edward Phillips, Sean Wu, Soheila Molaei, Danielle Belgrave, Anshul Thakur, David Clifton
   - **Summary**: The authors propose a geometric framework based on archetypal analysis of response embeddings to detect and correct hallucinations in LLMs. They introduce Geometric Volume for global uncertainty and Geometric Suspicion for local uncertainty, enabling hallucination reduction through preferential response selection.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Semantic Entropy Probes: Robust and Cheap Hallucination Detection in LLMs (arXiv:2406.15927)
   - **Authors**: Jannik Kossen, Jiatong Han, Muhammed Razzak, Lisa Schut, Shreshth Malik, Yarin Gal
   - **Summary**: This work presents Semantic Entropy Probes (SEPs), a method for uncertainty quantification in LLMs that approximates semantic entropy from hidden states of a single generation. SEPs are simple to train and do not require multiple model generations at test time, reducing computational overhead while maintaining high performance in hallucination detection.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Computational Overhead**: Many uncertainty quantification methods require multiple model generations or complex computations, leading to increased computational costs and latency.

2. **Granularity of Uncertainty Measurement**: Achieving fine-grained uncertainty quantification at the token or claim level remains challenging, especially in long-form text generation.

3. **Adaptive Calibration**: Developing adaptive confidence calibration mechanisms that can dynamically adjust to different tasks and contexts is complex and requires further research.

4. **Integration with Mitigation Strategies**: Effectively integrating uncertainty quantification with real-time mitigation strategies, such as retrieval-augmented generation or alternative decoding, is an ongoing challenge.

5. **Evaluation Metrics**: Establishing standardized and reliable evaluation metrics for assessing the effectiveness of uncertainty quantification methods in detecting and mitigating hallucinations is still an open issue.
