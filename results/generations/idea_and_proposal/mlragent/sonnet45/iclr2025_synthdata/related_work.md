Here is a literature review on the topic of adaptive synthetic-real data mixing via quality-aware curriculum learning, focusing on papers published between 2023 and 2025:

**1. Related Papers**

1. **Title**: Statistical Guarantees in Synthetic Data through Conformal Adversarial Generation (arXiv:2504.17058)
   - **Authors**: Rahul Vishwakarma
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces a framework that integrates conformal prediction methodologies into Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to provide distribution-free uncertainty quantification in synthetic data generation. The approach, termed Conformalized GAN (cGAN), offers enhanced calibration properties and produces synthetic data with provable statistical guarantees, making it suitable for high-stakes domains like healthcare and finance.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Debiasing Synthetic Data Generated by Deep Generative Models (arXiv:2411.04216)
   - **Authors**: Alexander Decruyenaere, Heidelinde Dehaene, Paloma Rabaey, Christiaan Polet, Johan Decruyenaere, Thomas Demeester, Stijn Vansteelandt
   - **Summary**: The authors address biases and imprecision in synthetic data generated by deep generative models (DGMs). They propose a strategy that targets synthetic data for specific analyses, enhancing convergence rates and facilitating the calculation of estimators with approximated large sample variances. This approach aims to improve the reliability of synthetic data in statistical inference.
   - **Year**: 2024

3. **Title**: Comprehensive Exploration of Synthetic Data Generation: A Survey (arXiv:2401.02524)
   - **Authors**: André Bauer, Simon Trapp, Michael Stenger, Robert Leppich, Samuel Kounev, Mark Leznik, Kyle Chard, Ian Foster
   - **Summary**: This survey reviews 417 synthetic data generation models over the past decade, providing an overview of model types, functionalities, and improvements. It identifies common attributes, offers a classification, and analyzes trends, revealing increased model performance and complexity, with neural network-based approaches prevailing, especially in computer vision.
   - **Year**: 2024

4. **Title**: Collapse or Thrive? Perils and Promises of Synthetic Data in a Self-Generating World (arXiv:2410.16713)
   - **Authors**: Joshua Kazdan, Rylan Schaeffer, Apratim Dey, Matthias Gerstgrasser, Rafael Rafailov, David L. Donoho, Sanmi Koyejo
   - **Summary**: The paper investigates the effects of generative models being pretrained on datasets containing data generated by earlier models. It examines different training workflows and their impact on model stability and performance, providing insights into the potential risks and benefits of using synthetic data in model training.
   - **Year**: 2024

5. **Title**: SK-VQA: Synthetic Knowledge Generation at Scale for Training Context-Augmented Multimodal LLMs (arXiv:2406.19593)
   - **Authors**: Xin Su, Man Luo, Kris W Pan, Tien Pei Chou, Vasudev Lal, Phillip Howard
   - **Summary**: This work presents SK-VQA, a large synthetic multimodal dataset containing over 2 million question-answer pairs requiring external knowledge. The dataset aims to adapt existing generative multimodal models for context-augmented generation, addressing the scarcity of naturally occurring data for training such systems.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: Synthetic Patient-Physician Dialogue Generation from Clinical Notes Using LLM (arXiv:2408.06285)
   - **Authors**: Trisha Das, Dina Albassam, Jimeng Sun
   - **Summary**: The authors propose SynDial, a method that utilizes large language models to generate high-quality synthetic patient-physician dialogues from clinical notes. This approach aims to address privacy concerns and the limited availability of real patient conversations for training medical dialogue systems.
   - **Year**: 2024

**2. Key Challenges**

1. **Quality Assessment of Synthetic Data**: Developing reliable metrics to evaluate the realism, diversity, and task relevance of synthetic data remains a significant challenge.

2. **Adaptive Data Mixing Strategies**: Creating dynamic methods that effectively balance synthetic and real data during training to optimize model performance is complex and underexplored.

3. **Bias and Fairness**: Ensuring that synthetic data does not introduce or amplify biases present in the original data is crucial for maintaining fairness in model outcomes.

4. **Domain Generalization**: Addressing the domain gap between synthetic and real-world data to ensure models trained on mixed datasets generalize well across different domains is a persistent issue.

5. **Privacy and Ethical Considerations**: Balancing the benefits of synthetic data in preserving privacy with ethical concerns and potential misuse requires careful consideration and regulation.
