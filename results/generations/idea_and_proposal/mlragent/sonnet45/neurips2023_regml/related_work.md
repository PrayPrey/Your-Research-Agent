1. **Title**: Metanetworks as Regulatory Operators: Learning to Edit for Requirement Compliance (arXiv:2512.15469)
   - **Authors**: Ioannis Kalogeropoulos, Giorgos Bouritsas, Yannis Panagakis
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces a framework where a graph metanetwork learns to edit neural networks to meet various requirements, such as regulatory compliance, fairness, and computational constraints. The metanetwork is trained to enforce these requirements while preserving the model's utility, offering an efficient alternative to traditional post-processing or retraining methods.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: On Responsible Machine Learning Datasets with Fairness, Privacy, and Regulatory Norms (arXiv:2310.15848)
   - **Authors**: Surbhi Mittal, Kartik Thakral, Richa Singh, Mayank Vatsa, Tamar Glaser, Cristian Canton Ferrer, Tal Hassner
   - **Summary**: The authors propose a framework to evaluate machine learning datasets through the lens of fairness, privacy, and regulatory compliance. They analyze 60 datasets and find that none are entirely free from issues related to these aspects, highlighting the need for improved dataset documentation and construction practices.
   - **Year**: 2023

3. **Title**: Machine Learning-Based Cloud Computing Compliance Process Automation (arXiv:2502.16344)
   - **Authors**: Yuqing Wang, Xiao Yang
   - **Summary**: This study presents a machine learning framework to automate compliance processes in cloud computing, addressing challenges like resource-intensive manual reviews and delayed risk identification. The framework integrates technologies such as BERT-based document processing and anomaly detection, significantly reducing compliance process duration and manual effort.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Machine Learning-Driven Convergence Analysis in Multijurisdictional Compliance Using BERT and K-Means Clustering (arXiv:2502.10413)
   - **Authors**: Raj Sonani, Lohalekar Prayas
   - **Summary**: The paper explores adaptive compliance by comparing regulations like GDPR and CCPA using natural language processing techniques. It identifies areas of overlap and divergence, providing strategies for better enforcement across different jurisdictions and discussing challenges in applying NLP to legal texts.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: Interpretability, Then What? Editing Machine Learning Models to Reflect Human Knowledge and Values (arXiv:2206.15465)
   - **Authors**: Zijie J. Wang, Alex Kale, Harsha Nori, Duen Horng Chau, Mihaela Vorvoreanu, Jennifer Wortman Vaughan, Peter Stella, Mark E. Nunnally, Rich Caruana
   - **Summary**: This work introduces GAM Changer, an interactive system that enables domain experts to edit Generalized Additive Models (GAMs) to align model behaviors with human knowledge and values. The tool facilitates the identification and correction of undesirable patterns in models, enhancing their interpretability and trustworthiness.
   - **Year**: 2022

6. **Title**: Global AI Governance in Healthcare: A Cross-Jurisdictional Regulatory Analysis (arXiv:2406.08695)
   - **Authors**: Attrayee Chakraborty, Mandar Karhade
   - **Summary**: The authors analyze AI regulations in healthcare across 14 jurisdictions, comparing them to WHO's ethical principles. They propose solutions for international harmonization of AI regulations and examine requirements for regulating generative AI, using China and Singapore as case studies.
   - **Year**: 2024

7. **Title**: Meta-Learning Adversarial Bandits (arXiv:2205.14128)
   - **Authors**: Maria-Florina Balcan, Keegan Harris, Mikhail Khodak, Zhiwei Steven Wu
   - **Summary**: This paper presents a meta-learning approach to online learning with bandit feedback across multiple tasks. The proposed meta-algorithm adapts to task similarities, improving performance in adversarial settings by tuning parameters of existing methods like Exp3 and online mirror descent.
   - **Year**: 2022

8. **Title**: Meta-Learning to Calibrate Gaussian Processes with Deep Kernels for Regression Uncertainty Estimation (arXiv:2312.07952)
   - **Authors**: Tomoharu Iwata, Atsutoshi Kumagai
   - **Summary**: The authors propose a meta-learning method to calibrate Gaussian processes with deep kernels, enhancing regression uncertainty estimation in few-shot settings. The model integrates task-shared and task-specific components, improving uncertainty estimation performance while maintaining high regression accuracy.
   - **Year**: 2023

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Regulatory Heterogeneity**: Navigating and integrating diverse and sometimes conflicting regulatory requirements across multiple jurisdictions poses significant challenges for ML systems.

2. **Dynamic Regulatory Landscapes**: Regulations evolve over time, necessitating continuous updates to compliance mechanisms, which can be resource-intensive and complex.

3. **Balancing Performance and Compliance**: Ensuring that ML models meet regulatory standards without compromising their performance or utility remains a critical challenge.

4. **Interpretability and Transparency**: Providing clear and interpretable compliance reports that satisfy auditors and stakeholders is essential but often difficult to achieve.

5. **Efficient Adaptation to New Regulations**: Developing frameworks that can quickly adapt to new regulatory constraints with minimal retraining or system redesign is crucial for maintaining compliance in a cost-effective manner.
