1. **Title**: MedLoRD: A Medical Low-Resource Diffusion Model for High-Resolution 3D CT Image Synthesis (arXiv:2503.13211)
   - **Authors**: Marvin Seyfarth, Salman Ul Hassan Dar, Isabelle Ayx, Matthias Alexander Fink, Stefan O. Schoenberg, Hans-Ulrich Kauczor, Sandy Engelhardt
   - **Summary**: MedLoRD introduces a diffusion model tailored for computationally constrained environments, capable of generating high-resolution 3D medical images. It addresses the challenge of data scarcity in medical imaging by producing synthetic data that closely adheres to segmentation mask conditions, thereby enhancing the feasibility of AI applications in healthcare settings with limited resources.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: LeFusion: Controllable Pathology Synthesis via Lesion-Focused Diffusion Models (arXiv:2403.14066)
   - **Authors**: Hantao Zhang, Yuhe Liu, Jiancheng Yang, Shouhong Wan, Xinyuan Wang, Wei Peng, Pascal Fua
   - **Summary**: LeFusion presents a lesion-focused diffusion model designed to generate lesion-containing image-segmentation pairs from lesion-free images. By focusing on lesion areas and integrating forward-diffused background contexts, it simplifies the learning process and improves control over the synthetic output, effectively addressing challenges in lesion texture synthesis and enhancing the performance of segmentation models.
   - **Year**: 2024

3. **Title**: Mechanistic Learning with Guided Diffusion Models to Predict Spatio-Temporal Brain Tumor Growth (arXiv:2509.09610)
   - **Authors**: Daria Laslo, Efthymios Georgiou, Marius George Linguraru, Andreas Rauschecker, Sabine Muller, Catherine R. Jutzeler, Sarah Bruningk
   - **Summary**: This study proposes a hybrid framework combining a mathematical tumor growth model with a guided denoising diffusion implicit model to predict the spatio-temporal progression of brain tumors. By synthesizing future MRIs from preceding scans, it offers biologically informed image generation in data-limited scenarios, providing valuable insights for clinical decision-making in neuro-oncology.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: seg2med: a segmentation-based medical image generation framework using denoising diffusion probabilistic models (arXiv:2504.09182)
   - **Authors**: Zeyu Yang, Zhilin Chen, Yipeng Sun, Anika Strittmatter, Anish Raj, Ahmad Allababidi, Johann S. Rink, Frank G. Zöllner
   - **Summary**: seg2med introduces a framework that utilizes denoising diffusion probabilistic models to generate high-quality synthetic medical images conditioned on anatomical masks. It demonstrates strong performance in cross-modality synthesis and multimodal imaging, achieving high anatomical fidelity and supporting applications in clinical imaging and diagnostic algorithm development.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: MedEdit: Counterfactual Diffusion-based Image Editing on Brain MRI (arXiv:2407.15270)
   - **Authors**: Malek Ben Alaya, Daniel M. Lang, Benedikt Wiestler, Julia A. Schnabel, Cosmin I. Bercea
   - **Summary**: MedEdit presents a conditional diffusion model for medical image editing, enabling the generation of counterfactual brain MRI scans that simulate hypothetical conditions such as disease progression. It balances the modeling of disease effects with the preservation of the original scan's integrity, offering a tool for realistic and clinically useful imaging applications.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Clinical Validity and Trustworthiness**: Ensuring that synthetic medical images generated by diffusion models accurately represent real-world clinical scenarios is crucial. The lack of mechanisms to enforce clinical validity and age-appropriate anatomical constraints can lead to mistrust among clinicians regarding the use of synthetic data.

2. **Data Scarcity and Privacy Concerns**: The limited availability of pediatric medical imaging data, due to stringent privacy regulations and ethical considerations, poses a significant challenge for training robust AI models. Generating synthetic data that respects patient confidentiality while being representative of diverse clinical conditions is essential.

3. **Integration of Domain Knowledge**: Incorporating clinical domain knowledge, such as age-conditioned anatomy priors and clinical validity constraints, into the generative process is complex. Developing methods to seamlessly integrate this knowledge to guide the generation of realistic and clinically valid images remains a challenge.

4. **Interpretable Validation Metrics**: Establishing quantitative metrics that assess the clinical realism of synthetic images is necessary for their adoption in medical practice. Developing interpretable validation frameworks that align with clinician-defined anatomical landmarks and pathology-specific features is an ongoing challenge.

5. **Computational Resource Constraints**: Deploying high-resolution diffusion models in resource-limited healthcare environments requires efficient algorithms that can operate on standard hardware without compromising image quality. Balancing computational efficiency with the generation of high-fidelity images is a persistent challenge.
