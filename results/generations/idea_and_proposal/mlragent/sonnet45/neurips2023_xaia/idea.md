# Title
**Cross-Domain XAI Transfer Learning: Bridging Healthcare and Legal Decision-Making Through Shared Explanation Patterns**

# Motivation
Current XAI methods are developed and validated in isolation within specific domains, leading to redundant efforts and missed opportunities for knowledge transfer. Healthcare and legal domains share fundamental requirements—high-stakes decisions, regulatory compliance, and human oversight—yet their XAI solutions remain disconnected. Understanding how explanation patterns transfer across domains could accelerate XAI deployment and reveal universal principles of interpretability.

# Main Idea
This research proposes a framework to systematically identify, extract, and transfer XAI explanation patterns between healthcare diagnostics and legal decision-making. The methodology involves:

1. **Pattern Mining**: Analyze successful XAI implementations in medical diagnosis (e.g., radiology, treatment recommendations) and legal case prediction to extract common explanation structures (causal chains, counterfactual reasoning, evidence hierarchies).

2. **Transfer Framework**: Develop a domain-agnostic representation of explanation types, mapping healthcare concepts (symptoms→diagnosis) to legal equivalents (evidence→verdict).

3. **Validation**: Test transferred explanation methods across domains, measuring stakeholder trust, decision accuracy, and regulatory compliance.

**Expected Outcomes**: A taxonomy of transferable XAI patterns, guidelines for cross-domain adaptation, and empirical evidence showing which explanation types generalize effectively.

**Impact**: Accelerate XAI deployment in new domains, reduce development costs, and establish foundational principles for domain-independent explainability, particularly benefiting resource-constrained fields seeking to implement trustworthy AI systems.
