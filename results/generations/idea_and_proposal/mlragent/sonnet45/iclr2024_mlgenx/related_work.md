1. **Title**: GRAPE: Heterogeneous Graph Representation Learning for Genetic Perturbation with Coding and Non-Coding Biotype (arXiv:2505.03853)
   - **Authors**: Changxi Chi, Jun Xia, Jingbo Zhou, Jiabei Cheng, Chang Yu, Stan Z. Li
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces GRAPE, a heterogeneous graph neural network that integrates gene descriptions and DNA sequences to predict genetic perturbations. By incorporating gene biotype information and employing graph structure learning, GRAPE constructs refined gene regulatory networks, achieving state-of-the-art performance on public datasets.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Interpretable Neural ODEs for Gene Regulatory Network Discovery under Perturbations (arXiv:2501.02409)
   - **Authors**: Zaikang Lin, Sei Chang, Aaron Zweig, Minseo Kang, Elham Azizi, David A. Knowles
   - **Summary**: The authors propose PerturbODE, a framework utilizing neural ordinary differential equations to model cell state trajectories under perturbations. This approach infers causal gene regulatory networks by analyzing dynamic biological processes, demonstrating efficacy in both simulated and real over-expression datasets.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: PRAGA: Prototype-aware Graph Adaptive Aggregation for Spatial Multi-modal Omics Analysis (arXiv:2409.12728)
   - **Authors**: Xinlei Huang, Zhiqi Ma, Dian Meng, Yanran Liu, Shiwei Ruan, Qingqiang Sun, Xubin Zheng, Ziyue Qiao
   - **Summary**: PRAGA presents a framework for spatial multi-modal omics analysis, constructing dynamic graphs to capture latent semantic relations and integrating spatial information with feature semantics. It employs dynamic prototype contrastive learning to optimize representations without prior biological annotations, outperforming existing methods on various datasets.
   - **Year**: 2024

4. **Title**: MuSe-GNN: Learning Unified Gene Representation From Multimodal Biological Graph Data (arXiv:2310.02275)
   - **Authors**: Tianyu Liu, Yuge Wang, Rex Ying, Hongyu Zhao
   - **Summary**: MuSe-GNN introduces a model combining multimodal machine learning and deep graph neural networks to learn gene representations from single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomic data. It creates informative graph structures and employs contrastive learning to capture cross-data gene-gene relationships, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in gene representation learning.
   - **Year**: 2023

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Integration of Multi-Modal Data**: Effectively combining diverse data types (e.g., RNA-seq, proteomics, imaging) into a unified model remains complex due to varying data structures and noise levels.

2. **Causal Inference Accuracy**: Accurately inferring causal relationships from observational and perturbation data is challenging, especially when dealing with high-dimensional and noisy datasets.

3. **Model Interpretability**: Developing models that provide clear and biologically interpretable insights is essential for clinical translation but often difficult to achieve with complex machine learning architectures.

4. **Scalability and Computational Efficiency**: Handling large-scale omics datasets requires models that are both computationally efficient and scalable without compromising performance.

5. **Validation and Generalization**: Ensuring that models generalize well across different biological contexts and experimental conditions, and validating predictions with experimental data, poses significant challenges.
