# Title
Developmental Curriculum Learning for Emergent Cognitive Abilities in LLMs

# Motivation
Current LLMs are trained on massive, unstructured datasets without consideration for the developmental progression observed in human cognitive acquisition. Humans develop cognitive abilities like theory of mind, causal reasoning, and planning through structured developmental stages, building complex skills upon simpler foundations. Understanding whether LLMs could benefit from similar developmental trajectories could reveal fundamental insights about the nature of emergent cognitive abilities and provide a principled approach to enhancing LLM performance on cognitive tasks while reducing computational costs.

# Main Idea
This research proposes a developmentally-inspired curriculum learning framework for training LLMs that mirrors human cognitive development stages. The methodology involves:

1. **Cognitive Milestone Mapping**: Identify key developmental stages in human cognition (e.g., object permanence, simple causality, abstract reasoning) and create corresponding synthetic datasets and tasks.

2. **Progressive Training Protocol**: Train LLMs through sequential phases, starting with basic perceptual-linguistic tasks, advancing to simple reasoning, then complex planning and theory of mind tasks.

3. **Comparative Analysis**: Benchmark against standard pre-training on cognitive tasks from BabyLM, ToMi, and planning benchmarks.

**Expected Outcomes**: Improved sample efficiency, better performance on cognitive benchmarks, and insights into whether emergent abilities can be guided rather than merely scaled. This could reveal whether LLM cognitive limitations are architectural or training-paradigm related, informing both AI development and cognitive science theories.
