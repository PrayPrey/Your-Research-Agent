1. **Title**: RiboGen: RNA Sequence and Structure Co-Generation with Equivariant MultiFlow (arXiv:2503.02058)
   - **Authors**: Dana Rubin, Allan dos Santos Costa, Manvitha Ponnapati, Joseph Jacobson
   - **Summary**: RiboGen introduces a deep learning model capable of simultaneously generating RNA sequences and their corresponding all-atom 3D structures. By integrating Flow Matching with Discrete Flow Matching in a multimodal data representation and utilizing Euclidean Equivariant neural networks, RiboGen efficiently produces chemically plausible and self-consistent RNA samples, highlighting the potential of co-generating sequence and structure for RNA modeling.
   - **Year**: 2025

   - **Summary**: This study presents a multi-modal model that connects DNA, RNA, and proteins by leveraging information from different pre-trained modality-specific encoders.    - **Year**: 2024

3. **Title**: Multimodal Modeling of CRISPR-Cas12 Activity Using Foundation Models and Chromatin Accessibility Data (arXiv:2506.11182)
   - **Authors**: Azim Dehghani Amirabad, Yanfei Zhang, Artem Moskalev, Sowmya Rajesh, Tommaso Mansi, Shuwei Li, Mangal Prakash, Rui Liao
   - **Summary**: This research investigates the use of pre-trained biological foundation models, originally trained on transcriptomic data, to improve guide RNA (gRNA) activity estimation for CRISPR-Cas12 genome editing. By integrating embeddings from existing RNA foundation models with chromatin accessibility data, the study demonstrates substantial gains over traditional baselines, highlighting the effectiveness of pre-trained models and multimodal data integration in gRNA activity prediction.

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Data Integration**: Effectively integrating diverse RNA data modalities (sequence, secondary structure, tertiary structure, functional annotations) remains challenging due to varying data formats, resolutions, and quality.

2. **Model Interpretability**: Ensuring that multimodal models provide interpretable insights into RNA structure-function relationships is crucial for biological understanding and practical applications.

3. **Computational Complexity**: Developing models that can efficiently process and learn from large-scale, multimodal RNA datasets without excessive computational resources is a significant hurdle.

4. **Generalization Across RNA Types**: Ensuring that models generalize well across different RNA types and contexts, including novel or less-studied RNAs, is essential for broad applicability.

5. **Limited Annotated Data**: The scarcity of high-quality, annotated RNA datasets for training and validation constrains the development and evaluation of robust multimodal models.
