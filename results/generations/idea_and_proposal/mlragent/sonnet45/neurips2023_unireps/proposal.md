# Dynamic Alignment Pathways: Learning to Stitch Heterogeneous Neural Models via Meta-Learned Transformation Networks

## 1. Introduction

### Background

The phenomenon of representational convergence in neural networks—where models trained on similar data develop analogous internal representations—has emerged as a fundamental principle bridging neuroscience and artificial intelligence. This convergence suggests that certain representational structures are not merely artifacts of specific architectures or training procedures, but rather reflect inherent properties of the data and learning tasks. Recent work has demonstrated that models with different initializations, architectures, and even training paradigms often converge to functionally similar representations, creating opportunities for model reuse, merging, and knowledge transfer.

However, despite this representational similarity, practical model stitching—the ability to compose new models by combining components from pre-trained models—remains challenging when architectures differ or training conditions vary. Current approaches either require expensive retraining of alignment layers, work only for nearly identical architectures, or fail to generalize across diverse model families. This limitation significantly constrains the practical benefits of representational convergence, preventing organizations from efficiently reusing pre-trained components and forcing redundant training of similar models.

The core challenge lies in the geometric mismatch between representation spaces: even when models encode similar semantic information, the specific geometric structures of their latent spaces can differ substantially. Linear transformations often prove insufficient, while learning complex non-linear alignments for each model pair is computationally prohibitive. Furthermore, existing methods lack mechanisms to adapt alignment strategies based on architectural characteristics or task-specific requirements.

### Research Objectives

This research proposes a novel meta-learning framework that addresses these limitations by learning to generate lightweight transformation networks that enable seamless stitching of heterogeneous neural models. Our specific objectives are:

1. **Develop a meta-learning architecture** that can learn a family of transformation functions conditioned on source and target model characteristics, enabling dynamic adaptation to diverse architectural configurations.

2. **Design task-adaptive alignment mechanisms** that leverage few-shot examples to refine transformation networks for specific downstream applications without extensive retraining.

3. **Establish theoretical foundations** using optimal transport theory and differential geometry to characterize the stitchability of model pairs and provide guarantees on semantic preservation.

4. **Create comprehensive evaluation frameworks** to assess stitching quality across multiple dimensions: semantic consistency, computational efficiency, and downstream task performance.

5. **Demonstrate practical applications** in model reuse, federated learning, and multi-modal systems, showcasing the real-world impact of our approach.

### Significance

This research addresses critical challenges in modern machine learning systems. First, it democratizes access to large-scale model development by enabling organizations to reuse and compose pre-trained components, reducing computational costs and carbon footprints associated with training from scratch. Second, it provides theoretical insights into the geometric structure of neural representations, advancing our understanding of why and when models develop similar internal structures. Third, it enables new architectures for modular neural systems where components can be swapped, updated, or combined dynamically based on task requirements. Finally, by revealing universal principles governing representation spaces, this work bridges artificial and biological neural systems, contributing to the broader understanding of learning and cognition.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Problem Formulation

Let $\mathcal{M}_s$ and $\mathcal{M}_t$ denote source and target neural models with potentially different architectures. For a given layer $l$, these models produce representations $\mathbf{h}_s^l \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$ and $\mathbf{h}_t^l \in \mathbb{R}^{d_t}$ respectively, where $d_s$ and $d_t$ may differ. Our goal is to learn a transformation network $T_{\theta}: \mathbb{R}^{d_s} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d_t}$ such that the transformed representation $\hat{\mathbf{h}}_t^l = T_{\theta}(\mathbf{h}_s^l)$ can seamlessly replace the original representation $\mathbf{h}_t^l$ in subsequent layers of $\mathcal{M}_t$.

The key innovation is that $T_{\theta}$ is generated by a meta-network $\mathcal{G}_{\phi}$ conditioned on architectural features and task characteristics:

$$T_{\theta} = \mathcal{G}_{\phi}(\mathbf{a}_s, \mathbf{a}_t, \mathcal{D}_{\text{task}})$$

where $\mathbf{a}_s, \mathbf{a}_t$ encode architectural properties of the source and target models, and $\mathcal{D}_{\text{task}}$ represents few-shot task examples.

### 2.2 Architectural Feature Extraction

We extract architectural signatures that capture the geometric and statistical properties of each model's representation space:

**Statistical Moments**: For each layer $l$, we compute:
$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{stat}} = [\boldsymbol{\mu}_l, \text{vec}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_l), \boldsymbol{\gamma}_l]$$

where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_l$ is the mean activation, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_l$ is the covariance matrix, and $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_l$ represents higher-order moments (skewness, kurtosis).

**Geometric Properties**: We characterize the local geometry using:
$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{geom}} = [\text{dim}_{\text{intrinsic}}, \kappa_{\text{mean}}, \text{spectrum}(\mathbf{L})]$$

where $\text{dim}_{\text{intrinsic}}$ is the estimated intrinsic dimensionality via PCA, $\kappa_{\text{mean}}$ is the mean curvature, and $\text{spectrum}(\mathbf{L})$ are eigenvalues of the graph Laplacian constructed from nearest neighbors.

**Architectural Metadata**: We encode structural information:
$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{arch}} = [d, n_{\text{layers}}, \text{act}_{\text{type}}, \text{norm}_{\text{type}}, n_{\text{params}}]$$

The complete architectural feature vector is: $\mathbf{a} = [\mathbf{a}_{\text{stat}}, \mathbf{a}_{\text{geom}}, \mathbf{a}_{\text{arch}}]$

### 2.3 Meta-Learning Framework

Our meta-learning approach operates on a distribution of model pairs. The meta-training objective is:

$$\min_{\phi} \mathbb{E}_{(\mathcal{M}_s, \mathcal{M}_t) \sim p(\mathcal{M})} \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_{\text{task}}} \left[\mathcal{L}_{\text{stitch}}(T_{\theta}, \mathcal{M}_s, \mathcal{M}_t, \mathcal{D}_{\text{task}}) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}(T_{\theta})\right]$$

where $\theta = \mathcal{G}_{\phi}(\mathbf{a}_s, \mathbf{a}_t, \mathcal{D}_{\text{task}})$.

**Meta-Network Architecture**: $\mathcal{G}_{\phi}$ consists of:
1. A cross-attention transformer that processes architectural features from both models
2. A task encoder that extracts prototypes from $\mathcal{D}_{\text{task}}$ via set encoding
3. A hypernetwork that generates weights for the transformation network $T_{\theta}$

The transformation network $T_{\theta}$ uses a residual architecture:

$$T_{\theta}(\mathbf{h}_s) = \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}\mathbf{h}_s + \text{MLP}_{\theta}(\mathbf{h}_s)$$

where $\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}$ handles dimension matching and $\text{MLP}_{\theta}$ learns non-linear refinements.

### 2.4 Loss Functions

**Primary Stitching Loss**: We combine three complementary objectives:

1. **Functional Consistency Loss**: Ensures that stitched models produce similar outputs for the same inputs:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{func}} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x} \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[\text{KL}\left(p_{\text{stitch}}(y|\mathbf{x}) \| p_{\mathcal{M}_t}(y|\mathbf{x})\right)\right]$$

2. **Representation Alignment Loss**: Based on optimal transport, measures the Wasserstein distance between representation distributions:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{OT}} = W_2(\mu_s, \mu_t) = \inf_{\gamma \in \Pi(\mu_s, \mu_t)} \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{h}_s, \mathbf{h}_t) \sim \gamma}\left[\|\mathbf{h}_s - \mathbf{h}_t\|_2^2\right]$$

We approximate this using Sinkhorn iterations for computational efficiency.

3. **Contrastive Semantic Loss**: Preserves semantic relationships using InfoNCE:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{contrast}} = -\mathbb{E}\left[\log \frac{\exp(\text{sim}(T_{\theta}(\mathbf{h}_s^i), \mathbf{h}_t^i)/\tau)}{\sum_{j} \exp(\text{sim}(T_{\theta}(\mathbf{h}_s^i), \mathbf{h}_t^j)/\tau)}\right]$$

where $\text{sim}(\cdot, \cdot)$ is cosine similarity and $\tau$ is temperature.

**Regularization Loss**: Encourages lightweight, stable transformations:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}} = \lambda_1 \|\theta\|_2^2 + \lambda_2 \mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla_{\mathbf{h}_s} T_{\theta}(\mathbf{h}_s)\|_F\right]$$

The second term penalizes large Jacobian norms to ensure smooth transformations.

### 2.5 Data Collection and Meta-Dataset Construction

We construct a diverse meta-dataset of pre-trained model pairs:

**Model Zoo**: We collect models spanning multiple architectures (ResNets, Vision Transformers, EfficientNets, ConvNeXts) trained on various datasets (ImageNet, CIFAR, Places365) with different training procedures (supervised, self-supervised, adversarial training).

**Pairing Strategy**: For each model, we create pairs with:
- Same architecture, different initializations
- Similar architectures (e.g., ResNet-50 and ResNet-101)
- Different architectural families (e.g., CNN and Transformer)

**Task Distribution**: We sample tasks from:
- Fine-tuning datasets (Stanford Cars, FGVC Aircraft, Flowers102)
- Transfer learning benchmarks (VTAB suite)
- Domain adaptation scenarios (synthetic-to-real)

### 2.6 Training Procedure

**Stage 1: Meta-Training**
1. Sample a batch of model pairs $\{(\mathcal{M}_s^i, \mathcal{M}_t^i)\}_{i=1}^B$
2. For each pair, extract architectural features $\mathbf{a}_s^i, \mathbf{a}_t^i$
3. Sample task examples $\mathcal{D}_{\text{task}}^i$ for each pair
4. Generate transformation networks: $\theta^i = \mathcal{G}_{\phi}(\mathbf{a}_s^i, \mathbf{a}_t^i, \mathcal{D}_{\text{task}}^i)$
5. Compute stitching losses for each pair
6. Update meta-parameters $\phi$ via gradient descent:
$$\phi \leftarrow \phi - \alpha \nabla_{\phi} \frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^B \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}^i$$

**Stage 2: Task-Specific Adaptation**
Given a new model pair and task:
1. Generate initial transformation: $\theta_0 = \mathcal{G}_{\phi}(\mathbf{a}_s, \mathbf{a}_t, \mathcal{D}_{\text{task}})$
2. Fine-tune $\theta$ (not $\phi$) on task examples for $K$ steps
3. Evaluate stitched model on validation set

### 2.7 Experimental Design

**Baseline Comparisons**: We compare against:
- Linear stitching (ridge regression alignment)
- Neural stitching with fixed MLPs
- CKA-based alignment
- Retraining-based methods (fine-tuning entire downstream model)

**Evaluation Metrics**:

1. **Stitching Quality**:
   - Representation similarity: Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) score between $T_{\theta}(\mathbf{h}_s)$ and $\mathbf{h}_t$
   - Downstream accuracy on target tasks
   - Functional equivalence: correlation between predictions

2. **Efficiency**:
   - Number of parameters in transformation network
   - Inference time overhead
   - Few-shot adaptation speed (examples needed)

3. **Generalization**:
   - Zero-shot stitching performance (no task-specific adaptation)
   - Cross-architecture transfer (train on CNN pairs, test on Transformers)
   - Cross-domain transfer (train on vision, test on language)

**Experimental Scenarios**:

1. **Architecture Heterogeneity Study**: Systematically vary architectural differences (depth, width, activation functions) and measure stitching performance
2. **Few-Shot Adaptation**: Evaluate performance with varying numbers of task examples (0, 1, 5, 10, 50)
3. **Multi-Point Stitching**: Stitch at multiple intermediate layers simultaneously
4. **Ablation Studies**: Remove components (OT loss, contrastive loss, architectural features) to assess contributions
5. **Real-World Applications**:
   - Federated learning: stitch heterogeneous client models
   - Model compression: stitch to smaller target models
   - Multi-modal alignment: stitch vision and language encoders

## 3. Expected Outcomes & Impact

### Expected Outcomes

**Technical Achievements**:

1. **Robust Stitching Framework**: We expect to achieve successful stitching across heterogeneous architectures with minimal performance degradation (≤5% accuracy drop compared to end-to-end trained models) while using transformation networks with <1% parameters of the original models.

2. **Stitchability Metrics**: Development of interpretable metrics based on optimal transport distances and geometric properties that predict stitching difficulty, enabling practitioners to assess model compatibility a priori.

3. **Few-Shot Efficiency**: Demonstrate that 5-10 task examples suffice for effective adaptation, making the approach practical for low-resource scenarios.

4. **Theoretical Insights**: Characterization of sufficient conditions for successful stitching based on representation geometry, providing guidance for training models that are inherently more stitchable.

**Scientific Contributions**:

1. **Unified Framework**: A principled approach connecting optimal transport, meta-learning, and representation alignment, bridging multiple research areas.

2. **Geometric Understanding**: New insights into how architectural choices influence representation geometry and stitchability, advancing our understanding of neural network learning dynamics.

3. **Scaling Laws**: Empirical characterization of how stitching performance scales with architectural similarity, model size, and task complexity.

### Broader Impact

**Practical Applications**:

1. **Democratizing AI Development**: Organizations can reuse and compose pre-trained components from different sources, reducing barriers to entry for resource-constrained researchers and practitioners.

2. **Environmental Sustainability**: By enabling model reuse instead of training from scratch, we significantly reduce the computational costs and carbon emissions associated with large-scale model development.

3. **Federated and Distributed Learning**: Enable heterogeneous federated learning where participants with different model architectures can effectively collaborate without requiring architectural uniformity.

4. **Continual Learning Systems**: Support modular neural architectures where components can be updated, replaced, or specialized independently, enabling more flexible and adaptive AI systems.

5. **Multi-Modal Systems**: Facilitate alignment between vision, language, and other modalities by providing principled methods for stitching encoders from different domains.

**Theoretical Implications**:

1. **Neural Representation Theory**: Our work contributes to understanding universal properties of learned representations, revealing which geometric structures are intrinsic to data versus artifacts of specific architectures.

2. **Bridge to Neuroscience**: By characterizing conditions under which artificial neural representations can be aligned, we provide computational tools for studying representational similarity in biological neural systems.

3. **Identifiability Research**: Our optimal transport formulation connects to recent work on identifiability in neural networks, potentially revealing fundamental limits on representation uniqueness.

**Limitations and Future Directions**:

While our approach addresses key challenges in model stitching, important limitations remain. First, our method requires a diverse meta-training dataset of model pairs, which may not cover all possible architectural combinations. Second, the theoretical guarantees on semantic preservation are limited to specific model families and may not generalize to highly novel architectures. Third, our approach focuses on feedforward stitching and may require extensions for recurrent or dynamical systems.

Future research directions include: (1) extending to generative models and diffusion models, (2) developing online adaptation mechanisms for non-stationary environments, (3) incorporating uncertainty quantification to detect when stitching is likely to fail, and (4) exploring connections to neural architecture search to design inherently stitchable models.

In conclusion, this research establishes a comprehensive framework for learning to stitch heterogeneous neural models through meta-learned transformation networks. By combining theoretical rigor from optimal transport theory with practical meta-learning techniques, we enable new paradigms for model reuse, composition, and understanding. The resulting system not only addresses immediate practical needs in efficient AI development but also advances fundamental understanding of representational convergence across neural systems, contributing to the broader scientific goals of unified theories of learning and cognition.
