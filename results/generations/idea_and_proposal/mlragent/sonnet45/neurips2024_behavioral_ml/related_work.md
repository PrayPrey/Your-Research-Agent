Here is a literature review on integrating cognitive load theory into large language model (LLM) alignment, focusing on papers published between 2023 and 2025.

**1. Related Papers**

The following academic papers are closely related to the research idea of aligning LLMs with human cognitive load models:

1. **Title**: EEG-based Cognitive Load Classification using Feature Masked Autoencoding and Emotion Transfer Learning (arXiv:2308.00246)
   - **Authors**: Dustin Pulver, Prithila Angkan, Paul Hungler, Ali Etemad
   - **Summary**: This study presents a transformer-based model that classifies cognitive load using EEG data. The model employs self-supervised masked autoencoding and transfer learning from emotion-related EEG datasets to enhance cognitive load classification.
   - **Year**: 2023

2. **Title**: CogniLoad: A Synthetic Natural Language Reasoning Benchmark With Tunable Length, Intrinsic Difficulty, and Distractor Density (arXiv:2509.18458)
   - **Authors**: Daniel Kaiser, Arnoldo Frigessi, Ali Ramezani-Kebrya, Benjamin Ricaud
   - **Summary**: CogniLoad introduces a benchmark grounded in Cognitive Load Theory, generating logic puzzles with adjustable parameters to assess LLMs' reasoning capabilities under varying cognitive loads.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Prism: Towards Lowering User Cognitive Load in LLMs via Complex Intent Understanding (arXiv:2601.08653)
   - **Authors**: Zenghua Liao, Jinzhi Liao, Xiang Zhao
   - **Summary**: Prism proposes a framework for complex intent understanding in LLMs, aiming to reduce user cognitive load by decomposing user intents and generating logically coherent clarification questions.
   - **Year**: 2026

4. **Title**: RePo: Language Models with Context Re-Positioning (arXiv:2512.14391)
   - **Authors**: Huayang Li, Tianyu Zhao, Richard Sproat
   - **Summary**: RePo introduces a context re-positioning mechanism in LLMs to reduce extraneous cognitive load by assigning token positions that capture contextual dependencies, enhancing performance on tasks with noisy or long contexts.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: Exclusion of Thought: Mitigating Cognitive Load in Large Language Models for Enhanced Reasoning in Multiple-Choice Tasks
   - **Authors**: Qihang Fu, Yongbin Qin, Ruizhang Huang, Yanping Chen, Yulin Zhou, Lintao Long
   - **Summary**: This paper presents the Exclusion of Thought (EoT) approach, which reduces cognitive load in LLMs by excluding irrelevant reasoning paths, improving performance in multiple-choice reasoning tasks.
   - **Year**: 2025

6. **Title**: Large Language Models with Controllable Working Memory
   - **Authors**: Daliang Li, Ankit Singh Rawat, Manzil Zaheer, Xin Wang, Michal Lukasik, Andreas Veit, Felix Yu, Sanjiv Kumar
   - **Summary**: This study explores LLMs with controllable working memory, allowing models to prioritize context over memorized knowledge, aligning outputs with user-provided information.
   - **Year**: 2022

7. **Title**: Challenging Cognitive Load Theory: The Role of Educational Neuroscience and Artificial Intelligence in Redefining Learning Efficacy
   - **Authors**: Not specified
   - **Summary**: This systematic review integrates Cognitive Load Theory with AI and educational neuroscience, examining their combined impact on optimizing learning environments and personalized education.
   - **Year**: 2025

8. **Title**: Unveiling the Learning Mind of Language Models: A Cognitive Framework and Empirical Study
   - **Authors**: Not specified
   - **Summary**: This paper presents a cognitive framework to analyze LLMs, providing insights into their learning processes and cognitive patterns.
   - **Year**: 2025

9. **Title**: How Large Language Models Encode Theory-of-Mind: A Study on Sparse Parameter Patterns
   - **Authors**: Y. Wu, W. Guo, Z. Liu, et al.
   - **Summary**: This study investigates how LLMs encode theory-of-mind capabilities, analyzing sparse parameter patterns to understand their cognitive representations.
   - **Year**: 2025

10. **Title**: Human-AI Collaboration in Cloud Security: Cognitive Hierarchy-Driven Deep Reinforcement Learning
    - **Authors**: Zahra Aref, Sheng Wei, Narayan B. Mandayam
    - **Summary**: This paper proposes a Cognitive Hierarchy Theory-driven Deep Q-Network framework to enhance human-AI collaboration in Security Operations Centers, demonstrating improved data protection and alignment with adaptive attackers.
    - **Year**: 2025

**2. Key Challenges**

The integration of cognitive load theory into LLM alignment presents several challenges:

1. **Accurate Measurement of Cognitive Load**: Developing reliable metrics to assess cognitive load in real-time is complex, especially when considering individual differences in cognitive capacity.

2. **Personalization**: Creating models that adapt to individual users' cognitive capacities and preferences requires sophisticated user modeling and data collection.

3. **Balancing Information Density**: Ensuring that LLM outputs are informative yet not overwhelming necessitates fine-tuning the balance between detail and brevity.

4. **Multimodal Integration**: Incorporating various modalities (e.g., text, diagrams) to reduce cognitive load adds complexity to model design and training.

5. **Evaluation Metrics**: Establishing standardized benchmarks to evaluate the effectiveness of cognitive load-aware LLMs is essential but challenging due to the subjective nature of cognitive load.
