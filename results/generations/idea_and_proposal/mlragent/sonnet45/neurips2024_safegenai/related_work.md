1. **Title**: Uncertainty Quantification and Confidence Calibration in Large Language Models: A Survey (arXiv:2503.15850)
   - **Authors**: Xiaoou Liu, Tiejin Chen, Longchao Da, Chacha Chen, Zhen Lin, Hua Wei
   - **Summary**: This survey examines the challenges of uncertainty quantification (UQ) in large language models (LLMs), highlighting their tendency to produce plausible yet incorrect responses. It introduces a taxonomy categorizing UQ methods based on computational efficiency and uncertainty dimensions, emphasizing the need for scalable, interpretable, and robust UQ approaches to enhance LLM reliability.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: SPUQ: Perturbation-Based Uncertainty Quantification for Large Language Models (arXiv:2403.02509)
   - **Authors**: Xiang Gao, Jiaxin Zhang, Lalla Mouatadid, Kamalika Das
   - **Summary**: This paper presents SPUQ, a novel method designed to address both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties in LLMs. By generating input perturbations and sampling outputs, SPUQ incorporates an aggregation module that generalizes the sampling uncertainty approach for text generation tasks, resulting in a significant reduction in Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and improved model uncertainty calibration.
   - **Year**: 2024

3. **Title**: Large Language Models are Overconfident and Amplify Human Bias (arXiv:2505.02151)
   - **Authors**: Fengfei Sun, Ningke Li, Kailong Wang, Lorenz Goette
   - **Summary**: This study investigates the overconfidence bias in LLMs, revealing that they overestimate the probability of their correctness by 20% to 60%. The research highlights that LLMs not only inherit human biases but also amplify them, especially when they are less certain about their answers, posing significant risks in decision-making tasks.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Trusted Uncertainty in Large Language Models: A Unified Framework for Confidence Calibration and Risk-Controlled Refusal (arXiv:2509.01455)
   - **Authors**: Markus Oehri, Giulia Conti, Kaviraj Pather, Alexandre Rossi, Laia Serra, Adrian Parody, Rogvi Johannesen, Aviaja Petersen, Arben Krasniqi
   - **Summary**: The authors introduce UniCR, a framework that integrates various uncertainty evidence to produce calibrated probabilities of correctness in LLM outputs. UniCR employs a lightweight calibration head and supports API-only models through black-box features, offering distribution-free guarantees using conformal risk control, thereby improving trustworthiness without fine-tuning the base model.
   - **Year**: 2025

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Computational Constraints**: Implementing uncertainty quantification methods in LLMs often requires significant computational resources, making real-time applications challenging.

2. **Decoding Inconsistencies**: Variability in decoding strategies can lead to inconsistent outputs, complicating the estimation of uncertainty and confidence calibration.

3. **Amplification of Human Biases**: LLMs may not only inherit but also amplify human biases, including overconfidence, which can adversely affect decision-making processes.

4. **Detection of Hallucinations**: Identifying and mitigating hallucinated content in LLM outputs remains a significant challenge, particularly in high-stakes applications.

5. **User Interpretation of Uncertainty**: Effectively communicating uncertainty to users in an interpretable manner is crucial for informed decision-making but remains an open problem.
