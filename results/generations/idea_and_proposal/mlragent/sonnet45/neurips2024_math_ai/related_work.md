1. **Title**: LeanTutor: A Formally-Verified AI Tutor for Mathematical Proofs (arXiv:2506.08321)
   - **Authors**: Manooshree Patel, Rayna Bhattacharyya, Thomas Lu, Arnav Mehta, Niels Voss, Narges Norouzi, Gireeja Ranade
   - **Summary**: LeanTutor is an AI tutoring system that interacts with students in natural language to assist with mathematical proofs. It autoformalizes student proofs into the Lean theorem prover, verifies their accuracy, identifies errors, and provides instructional guidance. The system comprises three modules: an autoformalizer/proof-checker, a next-step generator, and a natural language feedback generator. Evaluation on the PeanoBench dataset shows that LeanTutor correctly formalizes 57% of tactics in correct proofs and accurately identifies incorrect steps in 30% of erroneous proofs.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: GraphMASAL: A Graph-based Multi-Agent System for Adaptive Learning (arXiv:2511.11035)
   - **Authors**: Biqing Zeng, Mengquan Liu, Zongwei Zhen
   - **Summary**: GraphMASAL introduces a graph-based multi-agent system designed for adaptive learning in Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITSs). It integrates a dynamic knowledge graph for persistent learner modeling, a trio of agents (Diagnostician, Planner, Tutor) orchestrated via LangGraph, and a knowledge-graph-grounded neural information retrieval component. The system employs a multi-source multi-sink planning engine with a cognitively grounded cost function. Evaluations demonstrate that GraphMASAL outperforms baseline models in planning, achieving stronger structural alignment of learning paths, higher coverage of weak concepts, and lower learning costs.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: MathAgent: Leveraging a Mixture-of-Math-Agent Framework for Real-World Multimodal Mathematical Error Detection (arXiv:2503.18132)
   - **Authors**: Yibo Yan, Shen Wang, Jiahao Huo, Philip S. Yu, Xuming Hu, Qingsong Wen
   - **Summary**: MathAgent presents a Mixture-of-Math-Agent framework tailored for detecting mathematical errors in educational settings. The framework decomposes error detection into three phases: an image-text consistency validator, a visual semantic interpreter, and an integrative error analyzer. This architecture enables accurate processing of multimodal mathematical content by explicitly modeling relationships between problems and student solution steps. Evaluations on real-world educational data show that MathAgent achieves approximately 5% higher accuracy in error step identification and 3% improvement in error categorization compared to baseline models.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Stepwise Verification and Remediation of Student Reasoning Errors with Large Language Model Tutors (arXiv:2407.09136)
   - **Authors**: Nico Daheim, Jakub Macina, Manu Kapur, Iryna Gurevych, Mrinmaya Sachan
   - **Summary**: This study focuses on verifying student solutions in mathematical reasoning and demonstrates how grounding to such verification improves tutor response generation. The authors collected a dataset of 1,000 stepwise math reasoning chains with annotated first error steps. They found that identifying mistakes in student solutions is challenging for current models. By proposing and evaluating several verifiers for detecting these errors, the study shows that student solution verifiers steer generation models toward highly targeted responses to student errors, resulting in more accurate and less hallucinated feedback compared to existing baselines.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: Model-Based Runtime Monitoring with Interactive Imitation Learning (arXiv:2310.17552)
   - **Authors**: Huihan Liu, Shivin Dass, Roberto Martín-Martín, Yuke Zhu
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces a model-based runtime monitoring algorithm that learns from deployment data to detect system anomalies and anticipate failures. Integrated into an interactive imitation learning framework, the algorithm enables robots to self-monitor and predict errors during task execution. The approach involves learning a latent-space dynamics model and a failure classifier, allowing the system to simulate future action outcomes and detect high-risk states preemptively. Evaluations demonstrate that the method outperforms baselines, achieving higher success rates in both simulation and physical hardware deployments.

8. **Title**: Explainable Fraud Detection with Deep Symbolic Classification (arXiv:2312.00586)
   - **Authors**: Floris den Hengst, Erman Acar, Samantha Visbeek
   - **Summary**: This study introduces Deep Symbolic Classification (DSC), an extension of the Deep Symbolic Regression framework tailored for fraud detection. DSC casts classification as a search problem in the space of all analytic functions, optimizing for evaluation metrics directly. The approach leverages deep reinforcement learning to generate explainable models that balance predictive performance and interpretability. Evaluations on the PaySim dataset demonstrate that DSC achieves competitive predictive performance with state-of-the-art models while surpassing them in terms of explainability.
   - **Year**: 2023

9. **Title**: Early Detection of Performance Regressions by Bridging Local Performance Data and Architectural Models (arXiv:2408.08148)
   - **Authors**: Lizhi Liao, Simon Eismann, Heng Li, Cor-Paul Bezemer, Diego Elias Costa, André van Hoorn, Weiyi Shang
   - **Summary**: This paper proposes a novel approach for early detection of performance regressions by integrating local performance data from component-level testing with system-level architectural models. The method identifies deviations at the component level and propagates these to the architectural model to predict overall system performance regressions. Evaluations on open-source benchmark systems show that the approach effectively detects end-to-end system performance regressions from local performance deviations, enabling earlier detection compared to traditional system performance testing.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Error Identification and Classification**: Accurately detecting and categorizing specific types of errors in student solutions remains challenging, as models often struggle to pinpoint the exact nature of mistakes.

2. **Data Quality and Annotation**: High-quality, annotated datasets are essential for training effective models, yet obtaining such data is resource-intensive and may lack diversity, limiting model generalizability.

3. **Adaptive Feedback Generation**: Developing systems that provide personalized, context-aware feedback tailored to individual student errors is complex and requires nuanced understanding of pedagogical strategies.

4. **Integration of Multimodal Data**: Effectively processing and integrating various data modalities (e.g., text, images, equations) to understand and assess student solutions poses significant technical challenges.

5. **Scalability and Deployment**: Ensuring that AI tutoring systems can scale to accommodate diverse educational contexts and integrate seamlessly into existing educational infrastructures is a persistent challenge.
