1. **Title**: A Theory of Adaptive Scaffolding for LLM-Based Pedagogical Agents (arXiv:2508.01503)
   - **Authors**: Clayton Cohn, Surya Rayala, Namrata Srivastava, Joyce Horn Fonteles, Shruti Jain, Xinying Luo, Divya Mereddy, Naveeduddin Mohammed, Gautam Biswas
   - **Summary**: This paper proposes a framework combining Evidence-Centered Design with Social Cognitive Theory to develop adaptive scaffolding in large language model (LLM)-based agents for STEM+C education. The framework is exemplified through Inquizzitor, an LLM-based formative assessment agent that integrates human-AI hybrid intelligence to provide feedback grounded in cognitive science principles.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: SocraticAI: Transforming LLMs into Guided CS Tutors Through Scaffolded Interaction (arXiv:2512.03501)
   - **Authors**: Karthik Sunil, Aalok Thakkar
   - **Summary**: SocraticAI introduces a scaffolded AI tutoring system that integrates LLMs into undergraduate Computer Science education. The system enforces well-formulated questions, reflective engagement, and daily usage limits while providing Socratic dialogue scaffolds. Initial deployment shows that students progress from vague help-seeking to sophisticated problem decomposition within 2-3 weeks.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Reflecting in the Reflection: Integrating a Socratic Questioning Framework into Automated AI-Based Question Generation (arXiv:2601.14798)
   - **Authors**: Ondřej Holub, Essi Ryymin, Rodrigo Alves
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces a reflection-in-reflection framework for automated generation of reflection questions using LLMs. The approach involves two role-specialized agents engaging in a Socratic multi-turn dialogue to iteratively refine questions based on teacher-specified topics and student levels. Evaluation shows that the two-agent protocol produces questions judged to be more relevant and deeper than a one-shot baseline.
   - **Year**: 2026

4. **Title**: Analyzing Adaptive Scaffolds that Help Students Develop Self-Regulated Learning Behaviors (arXiv:2202.09698)
   - **Authors**: Anabil Munshi, Gautam Biswas, Ryan Baker, Jaclyn Ocumpaugh, Stephen Hutt, Luc Paquette
   - **Summary**: This study presents a framework for adaptive scaffolding in Betty's Brain, a learning-by-teaching environment for middle school science. The framework provides conversational feedback at key cognitive and metacognitive inflection points to support self-regulated learning. A classroom study with 98 students indicates that strategic hints supporting debugging and assessment of causal models are generally more useful than encouragement prompts.
   - **Year**: 2022

5. **Title**: Adaptive Data Quality Scoring Operations Framework using Drift-Aware Mechanism for Industrial Applications (arXiv:2408.06724)
   - **Authors**: Firas Bayram, Bestoun S. Ahmed, Erik Hallin
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces an adaptive data quality scoring framework that integrates a dynamic change detector mechanism to monitor and adapt to changes in data quality. While focused on industrial applications, the framework's adaptive mechanisms may offer insights into developing dynamic prompting systems in educational AI tutors.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: PI-Whisper: An Adaptive and Incremental ASR Framework for Diverse and Evolving Speaker Characteristics (arXiv:2406.15668)
   - **Authors**: Dancheng Liu, Amir Nassereldine, Chenhui Xu, Jinjun Xiong
   - **Summary**: PI-Whisper presents an adaptive automatic speech recognition (ASR) framework that improves recognition capabilities by identifying different speakers' characteristics in real-time. The framework's adaptive and incremental learning approaches could inform the development of context-aware prompting systems in AI tutoring.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Balancing Support and Challenge**: Developing AI tutors that effectively balance providing support with challenging students to promote deeper learning without fostering dependency.

2. **Context-Aware Adaptation**: Implementing dynamic prompting systems that accurately adjust intervention levels based on real-time assessment of student affect, attempt history, and knowledge state.

3. **Data Quality and Annotation**: Creating high-quality datasets of expert tutor-student interactions with detailed annotations of pedagogical strategies, which is resource-intensive and requires careful design.

4. **Evaluation Metrics**: Establishing robust evaluation metrics that measure student learning gains and transfer effectively, beyond traditional satisfaction surveys.

5. **Ethical Considerations**: Addressing ethical concerns related to student data privacy, consent, and the potential for AI tutors to inadvertently reinforce biases or misconceptions.
