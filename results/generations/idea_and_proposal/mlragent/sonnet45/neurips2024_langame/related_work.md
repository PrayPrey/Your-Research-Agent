1. **Title**: MARS: Reinforcing Multi-Agent Reasoning of LLMs through Self-Play in Strategic Games (arXiv:2510.15414)
   - **Authors**: Huining Yuan, Zelai Xu, Zheyue Tan, Xiangmin Yi, Mo Guang, Kaiwen Long, Haojia Hui, Boxun Li, Xinlei Chen, Bo Zhao, Xiao-Ping Zhang, Chao Yu, Yu Wang
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces MARS, an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework that enhances multi-agent reasoning in large language models (LLMs) through self-play in both cooperative and competitive games. MARS features a turn-level advantage estimator for credit assignment and agent-specific advantage normalization to stabilize training. The approach demonstrates significant performance improvements in strategic abilities and reasoning benchmarks.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: ReMA: Learning to Meta-think for LLMs with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (arXiv:2503.09501)
   - **Authors**: Ziyu Wan, Yunxiang Li, Yan Song, Hanjing Wang, Linyi Yang, Mark Schmidt, Jun Wang, Weinan Zhang, Shuyue Hu, Ying Wen
   - **Summary**: ReMA presents a framework that leverages multi-agent reinforcement learning to elicit meta-thinking behaviors in LLMs. It decouples the reasoning process into a high-level meta-thinking agent and a low-level reasoning agent, enabling improved generalization and robustness in complex reasoning tasks.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Compositional Instruction Following with Language Models and Reinforcement Learning (arXiv:2501.12539)
   - **Authors**: Vanya Cohen, Geraud Nangue Tasse, Nakul Gopalan, Steven James, Matthew Gombolay, Ray Mooney, Benjamin Rosman
   - **Summary**: This study introduces CERLLA, a method that combines reinforcement learning with language grounding to address compositional instruction following. By leveraging compositional policy representations and a semantic parser trained using reinforcement learning and in-context learning, CERLLA demonstrates significant improvements in sample complexity and success rates on tasks designed to test compositional generalization.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Compositional Learning for Modular Multi-Agent Self-Organizing Networks (arXiv:2506.02616)
   - **Authors**: Qi Liao, Parijat Bhattacharjee
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces two compositional learning approaches—Compositional Deep Reinforcement Learning (CDRL) and Compositional Predictive Decision-Making (CPDM)—for multi-agent systems. The proposed modular, two-tier framework manages heterogeneous agent granularities, leading to improved throughput, latency, scalability, and safer training in large-scale self-organizing networks.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: Learning to Simulate Self-Driven Particles System with Coordinated Policy Optimization (arXiv:2110.13827)
   - **Authors**: Zhenghao Peng, Quanyi Li, Ka Ming Hui, Chunxiao Liu, Bolei Zhou
   - **Summary**: This work develops Coordinated Policy Optimization (CoPO), a multi-agent reinforcement learning method that incorporates social psychology principles to simulate self-driven particle systems. Applied to traffic simulation, CoPO achieves superior performance and safety, with vehicles exhibiting complex and diverse social behaviors.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: MetaDrive: Composing Diverse Driving Scenarios for Generalizable Reinforcement Learning (arXiv:2109.12674)
   - **Authors**: Quanyi Li, Zhenghao Peng, Lan Feng, Qihang Zhang, Zhenghai Xue, Bolei Zhou
   - **Summary**: MetaDrive is a driving simulation platform that generates diverse driving scenarios through procedural generation and real data importing. It supports research in generalizable reinforcement learning, safe exploration, and multi-agent reinforcement learning, demonstrating that increased diversity in training scenarios enhances RL agents' generalizability.
   - **Year**: 2024

7. **Title**: KnowSR: Knowledge Sharing among Homogeneous Agents in Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning (arXiv:2105.11611)
   - **Authors**: Zijian Gao, Kele Xu, Bo Ding, Huaimin Wang, Yiying Li, Hongda Jia
   - **Summary**: KnowSR introduces a method for knowledge sharing among homogeneous agents in multi-agent reinforcement learning using knowledge distillation. This approach shortens the training phase and improves performance in both collaborative and competitive scenarios.
   - **Year**: 2024

8. **Title**: A Survey on Self-play Methods in Reinforcement Learning (arXiv:2408.01072)
   - **Authors**: Ruize Zhang, Zelai Xu, Chengdong Ma, Chao Yu, Wei-Wei Tu, Shiyu Huang, Deheng Ye, Wenbo Ding, Yaodong Yang, Yu Wang
   - **Summary**: This survey provides a comprehensive overview of self-play methods in reinforcement learning, clarifying the preliminaries, proposing a unified framework, and classifying existing self-play algorithms. It also discusses the role of self-play in various scenarios and highlights open challenges and future research directions.
   - **Year**: 2024

9. **Title**: Neural Module Networks for Reasoning Over Text (arXiv:1912.04971)
   - **Authors**: Nitish Gupta, Kevin Lin, Dan Roth, Sameer Singh, Matt Gardner
   - **Summary**: This paper extends neural module networks to answer compositional questions over text by introducing modules that perform symbolic reasoning over numbers and dates. The approach demonstrates significant improvements on a subset of the DROP dataset, showcasing the effectiveness of modular, compositional reasoning in natural language understanding.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Compositional Generalization**: Developing models that can generalize compositional reasoning to novel tasks remains a significant challenge, as current approaches often struggle with unseen compositions.

2. **Interactive Learning**: Implementing effective interactive learning frameworks where agents can negotiate and clarify meanings dynamically is complex and requires robust mechanisms for handling ambiguity.

3. **Semantic Drift**: Maintaining compositional consistency across conversation contexts is difficult, as agents may experience semantic drift, leading to misinterpretations and reduced task performance.

4. **Scalability**: Ensuring that multi-agent systems can scale effectively while maintaining performance and safety is challenging, particularly in large-scale, dynamic environments.

5. **Integration of Symbolic and Neural Reasoning**: Combining symbolic reasoning with neural network approaches to handle complex, compositional tasks requires careful design to leverage the strengths of both paradigms.
