1. **Title**: Learning Cell-Aware Hierarchical Multi-Modal Representations for Robust Molecular Modeling (arXiv:2511.21120)
   - **Authors**: Mengran Li, Zelin Zang, Wenbin Xing, Junzhou Chen, Ronghui Zhang, Jiebo Luo, Stan Z. Li
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces CHMR, a framework that integrates molecular structures with cellular responses, such as morphology and gene expression, to model chemical perturbations. It employs a tree-structured vector quantization module to capture hierarchical dependencies across molecular, cellular, and genomic levels, enhancing molecular property prediction.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Bidirectional Hierarchical Protein Multi-Modal Representation Learning (arXiv:2504.04770)
   - **Authors**: Xuefeng Liu, Songhao Jiang, Chih-chan Tien, Jinbo Xu, Rick Stevens
   - **Summary**: The authors propose a multimodal bidirectional hierarchical fusion framework that combines protein sequence and structural information. Utilizing attention and gating mechanisms, the model facilitates effective interaction between sequential and structural features, improving performance across various protein-related tasks.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Hierarchical Multi-Label Contrastive Learning for Protein-Protein Interaction Prediction Across Organisms (arXiv:2507.02724)
   - **Authors**: Shiyi Liu, Buwen Liang, Yuetong Fang, Zixuan Jiang, Renjing Xu
   - **Summary**: HIPPO is introduced as a hierarchical contrastive framework for predicting protein-protein interactions. It aligns protein sequences and their hierarchical attributes through multi-tiered biological representation matching, demonstrating strong zero-shot transferability to other species without retraining.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: HyboWaveNet: Hyperbolic Graph Neural Networks with Multi-Scale Wavelet Transform for Protein-Protein Interaction Prediction (arXiv:2504.20102)
   - **Authors**: Qingzhi Yu, Shuai Yan, Wenfeng Dai, Xiang Cheng
   - **Summary**: HyboWaveNet combines hyperbolic graph neural networks with multi-scale wavelet transforms to predict protein-protein interactions. By mapping protein features to Lorentz space and integrating wavelet transforms, the model captures hierarchical and scale-free biological relationships effectively.
   - **Year**: 2025

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Data Integration Across Scales**: Combining data from molecular, cellular, and phenotypic levels poses significant challenges due to differences in data types, resolutions, and measurement techniques.

2. **Capturing Hierarchical Dependencies**: Developing models that accurately represent the hierarchical nature of biological systems, from genes to phenotypes, requires sophisticated architectures capable of learning complex relationships.

3. **Generalization Across Biological Contexts**: Ensuring that learned representations generalize well across different biological contexts, such as various cell types or organisms, is crucial for the applicability of models in diverse settings.

4. **Interpretability of Learned Representations**: Understanding the features and mechanisms captured by models is essential for gaining biological insights and for the trustworthiness of predictions in critical applications like drug discovery.

5. **Limited Annotated Data**: The scarcity of annotated datasets across multiple biological scales hampers the training and validation of models, necessitating the development of methods that can leverage limited labeled data effectively.
