1. **Title**: Bias in the Loop: How Humans Evaluate AI-Generated Suggestions (arXiv:2509.08514)
   - **Authors**: Jacob Beck, Stephanie Eckman, Christoph Kern, Frauke Kreuter
   - **Summary**: This study investigates cognitive biases in human-AI collaboration, revealing that task design and individual attitudes toward AI significantly influence performance. Findings indicate that requiring corrections for AI errors can reduce engagement and increase acceptance of incorrect suggestions, while skepticism toward AI correlates with better error detection and higher accuracy.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Fostering Human Learning is Crucial for Boosting Human-AI Synergy (arXiv:2512.13253)
   - **Authors**: Julian Berger, Jason W. Burton, Ralph Hertwig, Thomas Kosch, Ralf H. J. M. Kurvers, Benito Kurzenberger, Christopher Lazik, Linda Onnasch, Tobias Rieger, Anna I. Thoma, Dirk U. Wulff, Stefan M. Herzog
   - **Summary**: This meta-analysis re-examines 74 studies on human-AI collaboration, emphasizing the importance of human learning mechanisms. It finds that providing outcome feedback and AI explanations together enhances synergy, suggesting that fostering human learning is essential for effective human-AI partnerships.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Scientific Judgment Drifts Over Time in AI Ideation (arXiv:2511.04964)
   - **Authors**: Lingyu Zhang, Mitchell Wang, Boyuan Chen
   - **Summary**: This research reveals that scientists' evaluations of AI-generated research ideas are not static but drift over time. A two-wave study shows systematic increases in quality scores and moderate test-retest reliability, highlighting the need for AI systems to adapt to evolving human standards in scientific judgment.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Moral Anchor System: A Predictive Framework for AI Value Alignment and Drift Prevention (arXiv:2510.04073)

MAS combines Bayesian inference, LSTM networks, and a human-centric governance layer to maintain AI behaviors consistent with human ethics and intentions over time.

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Temporal Bias Drift**: Human biases and AI recommendations can coevolve over time, leading to the amplification of subtle biases in decision-making processes. Current methods often fail to capture these dynamic changes, necessitating frameworks that monitor and mitigate temporal bias drift.

2. **Human Learning Mechanisms**: Effective human-AI collaboration requires fostering human learning. Many existing systems overlook design features that promote learning, such as providing outcome feedback, which can hinder the development of synergistic human-AI partnerships.

3. **Value Alignment and Drift Prevention**: Ensuring that AI systems remain aligned with human values over time is challenging due to potential value drift. Developing predictive frameworks and adaptive interventions is crucial to maintain consistent and ethical AI behaviors.

4. **Balancing Automation and Human Control**: Striking the right balance between automation and human oversight is essential to prevent overreliance on AI and to maintain human engagement in decision-making processes.

5. **Multi-Agent System Dynamics**: In environments with multiple AI agents, goal conflicts and collective behaviors can emerge, complicating alignment efforts. Addressing these challenges requires systemic approaches that consider the interactions and potential conflicts among AI agents.
