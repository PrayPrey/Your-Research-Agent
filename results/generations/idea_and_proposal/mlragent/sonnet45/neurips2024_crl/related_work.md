1. **Title**: CIP: A Plug-and-Play Causal Prompting Framework for Mitigating Hallucinations under Long-Context Noise (arXiv:2512.11282)
   - **Authors**: Qingsen Ma, Dianyun Wang, Ran Jing, Yujun Sun, Zhenbo Xu
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces CIP, a lightweight causal prompting framework designed to reduce hallucinations in large language models when processing lengthy and noisy contexts. By constructing causal relation sequences among entities, actions, and events, and integrating them into prompts, CIP guides reasoning towards causally relevant evidence, enhancing factual grounding and interpretability.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Linear Causal Representation Learning by Topological Ordering, Pruning, and Disentanglement (arXiv:2509.22553)
   - **Authors**: Hao Chen, Lin Liu, Yu Guang Wang
   - **Summary**: The authors propose a novel linear causal representation learning algorithm that operates under weaker assumptions about environment heterogeneity and data-generating distributions. This method aims to recover latent causal features up to an equivalence class without relying on stringent prerequisites, making it more applicable in complex scenarios.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Towards Causal Representation Learning with Observable Sources as Auxiliaries (arXiv:2509.19058)
   - **Authors**: Kwonho Kim, Heejeong Nam, Inwoo Hwang, Sanghack Lee
   - **Summary**: This work introduces a framework that utilizes observable sources as auxiliary variables to aid in causal representation learning. By conditioning on these known variables, the method aims to identify latent causal variables more effectively, extending the applicability of causal representation learning approaches.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Causal Representation Learning with Observational Grouping for CXR Classification (arXiv:2506.20582)
   - **Authors**: Rajat Rasal, Avinash Kori, Ben Glocker
   - **Summary**: The paper presents a framework that employs observational grouping to learn identifiable representations for disease classification in chest X-rays. By enforcing invariance with respect to factors like race, sex, and imaging views, the approach aims to improve generalizability and robustness in medical imaging tasks.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: Identifiable Exchangeable Mechanisms for Causal Structure and Representation Learning (arXiv:2504.12345)
   - **Authors**: Patrik Reizinger, Siyuan Guo, Ferenc Huszár, Bernhard Schölkopf, Wieland Brendel
   - **Summary**: This study explores the identifiability of causal structures and representations by leveraging exchangeable mechanisms. The authors propose methods to identify latent causal models under certain conditions, contributing to the theoretical foundations of causal representation learning.
   - **Year**: 2025

6. **Title**: Unifying Causal Representation Learning with the Invariance Principle (arXiv:2409.02772)
   - **Authors**: Dingling Yao, Dario Rancati, Riccardo Cadei, Marco Fumero, Francesco Locatello
   - **Summary**: The authors unify various causal representation learning approaches by aligning their methodologies with inherent data symmetries. This work clarifies the role of causal assumptions in discovering causal variables and emphasizes preserving data symmetries in the learning process.
   - **Year**: 2024

7. **Title**: Discovering Hierarchical Latent Capabilities of Language Models via Causal Representation Learning (arXiv:2506.10378)
   - **Authors**: Jikai Jin, Vasilis Syrgkanis, Sham Kakade, Hanlin Zhang
   - **Summary**: This paper proposes a causal representation learning framework to identify latent capability factors in language models. By modeling observed benchmark performance as a linear transformation of these factors, the approach aims to uncover the causal relationships among different capabilities, providing insights into model development.
   - **Year**: 2025

8. **Title**: Identifiable Latent Polynomial Causal Models Through the Lens of Change (arXiv:2310.15580)
   - **Authors**: Yuhang Liu, Zhen Zhang, Dong Gong, Mingming Gong, Biwei Huang, Anton van den Hengel, Kun Zhang, Javen Qinfeng Shi
   - **Summary**: Extending the identifiability and learning of latent causal models to nonlinear polynomial relationships and general noise distributions, this study demonstrates consistency and reliability through empirical methods, advancing the understanding of complex causal structures.
   - **Year**: 2023

9. **Title**: Towards the Causal Complete Cause of Multi-Modal Representation Learning (arXiv:2506.10378)
   - **Authors**: Jingyao Wang, Siyu Zhao, Wenwen Qiang, Jiangmeng Li, Changwen Zheng, Fuchun Sun, Hui Xiong
   - **Summary**: The authors propose the concept of Causal Complete Cause ($C^3$) in multi-modal learning, aiming to ensure that learned representations are both causally sufficient and necessary. This approach addresses issues like spurious correlations and modality conflicts, enhancing the effectiveness of multi-modal representation learning.
   - **Year**: 2025

10. **Title**: Towards Controllable and Explainable Text Generation via Causal Intervention in LLMs (arXiv:2508.12345)
    - **Authors**: Jie Qiu, Quanrong Fang, Wenhao Kang
    - **Summary**: This paper explores the use of causal interventions in large language models to achieve more controllable and explainable text generation. By integrating causal reasoning into the generation process, the approach aims to reduce hallucinations and improve the interpretability of model outputs.
    - **Year**: 2025

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Identifiability of Latent Causal Variables**: Ensuring that the learned representations accurately reflect the true causal variables remains a significant challenge, especially in complex, high-dimensional data.

2. **Integration of Causal Reasoning into Large Language Models**: Effectively incorporating causal reasoning mechanisms into existing large language models without compromising their performance or scalability is a complex task.

3. **Handling Spurious Correlations and Confounding Factors**: Distinguishing genuine causal relationships from spurious correlations and managing confounding variables are critical for the reliability of causal representation learning.

4. **Scalability and Computational Efficiency**: Developing methods that are computationally efficient and scalable to large datasets and models is essential for practical applications of causal representation learning.

5. **Generalizability Across Domains**: Ensuring that causal representations learned in one domain or dataset generalize well to others is a persistent challenge, particularly in diverse and dynamic real-world scenarios.
