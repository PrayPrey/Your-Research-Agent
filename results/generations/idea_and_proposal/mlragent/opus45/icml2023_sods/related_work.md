1. **Title**: D3PO: Preference-Based Alignment of Discrete Diffusion Models (arXiv:2503.08295)
   - **Authors**: Umberto Borso, Davide Paglieri, Jude Wells, Tim Rocktäschel
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces D3PO, an adaptation of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for discrete diffusion models formulated as continuous-time Markov chains. D3PO fine-tunes the generative process using preference data without requiring explicit reward models, demonstrating effectiveness in aligning model outputs with preferences while maintaining structural validity.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: DiffPattern-Flex: Efficient Layout Pattern Generation via Discrete Diffusion (arXiv:2505.04173)
   - **Authors**: Zixiao Wang, Wenqian Zhao, Yunheng Shen, Yang Bai, Guojin Chen, Farzan Farnia, Bei Yu
   - **Summary**: The authors present DiffPattern-Flex, a novel approach for generating reliable layout patterns efficiently. It employs a discrete diffusion model to generate diverse topologies and utilizes an optimization-based, white-box pattern assessment to ensure legal pattern generation, outperforming existing methods in producing reliable layout patterns.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Hierarchical Koopman Diffusion: Fast Generation with Interpretable Diffusion Trajectory (arXiv:2510.12220)
   - **Authors**: Hanru Bai, Weiyang Ding, Difan Zou
   - **Summary**: This work introduces Hierarchical Koopman Diffusion, a framework that achieves one-step sampling and interpretable generative trajectories by lifting nonlinear diffusion dynamics into a latent space governed by globally linear operators. The hierarchical architecture captures multi-scale image details, bridging the gap between fast sampling and interpretability in diffusion models.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Critical Iterative Denoising: A Discrete Generative Model Applied to Graphs (arXiv:2503.21592)
   - **Authors**: Yoann Boget, Alexandros Kalousis
   - **Summary**: The authors propose Iterative Denoising, a framework that simplifies discrete diffusion models by assuming conditional independence across time, addressing error accumulation issues. Enhanced with a Critic component, the model selectively retains or corrupts elements based on their likelihood, significantly outperforming existing discrete diffusion baselines in graph generation tasks.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: Generalized Interpolating Discrete Diffusion
   - **Authors**: D. von Rütte, J. Fluri, Y. Ding, A. Orvieto, B. Schölkopf, T. Hofmann
   - **Summary**: This paper presents a comprehensive framework for error analysis of discrete diffusion models using Lévy-type integrals, unifying and strengthening current theoretical results. The framework provides insights into the convergence and stability of discrete diffusion processes, contributing to a deeper understanding of their behavior.
   - **Year**: 2025

6. **Title**: Beyond Autoregression: Discrete Diffusion for Complex Reasoning and Planning (arXiv:2410.14157)
   - **Authors**: Jiacheng Ye, Jiahui Gao, Shansan Gong, Lin Zheng, Xin Jiang, Zhenguo Li, Lingpeng Kong
   - **Summary**: The authors introduce discrete diffusion models as a solution to challenges in complex reasoning and long-term planning tasks. They propose Multi-Granularity Diffusion Modeling (MGDM), which prioritizes subgoals based on difficulty during learning, significantly outperforming autoregressive models without using search techniques.
   - **Year**: 2025

7. **Title**: Fine-Tuning Discrete Diffusion Models via Reward Optimization with Applications to DNA and Protein Design (arXiv:2410.13643)
   - **Authors**: Chenyu Wang, Masatoshi Uehara, Yichun He, Amy Wang, Tommaso Biancalani, Avantika Lal, Tommi Jaakkola, Sergey Levine, Hanchen Wang, Aviv Regev
   - **Summary**: This work proposes an algorithm that enables direct backpropagation of rewards through entire trajectories generated by diffusion models. The method, DRAKES, generates sequences that are both natural-like and yield high rewards, demonstrating effectiveness in DNA and protein design applications.
   - **Year**: 2025

8. **Title**: Scalable Discrete Diffusion Samplers: Combinatorial Optimization and Statistical Physics (arXiv:2502.08696)
   - **Authors**: Sebastian Sanokowski, Wilhelm Berghammer, Martin Ennemoser, Haoyu Peter Wang, Sepp Hochreiter, Sebastian Lehner
   - **Summary**: The authors introduce two novel training methods for discrete diffusion samplers, yielding memory-efficient training and achieving state-of-the-art results in unsupervised optimization. They adapt SN-NIS and Neural Chain Monte Carlo, enabling the application of discrete diffusion models to combinatorial optimization and statistical physics problems.
   - **Year**: 2025

9. **Title**: Fine-Tuning Discrete Diffusion Models with Policy Gradient Methods (arXiv:2502.01384)
   - **Authors**: Oussama Zekri, Nicolas Boullé
   - **Summary**: This paper proposes an efficient, broadly applicable, and theoretically justified policy gradient algorithm for fine-tuning discrete diffusion models over non-differentiable rewards. Numerical experiments across several discrete generative tasks demonstrate the scalability and efficiency of the method.
   - **Year**: 2025

10. **Title**: Nested Diffusion Models Using Hierarchical Latent Priors (arXiv:2412.05984)
    - **Authors**: Xiao Zhang, Ruoxi Jiang, Rebecca Willett, Michael Maire
    - **Summary**: The authors introduce nested diffusion models, a hierarchical generative framework that enhances generation quality by employing a series of diffusion models to progressively generate latent variables at different semantic levels. This approach captures intricate structural details and demonstrates significant enhancements in image quality.
    - **Year**: 2025

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Capturing Long-Range Dependencies**: Effectively modeling long-range, high-order correlations in discrete structures remains a significant challenge, as existing methods often struggle to capture complex dependencies inherent in tasks like language modeling and protein sequence generation.

2. **Efficient Sampling in High-Dimensional Spaces**: Navigating high-dimensional discrete spaces efficiently is difficult due to the combinatorial explosion of possible configurations, leading to slow convergence and increased computational costs.

3. **Handling Black-Box Objectives**: Optimizing discrete structures with black-box objectives is challenging, as gradient information is often unavailable or unreliable, limiting the applicability of gradient-based methods.

4. **Balancing Exploration and Exploitation**: Ensuring sufficient exploration of the solution space while exploiting known good solutions is a delicate balance, particularly in discrete optimization tasks where local optima can trap algorithms.

5. **Maintaining Structural Validity**: Generating discrete structures that are both valid and diverse is challenging, as models must ensure that generated samples adhere to specific constraints and exhibit the desired properties.
