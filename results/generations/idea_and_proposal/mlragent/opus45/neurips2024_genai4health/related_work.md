1. **Title**: ArGen: Auto-Regulation of Generative AI via GRPO and Policy-as-Code (arXiv:2509.07006)
   - **Authors**: Kapil Madan
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces ArGen, a framework designed to align Large Language Models (LLMs) with complex, machine-readable rules encompassing ethical principles, operational safety protocols, and regulatory compliance standards. ArGen integrates principle-based automated reward scoring, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), and an Open Policy Agent (OPA)-inspired governance layer to ensure LLMs adhere to diverse governance requirements. A case study involving a medical AI assistant guided by Dharmic ethics demonstrates ArGen's adaptability, achieving a 70.9% improvement in domain-specific adherence over the baseline.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Agentic-AI Healthcare: Multilingual, Privacy-First Framework with MCP Agents (arXiv:2510.02325)
   - **Authors**: Mohammed A. Shehab
   - **Summary**: This paper presents Agentic-AI Healthcare, a privacy-aware, multilingual, and explainable research prototype that utilizes the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to orchestrate multiple intelligent agents for patient interactions, including symptom checking, medication suggestions, and appointment scheduling. The platform features a Privacy and Compliance Layer implementing role-based access control, AES-GCM field-level encryption, and tamper-evident audit logging, aligning with major healthcare data protection standards such as HIPAA, PIPEDA, and PHIPA.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Explainable Biomedical Claim Verification with Large Language Models (arXiv:2502.21014)
   - **Authors**: Siting Liang, Daniel Sonntag
   - **Summary**: This study introduces an interactive biomedical claim verification system that integrates Large Language Models (LLMs), transparent model explanations, and user-guided justification. The system enables users to retrieve relevant scientific studies and assess how different LLMs perform natural language inference to classify each study as "Support," "Contradict," or "Not Enough Information" regarding a claim. SHAP values provide word-level contributions to the final result, enhancing transparency and interpretability in AI-assisted decision-making within biomedical contexts.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: AI-generated data contamination erodes pathological variability and diagnostic reliability (arXiv:2601.12946)
   - **Authors**: Hongyu He, Shaowen Xiang, Ye Zhang, Yingtao Zhu, Jin Zhang, Hao Deng, Emily Alsentzer, Qingyu Chen, Kun-Hsing Yu, Andrew Marmenshall, Tingting Chen, Srinivas Anumasa, Daniel Ebner, Dean Ho, Kee Yuan Ngiam, Ching-Yu Cheng, Dianbo Liu
   - **Summary**: This research investigates the clinical consequences of AI-generated data contamination in medical records. The study reveals that without mandatory human verification, the self-referential cycle of AI-generated data leads to a rapid erosion of pathological variability and diagnostic reliability. Analysis of over 800,000 synthetic data points across clinical text generation, vision-language reporting, and medical image synthesis shows that models progressively converge toward generic phenotypes, with rare but critical findings disappearing from synthetic content.
   - **Year**: 2026

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Automated Policy Translation**: Accurately translating complex and evolving healthcare regulations into machine-verifiable constraints remains a significant challenge due to the nuanced and context-dependent nature of legal language.

2. **Real-Time Compliance Monitoring**: Developing efficient and lightweight runtime monitors that can continuously assess GenAI applications for compliance without introducing significant latency or performance degradation is complex.

3. **Data Privacy and Security**: Ensuring that compliance verification processes do not compromise patient data privacy and adhere to stringent data protection standards is critical.

4. **Interdisciplinary Collaboration**: Bridging the gap between policymakers, healthcare professionals, and AI developers to create cohesive and comprehensive compliance frameworks requires effective interdisciplinary communication and collaboration.

5. **Adaptability to Regulatory Changes**: Healthcare regulations are subject to frequent updates; thus, compliance frameworks must be adaptable and capable of integrating new requirements promptly to remain effective.
