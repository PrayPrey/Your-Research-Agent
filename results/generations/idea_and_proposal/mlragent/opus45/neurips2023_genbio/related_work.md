1. **Title**: GAPMAP: Mapping Scientific Knowledge Gaps in Biomedical Literature Using Large Language Models (arXiv:2510.25055)
   - **Authors**: Nourah M. Salem, Elizabeth White, Michael Bada, Lawrence Hunter
   - **Summary**: This study introduces GAPMAP, a framework that employs large language models (LLMs) to identify both explicit and implicit knowledge gaps in biomedical literature. The authors define explicit gaps as clear declarations of missing knowledge and implicit gaps as context-inferred missing knowledge. They conducted experiments on nearly 1,500 documents across four datasets, demonstrating that LLMs can effectively detect these gaps, thereby supporting early-stage research formulation and policy decisions.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Towards Enhancing Relational Rules for Knowledge Graph Link Prediction (arXiv:2310.13411)
   - **Authors**: Shuhan Wu, Huaiyu Wan, Wei Chen, Yuting Wu, Junfeng Shen, Youfang Lin
   - **Summary**: This paper presents RUN-GNN, a novel approach that enhances relational rules for knowledge graph reasoning. RUN-GNN employs a query-related fusion gate unit to model the sequentiality of relation composition and utilizes a buffering update mechanism to address lagged entity information propagation. The method demonstrates superior performance in both transductive and inductive link prediction tasks.
   - **Year**: 2023

4. **Title**: Meta-Weight Graph Neural Network: Push the Limits Beyond Global Homophily (arXiv:2203.10280)
   - **Authors**: Xiaojun Ma, Qin Chen, Yuanyi Ren, Guojie Song, Liang Wang
   - **Summary**: The authors propose the Meta-Weight Graph Neural Network (MWGNN), which adapts graph convolution layers for different nodes by modeling Node Local Distribution (NLD) from node features, topological structure, and positional identity. MWGNN generates adaptive graph convolutions to perform node-specific weighted aggregation, enhancing the expressive power of GNNs in handling graphs with various distributions.
   - **Year**: 2022

6. **Title**: Graph Convolutional Networks for Graphs Containing Missing Features (arXiv:2007.04583)
   - **Authors**: Hibiki Taguchi, Xin Liu, Tsuyoshi Murata
   - **Summary**: This study addresses the challenge of applying Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to graphs with missing node features. The authors propose an approach that integrates the processing of missing features and graph learning within the same neural network architecture by representing missing data using a Gaussian Mixture Model. This method significantly outperforms traditional imputation-based methods in node classification and link prediction tasks.
   - **Year**: 2020

7. **Title**: Heterogeneous Graph based Deep Learning for Biomedical Network Link Prediction (arXiv:2102.01649)
   - **Authors**: Jinjiang Guo, Jie Li, Dawei Leng, Lurong Pan
   - **Summary**: The authors propose the Graph Pair based Link Prediction model (GPLP), a Graph Neural Network method for predicting links in biomedical networks using topological interaction information. GPLP learns from 1-hop subgraphs extracted from known network interaction matrices and demonstrates superior performance over state-of-the-art baselines in predicting drug-target interactions, compound-protein interactions, and compound-virus inhibition networks.
   - **Year**: 2021

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Integration of LLMs with Knowledge Graphs**: Effectively combining large language models with dynamic knowledge graphs to accurately identify and represent complex biological relationships remains a significant challenge.

2. **Handling Incomplete and Noisy Data**: Biological datasets often contain missing or noisy information, which can hinder the performance of models designed for knowledge gap detection and hypothesis generation.

3. **Biological Plausibility Assessment**: Ensuring that the hypotheses generated by AI systems are biologically plausible and relevant requires sophisticated reasoning modules and validation mechanisms.

4. **Experimental Design Automation**: Developing AI systems capable of suggesting feasible and effective experimental validation strategies, including the use of generative AI tools for molecule design, is a complex task.

5. **Evaluation and Benchmarking**: Establishing robust benchmarks and evaluation metrics for assessing the performance of AI-driven knowledge gap detection and hypothesis generation systems is essential for their development and adoption.
