# Title: Hypothesis-Driven Biological Discovery via LLM-Guided Knowledge Gap Detection and Experimental Design

## Motivation:
Despite the explosion of biomedical literature (over 1 million papers annually), researchers struggle to identify knowledge gaps and formulate novel hypotheses that connect disparate findings. Current literature review approaches are manual, biased toward familiar domains, and miss cross-disciplinary connections. This creates a critical bottleneck: generative AI can design novel biomolecules, but identifying *which* molecules to design and *why* remains largely human-intuition-driven. Bridging this gap could accelerate scientific discovery by systematically uncovering unexplored biological questions worth investigating.

## Main Idea:
We propose **BioGapFinder**, a framework that leverages LLMs to systematically identify knowledge gaps in biological literature and generate testable hypotheses with corresponding experimental designs. The methodology involves: (1) constructing a dynamic knowledge graph from literature using LLM-based information extraction, capturing entities (genes, proteins, diseases) and their relationships with confidence scores; (2) training a graph neural network on this structure to predict "missing links" representing unexplored but plausible biological connections; (3) using an LLM-based reasoning module to evaluate biological plausibility and generate ranked hypotheses with mechanistic explanations; (4) automatically suggesting experimental validation strategies, including which generative AI tools (e.g., protein design models) could help test each hypothesis. We will evaluate on retrospective benchmarks (predicting discoveries from held-out recent papers) and prospective wet-lab validation. This closes the loop between generative biology and discovery, ensuring AI-designed molecules address meaningful biological questions.
