**Related Papers**

1. **Title**: "I am here for you": How relational conversational AI appeals to adolescents, especially those who are socially and emotionally vulnerable (arXiv:2512.15117)
   - **Authors**: Pilyoung Kim, Yun Xie, Sujin Yang
   - **Summary**: This study examines how conversational AI chatbots with relational styles (using first-person, affiliative, and commitment language) are perceived by adolescents aged 11-15. The findings indicate that adolescents, particularly those with higher stress and anxiety levels, prefer relational chatbots over transparent ones, perceiving them as more human-like, trustworthy, and emotionally close.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: AnnaAgent: Dynamic Evolution Agent System with Multi-Session Memory for Realistic Seeker Simulation (arXiv:2506.00551)
   - **Authors**: Ming Wang, Peidong Wang, Lin Wu, Xiaocui Yang, Daling Wang, Shi Feng, Yuxin Chen, Bixuan Wang, Yifei Zhang
   - **Summary**: AnnaAgent introduces an emotional and cognitive dynamic agent system equipped with tertiary memory to simulate seekers in psychological counseling. It incorporates an emotion modulator and a complaint elicitor trained on real counseling dialogues, enabling dynamic control of the simulator's configurations and effectively integrating short-term and long-term memory across sessions.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Controlled Yet Natural: A Hybrid BDI-LLM Conversational Agent for Child Helpline Training (arXiv:2509.16784)
   - **Authors**: Mohammed Al Owayyed, Adarsh Denga, Willem-Paul Brinkman
   - **Summary**: This paper presents a hybrid interactive agent that integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) into a rule-based Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) framework to simulate realistic virtual child chat conversations for helpline training. The system incorporates LLMs into intent recognition, response generation, and a bypass mechanism, resulting in more believable and engaging interactions compared to traditional rule-based agents.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Roleplaying with Structure: Synthetic Therapist-Client Conversation Generation from Questionnaires (arXiv:2510.25384)
   - **Authors**: Doan Nam Long Vu, Rui Tan, Lena Moench, Svenja Jule Francke, Daniel Woiwod, Florian Thomas-Odenthal, Sanna Stroth, Tilo Kircher, Christiane Hermann, Udo Dannlowski, Hamidreza Jamalabadi, Shaoxiong Ji
   - **Summary**: The authors propose SQPsych, an LLM-driven pipeline that generates synthetic counseling dialogues based on structured client profiles and psychological questionnaires. Grounded in Cognitive Behavioral Therapy principles, this method creates therapeutic conversations for disorders like anxiety and depression, addressing the scarcity of authentic therapy dialogues due to privacy regulations.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: Mental-LLM: Leveraging Large Language Models for Mental Health Prediction via Online Text Data (arXiv:2307.14385)
   - **Authors**: Xuhai Xu, Bingsheng Yao, Yuanzhe Dong, Saadia Gabriel, Hong Yu, James Hendler, Marzyeh Ghassemi, Anind K. Dey, Dakuo Wang
   - **Summary**: This work evaluates multiple LLMs on mental health prediction tasks using online text data. The study demonstrates that instruction fine-tuning significantly enhances LLM performance across tasks, with the fine-tuned models outperforming larger models like GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 in balanced accuracy.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: From Keyboard to Chatbot: An AI-powered Integration Platform with Large-Language Models for Teaching Computational Thinking for Young Children (arXiv:2405.00750)
   - **Authors**: Changjae Lee, Jinjun Xiong
   - **Summary**: The authors present an AI-powered integration platform that utilizes LLMs to teach computational thinking to children aged 4-9. The system features a hybrid pedagogy supporting both top-down and bottom-up approaches, allowing children to describe tasks in natural language, which the system translates into decomposed sub-tasks executed by a tangible robot, eliminating the need for keyboarding.
   - **Year**: 2024

7. **Title**: Sub-Goal Distillation: A Method to Improve Small Language Agents (arXiv:2405.02749)
   - **Authors**: Maryam Hashemzadeh, Elias Stengel-Eskin, Sarath Chandar, Marc-Alexandre Côté
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces a hierarchical agent comprising a planning module that learns through Knowledge Distillation from an LLM to generate sub-goals, and an execution module that accomplishes these sub-goals using elementary actions. The method reduces computational costs associated with LLM interactions while maintaining performance in interactive tasks.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges**

1. **Data Privacy and Ethical Concerns**: Developing AI systems for mental health, especially for children, involves handling sensitive data, raising significant privacy and ethical issues. Ensuring data confidentiality and obtaining informed consent are critical challenges.

2. **Age-Appropriate Interaction Design**: Creating conversational agents that effectively engage children across different developmental stages requires tailoring language complexity, content, and interaction styles to be age-appropriate and engaging.

3. **Accuracy and Reliability of Diagnoses**: Ensuring that AI-based screening tools accurately detect early signs of anxiety disorders and do not produce false positives or negatives is crucial for their effectiveness and trustworthiness.

4. **Integration of Multi-Modal Data**: Incorporating and effectively analyzing multi-modal data, such as speech prosody, response latency, and text, to improve detection accuracy presents technical challenges in data processing and model development.

5. **Accessibility and Deployment in Low-Resource Settings**: Deploying AI-based screening tools in low-resource settings requires addressing challenges related to infrastructure, internet connectivity, and device availability to ensure equitable access to mental health resources.
