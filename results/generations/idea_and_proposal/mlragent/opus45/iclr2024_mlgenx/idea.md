# Title: Active Learning with Uncertainty-Guided Perturbation Selection for Efficient Gene Function Discovery

## Motivation
Perturbation biology experiments (e.g., CRISPR screens) are essential for understanding gene function and identifying drug targets, but testing all possible genetic perturbations is prohibitively expensive. Current approaches either rely on random selection or simple heuristics, leading to inefficient experimental designs. A key challenge is that perturbation effects are highly context-dependent and exhibit complex interactions, making it difficult to predict which experiments will yield the most informative results. Developing intelligent active learning strategies that quantify uncertainty and prioritize high-value experiments could dramatically accelerate target identification while reducing costs.

## Main Idea
We propose an uncertainty-aware active learning framework that integrates foundation models for genomics with Bayesian deep learning to guide iterative perturbation experiment selection. The approach consists of: (1) training an ensemble of neural networks on existing perturbation data (e.g., Perturb-seq) to predict transcriptional outcomes, (2) decomposing predictive uncertainty into epistemic (model) and aleatoric (data) components to identify genes where additional experiments would maximally reduce uncertainty, and (3) incorporating a diversity-promoting acquisition function that balances exploration of uncertain regions with exploitation of promising target candidates. We will validate on held-out CRISPR screen data, measuring both prediction improvement and downstream target identification accuracy. Expected outcomes include 2-3x reduction in required experiments compared to random baselines, with direct applicability to drug discovery pipelines.
