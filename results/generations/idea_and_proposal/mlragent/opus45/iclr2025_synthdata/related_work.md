1. **Title**: On the Dangers of Bootstrapping Generation for Continual Learning and Beyond (arXiv:2512.11867)
   - **Authors**: Daniil Zverev, A. Sophia Koepke, Joao F. Henriques
   - **Summary**: This paper investigates the consequences of using synthetically generated data for training models, particularly focusing on continual learning. The authors highlight concerns about distribution drift and performance degradation due to repeated training on synthetic data. They present a statistical analysis showing that synthetic data introduces significant bias and variance into training objectives, weakening the reliability of maximum likelihood estimation. Empirical evidence demonstrates that popular generative models collapse under repeated training with synthetic data, raising critical concerns about its use in continual learning.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: A Closer Look at Model Collapse: From a Generalization-to-Memorization Perspective (arXiv:2509.16499)
   - **Authors**: Lianghe Shi, Meng Wu, Huijie Zhang, Zekai Zhang, Molei Tao, Qing Qu
   - **Summary**: This study examines model collapse in diffusion models, where iterative training on synthetic data leads to a transition from generalization to memorization. The authors identify that this transition is driven by declining entropy in the synthetic training data, causing models to replicate training data instead of generating novel content. They propose an entropy-based data selection strategy to mitigate this issue, showing that it significantly enhances visual quality and diversity in recursive generation, effectively preventing collapse.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Collapse or Thrive? Perils and Promises of Synthetic Data in a Self-Generating World (arXiv:2410.16713)
   - **Authors**: Joshua Kazdan, Rylan Schaeffer, Apratim Dey, Matthias Gerstgrasser, Rafael Rafailov, David L. Donoho, Sanmi Koyejo
   - **Summary**: This paper explores the implications of generative models being pretrained on datasets containing data generated by earlier models. The authors confirm that replacing real data with successive generations of synthetic data leads to model collapse. However, they also demonstrate that accumulating synthetic data alongside real data can prevent collapse, with models remaining stable and test losses not diverging. The study provides insights into managing synthetic data to avoid degradation in model performance.
   - **Year**: 2024

4. **Title**: Generative Conformal Prediction with Vectorized Non-Conformity Scores (arXiv:2410.13735)
   - **Authors**: Minxing Zheng, Shixiang Zhu
   - **Summary**: This work addresses the limitations of conventional conformal prediction methods, which often produce overly conservative uncertainty sets. The authors propose a generative conformal prediction framework that leverages a generative model to sample multiple predictions from the fitted data distribution. By computing non-conformity scores across these samples and estimating empirical quantiles at different density levels, they construct adaptive uncertainty sets, enhancing both flexibility and efficiency. The method outperforms state-of-the-art techniques on synthetic and real-world datasets.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Mode Collapse**: Synthetic data generators often produce samples that cluster around common patterns, missing rare but important edge cases. This leads to models that perform well on average but fail in underrepresented scenarios.

2. **Distribution Drift**: Repeated training on synthetic data can cause distributional mismatches between synthetic and real data, leading to performance degradation and reduced model reliability.

3. **Bias and Variance Introduction**: Synthetic data can introduce significant bias and variance into training objectives, weakening the reliability of maximum likelihood estimation and affecting model performance.

4. **Transition from Generalization to Memorization**: Iterative training on synthetic data can cause models to shift from generalizing to memorizing training data, reducing their ability to generate novel content and leading to model collapse.

5. **Evaluation and Verification**: Ensuring that synthetic data adequately represents the target distribution is challenging. Effective evaluation and verification methods are necessary to certify distributional coverage and enhance the reliability of models trained on synthetic data.
