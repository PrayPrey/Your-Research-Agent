## Title: Understanding the Disconnect Between Loss Landscape Flatness and Generalization in Large-Scale Models

## Motivation
The widely-held theoretical belief that "flat minima generalize better" has guided optimizer design (e.g., SAM) and model selection. However, recent empirical observations reveal troubling inconsistencies: (1) large language models often converge to sharp minima yet generalize remarkably well, (2) flatness measures are scale-sensitive and can be manipulated without affecting generalization, and (3) models trained with different batch sizes exhibit similar generalization despite vastly different flatness characteristics. This gap between the flatness-generalization theory and practical outcomes in modern deep learning demands investigation.

## Main Idea
We propose a systematic empirical and theoretical study to identify when and why flatness-based generalization bounds fail for large-scale models. **Methodology:** (1) Conduct controlled experiments across model scales (from small CNNs to billion-parameter transformers) measuring multiple flatness metrics (Hessian eigenspectrum, PAC-Bayes sharpness, average-case perturbation sensitivity) alongside actual generalization gaps; (2) Develop scale-aware flatness measures that account for parameter count and layer-wise heterogeneity; (3) Propose a refined theoretical framework incorporating "effective flatness" that considers the interaction between model architecture, optimization trajectory, and data structure. **Expected Outcomes:** Identification of specific regimes where flatness theory holds/fails, and new generalization bounds that better predict real-world performance. **Impact:** This work will guide practitioners on when flatness-based insights apply and inspire tighter, more practically relevant generalization theory.
