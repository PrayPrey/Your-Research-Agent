1. **Title**: InternVid: A Large-scale Video-Text Dataset for Multimodal Understanding and Generation (arXiv:2307.06942)
   - **Authors**: Yi Wang, Yinan He, Yizhuo Li, Kunchang Li, Jiashuo Yu, Xin Ma, Xinhao Li, Guo Chen, Xinyuan Chen, Yaohui Wang, Conghui He, Ping Luo, Ziwei Liu, Yali Wang, Limin Wang, Yu Qiao
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces InternVid, a vast video-text dataset comprising over 7 million videos and 234 million clips with detailed descriptions. The dataset aims to facilitate learning transferable video-text representations for multimodal understanding and generation.
   - **Year**: 2023

2. **Title**: SSVOD: Semi-Supervised Video Object Detection with Sparse Annotations (arXiv:2309.01391)
   - **Authors**: Tanvir Mahmud, Chun-Hao Liu, Burhaneddin Yaman, Diana Marculescu
   - **Summary**: The authors propose SSVOD, a semi-supervised framework that leverages motion dynamics to utilize large-scale unlabeled frames with sparse annotations. It introduces flow-warped predictions for temporal consistency and combines hard and soft pseudo-labels to balance confirmation bias and uncertainty noise.
   - **Year**: 2023

3. **Title**: LanguageBind: Extending Video-Language Pretraining to N-modality by Language-based Semantic Alignment (arXiv:2310.01852)
   - **Authors**: Bin Zhu, Bin Lin, Munan Ning, Yang Yan, Jiaxi Cui, HongFa Wang, Yatian Pang, Wenhao Jiang, Junwu Zhang, Zongwei Li, Wancai Zhang, Zhifeng Li, Wei Liu, Li Yuan
   - **Summary**: LanguageBind extends video-language pretraining to multiple modalities by using language as a semantic bridge. It introduces VIDAL-10M, a dataset aligning video, infrared, depth, audio, and textual descriptions, and demonstrates superior performance across 15 benchmarks.
   - **Year**: 2023

4. **Title**: VideoChat: Chat-Centric Video Understanding (arXiv:2305.06355)
   - **Authors**: KunChang Li, Yinan He, Yi Wang, Yizhuo Li, Wenhai Wang, Ping Luo, Yali Wang, Limin Wang, Yu Qiao
   - **Summary**: This paper presents VideoChat, an end-to-end system integrating video foundation models and large language models via a neural interface. It excels in spatiotemporal reasoning, event localization, and causal relationship inference, supported by a video-centric instruction dataset.
   - **Year**: 2023

5. **Title**: Synopses of Movie Narratives: a Video-Language Dataset for Story Understanding (arXiv:2203.05711)
   - **Authors**: Yidan Sun, Qin Chao, Yangfeng Ji, Boyang Li
   - **Summary**: The authors introduce SyMoN, a dataset containing 5,193 video summaries of movies and TV series, totaling 869 hours. It emphasizes multimodal story events and mental-state descriptions, providing benchmarks for video-text retrieval and zero-shot alignment.
   - **Year**: 2023

6. **Title**: PolyVivid: Vivid Multi-Subject Video Generation with Cross-Modal Interaction and Enhancement (arXiv:2506.07848)
   - **Authors**: Teng Hu, Zhentao Yu, Zhengguang Zhou, Jiangning Zhang, Yuan Zhou, Qinglin Lu, Ran Yi
   - **Summary**: PolyVivid introduces a framework for multi-subject video customization, enabling flexible and identity-consistent generation. It employs a text-image fusion module to embed visual identities into the textual space for precise grounding.
   - **Year**: 2025

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Annotation Scalability**: Manually annotating large-scale video datasets is labor-intensive and costly, limiting the availability of high-quality annotated data for training video-language models.

2. **Temporal Complexity**: Capturing and understanding temporal relationships and dynamics in videos require sophisticated models capable of processing and reasoning over sequences of frames.

3. **Multimodal Integration**: Effectively integrating and aligning information from multiple modalities (e.g., visual, textual, audio) remains a significant challenge in developing robust video-language models.

4. **Data Quality and Consistency**: Ensuring the quality and consistency of annotations, especially when generated automatically or semi-automatically, is crucial for the reliability of video-language datasets.

5. **Evaluation Benchmarks**: The lack of standardized and comprehensive benchmarks for video-language alignment and understanding hinders the objective evaluation and comparison of different models and approaches.
