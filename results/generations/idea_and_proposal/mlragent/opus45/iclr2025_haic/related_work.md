1. **Title**: Adjust for Trust: Mitigating Trust-Induced Inappropriate Reliance on AI Assistance (arXiv:2502.13321)
   - **Authors**: Tejas Srinivasan, Jesse Thomason
   - **Summary**: This paper explores how user trust biases reliance on AI recommendations in decision-making tasks. It proposes trust-adaptive interventions, such as providing explanations during low trust and inserting forced pauses during high trust, to mitigate inappropriate reliance. The study demonstrates up to a 38% reduction in inappropriate reliance and a 20% improvement in decision accuracy in scenarios involving laypeople and medical professionals.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Engaging with AI: How Interface Design Shapes Human-AI Collaboration in High-Stakes Decision-Making (arXiv:2501.16627)
   - **Authors**: Zichen Chen, Yunhao Luo, Misha Sra
   - **Summary**: This research examines the impact of various decision-support mechanisms on user engagement, trust, and performance in a diabetes management scenario. It evaluates six mechanisms, including text explanations and cognitive forcing functions, finding that certain mechanisms enhance performance and trust, while others increase cognitive effort and reduce performance. The study highlights the importance of balancing design elements to optimize human-AI collaboration.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Who Should I Trust: AI or Myself? Leveraging Human and AI Correctness Likelihood to Promote Appropriate Trust in AI-Assisted Decision-Making (arXiv:2301.05809)
   - **Authors**: Shuai Ma, Ying Lei, Xinru Wang, Chengbo Zheng, Chuhan Shi, Ming Yin, Xiaojuan Ma
   - **Summary**: This paper addresses the challenge of calibrating human trust in AI-assisted decision-making by considering both human and AI correctness likelihoods. It proposes strategies to promote appropriate trust, demonstrating that leveraging both sides' correctness likelihoods leads to more appropriate trust calibration compared to using AI confidence alone.
   - **Year**: 2023

4. **Title**: A Survey on Human-Centered Evaluation of Explainable AI Methods in Clinical Decision Support Systems (arXiv:2502.09849)
   - **Authors**: Alessandro Gambetti, Qiwei Han, Hong Shen, Claudia Soares
   - **Summary**: This survey examines human-centered evaluations of explainable AI (XAI) methods in clinical decision support systems. It categorizes existing works based on XAI methodologies, evaluation frameworks, and clinical adoption challenges, offering a structured understanding of the landscape and proposing a framework to align XAI evaluation methods with clinical needs.
   - **Year**: 2025

7. **Title**: Optimum Signal Duration for Human Activity Recognition Based on Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (arXiv:2406.11164)
   - **Authors**: Farhad Nazari, Arian Shajari, Darius Nahavandi, Navid Mohajer
   - **Summary**: This study investigates the optimal observation period for human activity recognition using inertial measurement unit sensors and deep convolutional neural networks. It finds that a 0.5-second observation duration achieves a classification accuracy of 99.95%, highlighting the importance of precise temporal analysis in human-centered devices.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Trust Calibration**: Developing mechanisms to accurately calibrate clinician trust in AI systems to prevent over-reliance or under-reliance remains a significant challenge.

2. **Temporal Drift Detection**: Identifying and addressing coevolutionary drifts in clinician-AI interactions over time to maintain care quality is complex and underexplored.

3. **Explainability and Transparency**: Ensuring that AI systems provide explanations that are comprehensible and actionable for clinicians to foster trust and effective collaboration.

4. **Regulatory Compliance**: Navigating the diverse and evolving regulatory landscapes across jurisdictions to implement AI systems ethically and legally in healthcare.

5. **Human-AI Interface Design**: Designing interfaces that effectively balance automation and human control, align with user expectations, and promote appropriate engagement without increasing cognitive burden.
