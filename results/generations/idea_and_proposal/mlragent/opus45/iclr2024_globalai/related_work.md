1. **Title**: Cultural Bias in Large Language Models: Evaluating AI Agents through Moral Questionnaires (arXiv:2507.10073)
   - **Authors**: Simon Münker
   - **Summary**: This study assesses the extent to which large language models (LLMs) represent diverse cultural moral frameworks. By applying the Moral Foundations Questionnaire across 19 cultural contexts, the research reveals significant gaps between AI-generated and human moral intuitions, indicating that LLMs homogenize moral diversity and fail to capture nuanced, culturally-specific moral values.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: WorldView-Bench: A Benchmark for Evaluating Global Cultural Perspectives in Large Language Models (arXiv:2505.09595)
   - **Authors**: Abdullah Mushtaq, Imran Taj, Rafay Naeem, Ibrahim Ghaznavi, Junaid Qadir
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces WorldView-Bench, a benchmark designed to evaluate the global cultural inclusivity of LLMs. It measures cultural polarization through free-form generative evaluation and implements strategies to enhance the representation of diverse worldviews, highlighting the potential of multiplex-aware AI evaluation in mitigating cultural bias.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: CultureScope: A Dimensional Lens for Probing Cultural Understanding in LLMs (arXiv:2509.16188)
   - **Authors**: Jinghao Zhang, Sihang Jiang, Shiwei Guo, Shisong Chen, Yanghua Xiao, Hongwei Feng, Jiaqing Liang, Minggui He, Shimin Tao, Hongxia Ma
   - **Summary**: Inspired by the cultural iceberg theory, this work proposes CultureScope, an evaluation framework comprising 140 dimensions to assess cultural understanding in LLMs. The framework guides the automated construction of culture-specific knowledge bases and evaluation datasets, revealing that existing LLMs lack comprehensive cultural competence.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Neuron-Level Analysis of Cultural Understanding in Large Language Models (arXiv:2510.08284)
   - **Authors**: Taisei Yamamoto, Ryoma Kumon, Danushka Bollegala, Hitomi Yanaka
   - **Summary**: This study conducts a neuron-level analysis to identify neurons responsible for cultural behavior in LLMs. It introduces a gradient-based scoring method to pinpoint culture-general and culture-specific neurons, revealing that suppressing these neurons significantly degrades performance on cultural benchmarks without affecting general natural language understanding tasks.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: Entangled in Representations: Mechanistic Investigation of Cultural Biases in Large Language Models (arXiv:2508.08879)
   - **Authors**: Haeun Yu, Seogyeong Jeong, Siddhesh Pawar, Jisu Shin, Jiho Jin, Junho Myung, Alice Oh, Isabelle Augenstein
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces CultureScope, a mechanistic interpretability-based method that probes the internal representations of LLMs to elicit the underlying cultural knowledge space. It reveals that LLMs encode Western-dominance bias and cultural flattening, with low-resource cultures being less susceptible to these biases due to limited training resources.
   - **Year**: 2025

6. **Title**: MCEval: A Dynamic Framework for Fair Multilingual Cultural Evaluation of LLMs (arXiv:2507.09701)
   - **Authors**: Shulin Huang, Linyi Yang, Yue Zhang
   - **Summary**: MCEval is a novel multilingual evaluation framework that employs dynamic cultural question construction and enables causal analysis through counterfactual and confounder rephrasing. It addresses the cultural biases and limited cross-cultural understanding capabilities of LLMs, particularly when serving diverse global user populations.
   - **Year**: 2025

7. **Title**: Detecting Implicit Biases of Large Language Models with Bayesian Hypothesis Testing (Scientific Reports)
   - **Authors**: Si Si, Xiaojun Jiang, Qian Su, et al.
   - **Summary**: This research utilizes Bayesian hypothesis testing to detect implicit biases in LLMs. By analyzing model outputs, the study identifies significant biases in the representation of different cultural groups, emphasizing the need for more robust methods to ensure fairness and cultural sensitivity in AI systems.
   - **Year**: 2025

8. **Title**: Not a Mirror, a Caricature: How LLMs Reproduce Cultural Identity? (AI and Ethics)
   - **Authors**: [Authors not specified]
   - **Summary**: This study examines whether LLMs reproduce human-like cultural identity or produce deterministic cultural prototypes. Using the Self-Construal Scale, the research finds that identity prompts yield almost perfectly separable cultural profiles with near-zero within-culture variance, indicating that LLMs may caricature rather than accurately mirror cultural identities.
   - **Year**: 2025

9. **Title**: Towards Realistic Evaluation of Cultural Value Alignment in Large Language Models: Diversity Enhancement for Survey Response Simulation (Information Processing & Management)
   - **Authors**: Haijiang Liu, Yong Cao, Xun Wu, Chen Qiu, Jinguang Gu, Maofu Liu, Daniel Hershcovich
   - **Summary**: This paper presents a diversity-enhanced framework to improve cultural value alignment and representativeness in LLMs. It designs multi-aspect evaluation metrics to understand cultural value misalignment, revealing divergence and preference biases across models when simulating survey responses in US and Chinese contexts.
   - **Year**: 2025

10. **Title**: Cultural Bias and Cultural Alignment of Large Language Models (PNAS Nexus)
    - **Authors**: Yan Tao
    - **Summary**: This research investigates the cultural biases and alignment of LLMs, analyzing how these models reflect or distort cultural values. It emphasizes the importance of developing AI systems that are culturally aware and aligned to ensure fair and effective deployment across diverse global populations.
    - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Lack of Comprehensive Cultural Representation**: LLMs often fail to capture the full spectrum of cultural values and norms, leading to homogenized outputs that do not reflect the diversity of global cultures.

2. **Implicit Cultural Biases**: These models may inadvertently encode and perpetuate cultural biases present in their training data, resulting in outputs that reinforce stereotypes or misrepresent certain cultural groups.

3. **Evaluation Limitations**: Existing benchmarks and evaluation frameworks may not adequately assess the cultural competence of LLMs, making it challenging to measure and improve their performance in diverse cultural contexts.

4. **Data Scarcity for Underrepresented Cultures**: There is often a lack of high-quality, culturally rich training data for many cultures, leading to models that are less effective in understanding and generating culturally appropriate content for these groups.

5. **Complexity of Cultural Nuances**: Capturing and accurately representing the subtle and complex aspects of cultural values, norms, and sensibilities in AI systems remains a significant challenge, requiring interdisciplinary collaboration and innovative methodologies.
