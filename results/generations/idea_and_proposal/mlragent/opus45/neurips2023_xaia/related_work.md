3. **Title**: Explainable AI in Healthcare: to Explain, to Predict, or to Describe? (arXiv:2508.05753)
   - **Authors**: Alex Carriero, Anne de Hond, Bram Cappers, Fernando Paulovich, Sanne Abeln, Karel GM Moons, Maarten van Smeden
   - **Summary**: This paper examines the role of XAI methods in healthcare, emphasizing the distinction between describing, predicting, and explaining AI model behaviors. The authors argue that while XAI methods are effective descriptive tools, they often fall short in providing causal explanations necessary for actionable advice. This limitation affects the ability to judge the face validity of AI-based models in medical contexts.
   - **Year**: 2025

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Domain-Specific Requirements**: Healthcare and legal domains have unique requirements regarding decision complexity, stakeholder expertise, and regulatory standards. Developing XAI methods that effectively address these specific needs is challenging.

2. **Transferability of Explanation Methods**: Adapting XAI techniques from one domain to another requires careful consideration of domain similarities and differences. Ensuring that transferred explanations maintain their effectiveness and relevance is a significant hurdle.

3. **Evaluation Frameworks**: Establishing robust evaluation frameworks that can assess the quality and trustworthiness of explanations across different domains is essential but complex, given the varying criteria for success in each field.

4. **Legal and Ethical Considerations**: Integrating legal and ethical considerations into XAI systems is crucial, especially in high-stakes domains. Designing explanations that are both legally compliant and ethically sound adds an additional layer of complexity.

5. **Stakeholder Diversity**: Different stakeholders (e.g., clinicians, lawyers, patients, clients) have varying informational needs and levels of expertise. Creating explanations that are comprehensible and useful to all relevant parties is a persistent challenge.
