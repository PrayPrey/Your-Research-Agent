Here is a literature review on the topic of "Adaptive Federated Learning for Privacy-Preserving Disease Surveillance in Low-Resource Settings," focusing on related papers published between 2023 and 2025, and discussing key challenges in the current research.

**1. Related Papers**

1. **Title**: Federated Learning with Gramian Angular Fields for Privacy-Preserving ECG Classification on Heterogeneous IoT Devices (arXiv:2511.03753)
   - **Authors**: Youssef Elmir, Yassine Himeur, Abbes Amira
   - **Summary**: This study presents a federated learning framework that transforms 1D ECG signals into 2D Gramian Angular Field images, enabling efficient feature extraction through Convolutional Neural Networks. The approach ensures sensitive medical data remains local to each device, achieving high classification accuracy across heterogeneous IoT devices.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Federated Learning in Low-Resource Settings: A Chest Imaging Study in Africa—Challenges and Lessons Learned (arXiv:2505.14217)
   - **Authors**: Jorge Fabila, Lidia Garrucho, Víctor M. Campello, Carlos Martín-Isla, Karim Lekadir
   - **Summary**: This study explores the use of federated learning for tuberculosis diagnosis using chest X-rays across eight African countries. It highlights challenges such as poor infrastructure, unreliable internet, limited digital literacy, and weak AI regulations, emphasizing the need for improvements in these areas for broader adoption.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: A Distributed Privacy-Preserving Model for the Detection of Alzheimer’s Disease (arXiv:2312.10237)
   - **Authors**: Paul K. Mandal
   - **Summary**: This paper proposes a vertical federated learning model for Alzheimer's disease detection, enabling collaborative learning across diverse medical data sources while respecting privacy constraints. The model achieves an accuracy rate of 82.9%, demonstrating the potential of federated learning in medical diagnostics.
   - **Year**: 2024

4. **Title**: FEDROBO: Federated Learning Driven Autonomous Inter-Robots Communication for Optimal Chemical Sprays (arXiv:2408.06382)
   - **Authors**: Jannatul Ferdaus, Sameera Pisupati, Mahedi Hasan, Sathwick Paladugu
   - **Summary**: This study introduces a federated learning framework for autonomous robots to optimize chemical spray applications in agriculture. The approach facilitates inter-robot communication, reducing waste and improving crop yields, and highlights challenges such as developing secure communication protocols and ensuring safety and reliability.
   - **Year**: 2024

5. **Title**: Fed-EINI: An Efficient and Interpretable Inference Framework for Decision Tree Ensembles in Vertical Federated Learning (arXiv:2105.09540)
   - **Authors**: Xiaolin Chen, Shuai Zhou, Kai Yang, Hao Fao, Hu Wang, Yongji Wang
   - **Summary**: This paper presents Fed-EINI, a framework that enhances the interpretability and efficiency of decision tree ensembles in vertical federated learning. It addresses the challenge of maintaining model interpretability while ensuring data privacy in collaborative learning scenarios.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: Adaptive Personalization in Federated Learning for Highly Non-i.i.d. Data (arXiv:2207.03448)
   - **Authors**: Yousef Yeganeh, Azade Farshad, Johann Boschmann, Richard Gaus, Maximilian Frantzen, Nassir Navab
   - **Summary**: This work investigates an adaptive hierarchical clustering method in federated learning to produce intermediate semi-global models, allowing clients with similar data distributions to form more specialized models. The approach demonstrates significant performance gains in heterogeneous data distributions.
   - **Year**: 2022

7. **Title**: Federated and Differentially Private Learning for Electronic Health Records (arXiv:1911.05861)
   - **Authors**: Stephen R. Pfohl, Andrew M. Dai, Katherine Heller
   - **Summary**: This study examines the efficacy of centralized versus federated learning in both private and non-private settings for clinical prediction tasks. It highlights the challenges of applying differentially private stochastic gradient descent in federated settings, emphasizing the need for careful consideration of privacy mechanisms.
   - **Year**: 2019

8. **Title**: Adaptive Data Quality Scoring Operations Framework Using Machine Learning (arXiv:2408.06724)
   - **Authors**: F. Bayram, P. Aupke, B. S. Ahmed, A. Kassler, A. Theocharis, J. Forsman
   - **Summary**: This paper presents a framework for adaptive data quality scoring using machine learning, addressing the challenges of data quality assessment in data-driven applications. The approach emphasizes the importance of data quality in federated learning scenarios.
   - **Year**: 2024

9. **Title**: Privacy-Preserving Evolutionary Computation: A Survey (arXiv:2304.01205)
   - **Authors**: B. Zhao, X. Liu, A. Song, W.-N. Chen, K.-K. Lai, J. Zhang, R. H. Deng
   - **Summary**: This survey explores privacy-preserving techniques in evolutionary computation, discussing methods such as homomorphic encryption and secure multi-party computation. It provides insights into balancing optimization performance and privacy in distributed learning environments.
   - **Year**: 2023

10. **Title**: Federated Learning for Healthcare: Opportunities and Challenges (arXiv:2007.09173)
    - **Authors**: Peter Kairouz, H. Brendan McMahan, Brendan Avent, et al.
    - **Summary**: This comprehensive review discusses the potential of federated learning in healthcare, highlighting opportunities for collaborative model training without data sharing. It also addresses challenges such as data heterogeneity, privacy concerns, and regulatory considerations.
    - **Year**: 2020

**2. Key Challenges**

1. **Infrastructure Limitations**: Low-resource settings often face inadequate computational resources, unreliable internet connectivity, and limited access to advanced technologies, hindering the implementation of federated learning frameworks.

2. **Data Heterogeneity**: Variations in data distributions across different regions, demographics, and healthcare systems pose significant challenges in developing models that generalize well across diverse datasets.

3. **Privacy and Security Concerns**: Ensuring data privacy while facilitating collaborative learning requires robust privacy-preserving techniques, such as differential privacy and secure multi-party computation, which can be complex to implement effectively.

4. **Regulatory and Ethical Issues**: Navigating the diverse regulatory landscapes and ethical considerations across countries complicates the deployment of federated learning systems in global health contexts.

5. **Model Interpretability**: Incorporating semi-mechanistic disease transmission priors into federated learning models to enhance interpretability for policymakers is challenging, especially when balancing model complexity and performance.
