# Title: Bridging Stability Guarantees and Regret Bounds: A Unified Framework for Safe Online Learning in Nonlinear Control Systems

## Motivation
A fundamental gap exists between RL and control theory: RL focuses on regret minimization and sample complexity, while control theory emphasizes stability and robustness guarantees. Current approaches often sacrifice one for the other—safe controllers may be overly conservative with poor learning efficiency, while regret-optimal algorithms may violate safety constraints during exploration. This disconnect limits deployment in high-stakes applications like industrial automation and autonomous vehicles, where both efficient learning and guaranteed stability are essential. A unified theoretical framework connecting these performance measures would enable algorithms that learn efficiently while maintaining rigorous safety guarantees.

## Main Idea
We propose developing a unified theoretical framework that explicitly characterizes the trade-off between regret bounds and stability margins in online learning for nonlinear control systems. Our methodology involves: (1) defining a "stability-regret Pareto frontier" that mathematically quantifies the fundamental limits of achieving both objectives simultaneously; (2) developing Lyapunov-guided exploration strategies where exploration decisions are constrained by learned certificate functions that guarantee forward invariance of safe sets; (3) establishing sample complexity bounds that depend explicitly on the system's stability margin and nonlinearity structure. We will derive information-theoretic lower bounds showing when stability requirements fundamentally limit learning speed. Expected outcomes include new algorithms with provable regret bounds *conditioned on* maintained stability, and theoretical results identifying system classes where safety comes "for free" versus cases requiring explicit trade-offs. This framework would provide practitioners with principled guidance for deploying learning-based control in safety-critical applications.
