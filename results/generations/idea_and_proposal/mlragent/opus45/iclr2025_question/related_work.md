1. **Title**: Uncertainty Quantification of Large Language Models through Multi-Dimensional Responses (arXiv:2502.16820)
   - **Authors**: Tiejin Chen, Xiaoou Liu, Longchao Da, Jia Chen, Vagelis Papalexakis, Hua Wei
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces a multi-dimensional uncertainty quantification framework for large language models (LLMs) that integrates semantic and knowledge-aware similarity analysis. By generating multiple responses and leveraging auxiliary LLMs to extract implicit knowledge, the authors construct similarity matrices and apply tensor decomposition to derive a comprehensive uncertainty representation. This approach captures both semantic variations and factual consistency, leading to more accurate uncertainty quantification.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: A Graph Signal Processing Framework for Hallucination Detection in Large Language Models (arXiv:2510.19117)
   - **Authors**: Valentin Noël
   - **Summary**: This work proposes a spectral analysis framework that models transformer layers as dynamic graphs induced by attention mechanisms, with token embeddings as signals on these graphs. Through graph signal processing, the author defines diagnostics such as Dirichlet energy and spectral entropy to detect hallucinations in LLMs. Experiments across GPT architectures suggest that spectral geometry may capture reasoning patterns and error behaviors, offering a framework for hallucination detection.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Uncertainty Quantification and Confidence Calibration in Large Language Models: A Survey (arXiv:2503.15850)
   - **Authors**: Xiaoou Liu, Tiejin Chen, Longchao Da, Chacha Chen, Zhen Lin, Hua Wei
   - **Summary**: This survey examines existing uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods for LLMs, categorizing them based on computational efficiency and uncertainty dimensions. The authors identify unique uncertainty sources in LLMs, such as input ambiguity and reasoning path divergence, and emphasize the need for scalable, interpretable, and robust UQ approaches to enhance LLM reliability.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Improving Uncertainty Quantification in Large Language Models via Semantic Embeddings (arXiv:2410.22685)
   - **Authors**: Yashvir S. Grewal, Edwin V. Bonilla, Thang D. Bui
   - **Summary**: This paper proposes a novel approach that leverages semantic embeddings to achieve smoother and more robust estimation of semantic uncertainty in LLMs. By capturing semantic similarities without depending on sequence likelihoods, the method reduces biases introduced by irrelevant words in the answers. An amortized version models semantics as latent variables in a joint probabilistic model, allowing for uncertainty estimation with a single forward pass, significantly reducing computational overhead.
   - **Year**: 2024

**Key Challenges**:

1. **Computational Efficiency**: Many existing uncertainty quantification methods require multiple forward passes or complex sampling techniques, leading to significant computational overhead that hinders real-time deployment.

2. **Distinguishing Uncertainty Types**: Current approaches often fail to effectively separate epistemic uncertainty (model's lack of knowledge) from aleatoric uncertainty (inherent ambiguity), which is crucial for appropriate response strategies.

3. **Scalability**: Methods that work well on smaller models or datasets may not scale effectively to the size and complexity of modern LLMs, limiting their practical applicability.

4. **Interpretability**: Providing clear and actionable uncertainty estimates that can be easily understood and utilized by end-users remains a significant challenge.

5. **Hallucination Detection**: Developing reliable mechanisms to detect and mitigate hallucinations in LLM outputs without compromising their generative capabilities is an ongoing research challenge.
