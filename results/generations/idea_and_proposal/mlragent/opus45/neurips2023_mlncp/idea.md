## Title: Noise-Aware Energy-Based Models for Analog Hardware Acceleration

## Motivation: 
Energy-based models (EBMs) have shown remarkable expressive power but remain computationally prohibitive on digital hardware due to expensive MCMC sampling during training and inference. Analog computing substrates naturally perform energy minimization through physical dynamics, making them ideal candidates for EBM acceleration. However, the inherent noise and device variability in analog systems have been viewed as obstacles. This research proposes to flip this perspective: rather than fighting analog noise, we can design EBMs that explicitly leverage stochasticity as a computational resource for sampling, turning a hardware limitation into an algorithmic advantage.

## Main Idea:
We propose co-designing EBMs with analog hardware by incorporating hardware noise profiles directly into the model's energy landscape. Our methodology involves: (1) characterizing the noise distribution of target analog devices (e.g., memristor crossbars or photonic systems), (2) training EBMs with noise-injection layers that match hardware characteristics, effectively performing "hardware-in-the-loop" training, and (3) using the physical system's natural thermal or shot noise as a source of stochasticity for Langevin dynamics sampling. We will develop a differentiable noise simulator calibrated to real hardware measurements, enabling gradient-based optimization of noise-robust energy functions. Expected outcomes include 10-100x speedup in EBM inference with comparable sample quality, and demonstration on generative modeling tasks. This approach establishes a principled framework for hardware-algorithm co-design that embraces imperfection.
