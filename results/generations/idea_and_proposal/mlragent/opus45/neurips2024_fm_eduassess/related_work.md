1. **Title**: Practical and Ethical Challenges of Large Language Models in Education: A Systematic Scoping Review (arXiv:2303.13379)
   - **Authors**: Lixiang Yan, Lele Sha, Linxuan Zhao, Yuheng Li, Roberto Martinez-Maldonado, Guanliang Chen, Xinyu Li, Yueqiao Jin, Dragan Gašević
   - **Summary**: This paper reviews 118 studies on the use of large language models (LLMs) in educational tasks, identifying 53 use cases across nine categories, including grading and feedback provision. It highlights challenges such as low technological readiness, lack of transparency, and insufficient privacy considerations, offering recommendations for future research to enhance the practical and ethical deployment of LLMs in education.
   - **Year**: 2023

2. **Title**: Assessing LLM Text Detection in Educational Contexts: Does Human Contribution Affect Detection? (arXiv:2508.08096)
   - **Authors**: Lukas Gehring, Benjamin Paaßen
   - **Summary**: This study introduces the Generative Essay Detection in Education (GEDE) dataset, comprising over 900 student-written essays and 12,500 LLM-generated essays. It evaluates state-of-the-art detectors' performance across varying levels of human and LLM contributions, revealing that detectors struggle with intermediate levels of human involvement, leading to high false positive rates.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: CoDAE: Adapting Large Language Models for Education via Chain-of-Thought Data Augmentation (arXiv:2508.08386)
   - **Authors**: Shuzhou Yuan, William LaCroix, Hardik Ghoshal, Ercong Nie, Michael Färber
   - **Summary**: The authors present CoDAE, a framework that fine-tunes LLMs for educational applications using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data augmentation. By collecting real-world dialogues and enriching them with CoT prompting, the study addresses issues like over-compliance and low response adaptivity, resulting in models that provide more pedagogically appropriate guidance and resist premature answer disclosure.
   - **Year**: 2025

4. **Title**: Adapting Large Language Models for Education: Foundational Capabilities, Potentials, and Challenges (arXiv:2401.08664)
   - **Authors**: Qingyao Li, Lingyue Fu, Weiming Zhang, Xianyu Chen, Jingwei Yu, Wei Xia, Weinan Zhang, Ruiming Tang, Yong Yu
   - **Summary**: This paper reviews recent research on LLMs' educational capabilities, focusing on areas like mathematics, writing, programming, reasoning, and knowledge-based question answering. It assesses the current state of LLMs in these domains, identifies existing deficiencies, and evaluates the generalizability of development methods for constructing comprehensive educational models.
   - **Year**: 2023

5. **Title**: Benchmarking Large Language Models on Homework Assessment in Circuit Analysis

6. **Title**: Elimination-based Reasoning with LLM for Multiple-Choice Educational Question Answering
   - **Authors**: Not specified
   - **Summary**: The authors introduce an elimination-based reasoning framework that enables LLMs to simulate human decision-making by sequentially eliminating incorrect options in multiple-choice questions. This approach incorporates structured prompting and intermediate decision steps, resulting in improved accuracy and reliability on educational QA benchmarks.
   - **Year**: 2025

7. **Title**: Bridging Text and Knowledge: Explainable AI for Knowledge Graph Classification and Concept Map-Based Semantic Domain Discovery with OBOE Framework
   - **Authors**: Raúl A. del Águila Escobar, María del Carmen Suárez-Figueroa, Mariano Fernández López, Boris Villazón Terrazas
   - **Summary**: This study extends the OBOE framework to address explainable domain discovery in knowledge graph classification. By integrating Knowledge Graph Embeddings, clustering algorithms, and LLMs, the approach reveals semantic domains and generates structural explanations, enhancing the interpretability of knowledge graphs derived from concept maps.
   - **Year**: 2025

8. **Title**: Explainable AI for Education: Enhancing Essay Scoring via Rubric-Aligned Chain-of-Thought Prompting
   - **Authors**: Not specified
   - **Summary**: The authors propose QwenScore+, a framework that integrates rubric-aware Chain-of-Thought prompting with reinforcement learning from human feedback to improve the transparency and quality of automated feedback. Evaluated on an IELTS writing dataset, QwenScore+ outperforms strong baselines in feedback generation and trait-level scoring, aligning generated feedback with human reasoning patterns.
   - **Year**: 2025

9. **Title**: Evaluating LLM-Generated Q&A Test: A Student-Centered Study
   - **Authors**: Anna Wróblewska, Bartosz Grabek, Jakub Świstak, Daniel Dan
   - **Summary**: This research develops an automatic pipeline for generating reliable question-answer tests using AI chatbots. By generating a GPT-4o-mini-based Q&A test for a Natural Language Processing course and evaluating its psychometric and perceived-quality metrics with students and experts, the study assesses the effectiveness of LLM-generated assessments in educational settings.
   - **Year**: 2025

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Lack of Explainability**: LLMs often provide holistic scores without transparent justifications, making it difficult for stakeholders to understand the reasoning behind specific scores.

2. **Technological Readiness**: The integration of LLMs into educational assessments faces challenges related to the models' readiness, including issues with accuracy, reliability, and scalability.

3. **Ethical Considerations**: Concerns about privacy, data security, and potential biases in LLM-generated assessments pose significant ethical challenges.

4. **Adaptability to Educational Contexts**: LLMs may struggle to adapt to the nuanced requirements of educational assessments, such as providing personalized feedback and accommodating diverse learning objectives.

5. **Detection of AI-Generated Content**: Effectively distinguishing between human-written and LLM-generated content remains a challenge, complicating the enforcement of academic integrity.
