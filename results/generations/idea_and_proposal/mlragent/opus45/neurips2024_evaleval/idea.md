# Title: Crowdsourced Stakeholder-Centric Evaluation Frameworks for Generative AI Impacts

## Motivation
Current evaluation practices for generative AI broader impacts remain siloed within ML research communities, lacking systematic input from affected stakeholders such as content creators, educators, healthcare workers, and marginalized communities. This disconnect leads to evaluations that miss real-world harms and fail to capture context-specific impacts. Without standardized methods for incorporating diverse perspectives, impact assessments remain incomplete and potentially biased toward technical metrics over lived experiences.

## Main Idea
I propose developing a **participatory evaluation protocol** that systematically integrates stakeholder feedback into generative AI impact assessments. The methodology involves:

1. **Stakeholder Mapping Taxonomy**: Create a structured framework categorizing affected groups (direct users, indirect affected parties, vulnerable populations) for different generative AI modalities (text, image, audio, video).

2. **Modular Evaluation Templates**: Design standardized yet adaptable survey instruments and interview protocols that non-ML experts can use to report observed impacts, validated through pilot studies with community organizations.

3. **Aggregation Pipeline**: Develop methods to synthesize qualitative stakeholder feedback into quantifiable impact indicators that complement technical benchmarks.

**Expected Outcomes**: A publicly available toolkit enabling researchers to conduct inclusive impact evaluations, alongside empirical findings comparing stakeholder-identified harms versus researcher-anticipated harms across 3-4 generative AI applications. This bridges the expertise gap highlighted in the workshop's focus on breadth of participation while creating reproducible community resources.
