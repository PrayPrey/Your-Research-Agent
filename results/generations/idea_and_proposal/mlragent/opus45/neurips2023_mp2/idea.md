# Title: Moral Foundations Theory for Pluralistic Value Alignment in Large Language Models

## Motivation
Current AI alignment approaches like RLHF often rely on homogeneous annotator pools, predominantly from Western, educated, industrialized populations. This creates systems that reflect narrow moral perspectives while claiming universal alignment. Moral Foundations Theory (MFT) from moral psychology identifies six cross-cultural moral foundations (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty) that vary in emphasis across cultures and political orientations. Leveraging MFT can help diagnose and address the monolithic value problem in AI systems.

## Main Idea
I propose a framework called **Moral Foundations-Aware Alignment (MFAA)** that incorporates MFT into the alignment pipeline. First, we develop a diagnostic tool that profiles LLM outputs across the six moral foundations, revealing which foundations are over/under-represented. Second, we create a diverse feedback dataset stratified by annotators' moral foundation profiles (measured via the Moral Foundations Questionnaire), ensuring representation across the moral spectrum. Third, we introduce a pluralistic reward modeling approach that learns foundation-specific reward functions rather than collapsing diverse values into a single signal. The system can then generate responses calibrated to different moral frameworks or explicitly acknowledge moral trade-offs. Expected outcomes include more culturally inclusive AI systems and transparent documentation of embedded values. This bridges moral psychology research with practical alignment methodology, offering a principled alternative to current homogenizing practices.
