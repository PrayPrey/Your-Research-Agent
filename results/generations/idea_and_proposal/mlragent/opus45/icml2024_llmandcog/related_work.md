1. **Title**: Cognitive Biases in Large Language Models: A Survey and Mitigation Experiments (arXiv:2412.00323)
   - **Authors**: Yasuaki Sumita, Koh Takeuchi, Hisashi Kashima
   - **Summary**: This paper surveys existing studies on cognitive biases in LLMs and evaluates mitigation techniques inspired by human bias reduction methods. The authors test the effectiveness of SoPro and AwaRe methods on GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, finding that AwaRe significantly reduces bias effects, leading to more rational responses.
   - **Year**: 2024

2. **Title**: Towards the Worst-case Robustness of Large Language Models (arXiv:2501.19040)
   - **Authors**: Huanran Chen, Yinpeng Dong, Zeming Wei, Hang Su, Jun Zhu
   - **Summary**: This study examines the vulnerability of LLMs to adversarial attacks and proposes DiffTextPure, a defense mechanism that diffuses input prompts and purifies them using a pre-trained language model. The authors provide theoretical robustness bounds and certify the defense's effectiveness against various attacks.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: A Comprehensive Evaluation of Cognitive Biases in LLMs (arXiv:2410.15413)
   - **Authors**: Simon Malberg, Roman Poletukhin, Carolin M. Schuster, Georg Groh
   - **Summary**: The authors present a large-scale evaluation of 30 cognitive biases across 20 state-of-the-art LLMs. They introduce a novel test framework and a benchmark dataset with 30,000 tests, confirming the presence of all tested biases in at least some models.
   - **Year**: 2024

4. **Title**: Benchmarking Cognitive Biases in Large Language Models as Evaluators (arXiv:2309.17012)
   - **Authors**: Ryan Koo, Minhwa Lee, Vipul Raheja, Jong Inn Park, Zae Myung Kim, Dongyeop Kang
   - **Summary**: This paper assesses the presence of cognitive biases in LLMs when used as evaluators. The authors introduce the Cognitive Bias Benchmark for LLMs as Evaluators (CoBBLEr) and find that LLMs exhibit significant biases, questioning their robustness as evaluators.
   - **Year**: 2023

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Detection and Measurement of Biases**: Accurately identifying and quantifying cognitive biases in LLMs remains challenging due to the complexity and subtlety of these biases.

2. **Mitigation Strategies**: Developing effective methods to reduce or eliminate biases without compromising model performance is an ongoing area of research.

3. **Alignment with Human Preferences**: Ensuring that LLM outputs align with human judgments and preferences, especially in the presence of biases, is difficult.

4. **Adversarial Robustness**: LLMs are susceptible to adversarial attacks that exploit biases, highlighting the need for robust defense mechanisms.

5. **Evaluation Frameworks**: Establishing comprehensive and reliable evaluation frameworks to assess cognitive biases and robustness in LLMs is essential for progress in this field.
