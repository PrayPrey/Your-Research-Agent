1. **Title**: RIDE: Difficulty Evolving Perturbation with Item Response Theory for Mathematical Reasoning (arXiv:2511.04120)
   - **Authors**: Xinyuan Li, Murong Xu, Wenbiao Tao, Hanlun Zhu, Yike Zhao, Jipeng Zhang, Yunshi Lan
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces RIDE, an adversarial question-rewriting framework that employs Item Response Theory (IRT) to measure question difficulty and generate more challenging variations of mathematical problems. By utilizing responses from 35 LLMs to simulate students, RIDE creates a difficulty ranker that guides a question-rewriting model through reinforcement learning. The perturbed questions produced by RIDE lead to a significant performance drop in advanced LLMs, highlighting their limited robustness in mathematical reasoning.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: ScaleDiff: Scaling Difficult Problems for Advanced Mathematical Reasoning (arXiv:2509.21070)
   - **Authors**: Qizhi Pei, Zhuoshi Pan, Honglin Lin, Xin Gao, Yu Li, Zinan Tang, Conghui He, Rui Yan, Lijun Wu
   - **Summary**: ScaleDiff presents a pipeline designed to scale the creation of difficult mathematical problems efficiently. By identifying challenging problems from existing datasets using an adaptive thinking model, the authors train a specialized problem generator (DiffGen-8B) capable of producing new difficult problems at scale. Fine-tuning models on the ScaleDiff-Math dataset results in substantial performance improvements, demonstrating the effectiveness of this approach in enhancing advanced reasoning capabilities without relying on larger, more expensive teacher models.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Mathador-LM: A Dynamic Benchmark for Mathematical Reasoning on Large Language Models (arXiv:2406.12572)
   - **Authors**: Eldar Kurtic, Amir Moeini, Dan Alistarh
   - **Summary**: Mathador-LM introduces a dynamic benchmark inspired by the Mathador game to evaluate mathematical reasoning in LLMs. The benchmark generates instances dynamically, following target difficulty levels, thereby mitigating concerns about test-set leakage into training data. Evaluations reveal that contemporary models struggle with Mathador-LM, scoring significantly lower than average third graders, in contrast to their strong performance on other mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
   - **Year**: 2024

4. **Title**: Teaching LLMs According to Their Aptitude: Adaptive Reasoning for Mathematical Problem Solving (arXiv:2502.12022)
   - **Authors**: Xin Xu, Yan Xu, Tianhao Chen, Yuchen Yan, Chengwu Liu, Zaoyu Chen, Yufei Wang, Yichun Yin, Yasheng Wang, Lifeng Shang, Qun Liu
   - **Summary**: This work proposes TATA, an adaptive framework that enables LLMs to personalize their reasoning strategies based on their intrinsic capabilities. By incorporating base-LLM-aware data selection during supervised fine-tuning, TATA equips LLMs to autonomously determine and apply appropriate reasoning strategies at test time. Experiments demonstrate that TATA effectively combines the strengths of Chain-of-Thought and Tool-Integrated Reasoning, achieving superior performance with improved inference efficiency.
   - **Year**: 2025

5. **Title**: BRIGHT: A Realistic and Challenging Benchmark for Reasoning-Intensive Retrieval (arXiv:2407.12883)

Augmenting queries with Chain-of-Thought reasoning generated by LLMs improves performance, highlighting the need for more realistic and challenging retrieval benchmarks.

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Static Benchmark Limitations**: Traditional mathematical reasoning benchmarks often have fixed difficulty levels, leading to rapid saturation as LLMs improve, making it challenging to measure genuine progress.

2. **Data Contamination**: The presence of test problems in training data raises concerns about models memorizing solutions rather than demonstrating true reasoning capabilities.

3. **Dynamic Benchmark Development**: Creating benchmarks that evolve with model capabilities requires sophisticated mechanisms to generate and calibrate problems dynamically.

4. **Discriminative Power Assessment**: Ensuring that generated problems effectively differentiate between models based on reasoning depth rather than memorization poses a significant challenge.

5. **Automated Solution Verification**: Implementing reliable automated methods to verify solutions, such as symbolic solvers, is crucial for maintaining the integrity of dynamic benchmarks.
