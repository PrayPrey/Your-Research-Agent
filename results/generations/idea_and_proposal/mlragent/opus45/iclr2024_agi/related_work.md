1. **Title**: Speaking in Words, Thinking in Logic: A Dual-Process Framework in QA Systems (arXiv:2507.20491)
   - **Authors**: Tuan Bui, Trong Le, Phat Thai, Sang Nguyen, Minh Hua, Ngan Pham, Thang Bui, Tho Quan
   - **Summary**: This paper introduces Text-JEPA, a framework that translates natural language into first-order logic, inspired by dual-process cognitive theory. It combines an efficient logic representation generator (System 1) with a Z3 solver for logical inference (System 2), aiming to enhance explainability in QA systems.
   - **Year**: 2025

2. **Title**: Planning Like Human: A Dual-process Framework for Dialogue Planning (arXiv:2406.05374)
   - **Authors**: Tao He, Lizi Liao, Yixin Cao, Yuanxing Liu, Ming Liu, Zerui Chen, Bing Qin
   - **Summary**: The authors propose the Dual-Process Dialogue Planning (DPDP) framework, inspired by dual-process theory, featuring an instinctive policy model for familiar contexts and a deliberative Monte Carlo Tree Search mechanism for complex scenarios. This approach aims to improve dialogue planning efficiency and quality.
   - **Year**: 2024

3. **Title**: Fast and Slow Generating: An Empirical Study on Large and Small Language Models Collaborative Decoding (arXiv:2406.12295)
   - **Authors**: Kaiyan Zhang, Jianyu Wang, Ning Ding, Biqing Qi, Ermo Hua, Xingtai Lv, Bowen Zhou
   - **Summary**: This study presents the Fast and Slow Generating (FS-GEN) framework, inspired by dual-process cognitive theory, where large language models (System 2) collaborate with small language models (System 1) during decoding. The framework aims to balance efficiency and performance by dynamically allocating tasks based on uncertainty.
   - **Year**: 2024

4. **Title**: Think More, Hallucinate Less: Mitigating Hallucinations via Dual Process of Fast and Slow Thinking (arXiv:2501.01306)
   - **Authors**: Xiaoxue Cheng, Junyi Li, Wayne Xin Zhao, Ji-Rong Wen

It employs a hierarchical thinking system switch mechanism inspired by dual-process theory to balance efficiency and quality.
   - **Year**: 2025

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Integration Complexity**: Developing architectures that effectively combine intuitive (Type I) and analytical (Type II) reasoning processes without introducing excessive computational overhead remains a significant challenge.

2. **Metacognitive Control**: Designing robust metacognitive controllers capable of accurately assessing task complexity and uncertainty to appropriately delegate tasks between reasoning pathways is complex and requires further research.

3. **Scalability**: Ensuring that dual-process architectures can scale efficiently to handle large-scale applications without compromising performance or efficiency is a critical concern.

4. **Explainability**: Balancing the need for transparent, explainable reasoning processes with the efficiency of intuitive responses poses a challenge in the design of hybrid cognitive architectures.

5. **Generalization**: Achieving robust generalization across diverse problem domains while maintaining the flexibility to switch between reasoning modes is an ongoing challenge in the pursuit of artificial general intelligence.
