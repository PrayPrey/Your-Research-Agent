# Title: Adversarial Language Games for Emergent Reasoning in LLMs

## Motivation
Current LLM training relies heavily on static supervision, limiting their ability to develop robust reasoning and planning capabilities. While humans sharpen cognitive skills through competitive and cooperative interactions, LLMs lack exposure to dynamic, goal-oriented exchanges that demand strategic thinking. Existing self-play approaches focus narrowly on specific tasks (e.g., debate) without systematically targeting reasoning emergence. By designing adversarial language games with varying complexity levels, we can create training environments where LLMs must develop genuine reasoning abilities to succeed, rather than pattern-matching from training data.

## Main Idea
We propose a multi-agent framework where LLMs engage in structured adversarial language games designed to elicit reasoning capabilities. The methodology involves: (1) designing a curriculum of games with increasing strategic depth—from simple referential games to complex negotiation and deduction scenarios (e.g., "20 Questions" with deceptive agents, collaborative mystery-solving); (2) training via self-play with population-based methods to prevent collapse and encourage diverse strategies; (3) using game-theoretic metrics (Nash equilibrium proximity, exploitability) alongside linguistic coherence to shape rewards. Expected outcomes include improved multi-step reasoning, better theory-of-mind modeling, and enhanced planning capabilities. We will evaluate on reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, ARC) and novel interactive testbeds. This approach bridges deep RL, language emergence, and modern NLP, offering a scalable path toward LLMs that genuinely reason through interaction rather than imitation.
