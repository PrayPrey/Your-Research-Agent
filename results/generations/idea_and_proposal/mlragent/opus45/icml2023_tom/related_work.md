1. **Title**: Mutual Theory of Mind in Human-AI Collaboration: An Empirical Study with LLM-driven AI Agents in a Real-time Shared Workspace Task (arXiv:2409.08811)
   - **Authors**: Shao Zhang, Xihuai Wang, Wenhao Zhang, Yongshan Chen, Landi Gao, Dakuo Wang, Weinan Zhang, Xinbing Wang, Ying Wen
   - **Summary**: This study investigates the impact of AI agents equipped with Theory of Mind (ToM) capabilities on human-AI team performance in real-time shared workspace tasks. Findings indicate that while ToM capabilities do not significantly enhance team performance, they improve human understanding of the AI agent and the feeling of being understood.
   - **Year**: 2024

2. **Title**: Automated Meta Prompt Engineering for Alignment with the Theory of Mind (arXiv:2505.09024)
   - **Authors**: Aaron Baughman, Rahul Agarwal, Eduardo Morales, Gozde Akay
   - **Summary**: The authors introduce a meta-prompting method that optimizes the alignment between human mental expectations and Large Language Models (LLMs) through agentic reinforcement learning. This approach aims to solve the Theory of Mind alignment problem by anticipating and incorporating human edits within LLM-generated text, enhancing content quality and alignment with human expectations.
   - **Year**: 2025

3. **Title**: Spontaneous Theory of Mind for Artificial Intelligence (arXiv:2402.13272)
   - **Authors**: Nikolos Gurney, David V. Pynadath, Volkan Ustun
   - **Summary**: This paper contrasts prompted Theory of Mind (ToM) with spontaneous ToM, emphasizing the need for AI systems to engage in unintentional, possibly uncontrollable social reasoning. The authors argue for a principled approach to developing AI ToM that includes both prompted and spontaneous reasoning to achieve robust Artificial Social Intelligence.
   - **Year**: 2024

4. **Title**: ChatEL: Entity Linking with Chatbots (arXiv:2402.14858)
   - **Authors**: Yifan Ding, Qingkai Zeng, Tim Weninger
   - **Summary**: ChatEL is a framework that enhances entity linking in chatbots by prompting Large Language Models (LLMs) with mention, context, and entity candidates. This approach improves the accuracy of entity linking, demonstrating the potential of prompt engineering in aligning LLM outputs with user intent.
   - **Year**: 2024

5. **Title**: Play Me Something “Icy”: Practical Challenges, Explainability and the Semantic Gap in Generative AI Music (arXiv:2408.07224)
   - **Authors**: Carlos G. Román, Morgan Weeks, Jesse Allison, Drew Farrar, Treya Nash, Fiona Xue Ju
   - **Summary**: This paper explores the challenges in using text-based prompts for generative AI music tools, highlighting the semantic gap between language and music. The authors discuss the need for improved explainability and user control to bridge this gap, which is relevant to prompt engineering in human-AI collaboration.
   - **Year**: 2024

6. **Title**: Mental-LLM: Leveraging Large Language Models for Mental Health Support (arXiv:2307.14385)
   - **Authors**: Xu et al.
   - **Summary**: The authors present Mental-LLM, a framework that utilizes Large Language Models to provide mental health support. By incorporating Theory of Mind principles, the system aims to understand and respond to users' mental states, improving the effectiveness of AI-driven mental health interventions.
   - **Year**: 2023

7. **Title**: Improving Humanness of Virtual Agents and Users’ Cooperation through Emotions (arXiv:1903.03980)
   - **Authors**: Jesse Hoey, Neil Budnarain, Moojan Ghafurian
   - **Summary**: This study examines how virtual agents designed with emotional capabilities, based on appraisal theories of human emotion, can enhance perceptions of humanness and cooperation in social dilemmas. The findings suggest that incorporating emotional understanding into AI agents can improve human-AI collaboration.
   - **Year**: 2024

8. **Title**: Collective Intelligence in Human-AI Teams: A Bayesian Theory of Mind Approach (arXiv:2208.11660)
   - **Authors**: Samuel Westby, Christoph Riedl
   - **Summary**: The authors develop a network of Bayesian agents that model the mental states of teammates from observed communication. The study shows that such agents can generate interventions that improve the collective intelligence of human-AI teams, highlighting the importance of Theory of Mind in collaborative settings.
   - **Year**: 2023

9. **Title**: On the Link Between Conscious Function and General Intelligence in Humans and Machines (arXiv:2204.05133)
   - **Authors**: Arthur Juliani, Kai Arulkumaran, Shuntaro Sasai, Ryota Kanai
   - **Summary**: This paper explores the relationship between conscious function and general intelligence, examining contemporary theories of conscious function and their relevance to artificial intelligence. The authors propose that integrating aspects of these theories can enhance the general intelligence of AI systems, which is pertinent to developing Theory of Mind capabilities.
   - **Year**: 2023

10. **Title**: Human–AI Collaboration Enables More Empathic Conversations in Text-Based Peer-to-Peer Mental Health Support (arXiv:2305.09617)
    - **Authors**: Ashish Sharma, Inna W. Lin, Adam S. Miner, David C. Atkins, Tim Althoff
    - **Summary**: This study demonstrates that human-AI collaboration can lead to more empathic conversations in text-based mental health support. By leveraging AI systems that understand and respond to users' mental states, the quality of support provided in peer-to-peer settings is improved.
    - **Year**: 2023

**Key Challenges:**

1. **Accurate User Intent Modeling**: Developing AI systems that can accurately infer and model user intent, including unstated goals and implicit assumptions, remains a significant challenge.

2. **Integration of Theory of Mind in AI**: Incorporating robust Theory of Mind capabilities into AI systems to enable effective perspective-taking and mental state inference is complex and requires further research.

3. **Balancing Communication Load**: Ensuring that AI agents can engage in effective communication without increasing the cognitive burden on users is a critical challenge in human-AI collaboration.

4. **Semantic Gap in Prompt Engineering**: Bridging the semantic gap between user language and AI understanding, especially in abstract domains like music or emotions, poses difficulties in prompt engineering.

5. **Evaluation Metrics for Human-AI Collaboration**: Establishing comprehensive and reliable metrics to evaluate the effectiveness of human-AI collaboration, particularly in terms of user satisfaction and task efficiency, is an ongoing challenge.
